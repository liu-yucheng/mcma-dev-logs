The bumpy sphere volume

Part 1: the volume

Step 1.1: the radius bounds

The sine terms stay in $[-1, 1]$, so the surface is a star about the origin.

$$ \frac{4}{5} \leq \rho \leq \frac{6}{5} $$

The exact bounds $4/5 = 0.8$ and $6/5 = 1.2$ are printed by part1_step1.py.

part1_step1.py compares the exact bounds with a sample of 200000 angle pairs.

A reader may take $\rho$ negative, but the bound stays above $4/5$.

Step 1.2: the exact volume

The star volume is one third of the surface integral of $\rho^3$.

$$ V = \frac{1}{3} \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi} \left(1 + \frac{1}{5} \sin 6\theta \sin 5\phi\right)^3 \sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta = \frac{136\pi}{99} $$

The exact value $\frac{136\pi}{99} \approx 4.315723$ is printed by part1_step2.py.

part1_step2.py compares the closed form with a 160 by 160 Gauss rule that gives $4.315723241$.

A reader may keep the odd terms, but they integrate to zero over the full angles.

Step 1.3: the grid check

A midpoint grid over the angles gives an independent estimate.

$$ V \approx \sum_i \sum_j \frac{\rho_{ij}^3 \sin \phi_j}{3} \Delta\theta \Delta\phi $$

The grid of 2400 by 1200 cells gives $4.315724$, and the sphere bounds $2.144661$ and $7.238229$ are printed by part1_step3.py.

part1_step3.py compares the grid with the closed form and with the two bounding sphere volumes.

A reader may forget the factor $\sin\phi$, but the solid angle element has it.

The surface encloses

$$ V = \frac{136\pi}{99} \approx 4.315723 \text{ cubic units} $$
  • The odd terms vanish over the full angles.
  • The squared term adds $4\pi/33$ to the double integral.
  • The solid lies between spheres of radius $4/5$ and $6/5$.
