Volume between two paraboloids

Use a triple integral to find the volume of the solid. The solid is enclosed by the paraboloids $y = x^2 + z^2$ and $y = 8 - x^2 - z^2$.

Part 1: find the volume $V$

Step 1.1: find the common circle

Both surfaces give the same $y$ on the circle $r = 2$, where $r^2 = x^2 + z^2$. Inside that circle the lower surface is $y = r^2$ and the upper one is $y = 8 - r^2$.

$$ r^2 = 8 - r^2, \text{ so } r = 2 \text{ and } y = 4 $$

The slices at $r = 0$, $1$, $2$ span $y$ from $0$ to $8$, from $1$ to $7$, and close at $y = 4$, and the shell sum gives $50.2655$, printed by part1_step1.py.

part1_step1.py compares the shell sum with the closed form $16\pi$.

A reader may reverse the two surfaces and integrate the negative band $r^2 - (8 - r^2)$.

Step 1.2: set up the cylindrical integral

In cylindrical coordinates about the y-axis the element is $r \, dr \, d\theta \, dy$, and the band height is $8 - 2r^2$.

$$ V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 \int_{r^2}^{8 - r^2} r \, dy \, dr \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^2 (8 - 2r^2) r \, dr \, d\theta = 16\pi $$

The exact volume is $16\pi = 50.2655$ and the grid disk area is $12.562$ against the target $12.566$, printed by part1_step2.py.

part1_step2.py compares the closed form $16\pi$ with the grid area of the disk of radius $2$.

A reader may forget the Jacobian $r$ and integrate $8 - 2r^2$ alone.

Step 1.3: Monte Carlo confirmation

The box $[-2,2] \times [0,8] \times [-2,2]$ contains the solid, so a random sample scales the hit ratio by the box volume.

$$ V \approx \frac{\text{Vol}(B)}{N} \sum_{p_i \in E} 1 $$

The Monte Carlo volume is $50.2963$ against the target $50.2655$, printed by part1_step3.py.

part1_step3.py compares the Monte Carlo estimate with the closed form $16\pi$.

A reader may forget the box volume factor and report the raw hit ratio.

Answer

The volume of the solid is $16\pi = 50.2655$.

Notes

  • The two paraboloids meet on the circle $r = 2$ at the height $y = 4$.
  • The cylindrical coordinates use the $x$-$z$ plane, since both surfaces are symmetric about the y-axis.
  • The order $dy$, $dr$, $d\theta$ keeps the band height a single expression.
