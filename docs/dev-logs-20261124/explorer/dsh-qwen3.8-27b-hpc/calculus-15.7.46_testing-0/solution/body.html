Moment of inertia of a cone about the z-axis

Assume the solid has constant density $k$. Find the moment of inertia about the z-axis of the solid cone $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \leq z \leq h$.

Part 1: the moment of inertia $I_z$

Step 1.1: the mass in polar form

The vertex sits at the origin, and the slice at height $z$ is the disk $0 \leq r \leq z$. The base disk lies in the plane $z = h$.

$$ M = \int_0^h \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^z k r \, dr \, d\theta \, dz = k\pi \frac{h^3}{3} $$

For $k = 1$ and $h = 1$ the exact mass is $1.0472$ and the grid mass is $1.04763$, printed by part1_step1.py.

part1_step1.py compares the closed form with a midpoint grid count of $120^3$ cells.

A reader may take the slice radius for $h$ and integrate a cylinder, but the limits give $r \leq z$.

Step 1.2: the z-axis inertia integral

The distance from the z-axis is $r$, so the integrand is $k r^2 \, dV$ and the element is $k r^3 \, dr \, d\theta \, dz$.

$$ I_z = \int_0^h \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^z k r^3 \, dr \, d\theta \, dz = k\pi \frac{h^5}{10} $$

For $k = 1$ and $h = 1$ the exact value is $0.31416$ and the grid value is $0.31428$, printed by part1_step2.py.

part1_step2.py compares the closed form with the midpoint grid using the weight $x^2 + y^2$.

A reader may forget the Jacobian $r$ and integrate $k r^2$ alone, which is a power too low.

Step 1.3: the horizontal slice check

Each disk of radius $z$ carries $dm = k\pi z^2 \, dz$ and disk inertia $\frac{1}{2} dm \, z^2$ about its own axis.

$$ I_z = \int_0^h \frac{1}{2} k\pi z^4 \, dz = k\pi \frac{h^5}{10}, \quad \frac{I_z}{M} = \frac{3 h^2}{10} $$

The slice route gives $k\pi h^5/10$ and the ratio $3h^2/10$, printed by part1_step3.py.

part1_step3.py compares the slice form with the grid value of step 1.2 at $k = 1$ and $h = 1$.

A reader may use the inertia of a rod for the slice, but the slice is a disk.

Answer

The moment of inertia about the z-axis is $I_z = k\pi h^5/10$, that is $0.31416$ for $k = 1$ and $h = 1$.

Notes

  • The vertex is at the origin and the base disk lies in the plane $z = h$.
  • The ratio $I_z / M = 3h^2/10$ depends only on the height.
  • The polar order $dr$, $d\theta$, $dz$ keeps the radius limit $r = z$ simple.
