Part 1: The Solid, Its Slices, and Its Volume

Step 1.1: Intersection circle and exact volume

Set the two surface heights equal to find the circle, then integrate the gap between the surfaces in cylindrical form.

$$r^2 = 5 - r^2 \;\Rightarrow\; r = \sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}, \quad z = \frac{5}{2}$$ $$V = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\sqrt{5/2}} (5 - 2r^2)\,r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{25\pi}{4} \approx 19.6350$$

The two surfaces meet at the circle $r = \sqrt{5/2} \approx 1.5811$, $z = 5/2 = 2.5000$; the volume is $V = \frac{25\pi}{4} \approx 19.6350$.

The script part1_step1.py checks the exact volume against a trapezoid sum with $4000$ radial steps.

A common slip reverses the surfaces and integrates $2r^2 - 5$, which is negative over the whole disk.

Step 1.2: Slice areas at three heights

A slice at height $z$ holds where $r^2 \le z$ and $r^2 \le 5 - z$, so the slice is a disk of radius $\min(\sqrt{z}, \sqrt{5 - z})$.

$$A(z) = \pi\min(z, 5 - z) \;\Rightarrow\; A(1) = \pi, \quad A\left(\frac{5}{2}\right) = \frac{5\pi}{2}, \quad A(4) = \pi$$

The slice areas are $A(1) \approx 3.1416$, $A(5/2) \approx 7.8540$, and $A(4) \approx 3.1416$.

The script part1_step2.py checks each area against a fixed-seed Monte Carlo count in the bounding square.

Using $r = \sqrt{5 - z}$ below $z = 5/2$ overstates the radius and doubles the area at $z = 1$.

Step 1.3: Monte Carlo volume check

Sample the bounding cylinder $r \le \sqrt{5/2}$, $0 \le z \le 5$ uniformly and count the points that sit between the two surfaces.

$$V \approx \pi\left(\frac{5}{2}\right) \cdot 5 \cdot \frac{N_{\text{in}}}{N} \approx 19.6350$$

The estimate from $N = 4\,000\,000$ samples lands within $0.05$ of the exact value $25\pi/4$.

The script part1_step3.py checks the estimate against the exact value from the symbolic integral.

Uniform $r$ sampling overweights the axis; use $r = \sqrt{5u/2}$ for a uniform disk.

The solid closes at the circle $r = \sqrt{5/2}$, $z = 5/2$. Its volume is $V = \frac{25\pi}{4} \approx 19.6350$.

  • Both surfaces rotate about the $z$-axis, so the angle integral reduces to a factor of $2\pi$.
