The triple integral of the height over a cylinder cut by two planes

Evaluate $\iiint_{E} z\,dV$, where $E$ is the solid bounded by the cylinder $y^{2} + z^{2} = 9$ and the planes $x = 0$, $y = 3x$ and $z = 0$ in the first octant.

Answer. $\iiint_{E} z\,dV = \dfrac{27}{8} = 3.375000000$.

The solid has the volume $3$, and the mean height of a point of the solid is $\dfrac{9}{8} = 1.125000000$.

The three projections have the areas $\dfrac{9\pi}{4}$ on the plane $x = 0$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ on the plane $y = 0$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$ on the plane $z = 0$.

Part 1: the solid and its three projections

Step 1.1: turn the four boundary words into four inequalities

Reason

The word "bounded" names one surface for each condition, and each point of the solid must stay on one chosen side of that surface. The first octant is the part of space where none of the three coordinates is negative. Write each condition as an inequality before you draw anything, because a picture of a curved surface hides the side that the solid keeps.

A reader may take the cylinder to be $x^{2} + y^{2} = 9$ with its axis on $z$, but the equation names $y$ and $z$, so the axis of this cylinder is the $x$ axis.

Computation

The cylinder gives $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$. The plane $x = 0$ gives $x \ge 0$. The plane $y = 3x$ gives $y \ge 3x$. The plane $z = 0$ gives $z \ge 0$. The first octant repeats $x \ge 0$ and $z \ge 0$ and adds $y \ge 0$.

The condition $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$ forces $z \le 3$. The conditions $0 \le 3x \le y$ with $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$ force $y \le 3$. The solid is therefore

$$E = \{(x, y, z) : 0 \le x,\ 3x \le y,\ y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9,\ 0 \le z\}.$$

Result

The solid is closed in the three directions, and the point $(0, 0, 0)$ belongs to it. Its volume is $3$.

Verification

code/part1_verify.py tests the membership of sample points against the four inequalities and counts the volume of the solid. The count gives $3.000067708$ against the exact $3.000000000$, and the Monte Carlo estimate gives $3.003885000$ with a standard error of $0.002122006$, which is $1.830814$ standard errors from the exact value.

Step 1.2: drop one coordinate to get the three projections

Reason

A projection keeps two coordinates and forgets the third one. Each pair that belongs to the projection is the pair that some point of the solid has. Take the inequalities of Step 1.1 and remove the forgotten coordinate from them.

A reader may name a projection by the coordinate that it drops, but a projection that drops $y$ lies on the plane $y = 0$ and keeps $x$ and $z$.

Computation

On the plane $x = 0$ the solid keeps $y$ and $z$, and the two conditions $y \ge 0$ and $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$ describe a quarter disk of radius $3$. Its area is $\dfrac{9\pi}{4} = 7.068583471$.

On the plane $y = 0$ the solid keeps $x$ and $z$. The conditions $0 \le z$ and $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$ give $0 \le z \le 3$, and the plane $y = 3x$ with the cylinder gives $0 \le x \le \dfrac{\sqrt{9 - z^{2}}}{3}$. The area is $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = 2.356194490$.

On the plane $z = 0$ the solid keeps $x$ and $y$, and the three conditions $0 \le x$, $3x \le y$ and $y \le 3$ describe the triangle with the corners $(0, 0)$, $(0, 3)$ and $(1, 3)$. Its area is $\dfrac{3}{2} = 1.500000000$.

Result

The three projections have the areas $\dfrac{9\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$. Figure 1 shows the solid and Figure 2 shows the three projections with the same areas.

Verification

code/part1_verify.py counts the cells of a fine grid that fall inside each projection and compares each count with its closed form. The relative gap of each count stays below the counted tolerance of $8.0\times 10^{-3}$.

Step 1.3: fix one coordinate and read the shape of the section

Reason

A section is the solid cut by one plane of constant coordinate. Fix $y$ and the remaining conditions give a region in the plane of that $y$. The shape of that region decides which integration order is easy, so read it from the inequalities and not from a picture of the cylinder.

A reader may call the section at a fixed $z$ an annulus because the cylinder is round, but the conditions give a triangle, because $x$ runs from $0$ to $y/3$ for every $y$ that the disk allows.

Computation

Fix $y$. The condition $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$ gives $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$, and the condition $y \ge 3x$ gives $0 \le x \le \dfrac{y}{3}$. The section is a rectangle with the area $\dfrac{y}{3}\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$.

Fix $z$. The cylinder gives $0 \le y \le \sqrt{9 - z^{2}}$, and the condition $y \ge 3x$ gives $0 \le x \le \dfrac{y}{3}$. The section is the triangle with the corners $(0, 0)$, $\left(0, \sqrt{9 - z^{2}}\right)$ and $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{9 - z^{2}}}{3}, \sqrt{9 - z^{2}}\right)$, and its area is $\dfrac{9 - z^{2}}{6}$.

Result

At $y = 1.500$ the section is a rectangle with the area $1.299038106$. At $z = 1.500$ the section is a triangle with the area $1.125000000$. At the ends of the range the section loses a dimension: at $y = 0$ it is a segment, at $y = 3$ it is a segment and at $z = 3$ it is a point, as the corner list of code/part1_verify.py reports. Figure 3 shows the two sections at the height $1.500$.

Verification

code/part1_verify.py builds the corner list of every section from the inequalities and classifies the list as a point, a segment, a triangle or a rectangle. It counts the area of each section on a grid and compares the count with the closed form, with a relative gap below $8.0\times 10^{-3}$.

Part 2: the limits of one integration order

Step 2.1: choose one order and write the three pairs of limits

Reason

An order of integration reads the solid from the inside out. The inner variable runs between two surfaces, the middle variable runs between two curves of a projection, and the outer variable runs between two numbers. Here the inner variable is $z$, because the cylinder gives $z$ as a function of $y$ alone, and $y$ then runs over one interval.

A reader may take $x$ from $0$ to $1$ in the middle integral, because the projection of the solid reaches $x = 1$, but the limit of $x$ is written at one fixed value of $y$, and there the plane gives $x \le y/3$.

Computation

The inner pair comes from the cylinder at a fixed $y$: $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$. The middle pair comes from the plane $y = 3x$: $0 \le x \le \dfrac{y}{3}$. The outer pair comes from the two ends of the disk: $0 \le y \le 3$.

The integral of the density $z$ over the solid is therefore

$$\iiint_{E} z\,dV = \int_{0}^{3} \int_{0}^{y/3} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} z\,dz\,dx\,dy.$$

Result

The order is $dz\,dx\,dy$ with the limits $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$, $0 \le x \le \dfrac{y}{3}$ and $0 \le y \le 3$. At the height $y = 1.500$ the inner limit gives $z \le 2.598076211$ and the middle limit gives $x \le 0.500000000$. Figure 4 shows both limits at that height.

Verification

code/part2_verify.py samples $480910$ points with the limits of this order and with the inequalities of Step 1.1, and it finds no point that one test accepts and the other test rejects. It integrates the same limits and compares the result $3.000000000$ with the volume of Part 1.

Step 2.2: check which variable each limit may use

Reason

The limit of an inner variable may use only the variables that the outer integrals still hold. If a limit uses a variable that the inner integrals already removed, the integral cannot be evaluated. The test moves one coordinate by a small step at a point inside the solid and records which coordinates change the answer of the membership test.

A reader may let the inner limit depend on $x$, but the integral over $z$ runs before the integral over $x$, so the limit of $z$ may use $y$ alone.

Computation

The point $(0.30, 1.20, 1.00)$ belongs to the solid. Move each coordinate by $0.05$ in turn and test the three limits of the order $dz\,dx\,dy$. The outer limit uses no coordinate. The middle limit $0 \le x \le \dfrac{y}{3}$ uses $y$. The inner limit $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$ uses $y$.

Result

The probe records the dependence $y$ for the middle limit and the dependence $y$ for the inner limit. No limit of this order uses $x$, so the order is admissible.

Verification

code/part2_verify.py compares the dependence set that the probe records with the set of variables that each limit declares, and it reports the same set for both limits.

Step 2.3: write a second order for the check of Part 4

Reason

A second order gives a second set of limits for the same solid. The check of Part 4 uses it to reach the answer by another route, because two orders that agree on the value test the limits as well as the algebra.

A reader may write $0 \le z \le 3$ for every $x$ in this order, but the cylinder caps $z$ at $3\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}$, which falls to zero at $x = 1$.

Computation

Take the outer variable $x$. The projection on the plane $y = 0$ gives $0 \le x \le 1$. At a fixed $x$ the cylinder gives $0 \le z \le 3\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}$. At a fixed pair $(x, z)$ the plane gives $3x \le y$, and the cylinder gives $y \le \sqrt{9 - z^{2}}$, which equals $3\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}$.

The volume of the solid in this order is

$$\int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{3\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}} \int_{3x}^{3} 1\,dy\,dz\,dx = 3.000000000.$$

Result

The order $dy\,dz\,dx$ gives the limits $3x \le y \le 3$, $0 \le z \le 3\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}$ and $0 \le x \le 1$, and its volume is $3.000000000$, which equals the volume of Part 1.

Verification

code/part2_verify.py integrates all six orders with the same nested quadrature and compares each result with the volume of Part 1. Every order agrees within $10^{-7}$.

Part 3: the value of the integral

Step 3.1: integrate the density in $z$

Reason

The inner integral holds $x$ and $y$ fixed, so only $z$ varies. The density is $z$ and the upper limit is a function of $y$, so the result of this step still carries $y$ and no longer carries $z$.

A reader may drop the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and write $9 - y^{2}$ for the inner integral, but the integral of $z$ from zero to a limit $b$ is $\dfrac{b^{2}}{2}$, and the square of the limit brings that factor.

Computation

$$\int_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} z\,dz = \left[\dfrac{z^{2}}{2}\right]_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} = \dfrac{9 - y^{2}}{2}$$

Result

The inner integral is $\dfrac{9 - y^{2}}{2}$ at every $y$ between $0$ and $3$.

Verification

code/part3_verify.py evaluates the inner integral in closed form and again with a quadrature at many values of $y$, and it compares the two results with a relative tolerance of $10^{-9}$.

Step 3.2: integrate in $x$ and then in $y$

Reason

The middle integral holds $y$ fixed and integrates the result of Step 3.1 over $x$ from zero to $y/3$. That integrand does not depend on $x$, so the middle integral is the integrand multiplied by the width of the interval. The outer integral then holds one variable only.

A reader may take $x$ from $0$ to $1$ at every $y$, but at a fixed $y$ the plane gives $x \le y/3$, and that choice gives $9.000000000$, which is $2.666667$ times the answer, as code/part3_verify.py prints.

Computation

$$\int_{0}^{y/3} \dfrac{9 - y^{2}}{2}\,dx = \dfrac{y\,(9 - y^{2})}{6}$$
$$\int_{0}^{3} \dfrac{y\,(9 - y^{2})}{6}\,dy = \left[\dfrac{3y^{2}}{4} - \dfrac{y^{4}}{24}\right]_{0}^{3} = \dfrac{27}{8} = 3.375000000$$

Result

The value of the integral is $\dfrac{27}{8} = 3.375000000$. The solid has the volume $3$, so the mean height of a point of the solid is $\dfrac{9}{8} = 1.125000000$. Figure 5 shows the outer integrand with its area and the antiderivative with its change from zero to three.

Verification

code/part3_verify.py differentiates the antiderivative by a central difference at many points and compares the derivative with the integrand, with the largest relative gap $2.636e-10$. It also evaluates the full nested integral by quadrature and compares the result with the closed form.

Part 4: a check with a second order and two quadratures

Step 4.1: reach the answer through the second order

Reason

The second order of Step 2.3 reads the solid in the other direction, so its algebra differs from the algebra of Part 3. A value that agrees with Part 3 checks both computations, because an error in the limits of one order would move its value away from the other one.

A reader may treat this value as a proof that the limits match the solid, but the value only checks the algebra, and the test that the limits match the solid is the membership count of code/part2_verify.py with $480910$ sample points.

Computation

The outermost integral of the order $dz\,dy\,dx$ has the integrand

$$\dfrac{9z - z^{3}}{6}$$

The integral of that quantity over $z$ from zero to three is $\dfrac{27}{8}$. The same nested integral in the order $dx\,dy\,dz$ gives the decimal value $3.375000000$.

Result

The second order gives $\dfrac{27}{8} = 3.375000000$, and that value equals the answer of Part 3. The exact value comes from fraction arithmetic and not from a floating point sum.

Verification

code/part4_verify.py computes the second order in exact rational arithmetic and compares the result with the answer that code/part3_verify.py writes to code/outputs/part3_values.json. The script holds no literal expected value.

Step 4.2: check the answer with two independent quadratures

Reason

A numerical method that uses another parameterization shares neither the antiderivative of Part 3 nor the fraction arithmetic of Step 4.1. Two such methods test the value from two more directions, and each method reports its own error.

A reader may take a Monte Carlo value as exact, but that value carries a standard error, and the meaningful statement is the gap in standard errors, not the gap in units.

Computation

The polar quadrature uses the radius $r$ and the angle $\theta$ of the plane $y^{2} + z^{2} = 9$ and the Jacobian $r$, with $x$ from $0$ to $\dfrac{r\cos\theta}{3}$. It gives $3.375000000$, and the same parameters give the volume $3.000000000$.

The Monte Carlo method samples $4\times 10^{6}$ points of the box $0 \le x \le 1$, $0 \le y \le 3$, $0 \le z \le 3$ and averages the density of the points that fall inside the solid. It gives $3.376856170$ with a standard error of $0.003051007$, which is $0.6084$ standard errors from the answer.

Result

The four values $3.375000000$, $3.375000000$, $3.375000000$ and $3.376856170$ agree within their stated errors. The answer of the problem is $\dfrac{27}{8} = 3.375$.

Verification

code/part4_verify.py runs both quadratures and the parameter map that samples $200000$ triples of the parameter box, and it finds no sampled triple outside the solid. Every value and every standard error that this step quotes comes from a print of that script.
