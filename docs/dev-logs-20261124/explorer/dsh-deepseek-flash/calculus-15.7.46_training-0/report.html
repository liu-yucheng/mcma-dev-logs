Moment of inertia of a solid cone about the $z$-axis

A step-by-step solution with one verification script for each part

Statement

Assume that the solid has constant density $k$. Find the moment of inertia about the $z$-axis of the solid cone

$$\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}} \le z \le h .$$

The statement prints no numerical value for $k$ and no numerical value for $h$, so the answer is an expression in these two symbols. The words "solid cone" carry the condition $h > 0$, which a body of positive volume needs.

What this tutorial does

  1. Part 1. Read the solid and the quantity The body is a right circular cone, and the answer is the integral of the density times the squared distance to the z-axis.
  2. Part 2. Choose coordinates and derive the limits Cylindrical coordinates turn the shape into the limits r ≤ z ≤ h, then 0 ≤ r ≤ h, then 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π.
  3. Part 3. Integrate and get the value Two integrations give I = π k h⁵/10.
  4. Part 4. Check the value by horizontal slices A second route stacks the moments of the horizontal slices and gives the same value.
  5. Part 5. State the answer in terms of the mass The mass of the cone turns the answer into (3/10) M h².

Part 1. Read the solid and the quantity

The body is a right circular cone, and the answer is the integral of the density times the squared distance to the z-axis.

Part 1, Step 1: Read the solid from the two inequalities

Reason

The symbol $\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$ is the distance from the point $(x,y,z)$ to the $z$-axis. The inequality $\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}} \le z$ says that the height of a point is at least this distance. The inequality $z \le h$ says that the height is at most $h$. Together the two inequalities cut one solid body out of the space.

Computation

Hold the height at the value $z = c$ with $0 \le c \le h$. At this height the condition $\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}} \le c$ describes the closed disk

$$x^{2}+y^{2} \le c^{2}$$

of radius $c$ in the plane $z = c$. At $c = h$ the disk has radius $h$. At $c = 0$ the disk shrinks to the single point $(0,0,0)$.

Result

The solid $E$ is a right circular cone. The apex is the origin, the base is the disk of radius $h$ in the plane $z = h$, and the wall is the cone $z = \sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$. The height is $h$. Figure 1 shows the body, the axis of rotation, and one horizontal slice.

Verification: code/part1_verify.py describes the same point twice, once by the two inequalities and once by the inequalities $r \le z \le h$ in cylindrical coordinates. The two descriptions agree on 2000000 of 2000000 sampled points. The same script measures the volume by Monte Carlo as 1.046738 plus or minus 0.001243, and the quadrature gives 1.047198, which is $\pi h^{3}/3$ at $h = 1$. The script also finds the radius of the base by bisection as 1.00000000 and the height of the apex as 0.00000000. Figure 1 is drawn by code/part1_figure1.py.

Part 1, Step 2: Write the definition of the moment of inertia about the z-axis

Reason

The moment of inertia of a body about an axis measures how far the mass of the body lies from that axis. Each small piece of mass $\rho\,dV$ contributes its mass multiplied by the square of its distance $d$ to the axis. For a body of constant density $k$ the definition of Section 15.7 gives

$$I_{z} = \iiint_{E} d^{2}\,k\,dV .$$

The statement of the problem gives the density as the constant $k$ and gives no other property of the material.

Computation

For a point $(x,y,z)$ the nearest point of the $z$-axis is $(0,0,z)$, so the distance to the axis is

$$d = \sqrt{(x-0)^{2}+(y-0)^{2}+(z-z)^{2}} = \sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}} .$$

Insert this distance into the definition. The density $k$ is constant, so it leaves the integral sign.

Result

The number that the problem asks for is

$$I_{z} = k \iiint_{E} \left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right) dV .$$

The statement prints no numerical value for $k$ and no numerical value for $h$, so the answer is an expression in these two symbols. The solid has a positive volume only when $h > 0$, and the words "solid cone" carry that condition.

Verification: code/part1_verify.py checks the geometric part of this step, the area of the slice that the distance $d$ produces. At the heights $z = 0.25$, $z = 0.50$, and $z = 0.75$ the area from quadrature is 0.196350, 0.785398, and 1.767146, and the area measured from the fraction of sampled points inside the slice is 0.197213, 0.785367, and 1.767861 with standard errors 0.001205, 0.002151, and 0.002464. Each measured area lies within four standard errors of $\pi z^{2}$, which is the area of the disk that the distance $d$ describes at that height.

Part 1 answer. $I_{z} = k \iiint_{E} \left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right) dV$, with $E$ the cone of apex $(0,0,0)$, base disk of radius $h$ in the plane $z = h$, and wall $z = \sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$.

Part 2. Choose coordinates and derive the limits

Cylindrical coordinates turn the shape into the limits r ≤ z ≤ h, then 0 ≤ r ≤ h, then 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π.

Part 2, Step 1: Use cylindrical coordinates

Reason

Every slice of the solid at a fixed height is a disk about the $z$-axis (Part 1, Step 1). A coordinate that measures the distance from the axis and a coordinate that measures the angle around the axis therefore follow the shape of the body. The two new coordinates are

$$x = r\cos\theta, \qquad y = r\sin\theta, \qquad z = z .$$

The symbol $r$ is the distance to the $z$-axis and the symbol $\theta$ is the angle in the $xy$ plane, so the integrand $x^{2}+y^{2}$ becomes the single square $r^{2}$.

Computation

The square of the distance to the axis is

$$x^{2}+y^{2} = r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta + r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta = r^{2} .$$

The volume element of the map $(r,\theta,z) \mapsto (x,y,z)$ is the absolute value of the Jacobian determinant,

$$\left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(r,\theta,z)}\right| = r , \qquad dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta .$$

The factor $r$ is not $1$: a step $d\theta$ at radius $r$ covers the arc length $r\,d\theta$.

Result

In cylindrical coordinates the integrand of Part 1 is $k r^{2}$ and the volume element is $r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$. The moment of inertia becomes

$$I_{z} = \iiint k\,r^{3}\,dz\,dr\,d\theta .$$

Verification: code/part2_verify.py compares the analytic Jacobian $r$ with a finite-difference Jacobian over 200000 random points, and the largest relative gap is 2.581e-10. The same script compares $x^{2}+y^{2}$ with $r^{2}$ over the same points, and the largest gap is 2.220e-16. It also compares the distance to the axis computed by a cross product with $z$ on the wall of the cone, and the largest gap is 1.110e-16.

Part 2, Step 2: Derive the three pairs of limits

Reason

Fix the pair $(r,\theta)$. The vertical line through the point $(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta, 0)$ meets the solid exactly where both inequalities of the statement hold. The first inequality gives $z \ge r$ and the second gives $z \le h$, so the line enters the body at the wall $z = r$ and leaves it at the base $z = h$. This interval is empty unless $r \le h$, and $r$ is a distance, so $0 \le r \le h$. The angle covers one full turn, $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$.

Computation

The three pairs of limits follow from the two inequalities without a guess. In the order $dz\,dr\,d\theta$ they are

$$I_{z} = \int_{\theta = 0}^{2\pi} \int_{r = 0}^{h} \int_{z = r}^{h} k\,r^{3}\,dz\,dr\,d\theta .$$

The inner integral is taken over $z$ from $r$ to $h$ at fixed $r$ and fixed $\theta$. Its lower limit is the variable $r$ of the middle integral and its upper limit is the constant $h$. The middle integral is taken over $r$ from $0$ to $h$ at fixed $\theta$. The outer integral is taken over $\theta$ from $0$ to $2\pi$.

Result

The bounds are $r \le z \le h$, then $0 \le r \le h$, then $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$, in that order. The inner integral carries the length $h - r$ of the vertical chord, and the order $dz$ first is the order that computes this length before any other integration. Figure 2 shows the triangle of the pairs $(r,z)$ and the two segments whose lengths the first two integrations use.

Verification: code/part2_verify.py integrates the volume element $r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$ over these three pairs of limits by adaptive quadrature and gets 1.047197551 at $h = 1$. A second method in the same script stacks the disks of radius $z$ from $z = 0$ to $z = h$ and gets 1.047197551, and the formula $\pi h^{3}/3$ gives 1.047197551. code/part2_figure1.py prints the limits of the two drawn segments: the vertical segment runs from $z = 0.4500$ to $z = 1.0000$ at $r = 0.4500$, and the horizontal segment runs from $r = 0.0000$ to $r = 0.7200$ at $z = 0.7200$.

Part 2 answer. In the order $dz\,dr\,d\theta$ the limits are $r \le z \le h$, $0 \le r \le h$, and $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$, so $I_{z} = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{h}\int_{r}^{h} k\,r^{3}\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$.

Part 3. Integrate and get the value

Two integrations give I = π k h⁵/10.

Part 3, Step 1: Integrate in z first

Reason

The integrand $k r^{3}$ of Part 2 holds no $z$. The inner integral of a constant over an interval is the constant multiplied by the length of the interval, so the first integration needs no antiderivative.

Computation

The inner limits are $z = r$ and $z = h$ at fixed $r$. Their difference is the length $h - r$ of the vertical chord, so

$$\int_{z=r}^{h} k\,r^{3}\,dz = k\,r^{3}\,(h - r) .$$

Insert this value into the two outer integrals. The integrand no longer holds $z$, so the two remaining integrals are

$$I_{z} = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{h} k\,r^{3}\,(h - r)\,dr\,d\theta .$$

The factor $k\,r^{3}\,(h - r)$ holds no $\theta$, and the limits of $\theta$ are $0$ and $2\pi$, so the outer integral multiplies the result by $2\pi$:

$$I_{z} = 2\pi k \int_{0}^{h} \left(h\,r^{3} - r^{4}\right) dr .$$

Result

One integration remains, over the single variable $r$:

$$I_{z} = 2\pi k \int_{0}^{h} \left(h\,r^{3} - r^{4}\right) dr .$$

Verification: code/part3_verify.py evaluates the inner integral $\int_{r}^{h} k\,r^{3}\,dz$ at $r = 0.3$, $h = 1$, and $k = 1$ by three methods. The symbolic computation gives $189/10000$, the exact arithmetic on fractions gives $189/10000$, and adaptive quadrature gives 0.01890000. The three values agree, and $k\,r^{3}(h - r) = 0.3^{3} \cdot 0.7 = 0.0189$ by hand.

Part 3, Step 2: Integrate in r and read the answer

Reason

The remaining integral is a polynomial in $r$. The power rule gives the antiderivative of each term, and the limits $0$ and $h$ turn the antiderivative into a single power of $h$.

Computation

Split the integral into two terms and apply the power rule:

$$\int_{0}^{h} h\,r^{3}\,dr = h \cdot \frac{h^{4}}{4} = \frac{h^{5}}{4}, \qquad \int_{0}^{h} r^{4}\,dr = \frac{h^{5}}{5} .$$

Subtract the second value from the first value:

$$\int_{0}^{h} \left(h\,r^{3} - r^{4}\right) dr = \frac{h^{5}}{4} - \frac{h^{5}}{5} = h^{5}\left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{5}\right) = \frac{h^{5}}{20} .$$

Now multiply by the constant $2\pi k$ of the previous step:

$$I_{z} = 2\pi k \cdot \frac{h^{5}}{20} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} .$$

Result

$$I_{z} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} .$$

The result is an expression in $k$ and $h$, as the statement requires, because the statement prints no numerical value for either symbol.

Verification: code/part3_verify.py evaluates $\int_{0}^{h}(h r^{3} - r^{4})\,dr$ at $h = 1$ by the symbolic computation, by exact arithmetic on fractions, by adaptive quadrature, and by the composite Simpson rule. The four values are $1/20$, $1/20$ = 0.05000000, 0.05000000, and 0.05000000. At $h = 1.7$ the formula $h^{5}/20$ gives 0.7099285000, and quadrature and the Simpson rule give 0.7099285000. The script also multiplies the exact value $1/20$ by $2\pi$ and compares the product with $\pi/10$: the two values are 0.3141592654 and 0.3141592654, so the constant $2\pi$ of the previous step is carried correctly.

Part 3 answer. $I_{z} = \dfrac{\pi k h^{5}}{10}$.

Part 4. Check the value by horizontal slices

A second route stacks the moments of the horizontal slices and gives the same value.

Part 4, Step 1: Compute the moment of one horizontal slice

Reason

The result of Part 3 needs a second route, because one route cannot expose a wrong pair of limits. Cut the solid into thin horizontal slices. Each slice is a disk that lies in a plane perpendicular to the $z$-axis, and the $z$-axis passes through the centre of that disk. The moment of the slice about the $z$-axis is therefore its polar second moment, an area integral over the disk.

Computation

Part 1 gives the slice at height $z$: it is the disk of radius $z$. In polar coordinates inside that disk the distance to the axis is $\rho$ and the area element is $\rho\,d\rho\,d\phi$, so the polar second moment of the slice is

$$J(z) = \int_{\phi = 0}^{2\pi} \int_{\rho = 0}^{z} \rho^{2} \cdot \rho\,d\rho\,d\phi = 2\pi \int_{0}^{z} \rho^{3}\,d\rho = 2\pi \cdot \frac{z^{4}}{4} = \frac{\pi z^{4}}{2} .$$

The symbol $z$ is fixed during this computation. It enters only as the radius of the disk.

Result

$$J(z) = \frac{\pi z^{4}}{2} .$$

The second moment of the slice grows as the fourth power of the height, because both the radius and the distance of the area from the axis grow with the height. Figure 3 shows the slice cut into rings and the curve $J(z)$ whose area under it is the moment of the whole cone.

Verification: code/part4_verify.py computes the polar second moment of the slice at the heights $z = 0.25$, $z = 0.50$, and $z = 0.75$ by two methods. The polar integral gives 0.006135923, 0.098174770, and 0.497009775, and a Cartesian area integral over the same disk gives the same three values, and the formula $\pi z^{4}/2$ gives the same three values. The largest gap between the polar value and the formula is 0.000e+00. code/part4_figure1.py draws Figure 3 and prints the value 0.232106 for $J$ at $z = 0.6200$ and 1.570796 for $J$ at $z = 1.0000$.

Part 4, Step 2: Stack the slices and compare with Part 3

Reason

A slice of thickness $dz$ at height $z$ has mass $k$ multiplied by its area, and the area of the disk of radius $z$ is $\pi z^{2}$. The definition of Part 1 puts the density $k$ outside the area integral, so the slice contributes $k\,J(z)\,dz$, where $J(z)$ of Step 1 is the area integral of the squared distance over that disk. The moment of the solid is the sum of the contributions of all slices, which is an integral over $z$.

Computation

$$I_{z} = \int_{0}^{h} k\,J(z)\,dz = k \int_{0}^{h} \frac{\pi z^{4}}{2}\,dz = \frac{k\pi}{2} \cdot \frac{h^{5}}{5} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} .$$

The limits of this integral are $z = 0$ at the apex and $z = h$ at the base, and these limits come from the same two inequalities that gave the limits of Part 2.

Result

$$I_{z} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} .$$

This value is the value of Part 3, and the two routes use different limits: Part 3 integrates $z$ from $r$ to $h$ inside the body, and this step integrates $z$ from $0$ to $h$ over the whole height with the radius $z$ inside the slice. The agreement supports both the limits of Part 2 and the constant of Part 3.

Verification: code/part4_verify.py computes the same number by four methods. The symbolic triple integral gives $\pi h^{5} k/10$. Triple adaptive quadrature gives 0.314159265359. The stack of the slice moments gives 0.314159265359. Monte Carlo sampling of the definition of Part 1 gives 0.313724 plus or minus 0.000408, which is 1.067 standard errors from 0.314159. At $k = 1$ and $h = 1$ the four methods agree.

Part 4 answer. $I_{z} = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{h} k\,\frac{\pi z^{4}}{2}\,dz = \dfrac{\pi k h^{5}}{10}$, the same value that Part 3 gives.

Part 5. State the answer in terms of the mass

The mass of the cone turns the answer into (3/10) M h².

Part 5, Step 1: Compute the mass of the cone

Reason

The answer of Part 3 holds the density $k$ and the height $h$. A textbook answer often states the moment of inertia in terms of the mass $M$, so this part computes the mass first. The density is constant, so the mass is the density multiplied by the volume of the body.

Computation

The volume of a cone with base radius $R$ and height $H$ is $\pi R^{2}H/3$. Here the base radius is $h$ and the height is $h$, so the volume is

$$V = \frac{\pi h^{2} \cdot h}{3} = \frac{\pi h^{3}}{3}, \qquad M = k\,V = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3} .$$

The same value comes from the integral form $M = \iiint_{E} k\,dV$ with the limits of Part 2:

$$M = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{h}\int_{r}^{h} k\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi k \int_{0}^{h} r\,(h - r)\,dr = 2\pi k \cdot \frac{h^{3}}{6} = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3} .$$

Result

$$M = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3} .$$

Verification: code/part5_verify.py computes the mass at $k = 1$, $h = 1$ by three methods. The symbolic triple integral gives $\pi h^{3}k/3$, the stack of the slice masses gives 1.047197551, and the formula $\pi k h^{3}/3$ gives 1.047197551. Monte Carlo sampling of the volume gives 1.045362 plus or minus 0.001243, which is 1.477 standard errors from 1.047198.

Part 5, Step 2: Rewrite the answer in terms of the mass and check it

Reason

The mass of Step 1 lets the moment of inertia take the standard form $I_{z} = c\,M h^{2}$. The constant $c$ shows how the mass is spread, and the checks of this step catch an algebra slip in that constant.

Computation

Replace $M$ by $\pi k h^{3}/3$ inside the answer of Part 3:

$$\frac{3}{10}\,M\,h^{2} = \frac{3}{10} \cdot \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3} \cdot h^{2} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} = I_{z} .$$

Two checks follow from the same expression.

  • The dimension: $k$ is a mass per unit volume, so $k h^{5}$ is a mass times a squared length, which

is the dimension of a moment of inertia. The constant $\pi/10$ carries no dimension.

  • The scaling: replace $h$ by $2h$. Then $I_{z}$ is multiplied by $2^{5} = 32$, because the answer

holds $h^{5}$ and no other power of $h$.

Result

$$I_{z} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} = \frac{3}{10}\,M\,h^{2}, \qquad M = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3} .$$

A solid cylinder of radius $h$ has $I_{z} = \tfrac{1}{2}M h^{2}$, so the cone has the smaller constant $3/10$: its mass lies nearer the axis than the mass of the cylinder.

Verification: code/part5_verify.py substitutes $M = \pi k h^{3}/3$ into $(3/10)M h^{2}$ and simplifies the result to $\pi h^{5}k/10$ by a symbolic computation. At $k = 2.3$ and $h = 1.7$ the formula gives 10.2594083368 and triple quadrature gives 10.2594083368. The script also computes the ratio $I_{z}(2h)/I_{z}(h)$ by quadrature as 32.0000000000 and from the formula as 32.0000000000, against the expected value 32.

Final answer. $I_{z} = \dfrac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} = \dfrac{3}{10}\,M h^{2}$, with $M = \dfrac{\pi k h^{3}}{3}$ the mass of the cone. At $k = 2.3$ and $h = 1.7$ the value is $10.2594083368$.

Answer

The moment of inertia of the solid cone about the $z$-axis is

$$I_{z} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} = \frac{3}{10}\,M h^{2},\qquad M = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3} .$$

The first form holds the data of the statement, the constant density $k$ and the height $h$. The second form holds the mass $M$ of the cone. At $k = 2.3$ and $h = 1.7$ the value is $10.2594083368$.
