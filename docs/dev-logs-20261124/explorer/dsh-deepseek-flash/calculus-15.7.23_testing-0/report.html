Wedge cut from a cylinder by two planes

Problem

(a) Express the volume of the wedge in the first octant that is cut from the cylinder $y^{2} + z^{2} = 1$ by the planes $y = x$ and $x = 1$ as a triple integral.

(b) Use either the Table of Integrals (on Reference Pages 6-10) or a computer algebra system to find the exact value of the triple integral in part (a).

Part 1. The inequality description of the wedge

Reason

A solid that three surfaces bound needs one inequality for each surface, and the first octant adds three sign conditions. Write the conditions before any integral, because every limit of the integral comes from one inequality.

Computation

The cylinder $y^{2}+z^{2}=1$ has the axis $x$ and the radius $1$. The solid lies inside it, so $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$.

The plane $y=x$ has two sides. The solid is the part of the cylinder that the two planes cut off between them, so the point stays on the side $x\le y$. The condition $x\le y$ is the statement that the point is on the far side of the plane $y=x$ from the $x$ axis.

The plane $x=1$ gives $x\le 1$. Inside the cylinder $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$ with $z\ge 0$ the coordinate $y$ obeys $0\le y\le 1$, so $x\le y$ already gives $x\le 1$. The plane $x=1$ therefore adds no new point; it touches the wedge at the single corner $(1,1,0)$.

The first octant gives $x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$, and $z\ge 0$.

The four conditions are

$$0\le x\le y,\qquad y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1,\qquad z\ge 0.$$

Common mistake

A reader may take the condition of the plane $y=x$ as $y\le x$ and then read the base triangle as the one with the corners $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,1)$. The inequality $x\le y$ gives the base triangle with the corners $(0,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,1)$; the two triangles are mirror images and they give different volumes.

Result

The wedge is the set of points with $0\le x\le y$, $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$, and $z\ge 0$. Figure 1 shows the four faces: the cylinder, the two planes, and the base.

The random sample of 60000 points finds 20112 points inside the solid, and the two descriptions of the set agree on every point of the sample. The Monte Carlo volume is 0.335200 with a standard error of 0.001927, which is 0.969 standard errors from the exact value of Part 3.

Verification: code/part1_verify.py.

Part 2. The slice at a fixed height and the iterated integral

Reason

An integral over a solid needs one order of the three variables, and each pair of limits must come from the inequalities of Part 1. The height $z$ is the best outer variable here, because the cylinder fixes its top and no other condition mentions it.

Computation

Fix the height $z$ with $0\le z\le 1$. The inequality $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$ gives $0\le y\le\sqrt{1-z^{2}}$, because $y\ge 0$. The inequality $x\le y$ then gives $0\le x\le y$.

The slice at the height $z$ is the triangle with the corners $(0,0)$, $(y_{\max},0)$, and $(y_{\max},y_{\max})$ in the plane of the pair $(x,y)$, where $y_{\max}=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}$. Its area is $y_{\max}^{2}/2=(1-z^{2})/2$.

The outer variable $z$ runs from $0$ to $1$, because $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$ with $y\ge 0$ needs $z\le 1$.

The orders of the limits are $0\le z\le 1$, then $0\le y\le\sqrt{1-z^{2}}$, then $0\le x\le y$. The volume is

$$V=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\int_{0}^{y}1\,dx\,dy\,dz .$$

Only the outer limit is a constant. The limit of $x$ is the variable $y$ of the middle integral, so the inner two limits must be written in this order and they cannot be interchanged.

Common mistake

A reader may take the slice at the height $z$ as a rectangle of width $1$ and height $1$, but the limits give the triangle $0\le x\le y\le\sqrt{1-z^{2}}$ and the area $(1-z^{2})/2$.

Result

The slice areas at the heights 0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75 are 0.500000000, 0.468750000, 0.375000000, 0.218750000, and the widths of the slice along $y$ at those heights are 1.000000, 0.968246, 0.866025, 0.661438. The integral of the slice area is 0.333333333333. The sum of the slice areas is the volume, so the iterated integral above is the answer to part (a).

Verification: code/part2_verify.py compares the closed form of the area with adaptive quadrature and with a count of random points at each height. Figure 2 shows the two panels: the quarter disk in the plane $x=0$ and the slice at $z=0.50$.

Part 3. The exact value of the integral

Reason

The integral of Part 2 has three nested integrals, and each one is a polynomial in its variable once the inner limit is in place. Evaluate from the inner integral to the outer integral, and check each antiderivative by a derivative.

Computation

The inner integral is

$$\int_{0}^{y}1\,dx=y .$$

The middle integral is

$$\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}y\,dy=\frac{1-z^{2}}{2}.$$

The outer integral is

$$V=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-z^{2}}{2}\,dz=\left[\frac{z}{2}-\frac{z^{3}}{6}\right]_{0}^{1}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{3}.$$

Common mistake

A reader may keep the factor $1/2$ from the inner integral and also divide the outer result by $2$, but the antiderivative $z/2-z^{3}/6$ already holds the factor, so the value is $1/3$ and not $1/6$.

Result

The exact volume is $1/3$, that is 0.333333333333. The check of code/part3_verify.py compares the fraction $1/3$ with adaptive quadrature, which gives 0.333333333333, and with the substitution $z=\sin t$, which gives 0.333333333333. The script also compares the derivative of $z/2-z^{3}/6$ with the slice area at 4 points. This is the answer to part (b).

Verification: code/part3_verify.py.

Part 4. A second order and a grid rule

Reason

One number from one order of integration is weak evidence, because a wrong limit still gives a finite number. Compute the volume again with a different order and with a rule that uses no limit at all. A value that two independent methods produce is a value that the page can state.

Computation

Integrate $y$ first. At a fixed pair $(x,z)$ the inequality $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$ gives $y\le\sqrt{1-z^{2}}$ and the inequality $x\le y$ gives $y\ge x$. A point exists only when $x\le\sqrt{1-z^{2}}$, that is $x^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$. The projection of the solid on the plane $y=0$ is the quarter disk of radius $1$.

$$V=\iint_{x^{2}+z^{2}\le 1,\,x\ge 0,\,z\ge 0}\left(\sqrt{1-z^{2}}-x\right)\,dx\,dz .$$

With $x=r\cos t$ and $z=r\sin t$ the integral is

$$V=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sqrt{1-r^{2}\sin^{2}t}-r\cos t\right)r\,dr\,dt .$$

The second method is a midpoint rule on a grid of 240 cells per axis of the unit cube. A cell counts when its center satisfies $x\le y$ and $y^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$. The rule uses the membership test only, and it needs no limit.

Common mistake

A reader may take the projection on the plane $y=0$ as the square $0\le x\le 1$, $0\le z\le 1$, but the limits give the quarter disk $x^{2}+z^{2}\le 1$, $x\ge 0$, $z\ge 0$, whose area is 0.785398163.

Result

The polar rule gives 0.333334043, the midpoint rule gives 0.334979962, and the exact value is 0.333333333. The three values agree to the accuracy that each method supports, so the volume of the wedge is $1/3$.

Verification: code/part4_verify.py.

Answers

Part (a). The volume is the iterated integral $V=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\int_{0}^{y}1\,dx\,dy\,dz$.

Part (b). The exact value is $V=1/3$, that is 0.333333333333 as a decimal.

The second method of Part 4 gives 0.333334043 by the polar rule and 0.334979962 by the grid rule.
