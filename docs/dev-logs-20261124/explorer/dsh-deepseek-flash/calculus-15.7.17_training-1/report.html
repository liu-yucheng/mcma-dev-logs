Triple Integral over a Paraboloid Cap

A complete solution of $\iiint_E x\,dV$, where the solid $E$ lies between the paraboloid $x = 4y^2 + 4z^2$ and the plane $x = 4$. Each step states its reason first, and each numerical claim is checked by a script.

Answer $\dfrac{16\pi}{3} \approx 16.7552$ Parts 4 Steps 9 Figures 3 Checks 4 scripts, 0 failures

1. The statement

Evaluate the triple integral

$$\iiint_E x \, dV,$$

where $E$ is bounded by the paraboloid $x = 4y^2 + 4z^2$ and the plane $x = 4$.

2. Part 1 — Describe the solid

Step 1.1

Read the two surfaces

WhyThe two equations are the only information about the solid. Read each one as a surface first. The shape of a surface shows which variable can serve as the inner variable of the integral.

The first surface is the paraboloid

$$x = 4y^2 + 4z^2 .$$

Write $r^2 = y^2 + z^2$. Then $x = 4r^2$, and the right side is a sum of two squares. The right side is never negative, and it is zero only at $y = z = 0$. Therefore the lowest point of the paraboloid is the origin $(0,0,0)$, and the bowl opens toward positive $x$.

The second surface is the plane $x = 4$. Its equation does not contain $y$ or $z$. Therefore the plane is vertical, it is parallel to the $yz$-plane, and it meets the $x$-axis at $x = 4$.

Step 1.2

Find where the two surfaces meet

WhyThe solid exists only where the paraboloid is below the plane. Solve the equality to get the curve that limits the solid, and project that curve on the $yz$-plane to get the region of the double integral.

Set the two right sides equal:

$$4y^2 + 4z^2 = 4 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y^2 + z^2 = 1 .$$

The equation contains no $x$, and the common value is $x = 4$. Therefore the intersection is the circle of radius $1$ in the plane $x = 4$. The condition $4(y^2+z^2) \le x \le 4$ gives $y^2 + z^2 \le 1$. Therefore every point of $E$ stands above the unit disk

$$D = \{(y,z) : y^2 + z^2 \le 1\}, \qquad \operatorname{area}(D) = \pi .$$

Checked bycode/part1_verify.py — radius from $4r^2 = 4$: 1.0, expected 1.0; largest $\lvert 4r^2-4 \rvert$ on $r=1$: 8.9e-16, expected 0; $\lvert 4r^2-4 \rvert$ at $r=0.98$ and $r=1.02$: 0.1584 and 0.1616; area of $D$ from $2\cdot10^6$ points: 3.14085, expected 3.14159; points that the two descriptions of $E$ classify differently: 0. pass

Step 1.3

Write the solid as one set and note its symmetry

WhyAn integral needs explicit limits. The surfaces give the two limits of $x$, and the projection gives the limits of $y$ and $z$. Record the symmetry before any computation, because a symmetry can save work, and a false symmetry gives a wrong answer.

$$E = \{(x,y,z) : y^2 + z^2 \le 1,\ 4(y^2+z^2) \le x \le 4\}.$$

All coordinates are finite, and all inequalities are non-strict. Therefore $E$ is closed and bounded, and the integral is finite.

Both $y^2+z^2 \le 1$ and $x = 4(y^2+z^2)$ contain $y$ and $z$ only as squares. Therefore $E$ is symmetric about the planes $y = 0$ and $z = 0$. The integrand $f = x$ does not contain $y$ or $z$. Therefore the two halves of the symmetry give equal contributions, and the contributions add. The symmetry gives no cancellation for this integrand.

For later use, the volume is

$$V = \iint_D \left(4 - 4(y^2+z^2)\right) dA = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\pi\cdot 1^2\cdot 4 = 2\pi .$$

Checked bycode/part1_verify.py and code/part2_verify.py — volume of $E$: 6.283185307179586, expected 2π = 6.283185307179586; volume against half of the enclosing cylinder: difference 0.0. pass

Answer of part 1 $E = \{(x,y,z) : y^2 + z^2 \le 1,\ 4(y^2+z^2) \le x \le 4\}$. The projection of $E$ is the unit disk $D$ of area $\pi$. The solid is closed and bounded, and its volume is $2\pi$.

3. Part 2 — Reduce the integral

Step 2.1

Integrate along $x$ first

WhyChoose the inner variable first. The variable $x$ has an explicit lower limit $4(y^2+z^2)$ and an explicit upper limit $4$. Therefore hold $y$ and $z$ fixed, and integrate $x$ along one straight segment. This choice gives a double integral without square roots.

Fix $(y,z)$ in $D$ and write $r^2 = y^2+z^2$. The variable $x$ runs from $4r^2$ to $4$:

$$\int_{4r^2}^{4} x \, dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{4r^2}^{4} = 8 - \frac{(4r^2)^2}{2} = 8 - 8r^4 = 8\left(1 - (y^2+z^2)^2\right).$$

Check the two ends of the disk. At the centre, the segment has the full height and the value is $8$. At the rim, the segment has zero length and the value is $0$. The value is never negative on $D$, because $r \le 1$.

Checked bycode/part2_verify.py — quad of $x$ on the segment against $8(1-r^4)$ at $r = 0,\ 0.25,\ 0.5,\ 0.75,\ 1$: computed 8.0, 7.96875, 7.5, 5.46875, 0.0, expected the same values, all differences 0.0. pass

Step 2.2

Write the double integral over the disk

WhyThe inner integral no longer contains $x$, so the outer integral runs over the projection $D$. Write the reduced integral in full, and bracket its value before the exact evaluation.

$$I = \iint_D 8\left(1 - (y^2+z^2)^2\right) dA = 8 \iint_D \left(1 - (y^2+z^2)^2\right) dA .$$

The integrand is positive inside $D$ and zero on the rim, so $I > 0$. On $D$ the factor $1 - r^4$ runs from $1$ down to $0$. Therefore

$$0 < I < 8 \cdot 1 \cdot \pi = 8\pi \approx 25.13 .$$

Answer of part 2 $I = 8\displaystyle\iint_D \left(1 - (y^2+z^2)^2\right) dA$, and $0 < I < 8\pi \approx 25.13$. The reduced integral has the numerical value $16.75516$, which part 3 derives exactly.

4. Part 3 — Evaluate in polar coordinates

Step 3.1

Use polar coordinates in the disk

WhyThe integrand depends on $y$ and $z$ only through $y^2+z^2$, and the region is a disk. Polar coordinates fit both facts, and they split the double integral into a product of one integral in $r$ and one integral in $\theta$.

$$y = r\cos\theta, \qquad z = r\sin\theta, \qquad dA = r \, dr \, d\theta,$$

with $0 \le r \le 1$ and $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$. Then

$$I = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 8\left(1-r^4\right) r \, dr \, d\theta .$$

The inner integral is

$$\int_0^1 8\left(r - r^5\right) dr = 8\left[\frac{r^2}{2} - \frac{r^6}{6}\right]_0^1 = 8\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{6}\right) = \frac{8}{3} .$$

The factor $r$ is necessary. Without it, the inner integral would be $\int_0^1 8(1-r^4)dr = 6.4$, which is the value for a square region, not for a disk.

Step 3.2

Multiply the two factors

WhyThe integrand of step 3.1 is a product of a function of $r$ and the constant $1$. Therefore the two integrals separate, and the answer is the product of two numbers.

The angular integral is the length of the full turn:

$$\int_0^{2\pi} d\theta = 2\pi .$$

Multiply the two factors:

$$I = \frac{8}{3}\cdot 2\pi = \frac{16\pi}{3} = 16.755160819145562\ldots \approx 16.7552 .$$

The value is inside the bracket $0 < I < 25.13$ of step 2.2, and it is close to two thirds of that bracket. The result is plausible, because the integrand falls from $8$ at the centre to $0$ at the rim.

5. Part 4 — Check the answer

Step 4.1

Integrate with $x$ outermost

WhyA second order uses different limits and a different integrand. When two independent orders agree, an error in the setup of one order is unlikely.

Fix $x$ with $0 \le x \le 4$. On that plane the solid satisfies $y^2 + z^2 \le x/4$. Therefore the section is a disk of radius $\sqrt{x}/2$, and its area is

$$A(x) = \pi\left(\frac{\sqrt{x}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi x}{4} .$$

The integrand is constant on the section, so the inner integral is $x A(x)$:

$$I = \int_0^4 x\cdot\frac{\pi x}{4}\, dx = \frac{\pi}{4}\int_0^4 x^2\, dx = \frac{\pi}{4}\cdot\frac{64}{3} = \frac{16\pi}{3} .$$

The same sections give the volume $\int_0^4 A(x)\,dx = 2\pi$, which agrees with step 1.3.

7. Final answer

Value of the integral
$$\iiint_E x\,dV = \frac{16\pi}{3} = 16.755160819145562\ldots \approx 16.7552$$

The solid is the cap between $x = 4(y^2+z^2)$ and $x = 4$. The exact answer is $\dfrac{16\pi}{3}$. The value is a positive number, as step 2.2 requires, and it is smaller than $8\pi$, the volume bound of that step.

8. Common errors

  1. Using $x = 4y^2$ only. The surface contains $z$ as well. With $x = 4y^2$ alone the region is a cylinder in the $z$ direction, and the answer is different.
  2. Using radius $4$ for the disk. The intersection is $4y^2+4z^2 = 4$, so the radius is $1$, not $4$. A radius of $4$ gives $I = 8\pi\cdot 16$ times the wrong factor.
  3. Leaving out the factor $r$. The area element is $r\,dr\,d\theta$. Without $r$ the radial integral is $6.4$ instead of $8/3$.
  4. Starting $x$ at $0$. The lower limit is $4(y^2+z^2)$, not $0$. The segment length is $4-4r^2$, and it is zero at the rim.
  5. Expecting the symmetry to cancel the integral. The integrand $x$ is not odd in $y$ or $z$, so the two halves of the solid add. The answer is positive.

9. Method summary

  • Read the two surfaces. The paraboloid gives the lower limit of $x$ and the plane gives the upper limit.
  • Solve the equality of the two surfaces. The result fixes the projection, which is the unit disk.
  • Integrate $x$ first, because both limits of $x$ are explicit functions of $(y,z)$.
  • Use polar coordinates, because the integrand and the region depend on $y^2+z^2$.
  • Check the answer with a second order and with a random estimate.
