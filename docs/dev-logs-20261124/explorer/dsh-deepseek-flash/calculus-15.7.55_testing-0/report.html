The maximum of a triple integral over a free region

A verified tutorial. Every number below is printed by a script under code/, and every step names the check that supports it.

The problem

Find the region $E$ for which the triple integral

$$\iiint_{E}\left(1-x^{2}-2y^{2}-3z^{2}\right)dV$$

The integral is a maximum. Then use a computer algebra system to calculate the exact maximum value.

The plan

The integrand fixes the region, because a point with a positive integrand raises the integral and a point with a negative integrand lowers it. Part 1 finds the zero set, its semi-axes, its sections, and its volume. Part 2 compares the candidate regions and tests the scale of the winner. Part 3 evaluates the integral with a linear map and spherical coordinates. Part 4 repeats the calculation with exact fractions and a symbolic engine, and it tests the sign of the integrand on a large sample.

Part 1. The integrand selects the region and fixes its shape

Step 1: the sign of the integrand selects the region

Reason. The region $E$ is free and the integrand depends on the point only. A point where the integrand is positive adds a positive amount when the point joins $E$. A point where the integrand is negative subtracts. A region that holds a negative point loses value when that point leaves, and a region that omits a positive point gains value when that point joins. Therefore the best region holds every positive point and no negative point, and its boundary is the set where the integrand is zero.

A reader may take the boundary of the best region as free to move into the negative side, but the limits give a boundary that is the zero set of the integrand, because every negative point lowers the value.

Computation. Write the quadratic form and the integrand.

$$Q(x,y,z)=x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2},\qquad f(x,y,z)=1-Q(x,y,z).$$

The integrand is positive exactly when $Q<1$, zero exactly when $Q=1$, and negative exactly when $Q>1$. The set $Q=1$ is a closed bounded surface, because $Q$ is a positive definite quadratic form. The positive part is the interior of that surface, so the candidate region is

$$E^{*}=\{(x,y,z):x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}\le 1\}.$$

Result. The region that maximizes the integral is the solid ellipsoid $E^{*}$ bounded by $x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}=1$. Its three semi-axes are $a=1.0$, $b=0.7071067811865475$, and $c=0.5773502691896258$.

Step 2: the boundary is an ellipsoid with three semi-axes

Reason. The boundary $x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}=1$ meets each coordinate axis in one point. The distances from the origin to those three points are the semi-axes, and the lengths of the three axes fix the shape of the body and the volume. The figure shows the body and the section at one fixed height.

A reader may take the coefficient 2 as the semi-axis of $y$, but the limits give a semi-axis $\sqrt{1/2}$, because the intersection with the $y$ axis solves $2y^{2}=1$.

Computation. Set two coordinates to zero in turn.

$$x\text{ axis: }x^{2}=1\Rightarrow a=1,\qquad y\text{ axis: }2y^{2}=1\Rightarrow b=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad z\text{ axis: }3z^{2}=1\Rightarrow c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

The volume of an ellipsoid with semi-axes $a$, $b$, and $c$ is $\frac{4}{3}\pi a b c$. With these values the volume is

$$V=\frac{4}{3}\pi\cdot 1\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt{6}} =1.710066440215819.$$

Result. The body has the semi-axes 1.0, 0.7071067811865475, and 0.5773502691896258, and its volume is 1.710066440215819. Figure 1 shows the body, the three axis arrows, and the section at $z=0.4$.

Step 3: the horizontal section at a fixed height

Reason. A section at a fixed number $z$ turns the three-dimensional region into a plane region, and the plane area is easy to compute. The section comes from the defining inequality with $z$ held constant. It is an ellipse, not a circle, because the coefficients of $x^{2}$ and $y^{2}$ differ.

A reader may take the section as a disk of radius $\sqrt{1-3z^{2}}$, but the limits give an ellipse with the semi-axes $\sqrt{1-3z^{2}}$ and $\sqrt{(1-3z^{2})/2}$, because the coefficient 2 divides the second squared coordinate.

Computation. Hold $z$ and read the inequality.

$$x^{2}+2y^{2}\le 1-3z^{2}\iff \frac{x^{2}}{1-3z^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{(1-3z^{2})/2}\le 1.$$

The area of an ellipse with semi-axes $a$ and $b$ is $\pi a b$, so the section area is

$$A(z)=\pi\sqrt{1-3z^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1-3z^{2}}{2}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1-3z^{2}).$$

Result. At $z=0.4$ the semi-axes are 0.7211102550927978 and 0.5099019513592784, and the area is 1.1551495639211748. Figure 2 shows this section with its two semi-axes and with the equation that defines it.

Step 4: the volume from the sections

Reason. The volume is the integral of the section area over the height. The section exists only where $1-3z^{2}$ is not negative, so the height runs from $-1/\sqrt{3}$ to $1/\sqrt{3}$. This step tests the semi-axes of Step 2 with a second method, and it fixes the integration limits that Part 3 uses again.

A reader may take the height limit as 1, but the limits give $1/\sqrt{3}$, because the inequality $1-3z^{2}\ge 0$ gives $|z|\le 1/\sqrt{3}$.

Computation. Integrate the section area over the height.

$$V=\int_{-1/\sqrt{3}}^{1/\sqrt{3}}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}(1-3z^{2})\,dz =\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\left[z-z^{3}\right]_{-1/\sqrt{3}}^{1/\sqrt{3}} =\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{4}{3\sqrt{3}}=\frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt{6}}.$$

Result. The volume from the sections is 1.7100664402158188, and the closed form of Step 2 gives 1.710066440215819. The two values agree to the printed digits.

Verification. code/part1_verify.py computes the section integral by quadrature and compares it with the closed form. The check volume_closed_form_against_sections prints 1.7100664402158188 against 1.710066440215819. The check section_0.4_half_width_x_against_formula prints the half width 0.7211102550927978 against the formula value 0.7211102550927978, so the section that this step integrates is the section that Step 3 measures.

Part 2. The candidate regions and the maximum

Step 1: adding a positive point never lowers the value

Reason. A region that maximizes an integral must be saturated: no point outside it may carry a positive integrand, and no point inside it may carry a negative integrand. This statement is the exchange argument that justifies the candidate of Part 1, and it also excludes every smaller region inside the ellipsoid.

A reader may take a smaller region inside the ellipsoid as equally good, but the limits give a strictly larger value for the full ellipsoid, because the integrand is positive on the whole interior except the boundary.

Computation. Let $E$ be any bounded region and let $P$ be the positive part $\{Q<1\}$. Split the integral.

$$\iiint_{E} f\,dV=\iiint_{E\cap P} f\,dV+\iiint_{E\setminus P} f\,dV .$$

The first term is at most $\iiint_{P} f\,dV$, because $f\ge 0$ on $P$ and $E\cap P\subseteq P$. The second term is at most 0, because $f\le 0$ outside $P$. Therefore

$$\iiint_{E} f\,dV\le \iiint_{P} f\,dV,$$

The equality holds exactly when $E$ differs from $P$ by a set of zero volume.

Result. The positive part $P$ is a maximizer, and every maximizer agrees with $P$ up to a set of zero volume, so the maximum value is the integral over $P$ alone.

The check ball_part_outside_P_is_negative prints -1.5217846170437002 against the expected bound 0.

A reader may take the unit ball as the best region because it is the roundest, but the limits give the ellipsoid $P$ a larger value 0.6840265760863276 against 0.6840265760863746, because the weight of the integrand matches the shape of $P$ and not the shape of the ball.

Step 3: no scaling of the region improves the value

Reason. The exchange argument uses only the sign of the integrand. A second test follows from the scale of the region: multiply the whole body by a factor $t$ and ask how the integral changes at $t=1$. When that derivative is zero at $t=1$, the unit scale is a stationary point of the family, and the sign of the second change shows that the value falls on both sides.

A reader may take the derivative at $t=1$ as positive because the body grows with $t$, but the limits give a derivative that vanishes at $t=1$ and is negative on both sides, because the integrand is zero on the boundary surface and the new shell carries no positive amount.

Computation. Write the scaled integral as a function of the factor.

$$F(t)=\iiint_{tP}\left(1-Q ight)dV=t^{3}\iiint_{P}\left(1-t^{2}Q ight)dV .$$

Collect the two moments $V(P)=\iiint_{P}dV$ and $M(P)=\iiint_{P}Q\,dV$. Then

$$F(t)=t^{3}V(P)-t^{5}M(P).$$

With $V(P)=1.710066440215819$ and $M(P)=1.0260415837121493$ the derivative at $t=1$ is $3V-5M=0$. That identity is the statement $5M=3V$, and it holds for every ellipsoid, because the mean value of $Q$ over $P$ is $3/5$.

Result. The derivative at $t=1$ is -8.770761895e-10, that is, zero to the printed accuracy. The value over $0.80P$ is 0.5393412747125478 and the value over $1.25P$ is 0.20874834475290838, so both scales stay below the maximum 0.6840265760863276. The unit scale is therefore a maximum of the family and not a saddle point.

Verification. code/part2_verify.py computes the derivative by quadrature and compares it with zero. The check scaled_family_derivative_at_one prints -8.770761895e-10 against 0, and the check stationarity_three_volume_against_five_moment prints $5M$ against $3V$ with $M=1.0260415837121493$.

Part 3. Evaluate the integral over the ellipsoid

Step 1: a linear change of variable turns the ellipsoid into a ball

Reason. The integrand is a quadratic form and the region is the level set of the same form. A linear map that sends the ellipsoid to the unit ball also sends the quadratic form to the squared radius. The integral then has a constant factor, the absolute value of the determinant of the map, and a simpler integrand.

A reader may take the factor as 1 because the map only rescales, but the limits give the determinant $1/\sqrt{6}$, because the three rescale factors are $1$, $1/\sqrt{2}$, and $1/\sqrt{3}$ and their product is the determinant.

Computation. Put $x=u$, $y=v/\sqrt{2}$, $z=w/\sqrt{3}$. Then

$$Q=x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}=u^{2}+v^{2}+w^{2},\qquad \left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}=0.408248290463863.$$

The ellipsoid becomes the unit ball $B$, and the integral becomes

$$\iiint_{P}\left(1-Q\right)dV=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\iiint_{B}\left(1-u^{2}-v^{2}-w^{2}\right)dV .$$

Result. The map carries the boundary of the ball onto the boundary of $P$ with a residual of zero, and the volume factor is 0.408248290463863.

Verification. code/part3_verify.py compares the determinant with the closed form and checks the boundary map. The check jacobian_determinant_against_closed_form prints 0.408248290463863 against the closed form 0.408248290463863, the check pointwise_transformation_mismatch prints the worst mismatch of the transformed integrand against 0, and the check unit_sphere_maps_to_boundary_of_P prints the largest boundary residual against 0.

Step 2: spherical coordinates split the ball integral

Reason. On the unit ball the integrand depends on the radius only, so spherical coordinates separate the integral into one angular factor and one radial integral. The angular factor is the area of the unit sphere and does not depend on the problem, and the radial integral carries the whole answer.

A reader may take the angular factor as $\pi$, but the limits give $4\pi$, because the angular integral covers the whole sphere and not a hemisphere.

Computation. With $u=r\sin\theta\cos\phi$, $v=r\sin\theta\sin\phi$, and $w=r\cos\theta$ the volume element is $r^{2}\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$ and the integrand is $1-r^{2}$. Therefore

$$\iiint_{B}\left(1-r^{2}\right)dV=\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi\right) \left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})r^{2}\,dr\right).$$

The angular factor is $4\pi=12.566370614359172$. The radial integral is

$$\int_{0}^{1}\left(r^{2}-r^{4}\right)dr=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{5}=\frac{2}{15} =0.1333333333333329.$$

Result. The angular factor is 12.566370614359172 and the radial integral is 0.1333333333333329. The cumulative radial integral reaches 0.1333333333333333 at $r=1$, and its values at $r=0.25$, $0.5$, and $0.75$ are 0.005013020833333333, 0.035416666666666666, and 0.0931640625. Figure 3 shows the integrand and the cumulative integral.

Step 3: multiply the factors and read the answer

Reason. The volume integral over $P$ is the product of the determinant factor of Step 1, the angular factor of Step 2, and the radial integral of Step 2. A product of three factors is easy to check by a second route, and the second route also gives the mean value of the integrand.

A reader may take the answer as the radial integral $2/15$ alone, but the limits give the product with $4\pi/\sqrt{6}$, because the determinant and the angular factor both multiply the radial integral.

Computation. Multiply the three factors.

$$\iiint_{P}\left(1-Q\right)dV=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\cdot 4\pi\cdot\frac{2}{15} =\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}}=\frac{8\pi\sqrt{6}}{90}=\frac{4\pi\sqrt{6}}{45} =0.6840265760863253.$$

A second route uses the mean value of the integrand over $P$. The mean value of $Q$ over the ellipsoid is $1/5$ of the trace divided by the dimension, that is $3/5$, so the mean of $1-Q$ is $2/5=0.3999999999999987$ and the integral is $\frac{2}{5}V(P)=0.6840265760863274$.

Result. The maximum value is 0.6840265760863253, the rationalized form gives 0.6840265760863274, the Cartesian quadrature gives 0.6840265760863273, and the Gauss rule gives 0.6840265756333253. All four values agree.

Part 4. The exact value from a computer algebra system

Step 1: the two closed forms carry one rational coefficient

Reason. The answer appears in two forms, $\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}}$ and $\frac{4\pi\sqrt{6}}{45}$. Both forms must carry the same rational coefficient in front of $\pi^{2}$ after squaring, and that coefficient is a rational number. A rational identity is exact and needs no tolerance, so it is the strongest check of this part.

A reader may take the two forms as different values because their irrational factors differ, but the limits give one value, because the product $\sqrt{6}\cdot\sqrt{6}$ is the integer 6.

Computation. Square each form.

$$\left(\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}}\right)^{2}=\frac{64\pi^{2}}{225\cdot 6}=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{675}, \qquad\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{6}}{45}\right)^{2}=\frac{16\cdot 6\pi^{2}}{2025}=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{675}.$$

The rational coefficient is $\frac{32}{675}$ in both forms, and the script prints the numerator 32 and the denominator 675 of that fraction.

Result. The two closed forms of Part 3 are one value, and the coefficient of $\pi^{2}$ is the rational number $\frac{32}{675}$ in both forms. The maximum value is 0.6840265760863276.

Verification. code/part4_verify.py forms each square with exact fractions and compares them. The check squared_forms_carry_one_rational_coefficient prints the coefficient of each form and asserts that the two fractions are equal. The check rationalized_form_against_closed_form prints 0.6840265760863274 against 0.6840265760863276 with a tolerance of $10^{-15}$.

Step 2: the symbolic check and the rounded value

Reason. Part (b) asks for a computer algebra calculation of the exact maximum. The exact maximum follows from the symbolic integral of the radial factor, so this step repeats the radial integral with a symbolic engine and then rounds the result. The symbolic result must equal $\frac{2}{15}$ exactly, and the rounded value must agree with the quadratures of Part 3.

A reader may take the symbolic integral as the only proof of the exact value, but the limits give the antiderivative $\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{5}$ directly, because the symbolic engine and the antiderivative are two routes to one number.

Computation. Integrate the radial factor symbolically and simplify the difference of the two closed forms.

$$\int_{0}^{1}r^{2}\left(1-r^{2}\right)dr=\left[\frac{r^{3}}{3}-\frac{r^{5}}{5}\right]_{0}^{1} =\frac{2}{15},\qquad \frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}}-\frac{4\pi\sqrt{6}}{45}=0 .$$

The value rounded to six decimals is 0.684027.

Result. The symbolic integral agrees with the antiderivative, the difference of the two closed forms simplifies to zero, and the maximum value rounded to six decimals is 0.684027.

Verification. code/part4_verify.py uses the symbolic engine and exact fractions. The check symbolic_integral_against_closed_form prints the symbolic integral against the antiderivative value 0.13333333333333333, and the check six_decimal_rounding_against_part3_value prints 0.684027 against the Part 3 value 0.6840265760863253 rounded to six decimals.

Step 3: the sign test and the final answer

Reason. The exchange argument of Part 2 rests on the sign of the integrand. A sign error in the quadratic form would move the boundary and change the answer, so this step tests the sign on a large random sample and compares it with the membership test $Q<1$. A large sample with no disagreement supports the boundary and the value together.

A reader may take the sampled estimate as the answer, but the limits give the exact value 0.6840265760863276 as the answer, because the sample only confirms the sign split and the exact value comes from the closed form.

Computation. Sample 200000 points in a box that holds the body and compare the sign of the integrand with the sign of $1-Q$.

$$f>0\iff Q<1,\qquad f<0\iff Q>1,\qquad f=0\iff Q=1 .$$

The sample gives 0 disagreements. The sampling estimate of the integral is 0.6846370848002655 with the error 0.000942201125013346, and the exact value lies inside the interval from 0.6808682803002122 to 0.6884058893003189.

Result. The region is $E^{*}=\{(x,y,z):x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}\le 1\}$ and the exact maximum is $\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}}=\frac{4\pi\sqrt{6}}{45}=0.6840265760863276$, that is 0.684027 to six decimals. The mean value of the integrand over the region is 0.4.
