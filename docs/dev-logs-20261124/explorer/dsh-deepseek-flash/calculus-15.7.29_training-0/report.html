Six iterated integrals over one solid

A verified tutorial for Section 15.7, Exercise 29. The page shows the reason of each step before its computation, and it names the script that checks every number.

Problem

Express the integral $\iiint_E f(x, y, z)\, dV$ as an iterated integral in six different ways, where $E$ is the solid bounded by the given surfaces, $y = 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}$ and $y = 0$.

The source image holds no figure. Every figure of this page is a product of this workspace, and every figure was drawn by the script that the page names.

Answers at a glance

PartAnswer
Part 1The solid is $E = \{(x, y, z) : 0 \leq y \leq 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}\}$, with base area $2\pi$ and largest height $4$.
Part 2The projection on the plane $y = 0$ is $x^{2} + 4z^{2} \leq 4$. The section at a height $y$ is a filled ellipse of area $\pi(4 - y)/2$, and it is a single point at $y = 4$.
Part 3The two orders with the inner integral in $y$ are $dy\,dz\,dx$ and $dy\,dx\,dz$.
Part 4The two orders with the inner integral in $z$ are $dz\,dy\,dx$ and $dz\,dx\,dy$. Neither needs a split.
Part 5The two orders with the inner integral in $x$ are $dx\,dz\,dy$ and $dx\,dy\,dz$. Neither needs a split.
Part 6The six forms are equal for every integrand $f$ that is continuous on $E$.

Part 1. Read the solid from the two equations

GoalTurn the two surface equations into one inequality chain, then read the base and the top of the solid from that chain. No figure takes part in this reading.

Step 1.1. The inequality chain on $y$

Reason

Both surfaces have the form $y = g(x, z)$. A vertical line therefore meets the solid once, between the lower surface and the upper surface. The chain follows from the two equations alone.

Computation

The upper surface is $y = 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}$ and the lower surface is $y = 0$. A point belongs to $E$ when its height lies between them:

$$E = \{(x, y, z) : 0 \leq y \leq 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}\}.$$

The coefficient on $z^{2}$ is $4$, not $1$. The chain is not empty: $x^{2} + 4z^{2} \geq 0$, so the upper bound is never below the lower bound, and the point $(0, 0, 0)$ belongs to $E$.

Result

Every bound of every order comes from this one chain. The chain is the single source of the six answers.

Verification

 compares two membership tests: the direct chain, and the substitution $x = 2u$, $z = w$, $y = 4s$, which maps $E$ onto the unit disk times the unit interval. On 200 000 random points the two tests give 0 disagreements.

Step 1.2. The trace on the plane $y = 0$

Reason

The lower wall is the plane $y = 0$. A point of that plane belongs to the solid exactly when the upper bound at its position is not negative.

Computation

Set $y = 0$ in the chain: $0 \leq 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}$, which is the same statement as

$$x^{2} + 4z^{2} \leq 4.$$

This is an ellipse with semi-axis $2$ along $x$ and semi-axis $1$ along $z$. Its area is $\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 2\pi = 6.283185307$. The four extreme points are $(\pm 2, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 0, \pm 1)$.

Result

The base of the solid is a filled ellipse of area $2\pi$. The solid stands on the plane $y = 0$.

Verification

 compares a quadrature of the base area with the product of the two semi-axes, and both with a Monte Carlo estimate that carries its standard error. The three values agree within one standard error.

Step 1.3. The top of the solid, and the closure of the boundary

Reason

The statement names two surfaces. A reader must check from the equations that no third wall exists, and must find the largest height.

Computation

The upper bound $4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}$ is largest where $x = 0$ and $z = 0$. There it equals $4$. So the largest height is $y = 4$, at the single point $(0, 4, 0)$.

The boundary closes because the upper bound falls to $0$ on the ellipse $x^{2} + 4z^{2} = 4$, where the paraboloid meets the base. Above that ellipse the upper bound is negative, so no point of the solid lies there.

Result

The solid has exactly two pieces of boundary: the paraboloid, and the filled ellipse on the plane $y = 0$. Its largest height is $4$, reached at one point.

Verification

 casts 400 rays from interior points and records where each ray leaves the solid. Every exit lies on the plane $y = 0$ or on the paraboloid, with a residual below $1.4 \times 10^{-15}$. No ray leaves through a third surface. The same script compares the largest height of a grid with the value $4$ of the apex.

Part 2. The projection on the plane $y = 0$ and the sections at a fixed height

GoalDescribe the projection that the two orders with an outer integral in $x$ or in $z$ need, and describe the section at a fixed height.

Step 2.1. The outline of the projection

Reason

A projection drops the coordinate that the plane does not keep. Here the coordinate $y$ drops. A position $(x, z)$ belongs to the projection when at least one height $y$ belongs to the solid at that position.

Computation

The chain allows a height at $(x, z)$ exactly when $4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2} \geq 0$. So the projection is

$$D = \{(x, z) : x^{2} + 4z^{2} \leq 4\}$$

on the plane $y = 0$, with area $2\pi$. The projection keeps the coordinates $x$ and $z$, so its plane is $y = 0$.

Result

The projection is one filled ellipse. It is the same set as the base, because the tallest wall stands directly above the base.

Verification

 compares three descriptions of $D$: the inequality, a sweep in $x$, and a sweep in $z$. On 200 000 random points of the plane $y = 0$ the three descriptions give 0 disagreements.

Step 2.2. The section at a fixed height

Reason

At a fixed height $y$ the section is the set of positions where the upper bound reaches at least that height. The section is a set in a plane of constant $y$, so it keeps the coordinates $x$ and $z$.

Computation

Set $y$ to a constant in the chain: $x^{2} + 4z^{2} \leq 4 - y$. This is a filled ellipse with semi-axes $\sqrt{4 - y}$ and $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y}$, so its area is

$$A(y) = \pi \cdot \sqrt{4 - y} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{4 - y}}{2} = \frac{\pi (4 - y)}{2}.$$

The section is filled, not a ring: the inequality has the form $x^{2} + 4z^{2} \leq$ a number, so it holds at the center $(0, y, 0)$ and along every radial line from the center to the outline.

Result

The area falls from $2\pi = 6.283185307$ at $y = 0$ to $0$ at $y = 4$. At $y = 2$ the area is $\pi = 3.141592654$.

Verification

 measures the area at seven heights by three methods: a quadrature of the half width, the ellipse formula with the measured semi-axes, and the closed form $\pi (4 - y)/2$. The largest difference is below $1.1 \times 10^{-14}$. The same script walks 720 radial directions at the heights $0$, $2$, and $3.9$ and tests a point at half the radius: a ring would fail that test, and it never fails.

Step 2.3. The section at each boundary value of $y$

Reason

A formula for the semi-axes returns $0$ at $y = 4$. A reader must read the printed corner list before naming any shape, because a shape with equal corners is not an ellipse.

Computation

The script prints the four extreme points of the section at each height.

At $y = 0$ the corners are $(2, 0, 0)$, $(-2, 0, 0)$, $(0, 0, 1)$, and $(0, 0, -1)$: four distinct positions, so the section is a filled ellipse.

At $y = 2$ the corners are $(\sqrt{2}, 2, 0)$, $(-\sqrt{2}, 2, 0)$, $(0, 2, \sqrt{2}/2)$, and $(0, 2, -\sqrt{2}/2)$: four distinct positions, so the section is a filled ellipse.

At $y = 4$ the four corners coincide at $(0, 4, 0)$: one position, so the section is a single point.

Result

The section is a filled ellipse for $0 \leq y < 4$, and a single point at $y = 4$. The collapse happens first at the top of the solid, and nowhere else.

Verification

 prints the corner list of each section and counts the distinct positions. It also counts the points of a grid of $2001$ by $2001$ positions at $y = 4$ that belong to the section, and the count is exactly $1$.

Part 3. The two orders with the inner integral in $y$

GoalWrite the two orders whose innermost differential is $dy$, and check each one against the exact value of a test integral.

Step 3.1. The order $dy\,dz\,dx$

Reason

The chain gives the limits in $y$ at once, for each position in the projection. The remaining two integrals run over the projection $D$, and the outer one is the widest.

Computation

At a fixed $(x, z)$ the chain reads $0 \leq y \leq 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}$. Then $(x, z)$ runs over $D$, where $x$ is the widest variable: $x$ from $-2$ to $2$, and at a fixed $x$ the variable $z$ runs from $-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}$ to $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f\, dV = \int_{-2}^{2} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}/2}^{\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}/2} \int_{0}^{4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}} f(x, y, z)\, dy\, dz\, dx.$$

The order is not the order $dz\,dy\,dx$: here the innermost integral is the one in $y$, and the outermost is the one in $x$.

Result

The first form. For $f = 1$ its value is $4\pi = 12.566370614$.

Verification

 compares the adaptive quadrature of this form with the exact value $4\pi$ from , and with the exact value $16\pi/3$ of the integral of $f = y$. It also compares the printed limits with the defining inequality in both directions.

Step 3.2. The order $dy\,dx\,dz$

Reason

The inner integral still runs in $y$, so its limits do not change. Only the two outer integrals change place, and the wider of the two variables must be the outer one.

Computation

The projection is $x^{2} + 4z^{2} \leq 4$. Sweep $z$ first: $z$ lies between $-1$ and $1$, and at a fixed $z$ the variable $x$ runs between $-2\sqrt{1 - z^{2}}$ and $2\sqrt{1 - z^{2}}$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f\, dV = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-2\sqrt{1 - z^{2}}}^{2\sqrt{1 - z^{2}}} \int_{0}^{4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}} f(x, y, z)\, dy\, dx\, dz.$$
Result

The second form. Its value for $f = 1$ is $4\pi$, the same as the first form.

Verification

 compares this form with the exact values $4\pi$ and $16\pi/3$, and compares its limits with the defining inequality in both directions.

Part 4. The two orders with the inner integral in $z$

GoalWrite the two orders whose innermost differential is $dz$, and decide whether the outer range needs a split.

Step 4.1. The interval in $z$, and the question of a split

Reason

When the innermost integral is the one in $z$, the limits follow from the chain solved for $z$. A split is necessary only when the remaining region has two separate pieces at one value of the outer variable.

Computation

Solve the chain for $z$: $4z^{2} \leq 4 - x^{2} - y$, so

$$-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y} \leq z \leq \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}.$$

The full width in $z$ is $\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}$. The remaining variables $(x, y)$ run over the set $0 \leq y \leq 4 - x^{2}$. At a fixed $x$ that set is one interval in $y$, so no split is needed.

Result

The interval in $z$ is one interval, and the region of $(x, y)$ is one piece at every $x$. Neither of the two orders needs a split.

Verification

 finds the half width in $z$ by a root search on the upper surface and compares it with the closed form $\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}/2$ at five positions. It then walks a line across the region of $(x, y)$ at 41 positions of $x$ and counts the separate runs: the count is $1$ everywhere.

Step 4.2. The order $dz\,dy\,dx$

Reason

The inner limits come from step 4.1. The middle integral runs in $y$ at a fixed $x$, and the outer integral runs in $x$ over the widest range.

Computation

For a fixed $x$ the variable $y$ runs from $0$ to $4 - x^{2}$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f\, dV = \int_{-2}^{2} \int_{0}^{4 - x^{2}} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}/2}^{\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}/2} f(x, y, z)\, dz\, dy\, dx.$$
Result

The third form. Its value for $f = 1$ is $4\pi$. For $f = x^{2}$ its value is $8\pi/3 = 8.377580410$.

Verification

 compares this form with the exact values $4\pi$ and $8\pi/3$ from , and compares its limits with the defining inequality in both directions.

Step 4.3. The order $dz\,dx\,dy$

Reason

The inner integral still runs in $z$. The outer variable is now $y$, because $y$ runs over the full height of the solid and $x$ is narrower at each height.

Computation

The height runs from $0$ to $4$. At a fixed height the variable $x$ runs from $-\sqrt{4 - y}$ to $\sqrt{4 - y}$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f\, dV = \int_{0}^{4} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - y}}^{\sqrt{4 - y}} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - y - x^{2}}/2}^{\sqrt{4 - y - x^{2}}/2} f(x, y, z)\, dz\, dx\, dy.$$
Result

The fourth form. Its value for $f = 1$ is $4\pi$, the same as the third form.

Verification

 compares this form with the exact values $4\pi$ and $8\pi/3$, and compares its limits with the defining inequality in both directions.

Part 5. The two orders with the inner integral in $x$

GoalWrite the two orders whose innermost differential is $dx$, and decide whether the outer range needs a split.

Step 5.1. The interval in $x$, and the question of a split

Reason

When the innermost integral is the one in $x$, the limits follow from the chain solved for $x$. A split is necessary only when the remaining region has two separate pieces at one value of the outer variable.

Computation

Solve the chain for $x$: $x^{2} \leq 4 - y - 4z^{2}$, so

$$-\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}} \leq x \leq \sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}.$$

The full width in $x$ is $2\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}$. The remaining variables $(y, z)$ run over the set $0 \leq y \leq 4 - 4z^{2}$ and $-1 \leq z \leq 1$. At a fixed $z$ that set is one interval in $y$, so no split is needed.

Result

The interval in $x$ is one interval, and the region of $(y, z)$ is one piece at every $z$. Neither of the two orders needs a split.

Verification

 finds the half width in $x$ by a root search on the upper surface and compares it with the closed form $\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}$ at six positions. It then walks a line across the region of $(y, z)$ at 41 positions and counts the separate runs: the count is $1$ everywhere.

Step 5.2. The order $dx\,dz\,dy$

Reason

The inner limits come from step 5.1. The middle integral runs in $z$ at a fixed height, and the outer integral runs in $y$ over the full height.

Computation

At a fixed height $y$ the variable $z$ runs from $-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y}$ to $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y}$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f\, dV = \int_{0}^{4} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - y}/2}^{\sqrt{4 - y}/2} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}}^{\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}} f(x, y, z)\, dx\, dz\, dy.$$
Result

The fifth form. Its value for $f = 1$ is $4\pi$. For $f = z^{2}$ its value is $2\pi/3 = 2.094395102$.

Verification

 compares this form with the exact values $4\pi$ and $2\pi/3$ from , and compares its limits with the defining inequality in both directions.

Step 5.3. The order $dx\,dy\,dz$

Reason

The inner integral still runs in $x$. The outer variable is now $z$, because $z$ runs over the full width of the base and $y$ is narrower at each $z$.

Computation

The variable $z$ runs from $-1$ to $1$. At a fixed $z$ the height $y$ runs from $0$ to $4 - 4z^{2}$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f\, dV = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{4 - 4z^{2}} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - 4z^{2} - y}}^{\sqrt{4 - 4z^{2} - y}} f(x, y, z)\, dx\, dy\, dz.$$
Result

The sixth form. Its value for $f = 1$ is $4\pi$, the same as the fifth form.

Verification

 compares this form with the exact values $4\pi$ and $2\pi/3$, and compares its limits with the defining inequality in both directions.

Part 6. The six forms in one table

GoalCollect the six forms, then check that all six agree for five test functions with an exact value.

Step 6.1. The table of the six forms

Reason

A table lets the reader compare the six orders. The innermost integral always reads the chain; the outer two integrals read the projection or the height range.

Computation

The six forms, with the innermost integral first in each label:

$$\begin{aligned}dy\,dz\,dx &: \int_{-2}^{2} \int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2}}} \int_{0}^{4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}} f\, dy\, dz\, dx,\\dy\,dx\,dz &: \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-2\sqrt{1 - z^{2}}}^{2\sqrt{1 - z^{2}}} \int_{0}^{4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}} f\, dy\, dx\, dz,\\dz\,dy\,dx &: \int_{-2}^{2} \int_{0}^{4 - x^{2}} \int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y}} f\, dz\, dy\, dx,\\dz\,dx\,dy &: \int_{0}^{4} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - y}}^{\sqrt{4 - y}} \int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y - x^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y - x^{2}}} f\, dz\, dx\, dy,\\dx\,dz\,dy &: \int_{0}^{4} \int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4 - y}} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}}^{\sqrt{4 - y - 4z^{2}}} f\, dx\, dz\, dy,\\dx\,dy\,dz &: \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{4 - 4z^{2}} \int_{-\sqrt{4 - 4z^{2} - y}}^{\sqrt{4 - 4z^{2} - y}} f\, dx\, dy\, dz.\end{aligned}$$
Result

Six forms, one for each order of the three differentials. No form uses a split, because every outer region is one piece.

Verification

 evaluates all six forms for five test functions.  prints the same six limit sets as a picture, and every string of that picture is copied from the docstring of the matching function.

Step 6.2. The check that the six forms agree

Reason

A single order with a wrong limit can still look right. Six orders that disagree would show the fault at once, so the six values of one test function must agree with each other and with the exact value.

Computation

The exact value of the integral of $x^{a} y^{b} z^{c}$ over $E$ follows by the substitution $x = 2u$, $z = w$, $y = 4s$, which maps $E$ onto the unit disk times the unit interval and multiplies the volume element by $8$. The result is a rational multiple of $\pi$.

The five test functions give: $f = 1$ gives $4\pi = 12.566370614$; $f = y$ gives $16\pi/3 = 16.755160819$; $f = z$ gives $0$; $f = x^{2}$ gives $8\pi/3 = 8.377580410$; $f = z^{2}$ gives $2\pi/3 = 2.094395102$.

Result

Every order gives the exact value of every test function. The largest difference over the 30 pairs is below $7 \times 10^{-13}$, and the largest spread over the six orders of one function is below $8 \times 10^{-14}$. The six forms are equal.

Verification

 compares each of the 30 values with the exact rational multiple of $\pi$ from , and compares the six values of each function with each other.
