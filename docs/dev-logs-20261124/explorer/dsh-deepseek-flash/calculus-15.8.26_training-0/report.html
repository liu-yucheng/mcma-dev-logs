Problem 17: the volume that a cylinder cuts out of a sphere

Stewart Calculus, section 15.8, exercise 26. The answer stays symbolic in the radius $a$, because the statement gives no numeric value for it.

Statement

Use cylindrical coordinates. (a) Find the volume of the solid that the cylinder $r = a \cos \theta$ cuts out of the sphere of radius $a$ centered at the origin. (b) Illustrate the solid of part (a) by graphing the sphere and the cylinder on the same screen.

Part 5 checks the volume by three methods that do not use the closed form. Part 6 draws the two surfaces for part (b) of the statement.

Part 1

Step 1.1: The equation $r = a\cos\theta$ draws a circle in the plane $z = 0$

Reason. The instruction asks for cylindrical coordinates, so the first task is to read the given surface in the plane $z = 0$. Multiply the equation by $r$ and use $x = r\cos\theta$ and $y = r\sin\theta$. Then $r^{2} = a r\cos\theta$ becomes $x^{2} + y^{2} = a x$. Complete the square in $x$ and the equation becomes $(x - a/2)^{2} + y^{2} = (a/2)^{2}$. The trace in the plane $z = 0$ is therefore a circle of radius $a/2$ about the point $(a/2, 0)$, and the surface itself is the vertical cylinder over that circle.

Computation. Take the reference radius $a = 1$. The trace has center $(0.5, 0)$ and diameter $1.0$, and its area is $\pi a^{2}/4 = 0.785398163397$. The trace also passes through the origin, where $r = 0$, and it stays in the half plane $x \ge 0$.

Common mistake. A reader may take $r = a\cos\theta$ for the circle of radius $a$ about the origin, but completing the square gives the radius $a/2$ about $(a/2, 0)$.

Result. The cutting surface is the vertical cylinder of radius $a/2$ about the line $x = a/2$, $y = 0$. Figure 1 draws its trace in the plane $z = 0$, the center $C$, and one radius of length $a/2$.

Verification. code/part1_verify.py prints base_area_grid as $0.785408632020912$ and base_area_lens_formula as $0.7853981633974483$, with a tolerance of $2.0\times10^{-3}$. It also prints cylinder_predicates_agree as $0.0$ over $60000$ sample points, and tangency_center_distance as $0.5$ against $0.5$.

Step 1.2: The angle range is $-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2$

Reason. The symbol $r$ is a distance from the $z$-axis, so $r \ge 0$. The right side $a\cos\theta$ must therefore be not negative, and $a > 0$ gives $\cos\theta \ge 0$. The equation then describes points only for $-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2$, taken modulo $2\pi$. The statement of the problem does not print this range, so the range is a result and not a given fact.

Computation. At both ends of the range, $a\cos(\pm\pi/2) = 6.123\times10^{-17}$, which is zero up to round-off, so the trace closes at the origin. At $\theta = 3\pi/4$ the right side is $-0.7071067811865475$, so no point of the cylinder exists there. Inside the range the distance $r$ reaches its largest value $a = 1.0$ at $\theta = 0$.

Common mistake. A reader may take the full circle $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$, but the limit $r \ge 0$ gives $-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2$, and every angle outside that range returns a negative distance.

Result. The angle range is $-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2$. At both ends the base radius is $0$, and the largest base radius is $a$ at $\theta = 0$.

Verification. code/part1_verify.py prints angle_range_right_end_radius as $6.123\times10^{-17}$ and angle_range_left_end_radius as $6.123\times10^{-17}$, both against $0.0$. It prints angle_outside_range_is_negative as $-0.7071067811865475$ at $\theta = 3\pi/4$.

Step 1.3: The sphere gives the two heights above one base point

Reason. A base point at distance $r$ from the $z$-axis belongs to the solid only at the heights that the sphere allows. Put $x^{2} + y^{2} = r^{2}$ in $x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2} \le a^{2}$ and the bound becomes $z^{2} \le a^{2} - r^{2}$. The two heights are therefore $z = \pm\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$. The upper surface $z = +\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ falls away from the $z$-axis, because its slope $-r/\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ is negative. The lower surface $z = -\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ rises toward the $z$-axis, because its slope $+r/\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ is positive.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. The slope of the upper surface is $-0.314485$ at $r = 0.3$, $-0.75$ at $r = 0.6$, and $-2.064742$ at $r = 0.9$. The slope of the lower surface has the same magnitude with the opposite sign at each of the three radii. The residual of $z^{2} + r^{2} - a^{2}$ at the upper surface is $2.22\times10^{-16}$.

Common mistake. A reader may take the vertical extent above a base point for the constant $2a$, but the sphere gives $2\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$, which falls to $0$ at $r = a$.

Result. Over a base point at distance $r$ the solid is the single vertical segment from $-\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ to $+\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$, of length $2\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$. The right panel of Figure 2 draws this segment and names both of its ends.

Verification. code/part1_verify.py prints vertical_extent_on_sphere as $2.220446049250313\times10^{-16}$ against $0.0$, and upper_surface_slope_at_r_0 with lower_surface_slope_at_r_0 at the radii $0.3$, $0.6$, and $0.9$ with a tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-6}$.

Part 2

Step 2.1: Write the solid as three pairs of limits

Reason. Step 1.2 gives the angle range, step 1.1 gives the radial bound, and step 1.3 gives the height bound. Each bound comes from one surface, so the solid is the set of points that satisfy all three pairs of limits at once:

$$E = \left\{(r,\theta,z) : -\frac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2},\ 0 \le r \le a\cos\theta,\ -\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}} \le z \le \sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}\right\}.$$

The outer variable is $\theta$, the middle variable is $r$, and the inner variable is $z$. This order is legal for one reason only: the limits of $r$ depend on $\theta$ alone, and the limits of $z$ depend on $r$ alone. No inner limit depends on a variable that is still outside it.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. Bisection on the equation of the trace gives the largest radius at the angle $\theta$: the value is $1.0$ at $\theta = 0$, $0.707106781186549$ at $\theta = \pi/4$, and $0.5000000000000022$ at $\theta = \pi/3$. A ray of a fixed angle meets the solid in one segment, so the count of segments along each ray is $1$.

Common mistake. A reader may put the outer integral over $r$ from $0$ to $a$, but the inner limit of $r$ is $a\cos\theta$, which depends on $\theta$ and reaches $0$ at both ends of the range.

Result. The solid is described by the three pairs of limits above, in the order $\theta$, then $r$, then $z$. The left panel of Figure 2 draws the footprint and one polar cell of it.

Verification. code/part2_verify.py prints base_radius_at_theta_0 as $1.0$, base_radius_at_theta_1 as $0.707106781186549$, and single_vertical_segment as $1.0$ for both the smallest and the largest count. It prints region_predicates_agree as $0.0$ disagreements over $80000$ samples, while the control with a shortened angle range prints $6905$ disagreements.

Step 2.2: The volume element carries the factor $r$

Reason. A volume integral in cylindrical coordinates adds the small cells of the grid. A cell between the radii $r$ and $r + dr$ and between the angles $\theta$ and $\theta + d\theta$ has the area $r\,dr\,d\theta$, because the arc length at distance $r$ is $r\,d\theta$ and the radial width is $dr$. The height of the cell is $dz$. The volume element is therefore $dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$, and the factor $r$ is part of the geometry and not of the integrand.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. The polar cell of the figure has the computed area $0.2371875$, and a grid count of the same cell gives $0.23718736$. The volume computed without the factor $r$ is $3.467401100272$, and the volume computed with the factor $r$ is $1.205506213504$.

Common mistake. A reader may drop the factor $r$ from the volume element, but the cell area is $r\,dr\,d\theta$: code/part2_verify.py prints $3.467401100272$ for that integral against $1.205506213504$ for the integral with the factor $r$.

Result. The volume is $V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta}\int_{-\sqrt{a^{2}-r^{2}}}^{\sqrt{a^{2}-r^{2}}} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$.

Verification. code/part2_verify.py prints jacobian_cell_area as $0.2371875$ against the grid value $0.23718736$ with a tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-4}$, and it prints the two volumes above as the control of the factor $r$.

Step 2.3: The inner integral gives $2r\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$

Reason. The integrand $r$ does not contain $z$, so the inner integral is the integrand multiplied by the length of the segment of $z$. Step 1.3 gives the length $2\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$, so the inner integral is $r \cdot 2\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$.

Computation. Take $a = 1$ and $r = 0.5$. Bisection gives the length of the segment as $1.732050807569$, so the inner integral is $0.5 \times 1.732050807569 = 0.866025403785$.

Common mistake. A reader may integrate $z$ from $0$ to $\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$, but the limits are $-\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ and $+\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$, so the length is $2\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$ and the factor $2$ belongs to the value.

Result. After the inner integration,

$$V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}\,dr\,d\theta.$$

Verification. code/part2_verify.py prints vertical_length_by_bisection with an error of $1.44\times10^{-15}$ against the closed length, and single_vertical_segment as $1.0$ for the count of segments along a ray.

Part 3

Step 3.1: The radial integral gives $\frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(1 - |\sin\theta|^{3}\right)$

Reason. Put $u = a^{2} - r^{2}$, so that $du = -2r\,dr$. The integrand becomes $\sqrt{u}$, and the antiderivative is $-\frac{2}{3}u^{3/2}$. At the upper limit $r = a\cos\theta$ the term $a^{2} - r^{2}$ equals $a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta$, so its three halves power is $a^{3}|\sin\theta|^{3}$. The absolute value is necessary, because a power of an even exponent loses the sign of $\sin\theta$.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. At $\theta = 0$ the radial integral is $0.666666666667$. At $\theta = -\pi/4$ it is $0.430964406271$, which is $\frac{2}{3}\left(1 - 0.353553\right)$ because $|\sin(-\pi/4)|^{3} = 0.353553$. The substitution $u = r^{2}$ gives the same value $0.43096440627115096$ as the direct quadrature $0.4309644062711509$.

Common mistake. A reader may keep the sign of $\sin\theta$ and write $\frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(1 - \sin^{3}\theta\right)$, but the limits give $\frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(1 - |\sin\theta|^{3}\right)$: code/part3_verify.py prints $0.902368927062$ for the signed form at $\theta = -\pi/4$ against $0.430964406271$ for the correct form.

Result. After the radial integration,

$$V = \frac{2}{3}a^{3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\left(1 - |\sin\theta|^{3}\right)d\theta.$$

Verification. code/part3_verify.py prints substituted_radial_integral as $0.43096440627115096$ against $0.4309644062711509$, the slope checks antiderivative_slope_at_r_0 at three radii with a tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-7}$, and upper_bound_maximum as $1.0$ against $a$.

Step 3.2: The radial integral vanishes at both ends of the angle range

Reason. At $\theta = \pm\pi/2$ the upper limit $a\cos\theta$ equals $0$, so the interval of $r$ is the single point $r = 0$ and every integral over it is $0$. At $\theta = 0$ the upper limit is $a$, which gives the largest value of the radial integral. On the whole interval $a^{2} - r^{2} \ge 0$, because the upper limit never passes the sphere radius, so the height stays a real number.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. The radial integral is $0.666666666667$ at $\theta = 0$ and $0$ at $\theta = \pm\pi/2$. The largest upper limit is $1.0$, the smallest upper limit is $6.123\times10^{-17}$, and the smallest value of $a^{2} - r^{2}$ on the interval is $0.0$.

Common mistake. A reader may expect a large value at $\theta = \pm\pi/2$ because the vertical segment there is long, but the radial interval has zero width, so the limits give the value $0$.

Result. The radial integral is $0$ at both ends of the angle range and $\frac{2}{3}a^{3}$ at $\theta = 0$, and it stays real on the whole range.

Verification. code/part3_verify.py prints the eleven angles of radial_integral_theta_ with a worst error of $2.22\times10^{-16}$, upper_bound_maximum as $1.0$ against $a$, upper_bound_minimum as $6.123\times10^{-17}$, and upper_bound_keeps_height_real as $0.0$.

Part 4

Step 4.1: The angle integral gives $\pi - \frac{4}{3}$

Reason. The function $1 - |\sin\theta|^{3}$ is even, so its integral over the symmetric range is twice its integral over $[0, \pi/2]$. The antiderivative of $\sin^{3}\theta$ is $-\cos\theta + \frac{1}{3}\cos^{3}\theta$, and on $[0, \pi/2]$ its difference is $0 - \left(-1 + \frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{2}{3}$. The whole integral is therefore $2\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{2}{3}\right) = \pi - \frac{4}{3}$.

Computation. The quadrature of $1 - |\sin\theta|^{3}$ over the range gives $1.808259320256$, and $\pi - \frac{4}{3}$ is also $1.808259320256$. The quadrature of $\sin^{3}\theta$ over the first quadrant is $0.666666666667$, and twice the half interval is $1.808259320256$, which is the whole interval.

Common mistake. A reader may drop the absolute value and use the fact that $\sin^{3}\theta$ is odd, but the integrand carries $|\sin\theta|^{3}$: code/part3_verify.py prints $0.902368927062$ for the signed form at $\theta = -\pi/4$ against $0.430964406271$ for the correct form, so the sign does not cancel.

Result. The angle integral is $\pi - \frac{4}{3} = 1.808259320256$, so

$$V = \frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(\pi - \frac{4}{3}\right).$$

Verification. code/part4_verify.py prints angle_integral_matches_quadrature as $1.8082593202564599$ against $\pi - \frac{4}{3}$, sin_cubed_matches_quadrature as $0.6666666666666666$ against the antiderivative difference, sin_cubed_matches_two_thirds as $0.6666666666666666$, symmetry_doubles_half_interval as $1.8082593202564599$, and the four checks angle_antiderivative_slope_at_0.1 to angle_antiderivative_slope_at_1.3 with a tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-7}$.

Step 4.2: Expand the product and state the volume of the solid

Reason. Multiply $\frac{2}{3}a^{3}$ into the parenthesis of step 4.1. The two terms are $\frac{2}{3}\pi a^{3}$ and $\frac{2}{3}a^{3} \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{9}a^{3}$. The difference is the volume, and the common denominator $9$ gives the single fraction $\frac{a^{3}\left(6\pi - 8\right)}{9}$.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. The value is $1.205506213504$, and the two terms are $2.094395102393$ and $0.888888888889$. At $a = 2$ the value is $9.644049708034$, and at $a = 3$ it is $32.548667764616$. The solid holds the fraction $0.287793409211$ of the ball, and the rest of the ball has the volume $2.983283991282$.

Common mistake. A reader may report the first term alone, but the second term is present: code/part4_verify.py prints $2.094395102393$ for $\frac{2}{3}\pi a^{3}$ against $1.205506213504$ for the volume at $a = 1$.

Result. The volume of part (a) is

$$V = \frac{2}{3}\pi a^{3} - \frac{8}{9}a^{3} = \frac{a^{3}\left(6\pi - 8\right)}{9}.$$

Verification. code/part4_verify.py prints two_forms_at_a_1, two_forms_at_a_2, and two_forms_at_a_3 as $1.205506213504$, $9.644049708034$, and $32.548667764616$, closed_form_matches_triple_integral_a_1 and closed_form_matches_triple_integral_a_2 against the adaptive triple quadrature, fraction_of_sphere as $0.2877934092108062$ against $\frac{6\pi - 8}{12\pi}$, and solid_plus_complement_is_ball as $4.18879020478639$ against $\frac{4\pi}{3}$.

Part 5

Step 5.1: Check the volume with horizontal slices

Reason. A second route to the same number does not use the angle integral. At the height $z$ the slice of the solid is the set of base points that lie inside the sphere and inside the cylinder at once. The sphere keeps the disk of radius $\sqrt{a^{2} - z^{2}}$ about the origin, and the cylinder keeps the disk of radius $a/2$ about $(a/2, 0)$. The slice is the lens where the two disks overlap, and the volume is the integral of the lens area over $z$ from $-a$ to $a$.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. The lens area is $0.785398163397$ at $z = 0$, $0.699791506351$ at $z = 0.5$, and $0.242119161289$ at $z = 0.9$. The integral of the lens area over the whole range of $z$ is $1.205506213504$, which is the volume of step 4.2.

Common mistake. A reader may take a slice for the disk of the sphere alone, but the cylinder keeps only the part of that disk inside its own disk: code/part5_verify.py prints $0.699817782211$ for a grid count of the lens at $z = 0.5$ against $0.699791506351$ for the lens formula, so the slice is smaller than the sphere disk.

Result. The slice construction gives $1.205506213504$ at $a = 1$, which agrees with $\frac{6\pi - 8}{9}$.

Verification. code/part5_verify.py prints horizontal_slices_match_closed_form as $1.205506213504$ against the closed form with a tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-7}$, and lens_area_at_z_0.5 as $0.699791506351$ against the grid value $0.699817782211$ with a tolerance of $3.0\times10^{-3}$.

Step 5.2: Check the answer by scaling, by a second center, and by sampling

Reason. Three independent properties must hold for the answer of step 4.2. First, the geometry grows with the radius, so a change of $a$ to $ka$ multiplies the volume by $k^{3}$. Second, the volume does not depend on the center of the polar coordinates, so the same integral about the axis of the cylinder must give the same number. Third, a sampling estimate must agree with the value within its own standard error.

Computation. Take $a = 1$, $a = 2$, and $a = 3$. The slice integral gives $1.205506214$, $9.644049708$, and $32.548667765$. The ratios are $8.0$ and $27.00000000000001$. The polar integral about the cylinder axis gives $1.205506213504$. The sampling estimate with four million samples is $1.205697$ with a standard error of $0.000489307864$, which is $0.389911$ standard errors from the closed form.

Common mistake. A reader may expect the volume to grow in proportion to the area of the footprint, but the height of the solid grows with $a$ as well, so the report of code/part5_verify.py is the cube: scaling_ratio_at_two gives $8.0$ and scaling_ratio_at_three gives $27.00000000000001$.

Result. All three checks agree with $V = \frac{a^{3}\left(6\pi - 8\right)}{9}$, so the answer of part (a) is

$$V = \frac{2}{3}\pi a^{3} - \frac{8}{9}a^{3}.$$

Verification. code/part5_verify.py prints slice_volume_at_a_1, slice_volume_at_a_2, and slice_volume_at_a_3, scaling_ratio_at_two as $8.0$, scaling_ratio_at_three as $27.00000000000001$, cylinder_axis_polar_matches_closed_form as $1.205506213504$, and monte_carlo_agreement at four standard errors.

Part 6

Step 6.1: Draw the sphere and the cylinder on one screen

Reason. Part (b) asks for one figure of both surfaces. Each surface needs its own parameters: the sphere is $\left(a\sin\varphi\cos\psi,\ a\sin\varphi\sin\psi,\ a\cos\varphi\right)$ with $\varphi \in [0, \pi]$ and $\psi \in [0, 2\pi]$, and the cylinder is $\left(a\cos^{2}\theta,\ a\cos\theta\sin\theta,\ t\,a|\sin\theta|\right)$ with $\theta \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$ and $t \in [-1, 1]$. The third coordinate of the cylinder stops at the heights that step 1.3 gives, so the drawn sheet is the wall of the solid and not a cylinder with flat caps. The two surfaces meet where both descriptions hold: the curve $\left(a\cos^{2}\theta,\ a\cos\theta\sin\theta,\ \pm a|\sin\theta|\right)$.

Computation. Take $a = 1$. The worst residual of the drawn sphere over its sample points is $2.22\times10^{-16}$, and the worst residual of the drawn wall against $r = a\cos\theta$ is $1.11\times10^{-16}$. The drawn wall stays inside the sphere to $5.00\times10^{-16}$. The drawn cut lies on the sphere to $2.22\times10^{-16}$ and on the cylinder to $1.11\times10^{-16}$, and its height runs from $0$ to $1.0$. The label of the sphere sits at the distance $0.999953$ from the origin. The label of the cylinder sits at the radius $0.408487440884$, which is $a\cos(1.15)$, and at a height below the bound $0.912763940261$.

Common mistake. A reader may draw the cylinder as a full tube with flat top and bottom, but the solid stops at the heights $\pm\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}$: code/part6_verify.py prints the excess of the drawn wall over the sphere bound as $5.00\times10^{-16}$, so a flat cap would lie outside the sphere.
