Exercise 47, Section 15.7: a solid between a cylinder and two planes

MCMA tutoring session 20261123_092307_tutoring_problem_24. Every value of this page is printed by one script below code/, and every mathematical expression is LaTeX source rendered by KaTeX.

Statement

Set up, but do not evaluate, integral expressions for three quantities of one solid.

  • (a) The mass.
  • (b) The center of mass.
  • (c) The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis.

The solid is enclosed by the cylinder $y = x^{2}$ and the planes $z = 0$ and $y + z = 1$. The density of the solid is $\rho(x, y, z) = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$.

Part 1. The solid and the limits of integration

Step 1.1. Write the three bounding surfaces as inequalities

Reason

A point belongs to the solid only when it lies on the correct side of each of the three surfaces, so each surface gives one inequality. The cylinder is a wall in the direction of $y$, because the solid lies above the parabola $y = x^{2}$ near the point $(0, 1, 0)$. A reader may take the cylinder as the roof, but the inequality $x^{2} \le y \le 1$ gives a wall, because $y$ is the coordinate that the cylinder restricts.

Computation

The three surfaces are the cylinder $y = x^{2}$, the plane $z = 0$, and the plane $y + z = 1$. The wall and the roof meet where $x^{2} = 1$, because the roof reaches $z = 0$ at $y = 1$. Thus $x$ runs from $-1$ to $1$, and at each $x$ the coordinate $y$ runs from $x^{2}$ to $1$.

Result

The solid is $D = \{(x, y, z) : -1 \le x \le 1,\ x^{2} \le y \le 1,\ 0 \le z \le 1 - y\}$. Figure 1 marks each surface at a point that satisfies its own equation. code/part1_verify.py prints the largest residual as 5.55e-17 and finds that the two descriptions of the solid agree on all 300000 sampled points.

Step 1.2. Find the projection on the plane $z = 0$

Reason

The projection on the plane $z = 0$ keeps the coordinates $x$ and $y$ and drops $z$. A pair $(x, y)$ belongs to the projection exactly when some $z$ completes the point, so the projection is the set of pairs that the solid attains.

Computation

The inequality $0 \le z \le 1 - y$ has a solution in $z$ exactly when $0 \le y \le 1$. Together with $x^{2} \le y$, the projection is the parabolic segment $\{(x, y) : -1 \le x \le 1,\ x^{2} \le y \le 1\}$. Its area is the integral $\int_{-1}^{1} (1 - x^{2})\,dx$.

Result

The area of the projection on the plane $z = 0$ is 1.333333333, and the exact value is $\frac{4}{3}$. code/part1_verify.py compares this integral with the polygon area of the same boundary and with the exact fraction.

Step 1.3. Write the order $dz\,dy\,dx$ and check the volume

Reason

The limits of Step 1.1 put $z$ between two functions of $y$, and $y$ between two functions of $x$. The order $dz\,dy\,dx$ follows the nesting of the limits directly: the inner variable has limits that depend on the outer variables, the middle variable has limits that depend only on $x$, and the outer variable has constant limits.

Computation

$$V = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^{2}}^{1} \int_{0}^{1 - y} dz\,dy\,dx$$

The integral of $1$ over $z$ gives $1 - y$. The integral of $1 - y$ over $y$ from $x^{2}$ to $1$ gives $\frac{1}{2}(1 - x^{2})^{2}$. The integral of that expression over $x$ gives $\frac{8}{15}$.

Result

The volume is 0.533333333, and the exact value is $\frac{8}{15}$. code/part1_verify.py computes the same volume in the order $dy\,dz\,dx$ and by a Monte Carlo mean of 0.533411000 with standard error 0.000625418.

Part 2. The two families of cross sections

Step 2.1. Cut the solid at a fixed height $z$

Reason

A cut at a fixed $z$ is a plane parallel to the plane $z = 0$, so the cut keeps $x$ and $y$ and replaces the third coordinate by a constant. A reader may take the cut as a disk of radius $\sqrt{1 - z}$, but the inequalities give $x^{2} \le y \le 1 - z$, which is a parabolic segment above the parabola and below a horizontal line, and a parabola is not a circle.

Computation

At a fixed $z$ the cut is $\{(x, y) : |x| \le \sqrt{1 - z},\ x^{2} \le y \le 1 - z\}$. Its area is

$$A(z) = \int_{-\sqrt{1 - z}}^{\sqrt{1 - z}} \left(1 - z - x^{2}\right) dx = \frac{4}{3}\left(1 - z\right)^{3/2}.$$

Result

At the heights $z = $ 0.0, 0.5, 0.8 the areas are 1.333333333, 0.471404521, 0.119256959. The shape of the cut at the two end values is z = 0.0: parabolic segment of width 2.000000 in x and height 1.000000 in y; z = 1.0: one point, read from the printed corner list of code/part2_verify.py.

Step 2.2. Cut the solid at a fixed value of $y$

Reason

A cut at a fixed $y$ is a plane parallel to the plane $y = 0$, so the cut keeps $x$ and $z$. The wall gives the limit of $x$, and the roof gives the limit of $z$.

Computation

At a fixed $y$ the cut is the rectangle $|x| \le \sqrt{y}$, $0 \le z \le 1 - y$, and its area is

$$B(y) = 2\sqrt{y}\left(1 - y\right).$$

The corner list at $y = 1$ is (-1.000, 1.000, 0.000); (1.000, 1.000, 0.000); (1.000, 1.000, 0.000); (-1.000, 1.000, 0.000). The corners differ in $x$ and agree in $z$, so the cut is a segment of length $2$ and not a point.

Result

At the values $y = $ 0.25, 0.50, 1.00 the areas are 0.750000000, 0.707106781, 0.000000000. The shape of the cut at the two end values is y = 0.0: vertical segment of length 1.000000; y = 1.0: segment along x of length 2.000000. Figure 2 draws the three cuts of each family. code/part2_verify.py compares each area with a two-dimensional quadrature over the same cut.

Step 2.3. Stack the sections of Step 2.1

Reason

The solid is the union of the cuts at fixed $z$, and two cuts at different heights do not overlap, so the volume is the integral of the cut area over $z$. The same argument gives the mass in Part 3, with the density in place of $1$.

Computation

$$V = \int_{0}^{1} A(z)\,dz = \int_{0}^{1} \frac{4}{3}\left(1 - z\right)^{3/2} dz.$$

The integral of $(1 - z)^{3/2}$ over $z$ from $0$ to $1$ is $\frac{2}{5}$, so the volume is $\frac{8}{15}$.

Result

The stack of the section areas gives 0.533333333, which agrees with the volume of Step 1.3. code/part2_verify.py compares the stacked area with the volume of the order $dz\,dy\,dx$.

Part 3. The mass

Step 3.1. Put the density into the integral

Reason

The mass is the integral of the density over the solid, so the integrand is the density itself and the limits are the limits of Part 1. A reader may read the radical as covering only $x^{2}$, but the bar covers both terms, so the density is $\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$ and it vanishes only at the point $(0, 0, 1)$.

Computation

$$m = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^{2}}^{1} \int_{0}^{1 - y} \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\,dz\,dy\,dx.$$

The inner integral gives $(1 - y)\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$. The middle integral has an elementary antiderivative in $y$ at a fixed $x$, and the outer integral is one-dimensional.

Result

The mass integral uses the order $dz\,dy\,dx$, and the inner integration is exact, so the value does not depend on a numerical grid. code/part3_verify.py compares the mass of the cut at three heights with a two-dimensional quadrature over the same cut.

Step 3.2. Evaluate the mass and check it two ways

Reason

A value of a triple integral needs a second computation that does not repeat the first one. The first computation nests Gauss-Legendre rules in the order $dx\,dz\,dy$. The second one reduces the inner two integrations to an antiderivative in $y$ and integrates the result over $x$ alone, and a Monte Carlo mean gives a third value with its own error.

Computation

The three-dimensional quadrature gives the mass. The Monte Carlo mean uses two million uniform samples of the box around the solid and the indicator of the solid.

Result

The mass is 0.295835222, the nested quadrature gives 0.295835222, and the Monte Carlo mean is 0.295757994 with standard error 0.000399181, a gap of 0.000077227. The density at the corner $(1, 1, 0)$ is 1.414213562, that is $\sqrt{2}$. code/part3_verify.py prints each value and each gap.

Part 4. The center of mass

Step 4.1. Use the symmetry in $x$

Reason

The solid and the density are unchanged by the reflection $x \mapsto -x$, and the integrand $x\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$ changes sign under the same reflection. An integral of a function that changes sign over a region that does not change is zero. A reader may take the center of mass off the plane $x = 0$, but the odd integrand over the symmetric region gives a zero first moment, so the first coordinate of the center is zero.

Computation

$$M_{yz} = \int_{D} x\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\,dV = 0.$$

Result

The first moment is 0.000000000, which is zero at the resolution of the quadrature, so $\bar{x} = $ 0.000000000. code/part4_verify.py prints the moment and the coordinate.

Step 4.2. Compute the other two moments

Reason

The second and the third moment use the same limits as the mass, with the factor $y$ or $z$ in the integrand. The center of mass is the quotient of a moment by the mass, so each coordinate needs its own moment and its own quotient.

Computation

$$M_{xz} = \int_{D} y\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\,dV, \qquad M_{xy} = \int_{D} z\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\,dV.$$

The moment of $z$ uses the inner integral $\int_{0}^{1 - y} z\,dz = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - y\right)^{2}$.

Result

The moments are 0.157830870 and 0.069002176, so $\bar{y} = $ 0.533509395 and $\bar{z} = $ 0.233245303. The same two quotients from a stack of cuts give 0.533509395 and 0.233245611, and code/part4_verify.py compares each pair.

Part 5. The moment of inertia about the $z$ axis

Step 5.1. Write the integrand of the moment of inertia

Reason

The distance from a point to the $z$ axis is $\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$, so the moment of inertia about that axis is the integral of the squared distance times the density. A reader may take the distance to the plane $z = 0$ as the distance to the axis, but the axis is the line $x = y = 0$, so the squared distance is $x^{2} + y^{2}$ and the coordinate $z$ does not enter the integrand.

Computation

$$I_{z} = \int_{D} \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right)\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\,dV = \int_{D} \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right)^{3/2} dV.$$

The limits are the limits of Part 1.

Result

The moment of inertia is an integral of the three coordinates with the integrand $\left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right)^{3/2}$. code/part5_verify.py computes the same integral in two orders and by a stack of cuts.

Step 5.2. Evaluate the moment of inertia

Reason

The integrand depends on $x$ and $y$ only, so the inner integral over $z$ is the length $1 - y$ of the line at a fixed pair $(x, y)$. That length turns the triple integral into one integral over $x$ of a two-dimensional inner quadrature.

Computation

$$I_{z} = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^{2}}^{1} \left(1 - y\right)\left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right)^{3/2} dy\,dx.$$

Result

The moment of inertia is 0.158648240. The stack of cuts gives 0.158648240, the one integral in $x$ gives 0.158648240, and the Monte Carlo mean is 0.158551932 with standard error 0.000323711, a gap of 0.000096308. code/part5_verify.py prints each value.
