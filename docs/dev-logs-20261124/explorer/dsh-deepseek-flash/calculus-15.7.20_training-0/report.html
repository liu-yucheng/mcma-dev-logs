1. The problem

Use a triple integral to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the paraboloids $y=x^{2}+z^{2}$ and $y=8-x^{2}-z^{2}$.

2. Plan of the solution

The solid is thin in the vertical direction and round in the horizontal plane. The plan follows that shape. First find where the two surfaces meet, then find the region that the solid covers in the horizontal plane. Next choose the order of the integrations and use polar coordinates. Last evaluate the integral and check the answer with a second method.

3. Part 1 — Geometry of the solid

Step 1.1

Find the height where the two surfaces meet

Reason

The solid ends where the two surfaces meet. At a meeting point the two surfaces have the same height. So set the right side of the lower surface equal to the right side of the upper surface. This one equation gives both the height of the meeting curve and the radius of the projection.

Computation

Equal heights at a meeting point give

$$x^{2}+z^{2}=8-x^{2}-z^{2}.$$

Move the two squared terms to the left side and divide by $2$:

$$2\left(x^{2}+z^{2}\right)=8 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x^{2}+z^{2}=4.$$

Substitute this value into the lower surface:

$$y=x^{2}+z^{2}=4.$$

Result of the step

The two surfaces meet on the circle $x^{2}+z^{2}=4$ in the plane $y=4$. The radius of that circle is $2$, and its height is $4$. The highest point of the solid is $y=8$ above the origin, and the lowest point is $y=0$.

Check. code/part1_verify.py finds the meeting radius as the root of the thickness $8-2r^{2}$ and prints meeting radius: computed = 2.0, expected = 2.0, difference = 0.000e+00. It also prints lower surface at radius 2: computed = 4.0 and upper surface at radius 2: computed = 4.0, and it confirms that eight sampled points of the meeting curve satisfy both surface equations.

Step 1.2

Find the region that the solid covers in the horizontal plane

Reason

A triple integral needs the region of the two horizontal variables. Look straight down the vertical axis. The solid covers a point $(x,z)$ only when the upper surface is above the lower surface at that point. The difference of the two heights is the vertical thickness, so a positive thickness means that the solid covers the point.

Computation

Write $s=x^{2}+z^{2}$ for the squared radius. The vertical thickness is

$$T(s)=\left(8-s\right)-s=8-2s .$$

The thickness is positive exactly when

$$8-2s\gt 0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad s\lt 4 .$$

The thickness is zero on the circle $s=4$ and negative outside that circle. So the solid covers exactly the disk

$$D=\left\{(x,z)\;:\;x^{2}+z^{2}\le 4\right\}$$

of radius $2$, and the area of $D$ is $\pi\cdot 2^{2}=4\pi$. The largest thickness is $T(0)=8$ at the center.

Result of the part

Final answer of part 1. The two surfaces meet at the circle $x^{2}+z^{2}=4$, $y=4$. The projection of the solid is the closed disk of radius $2$, whose area is $4\pi$. Every vertical line through the inside of the disk cuts the solid in one segment, and every vertical line through the outside of the disk misses the solid.

Check. code/part1_verify.py samples the thickness inside and outside the rim and prints smallest thickness on 0 <= radius <= 1.999: computed = 0.007998 and largest thickness on 2.001 <= radius <= 3: computed = -0.008002. The first value is positive and the second value is negative, so the projection is the disk and nothing else. The same script prints area of the projection disk: computed = 12.566370614359172, expected = 12.566370614359172 for $4\pi$.

4. Part 2 — Setup of the triple integral

Step 2.1

Choose the order of the three integrations

Reason

A triple integral adds the volume of small boxes $dx\,dy\,dz$ over the solid, so the order of the integrations must follow the shape of the solid. For a fixed horizontal point, the solid starts on the lower surface and ends on the upper surface. The inner integration therefore runs in the vertical direction first, and the two horizontal integrations stay for the disk.

Computation

The statement gives the two surfaces as functions of $x$ and $z$:

$$y_{\text{lower}}=x^{2}+z^{2}, \qquad y_{\text{upper}}=8-x^{2}-z^{2}.$$

For a fixed point $(x,z)$ of the disk $D$, the height $y$ runs from $y_{\text{lower}}$ to $y_{\text{upper}}$. So

$$V=\iint_{D}\left[\int_{x^{2}+z^{2}}^{\,8-x^{2}-z^{2}} dy\right] dA =\iint_{D}\left(8-2x^{2}-2z^{2}\right) dA .$$

Result of the step

The inner integral removes the variable $y$ and leaves the thickness $8-2x^{2}-2z^{2}$ as the integrand of a double integral over the disk $D$.

Check. code/part2_verify.py integrates the constant $1$ over the segment $\left[r^{2},\,8-r^{2}\right]$ for five radii. It prints inner integral over y at radius 0.5: computed = 7.500000000000, expected = 7.500000000000, which is the thickness $8-2(0.25)$. The largest gap over the five radii is $4.4\times10^{-16}$, and the script asserts that this gap is below $10^{-8}$.

Step 2.2

Use polar coordinates in the horizontal plane

Reason

The disk $D$ has a circular rim, and the integrand $8-2x^{2}-2z^{2}$ contains only the squared radius. Polar coordinates remove the square root from the rim and turn the integrand into a polynomial. The area element of polar coordinates needs the Jacobian factor $r$, and that factor is the weight of one ring.

Computation

Set

$$x=r\cos\theta, \qquad z=r\sin\theta, \qquad x^{2}+z^{2}=r^{2},$$

with $0\le r\le 2$ and $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$. The area element is

$$dA=r\,dr\,d\theta .$$

The thickness becomes $8-2r^{2}$, so the volume is

$$V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}\int_{r^{2}}^{\,8-r^{2}} r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta =\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2} r\left(8-2r^{2}\right) dr\,d\theta .$$

Result of the part

Final answer of part 2. The volume is $V=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}\int_{r^{2}}^{\,8-r^{2}} r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta$. Both outer integrations have constant limits, and no limit and no integrand depends on the angle, so the volume is the product of the angle integral and the radius integral.

Check. code/part2_verify.py computes the volume three times: in Cartesian order $dy\,dz\,dx$, in the reversed horizontal order $dy\,dx\,dz$, and in polar order $r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta$. It prints volume from the Cartesian double integral: computed = 50.265482456768, expected = 50.265482457437 and volume from the polar double integral: computed = 50.265482457437, expected = 50.265482457437. The same script checks the Jacobian alone: the polar integral of $r$ gives $12.566370614359$, which is the area $4\pi$ of the disk.

5. Part 3 — Evaluation of the integral

Step 3.1

Integrate over the radius

Reason

The radius integral has a polynomial integrand, so one antiderivative gives its exact value. Use the power rule on each term separately. The result is the volume of one thin wedge of angle $1$ radian, so it must be smaller than the volume of the whole solid.

Computation

$$I_r=\int_{0}^{2}\left(8r-2r^{3}\right) dr .$$

An antiderivative of $8r$ is $4r^{2}$, and an antiderivative of $-2r^{3}$ is $-\tfrac{1}{2}r^{4}$. So

$$I_r=\left[4r^{2}-\frac{r^{4}}{2}\right]_{0}^{2} =\left(4\cdot 4-\frac{16}{2}\right)-0=16-8=8 .$$

Result of the step

The radius integral has the value $8$. This value is the area under the curve $r\left(8-2r^{2}\right)$ on $0\le r\le 2$, and it is the volume of the part of the solid between the angles $0$ and $1$ radian.

Check. code/part3_verify.py prints the antiderivative at both limits, antiderivative at radius 2: computed = 8.000000000000, expected = 8.000000000000 and antiderivative at radius 0: computed = 0.000000000000, expected = 0.000000000000. It also integrates the integrand with scipy.integrate.quad and prints radius integral over 0 to 2: computed = 8.000000000000, expected = 8.000000000000. The script compares the increment of the antiderivative with the integral of the integrand on a small interval at four radii; the largest gap is $6.4\times10^{-16}$.

Step 3.2

Integrate over the angle and state the volume

Reason

The angle integral has the constant integrand $1$ and the limits $0$ and $2\pi$. The two outer integrals multiply, because the radius integral does not depend on the angle. The product is the exact volume.

Computation

$$I_\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi} d\theta=2\pi .$$

Multiply the two outer integrals:

$$V=I_\theta\cdot I_r=2\pi\cdot 8=16\pi .$$

The numerical value is $V=16\pi\approx 50.2654824574$.

Result of the part

Final answer of part 3. $V=16\pi\approx 50.27$ cubic units. The statement gives no unit of length, so the answer uses cubic units of the given coordinates.

Check. code/part3_verify.py evaluates the same triple integral with scipy.integrate.tplquad in cylindrical coordinates and prints volume from the cylindrical triple integral: computed = 50.265482457437, expected = 50.265482457437. It also prints the product of the outer integrals as $50.265482457437$ and asserts an absolute difference below $10^{-9}$ between that product and $16\pi$. The numerical triple integral has the looser bound $10^{-8}$, because a quadrature routine adds its own rounding error.

Answer of the exercise

$$V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}\int_{r^{2}}^{\,8-r^{2}} r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta=16\pi\approx 50.27$$

The solid between $y=x^{2}+z^{2}$ and $y=8-x^{2}-z^{2}$ has the volume $16\pi$ cubic units.

6. Part 4 — Independent checks

Step 4.1

Check the volume with horizontal slices

Reason

An answer is credible only when a second method gives the same value. Slice the solid by horizontal planes instead of vertical lines. Each slice is an annulus, because the solid is a region between two surfaces of the height $y$. The sum of the slice volumes is a single integral in $y$, so a different formula must give the same number.

Computation

At the height $y$ the upper surface has the squared radius $8-y$, and the lower surface has the squared radius $y$. The slice area is

$$A(y)=\pi\left(8-y\right)-\pi y=\pi\left(8-2y\right).$$

The height runs from $0$ to $4$, so

$$V=\int_{0}^{4}\pi\left(8-2y\right) dy =\pi\left[8y-y^{2}\right]_{0}^{4} =\pi\left(32-16\right)=16\pi .$$

Result of the step

The washer method gives $16\pi$, which agrees with the triple integral. The largest slice area is $A(0)=8\pi$ at the bottom of the solid, and the smallest slice area is $A(4)=0$ at the meeting circle.

Check. code/part4_verify.py prints volume from the washer method: computed = 50.265482457437, expected = 50.265482457437, largest cross section area: computed = 25.132741228718 for $8\pi$, and smallest cross section area: computed = 0.000000000000 for the area at the meeting circle. The script also asserts that no slice area is negative.

Step 4.2

Check the volume with random points and with two bounds

Reason

A random estimate gives an independent numerical value with its own error bar, and the error bar must contain the exact value. Two simple solids also give bounds: one solid fits inside the given solid, and the second solid contains the given solid. The exact value must lie between the two bounds.

Computation

The box $\left[-2,2\right]\times\left[0,8\right]\times\left[-2,2\right]$ contains the solid and has the volume $128$. Draw $400000$ points at random in the box and count the points with $x^{2}+z^{2}\le y\le 8-x^{2}-z^{2}$. The measured fraction is $p=0.393427$, so the estimate is $128\cdot p=50.358720$, with the standard error

$$128\sqrt{\frac{p\left(1-p\right)}{400000}}=0.098868 .$$

The difference from $16\pi$ is $0.093238$, which is $0.94$ standard errors.

The inner solid is a double cone with base radius $2$ and height $4$:

$$2\cdot\frac{1}{3}\pi\cdot 2^{2}\cdot 4=\frac{32\pi}{3}\approx 33.51 .$$

The outer solid is the cylinder of radius $2$ and height $8$, with the volume $32\pi\approx 100.53$. The cone fits inside the solid because $\sqrt{y}\ge y/2$ on $0\le y\le 4$, so the paraboloid is at least as wide as the cone at every height.

Result of the part

Final answer of part 4. Both checks agree with $V=16\pi$: the random estimate lies inside its error bar, and $16\pi$ lies between $\frac{32\pi}{3}$ and $32\pi$. The volume is exactly one half of the bounding cylinder, which is plausible because the two paraboloids are mirror images about the plane $y=4$.

Check. code/part4_verify.py uses the seed $20261122$ and prints random check: volume = 50.358720, expected = 50.265482, difference = 0.093238 together with random check: standard error = 0.098868, allowed difference = 4.0 * standard error = 0.395470. The script asserts three conditions: the standard error is below $0.3$, the absolute difference is below four standard errors, and the inside fraction stays between $0$ and $1$. It also prints volume of the inscribed double cone: computed = 33.510321638291, volume of the bounding cylinder: computed = 100.530964914873, and the bound check 33.510322 < 50.265482 < 100.530965.

8. The solution in short

  1. Set $x^{2}+z^{2}=8-x^{2}-z^{2}$, which gives the meeting circle $x^{2}+z^{2}=4$ at the height $y=4$.
  2. Read the projection: the disk $D$ of radius $2$, because the thickness $8-2\left(x^{2}+z^{2}\right)$ is positive only inside that disk.
  3. Integrate first in $y$: the inner integral is the thickness $8-2r^{2}$ after the substitution to polar coordinates.
  4. Include the Jacobian factor $r$: $V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}r\left(8-2r^{2}\right)dr\,d\theta$.
  5. Evaluate: $\int_{0}^{2}\left(8r-2r^{3}\right)dr=8$ and $\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta=2\pi$, so $V=16\pi$.
  6. Check with washers: $\int_{0}^{4}\pi\left(8-2y\right)dy=16\pi$. Check with random points and with the cone and cylinder bounds.

9. Common errors to avoid

  • Forgetting the Jacobian factor $r$. Without it the integral gives $\frac{64\pi}{3}\approx 67.02$, which is larger than the correct volume, because every ring is then counted as thin. code/part3_verify.py prints volume when the Jacobian factor is missing: computed = 67.020643276582, expected = 67.020643276582.
  • Using the radius $4$ instead of $2$. The equation $x^{2}+z^{2}=4$ gives the radius $2$; the number $4$ is the squared radius.
  • Integrating $y$ from $0$ to $8$. The solid does not fill the whole vertical segment above every point; the segment starts on the lower surface.
  • Swapping the radius and the height of the meeting circle. The circle has the radius $2$ at the height $y=4$, so the outer limit of the radius integral is $2$ and the height range is $0\le y\le 8$.
