Sketch the solid of an iterated integral

Calculus: Early Transcendentals, Section 15.7, Exercise 28. A verified tutorial: every number on this page comes from a script that prints the computed value and the expected value.

The problem

Sketch the solid whose volume is given by the iterated integral.
$$\int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\,2-y}\int_{0}^{\,4-y^{2}} dx\,dz\,dy$$

The integral holds three pairs of limits, the order $dx\,dz\,dy$, and the integrand $1$. These data are the only data of the problem. The problem asks for one product: a sketch of the solid. It asks for no number and no evaluation of the integral.

Part 1. Read the limits as a region of space

The first part turns the three pairs of limits into a set of points. The set is the solid that the sketch must show.

Part 1, Step 1: Read the three limits and the order of the differentials

The reason

The problem gives three limits and one integrand. The limits are the only description of the solid, because the problem asks for a sketch and gives no other information. So the first task is to read the limits without a change, and to record which limit holds which variable.

The computation

The integral of the exercise is

$$\int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{2-y}\int_{0}^{4-y^{2}} 1\,dx\,dz\,dy .$$

The differentials at the right end are $dx\,dz\,dy$. So the order of the integration is $x$ first, then $z$, then $y$. The rule of the iterated integral gives one limit pair for each differential:

  • the inner pair belongs to $x$: $x$ runs from $0$ to $4-y^{2}$;
  • the middle pair belongs to $z$: $z$ runs from $0$ to $2-y$;
  • the outer pair belongs to $y$: $y$ runs from $0$ to $2$.

The inner pair may use only the variables of the outer pairs. Here the pair of $x$ holds $y$ and does not hold $z$. The pair of $z$ holds $y$ alone. So each pair uses only a variable that is already fixed when that pair is read. The nesting is correct, and the order needs no change.

A point $(x,y,z)$ belongs to the solid exactly when all three pairs hold:

$$0 \le y \le 2, \qquad 0 \le z \le 2-y, \qquad 0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}.$$

The result

The solid is the set

$$R = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le y \le 2,\ 0 \le z \le 2-y,\ 0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}\}.$$

The order $x$, then $z$, then $y$ is correct for these limits. The variable $x$ is free of $z$, and the variable $z$ is free of $x$. Only $y$ controls both of them.

The check

code/part1_verify.py, section "region". The script compares the three pairs with two other descriptions of the same set on 40000 seeded points: the flat form of all inequalities, and the form that solves each inequality for one variable. It reports 0 disagreements with both forms. It also compares the pairs with the common misreading $0 \le x \le 4-z^{2}$, and it reports disagreements there, so the comparison can fail.

Part 1, Step 2: Find the extent of the solid and the faces of the box that it touches

The reason

A sketch needs a frame. The frame is the box that holds the solid. To find the box, we need the largest and the smallest value of each of the three variables on the solid. The limits give these values without a computation of an area or a volume.

The computation

The outer pair gives $0 \le y \le 2$ at once, so the solid stays between the planes $y = 0$ and $y = 2$.

For a fixed $y$, the pair of $z$ gives $0 \le z \le 2-y$. Because $y \ge 0$, the largest value of $2-y$ is $2$, at $y = 0$. So $0 \le z \le 2$ on the whole solid.

For a fixed $y$, the pair of $x$ gives $0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}$. Because $y^{2} \ge 0$, the largest value of $4-y^{2}$ is $4$, at $y = 0$. So $0 \le x \le 4$ on the whole solid.

The solid therefore lies in the box $[0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]$. Each of the six faces of this box meets the solid in a non-empty set:

Face of the boxSet that the solid shares with the face
$y = 0$the whole rectangle $0 \le x \le 4$, $0 \le z \le 2$
$y = 2$the single point $(0,2,0)$
$z = 0$the region $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}$
$z = 2$the segment from $(0,0,2)$ to $(4,0,2)$
$x = 0$the triangle $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 2-y$
$x = 4$the segment from $(4,0,0)$ to $(4,0,2)$

So the box is the smallest box with faces parallel to the coordinate planes that holds the solid. Every one of its faces touches the solid, and no smaller box does.

The result

The extent of the solid is $4$ in the direction of $x$, $2$ in the direction of $y$, and $2$ in the direction of $z$. The smallest box is $[0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]$.

The check

code/part1_verify.py, section "extent". The script computes the extent two ways: from the limits by the argument above, and from the smallest and largest coordinates of the sampled points that satisfy the limits. It reports the same triple $(4, 2, 2)$ from both ways.

code/part1_verify.py, section "volume". The script evaluates the integral in three different orders of integration. The three values agree to the last digit, with a largest gap of $0$. The script then evaluates the same integral a fourth time with exact rational arithmetic: it multiplies the two sides $4-y^{2}$ and $2-y$ as polynomials with rational coefficients, and it evaluates the antiderivative at the two ends of the interval. The exact value is $20/3$, and each of the three numeric values agrees with it to $10^{-9}$. A numeric method and an exact method are independent, so this comparison can fail. The problem asks for no number. This section is a check of the reading of the limits, and it is not the answer to the exercise.

Final answer of Part 1

The solid is $R = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le y \le 2,\ 0 \le z \le 2-y,\ 0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}\}$. It lies in the box $[0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]$, and it touches all six faces of that box.

Part 2. Cut the solid at a fixed height

The second part reads the shape of one horizontal cut. The cut is the object that the reader of a sketch must recognize first.

Part 2, Step 1: Cut the solid at a fixed height, and name the shape of the cut

The reason

A three dimensional solid is hard to read from its limits directly. A cut at one fixed height turns the solid into a flat region. A flat region has a shape, and a shape has a name, a boundary, and an area. The shape is the first thing that a reader must see in the sketch.

The computation

Fix $y = c$ with $0 \le c \le 2$. The three pairs of Part 1, Step 1 become two conditions on $x$ and $z$, because the condition on $y$ is now true:

$$0 \le z \le 2-c, \qquad 0 \le x \le 4-c^{2}.$$

The condition on $z$ holds the number $c$ and does not hold $x$. The condition on $x$ holds the number $c$ and does not hold $z$. So the value of $x$ is free inside its interval, whatever the value of $z$ may be. The cut is therefore the set of all pairs $(x,z)$ with $x$ in one interval and $z$ in another interval. That set is the product of the two intervals:

$$\text{cut}(c) = [0,\,4-c^{2}] \times [0,\,2-c].$$

A product of two closed intervals is a rectangle. Its four sides lie on the lines $x = 0$, $x = 4-c^{2}$, $z = 0$, and $z = 2-c$. Its four corners are

$$(0,c,0), \quad (0,c,2-c), \quad (4-c^{2},c,0), \quad (4-c^{2},c,2-c).$$

The cut is a rectangle and it is not a disk. The reason is the structure of the limits: no limit ties $x$ to $z$. A disk would need a limit of the form $x \le \sqrt{\rho^{2}-z^{2}}$, and no such limit appears here.

The result

For every $c$ with $0 \le c < 2$, the cut of the solid at the height $y = c$ is the rectangle $[0,4-c^{2}] \times [0,2-c]$ in the plane $y = c$. Its sides are $4-c^{2}$ and $2-c$.

The check

code/part2_verify.py, section "shape". For each of five heights, the script tests the sampled points of the cut against the rectangle and against the disk of the same corner. It reports $0$ disagreements with the rectangle at every height, and it reports more than 1000 disagreements with the disk at every height. So the test separates the two shapes, and the rectangle survives.

Part 2, Step 2: Compute the area of the cut at five heights, and read the top of the solid

The reason

The area of the cut is a function of the height. This function tells the reader how the solid narrows as it rises. The same function later checks the drawing, because the drawn cuts must have these areas.

The computation

The cut is a rectangle with sides $4-y^{2}$ and $2-y$. The area of a rectangle is the product of its two sides, so

$$A(y) = (4-y^{2})(2-y), \qquad 0 \le y \le 2 .$$

Then:

$y$$4-y^{2}$$2-y$$A(y) = (4-y^{2})(2-y)$
$0$$4$$2$$8$
$0.5$$3.75$$1.5$$5.625$
$1$$3$$1$$3$
$1.5$$1.75$$0.5$$0.875$

Each value is smaller than the value below it. So each cut sits inside the cut below it, and the solid narrows at every step upward.

At $y = 2$ both sides are $0$. The rectangle becomes the single point $(0,2,0)$, which is the corner $T$ of Part 3. The point is the whole cut, and its area is $0$. The printed corner list of the check gives one corner at that height and four corners at every lower height, so the collapse is a collapse to a point and not to a segment.

The result

The area of the cut is $A(y) = (4-y^{2})(2-y)$. Its values at the heights $0$, $0.5$, $1$, $1.5$, and $2$ are $8$, $5.625$, $3$, $0.875$, and $0$. The highest cut is the single point $T = (0,2,0)$.

The check

code/part2_verify.py, section "area". For each height, the script measures the area of the cut in two ways. The first way multiplies the two sides of the rectangle. The second way draws a large random sample in the box $[0,4] \times [0,2]$, keeps the points that satisfy the two limits, and counts them. The relative gap of the two ways is at most $4.71\times10^{-4}$, and the script asserts a gap below $5\times10^{-3}$.

code/part2_verify.py, section "misreading". The script also computes the area of the cut that the misreading $0 \le x \le 4-z^{2}$ would give. At $y = 0$ that area is $5.333\ldots$ and the correct area is $8$. At $y = 1$ that area is $3.666\ldots$ and the correct area is $3$. The two readings differ, so the checks of this part can tell them apart.

Final answer of Part 2

At the height $y$ the cut is the rectangle $[0, 4-y^{2}] \times [0, 2-y]$ with area $A(y) = (4-y^{2})(2-y)$. The areas at $y = 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2$ are $8$, $5.625$, $3$, $0.875$, and $0$. The cut at $y = 2$ is the single point $T = (0,2,0)$.

Part 3. Name the patches, the corners, and the edges

The third part gives the boundary of the solid a name for each piece, so that the drawing and the reader can point at the same piece.

Part 3, Step 1: Turn each equality of the limits into one patch of the boundary

The reason

A closed solid is bounded by surfaces. The sketch must show these surfaces, and it must name each one by an equation. The equations are already present in the limits: every limit that is an inequality gives one surface when it becomes an equality.

The computation

The solid is $R = \{0 \le y \le 2,\ 0 \le z \le 2-y,\ 0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}\}$. The five inequalities that change with the point give five surfaces:

PatchEqualitySurfaceInside the other limits
$S_1$$y = 0$the plane $y = 0$$0 \le x \le 4$, $0 \le z \le 2$
$S_2$$x = 0$the plane $x = 0$$0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 2-y$
$S_3$$z = 0$the plane $z = 0$$0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}$
$S_4$$y + z = 2$the plane $y + z = 2$$0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le x \le 4-y^{2}$
$S_5$$x = 4 - y^{2}$the parabolic cylinder $x = 4-y^{2}$$0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 2-y$

The limit $y \le 2$ is an inequality too, but its equality $y = 2$ gives no patch. At $y = 2$ the other two limits force $z = 0$ and $x = 0$, so the set at that value is the single point $(0,2,0)$. A single point has no area, and it cannot be a patch of the boundary. Part 2, Step 2 already showed this collapse, and the corner list of the check confirms it.

The area of each patch is a separate computation:

  • $S_1$ is the rectangle $4 \times 2$, so its area is $8$.
  • $S_2$ is the triangle with legs $2$ and $2$, so its area is $2$.
  • $S_3$ is the region under the parabola, so its area is $\int_{0}^{2}(4-y^{2})\,dy = 16/3 = 5.333\ldots$
  • $S_4$ is the region of $S_3$ on a plane of slope $\sqrt{2}$, so its area is $(16/3)\sqrt{2} = 7.542\ldots$
  • $S_5$ is the region under the parabola on a surface of slope $\sqrt{1+4y^{2}}$, so its area is

$\int_{0}^{2}(2-y)\sqrt{1+4y^{2}}\,dy = 3.5358\ldots$

The result

The boundary of the solid is the union of five patches. Their equations are $y = 0$, $x = 0$, $z = 0$, $y + z = 2$, and $x = 4-y^{2}$. Their areas are $8$, $2$, $16/3$, $(16/3)\sqrt{2}$, and $3.5358\ldots$. The limit $y = 2$ gives the single point $T = (0,2,0)$ and no patch.

The check

code/part3_verify.py, section "surfaces". For each patch, the script takes 600 points that lie on the equation of the patch, and it makes three tests. The residual of the equation is $0$ at every point. Every point satisfies the other four inequalities. A step of $10^{-5}$ along the outward normal leaves the solid, and a step of the same size inward stays inside the solid. The last two tests fail for a point that lies on the wrong surface, so they can fail.

Part 3, Step 2: Find the corners and the edges, and show that the boundary is closed

The reason

A sketch of five separate patches can still be wrong at the joins. The patches must meet along shared edges, and the edges must meet at shared corners. The corners and the edges are also the points and the lines that a reader uses to check the shape of the drawing.

The computation

A corner is a point where three of the five equations hold at the same time. Take each triple in turn and keep the point inside the solid:

The check

The residual of each of the other three is at least $1.414$, so the edge does not lie on a third patch. The two end points match the named corners exactly. The arc length and the chord length of the same 401 points agree within $10^{-4}$.

Final answer of Part 3

The boundary is the union of five patches: $y = 0$, $x = 0$, $z = 0$, $y + z = 2$, and $x = 4-y^{2}$. It has five corners $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $T$ and eight edges. Every edge lies on two patches, so the boundary is closed.

Part 4. Draw the solid and check the drawing

The fourth part draws the solid under fixed rules, and then measures the drawing against the limits.

Part 4, Step 1: Decide the rules of the drawing before drawing

The reason

The exercise asks for a sketch. A sketch is not a free picture: it must show the shape that the limits describe, and a reader must be able to read the equations from it. So the drawing needs rules, and each rule must come from Part 1 to Part 3 and not from taste.

The computation

The rules follow from the facts that the earlier parts established.

  1. Frame. The box is $[0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]$, from Part 1, Step 2. The axes carry one

arrow and one letter each, and no second mark for the same axis.

  1. Patches. The drawing shows the five patches of Part 3, Step 1, and no other surface. Each

patch is a graph over a parameter rectangle, so the drawing samples each patch on a grid.

  1. Opacity. A patch that stands in front of the body must stay above one half in opacity, so

the body reads as a closed body. The near face may go below one half, so that the interior stays visible through it.

  1. Labels. The label of a patch hangs from a point that lies on that patch. A label that floats

near a surface but does not touch it gives a false position.

  1. Corners. The five corners of Part 3, Step 2 carry their letters, and the letters are the

letters of the corner table.

  1. Cut. One horizontal cut is drawn, because the cut is the reason for the shape of the

boundary. The cut is the rectangle of Part 2, Step 1.

These rules are the same for the reader and for the check. The check of Part 4, Step 2 measures the drawn mesh, so a rule that the drawing ignores also fails the check.

The result

The drawing obeys six rules: the box and one arrow per axis, the five patches, an opacity above one half for the four far patches, one label per patch at a point of that patch, the five corner letters, and one drawn cut. The scripts code/part4_figure1.py and code/part4_figure2.py build the drawing from these rules.

The check

code/part4_verify.py, section "labels". For every label point, the script computes the residual of its own equation, the smallest residual over the other four equations, and the test of membership in the solid. The residual of its own equation is $0$, the smallest other residual is at least $0.354$, and the point is inside the solid. So each label sits on exactly one patch and inside the solid. The label of the patch $y = 0$ is not drawn in the assembled figure, because that patch is the far face in the chosen point of view and its label would fall on a nearer patch.

Part 4, Step 2: Check the drawing against the limits, and state the answer

The reason

A drawing can look right and be wrong. The drawing is a set of triangles, so every claim about it can be measured: a volume, an area, a cut, a projection. Each measurement is compared with a value that the limits give. The answer of the exercise is the drawing that survives these comparisons.

The computation

The drawing is a mesh of 15840 triangles, built from the five patches on a grid of 40 subdivisions in each parameter direction. A triangle of zero area is dropped, because the collapse at $y = 2$ produces such triangles.

The mesh is closed. Each edge of the mesh belongs to two triangles. The count of edges that belong to one triangle is $0$.

The volume agrees. The divergence theorem on the closed mesh gives the volume $6.665833$. The integral of the cut area from Part 2, Step 2 gives $6.666667$. The iterated integral of the problem gives $6.666667$. The relative gap of the mesh value and the cut value is $1.25\times10^{-4}$.

The areas agree. The drawn area of each patch and the integral of Part 3, Step 1 give the same value within $1.56\times10^{-4}$ in relative terms.

The cuts agree. The drawn mesh is cut at the three heights $0.51$, $1.01$, and $1.51$. Each height lies between two rows of the mesh, so each crossing point comes from an interpolation and not from a point that the parameter map placed there. The measured areas are $5.571855$, $2.949705$, and $0.842555$. The limits give $5.572451$, $2.950101$, and $0.842751$. The largest relative gap is $2.33\times10^{-4}$.

The projection agrees. The projection of the solid on the plane $x = 0$ is the rectangle $[0,4] \times [0,2]$. A sample of 200000 points of that rectangle all reach the solid, so the fraction is $1$. The area of the rectangle outside the disk $x^{2} + z^{2} \le 4$ is $4.841080$, so the projection is not a disk.

The volume $20/3$ appears in this part only as a check. The exercise asks for a sketch of the solid. It asks for no number and no evaluation of the integral. The answer is the drawing.

The result

The answer to the exercise is the sketch of the solid: the body in the box $[0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]$, closed by the five patches $y = 0$, $x = 0$, $z = 0$, $y + z = 2$, and $x = 4-y^{2}$, with the five corners $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $T$, and a cut that is a rectangle at every height below $y = 2$. Every measurement of the drawing agrees with the limits.

The check

code/part4_verify.py, sections "volume", "areas", "cuts", "projection", and "labels". Each section computes its value two ways and asserts the gap. The script prints the computed value and the expected value of every row, and it stops with an assertion error when a gap is too large.

Final answer of Part 4 and of the exercise

The answer to the exercise is the sketch of the solid: the body inside the box $[0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]$, closed by the five patches $y = 0$, $x = 0$, $z = 0$, $y + z = 2$, and $x = 4-y^{2}$, with the corners $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $T$ and a rectangular cut at every height below $y = 2$. The problem asks for this sketch and for no number.

What the checks can and cannot tell

Part 4 measures a volume of the drawn mesh and compares it with the volume of the cuts. This comparison does not detect a misreading of the limits, because the misreading $0 \le x \le 4 - z^{2}$ gives the same volume in this problem. The comparison that detects the misreading is the comparison of the shapes: the cut is a rectangle and not a disk, and the projection is the rectangle of area $8$ and not the disk of area $2\pi$. Part 2 and Part 4 give that comparison, and both of them can fail.
