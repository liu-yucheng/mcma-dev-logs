Sketch the solid whose volume is given by an iterated integral

Calculus, Early Transcendentals, Section 15.7, Exercise 27. A tutorial with one checked computation for every numerical claim.

Statement. Sketch the solid whose volume is given by the iterated integral.

$$\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1-x}\int_{0}^{2-2z} dy\,dz\,dx$$

What the exercise asks for. One sketch of one solid region. It asks for no number and for no value of the integral.

The integrand is $1$, so the integral equals the volume of the region. That fact is a check of the drawing, and it is not the answer of the exercise. The tutorial keeps the two apart.

How to read this page. Part 1 reads the three pairs of limits and turns them into inequalities. Part 2 cuts the solid in three directions. Part 3 derives the corner points and the faces. Part 4 draws the solid and checks the drawing. Every step names the script that checks its numbers, and the last section of the page holds the printed output of all four scripts.

Part 1 — Read the three limits

The three pairs of limits are the only data of the exercise. This part reads them in the order that the differentials give, and it turns the reading into six inequalities.

Step 1.1 — The innermost limit fixes the variable y

Why. The integral is written with the differentials $dy\,dz\,dx$. The innermost integral therefore runs in $y$. Its lower limit is $0$ and its upper limit is $2-2z$. The upper limit uses the variable $z$ alone, and it uses no $x$. So the height in the direction $y$ depends on $z$ alone.

Step. Hold the pair $(x,z)$ fixed. The innermost integral covers the segment

$$\int_0^{2-2z} dy = 2-2z .$$

This number is the length of the segment in $y$ above the point $(x,z)$. It is the height of the solid at that point.

Result. Above a base point $(x,z)$ the solid spans $0 \le y \le 2-2z$. The height is $2-2z$, and it is the same for every $x$. Three values follow at once: the height is $2$ at $z=0$, it is $1$ at $z=1/2$, and it is $0$ at $z=1$.

Check. code/part1_verify.py evaluates the upper limit at two points with the same $z$ but different $x$, and it obtains the same height for both points. It also evaluates the height at $z=0$, $z=1/2$, and $z=1$, and it compares each value with the value that the limit function returns.

Step 1.2 — The middle limit fixes the variable z

Why. Step 1.1 uses the differential $dy$. The next differential in the order is $dz$, so the middle integral runs in $z$. Its lower limit is $0$ and its upper limit is $1-x$.

Step. The middle limit uses $x$. For a fixed value of $x$ the variable $z$ covers the segment

$$0 \le z \le 1-x .$$

The segment has the length $1-x$. The variable $y$ is no longer free at this point: step 1.1 ties $y$ to $z$.

Result. For a fixed $x$ the pair $(z,y)$ fills the region $0 \le z \le 1-x$ and $0 \le y \le 2-2z$. The variable $y$ does not use $x$, but it uses $z$, and $z$ uses $x$. The dependence on $x$ therefore reaches $y$ through $z$.

Check. code/part1_verify.py compares two tests of membership for $240$ random points. The first test uses the three limit statements. The second test solves a small linear program over the six half-spaces. The two tests agree on every point, and the script prints the count.

Step 1.3 — The outer limit fixes the variable x, and the limits give six inequalities

Why. The last differential is $dx$. The outer integral runs in $x$ from $0$ to $1$, and this limit uses no other variable. The three limits now describe the solid completely.

Step. Write the three statements of the limits as one set:

$$R = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le x \le 1,\ 0 \le z \le 1-x,\ 0 \le y \le 2-2z\} .$$

Each statement with a limit that uses a variable becomes one inequality with two variables. The set $R$ is therefore the common part of six half-spaces:

$$x \ge 0,\quad x \le 1,\quad z \ge 0,\quad x+z \le 1,\quad y \ge 0,\quad y+2z \le 2 .$$

Result. The solid $R$ is the set of points in the first octant that satisfy $x \le 1$, $x+z \le 1$, and $y+2z \le 2$. It fits in the box $0 \le x \le 1$, $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$. The section at $y=0$ is the triangle with the corners $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$ in the $xz$-plane, and its area is $1/2$.

Check. code/part1_verify.py builds the box from the corner points of $R$ and compares it with the box that the limits give. It also computes the area of the section at $y=0$ in two ways: by clipping the plane with the six half-spaces, and by the exact formula for the triangle.

Answer of Part 1. the solid is $R = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le x \le 1,\ 0 \le z \le 1-x,\ 0 \le y \le 2-2z\}$, which is the part of the box $0 \le x \le 1$, $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$ that also holds $x+z \le 1$ and $y+2z \le 2$.

Part 2 — The three families of cross sections

A cross section shows the shape of the solid in one plane. This part cuts the solid in three directions, and it derives every section from the inequalities of Part 1.

Step 2.1 — A section at constant z is a rectangle

Why. A cross section shows the shape of the solid in one plane. Take the plane $z = z_0$ with $0 \le z_0 \le 1$. Put the value into the six inequalities of Step 1.3 and keep only those that still hold.

Step. The six inequalities become

$$0 \le x \le 1,\quad x \le 1-z_0,\quad 0 \le y \le 2-2z_0 .$$

The two bounds on $x$ combine into $0 \le x \le 1-z_0$, because $z_0 \ge 0$ makes $1-z_0$ no larger than $1$. The bound on $y$ uses no $x$. The section is therefore the product of two segments, so it is a rectangle with the width $1-z_0$ and the height $2-2z_0$. Its area is

$$A(z) = (1-z)(2-2z) = 2(1-z)^2 .$$

Result. The section at constant $z$ is a rectangle with the area $A(z) = 2(1-z)^2$. Four values are $A(0) = 2$, $A(1/4) = 9/8$, $A(1/2) = 1/2$, and $A(3/4) = 1/8$. The area falls as $z$ grows, and at $z=1$ the section is the single point $P_2 = (0,0,1)$.

Check. code/part2_verify.py clips the plane $z=z_0$ with the six half-spaces at four values of $z_0$. It compares the area of the clipped polygon with the closed form $2(1-z)^2$ by exact fraction arithmetic. It also compares the width and the height of the rectangle with $1-z$ and $2-2z$, and it takes the shape name from the number of corners and the number of parallel pairs.

Step 2.2 — A section at constant x is a trapezoid

Why. Take the plane $x = x_0$ with $0 \le x_0 \le 1$. Put the value into the six inequalities of Step 1.3.

Step. The six inequalities become

$$0 \le z \le 1-x_0,\quad 0 \le y \le 2-2z .$$

Over the segment $0 \le z \le 1-x_0$ the upper bound on $y$ is a straight line that falls from $2$ at $z=0$ to $2x_0$ at $z=1-x_0$. The region under a straight line over a segment is a trapezoid. Its two parallel sides are the vertical sides at $z=0$ and at $z=1-x_0$, with the lengths $2$ and $2x_0$, and the distance between them is $1-x_0$. Its area is

$$A(x) = \frac{1}{2}\,(1-x)(2+2x) = 1-x^2 .$$

Result. The section at constant $x$ is a trapezoid with the area $A(x) = 1-x^2$. Four values are $A(0) = 1$, $A(1/4) = 15/16$, $A(1/2) = 3/4$, and $A(3/4) = 7/16$. At $x=0$ one parallel side has the length $0$, so the section is the base triangle. At $x=1$ the distance between the parallel sides is $0$, so the section is the single segment from $(1,0,0)$ to $(1,2,0)$.

Check. code/part2_verify.py clips the plane $x=x_0$ with the six half-spaces at the same four values. It compares the area of the clipped polygon with $1-x^2$ by exact fraction arithmetic, compares the two parallel sides with $2$ and $2x$, and compares the distance between them with $1-x$.

Step 2.3 — A section at constant y is a trapezoid, and the three families agree

Why. Take the plane $y = y_0$ with $0 \le y_0 < 2$. Put the value into the six inequalities of Step 1.3. This family completes the picture, because it shows how the base triangle shrinks as $y$ grows.

Step. The six inequalities become

$$0 \le x,\quad x+z \le 1,\quad 0 \le z \le 1-\frac{y_0}{2} .$$

For a fixed $z$ the variable $x$ runs from $0$ to $1-z$. The section is therefore the base triangle with the top corner cut off at the level $z = 1-y_0/2$. It is a trapezoid. Its lower parallel side lies at $z=0$ and runs from $x=0$ to $x=1$, so its length is $1$. Its upper parallel side lies at $z=1-y_0/2$ and runs from $x=0$ to $x=y_0/2$, so its length is $y_0/2$. The distance between the two sides is $1-y_0/2$. Its area is

$$A(y) = \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{y}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{y}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}-\frac{y^2}{8} .$$

Result. The section at constant $y$ is a trapezoid with the area $A(y) = 1/2-y^2/8$. Four values are $A(0) = 1/2$, $A(1/2) = 15/32$, $A(1) = 3/8$, and $A(3/2) = 7/32$. At $y=0$ the section is the base triangle $D$. At $y=2$ the section is the single point $P_2 = (0,0,1)$.

Check. code/part2_verify.py clips the plane $y=y_0$ at four values, compares each area with $1/2-y^2/8$, and then integrates each of the three families over its own variable. All three give the same total, and the exact fraction arithmetic gives the value $2/3$. The script also sums $240$ midpoint slices for the families in $z$ and in $x$ and compares each sum with the exact total. Figure 1 draws one section from each family.

Answer of Part 2. $A(z) = 2(1-z)^2$ at constant $z$, $A(x) = 1-x^2$ at constant $x$, and $A(y) = 1/2-y^2/8$ at constant $y$. All three families fall as their variable grows.

Part 3 — The corner points and the faces

The corner points and the faces follow from the inequalities, not from a picture. This part produces both, so that the drawing of Part 4 rests on checked data.

Step 3.1 — The five corner points of the solid

Why. A drawing of a solid needs its corner points. A corner point is the common point of three boundary planes that also satisfies all six inequalities. So the corner points follow from the inequalities of Step 1.3, and not from a picture.

Step. The six boundary planes are $x=0$, $x=1$, $z=0$, $x+z=1$, $y=0$, and $y+2z=2$. Try every triple of planes, solve the three equations, and keep the point when it satisfies all six inequalities. Five points remain:

$$P_0=(0,0,0),\quad P_1=(1,0,0),\quad P_2=(0,0,1),\quad P_3=(0,2,0),\quad P_4=(1,2,0).$$

Result. The solid has five corner points. The plane $x=1$ meets the solid only along the edge from $P_1$ to $P_4$, so it cuts no face. Five planes cut a face, and the sixth plane touches the solid along one edge.

Check. code/part3_verify.py tries all $20$ triples of the six planes, keeps the points that satisfy the six inequalities, and compares the result with the five points above. It also counts the corner points on the plane $x=1$ and prints the count $2$.

Step 3.2 — The five faces and their areas

Why. Each inequality that cuts the solid owns one face. The face of an inequality is the set of points of the solid that satisfy that inequality with equality. The corner points of a face are the corner points of the solid that lie on the plane of the face.

Step. Take the five cutting planes in turn and collect the corner points on each plane.

Answer of Part 3. five corner points $P_0$ to $P_4$ and five faces: the base $D$, the top face $y+2z=2$, and the three walls $x=0$, $z=0$, and $x+z=1$.

Part 4 — The sketch and its check

This part draws the solid and then checks the drawing against the integral in three independent ways.

Step 4.1 — Draw the base, then raise each point by the height

Why. The drawing follows the reading of the limits in the same order. The outer two limits give the base in the plane $y=0$. The innermost limit gives the height above each base point. Draw the base first, and draw the height second.

Step. Draw the axes with $y$ pointing up. Mark the base triangle $D$ in the plane $y=0$ with the corners $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$ in the $xz$-plane. Then, above a base point $(x,z)$, draw a vertical segment from $y=0$ up to $y=2-2z$. The upper ends of these segments sweep the top face.

Result. The base is a right triangle with two sides of length $1$ along the axes, and the height falls from $2$ on the edge $z=0$ to $0$ at the point $P_2=(0,0,1)$. Above the point $(1/4, 2/5)$ the height is $2-2\cdot 2/5 = 6/5$. Figure 2 shows the base and one level line, and Figure 3 carries the height bar at that point.

Check. code/part3_verify.py tests $200$ points of the height segment at $(1/4, 2/5)$ against the six inequalities, and every point passes. It compares the length of the segment with $2-2z = 6/5$, and it compares the drawn base triangle with the section $y=0$ of the solid.

Step 4.2 — Close the body with the three walls and the top face

Why. The base and the heights alone give an open surface. The three walls close the sides, and the top face closes the body. Each wall is one inequality with equality, so the walls come from Step 3.2 and not from the picture.

Step. Draw in this order:

  1. the base triangle $D$ in the plane $y=0$, with the corners $P_0$, $P_1$, and $P_2$;
  2. the wall $z=0$, the rectangle $P_4 P_1 P_0 P_3$, which stands on the edge $P_0P_1$;
  3. the wall $x=0$, the triangle $P_3 P_0 P_2$, which stands on the edge $P_0P_2$;
  4. the wall $x+z=1$, the triangle $P_4 P_2 P_1$, which stands on the edge $P_1P_2$;
  5. the top face $y+2z=2$, the triangle $P_4 P_3 P_2$, which closes the body.

Result. The closed body is the sketch of the solid: a triangle base, three vertical walls, and one slanted top face. The wall $z=0$ is the tallest wall, because the height $2-2z$ is largest there. The body has five corner points and five faces.

Check. code/part4_verify.py prints the face table with the inequality, the face name, the corner points, and the area of every face. It also checks that the midpoint of every pair of corner points lies inside the solid, so the body that the five faces close is convex.

Step 4.3 — The answer is the sketch, and a check confirms the sketch

Why. The exercise asks for a sketch of the solid whose volume the integral gives. The answer is therefore the drawing with the data that fixes it. The volume is not the answer. The volume is a check of the drawing, because the integrand is $1$.

Step. Compare the drawing with the integral in three independent ways.

  • The divergence theorem over the five faces gives the volume of the drawn body.
  • The three families of cross sections of Step 2 each integrate to the same total.
  • A rejection sample of the drawn body estimates the same total inside three standard errors.

Result. The drawn body is the solid $R$ of Step 1.3, and every one of the three checks returns $2/3$ within its own tolerance. The final answer of the exercise is the description and the sketch below.

The solid is $R = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le x \le 1,\ 0 \le z \le 1-x,\ 0 \le y \le 2-2z\}$. It sits in the box $0 \le x \le 1$, $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$. Its base is the triangle $D$ with the corners $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$ in the plane $y=0$, and its height above a base point $(x,z)$ is $2-2z$. Its surface has five faces: the base $D$, the top face $y+2z=2$, and the three walls $x=0$, $z=0$, and $x+z=1$. The sketch is Figure 3, with the base in Figure 2.

Check. code/part4_verify.py computes the volume three ways and prints the three values. It also prints the volume of the drawn body from the divergence theorem and the estimate from the rejection sample with its standard error.

Answer of Part 4. the sketch in Figure 3. The volume $2/3$ is a check of that sketch, and it is not the requested answer.
