A triple integral over a tetrahedron in six orders

Calculus, early transcendentals, section 15.7, exercise 32.

Problem. Express the integral $\iiint_E f(x, y, z)\,dV$ as an iterated integral in six different ways, where $E$ is the solid bounded by the given surfaces: $x = 2$, $y = 2$, $z = 0$, and $x + y - 2z = 2$.

Part 1. The solid, its volume, and its three projections

Step 1.1 The four surfaces and the four vertices

Reason. The statement names four surfaces and no other surface, so every point of the solid $E$ obeys the four conditions $x \le 2$, $y \le 2$, $z \ge 0$, and $x + y - 2z \ge 2$. A vertex of a solid that four planes bound is the meeting point of three of them, so the candidate vertices are the solutions of the four triples of planes.

Computation. Each triple of the four planes has one meeting point:

Triple of planesMeeting point
$x = 2$, $y = 2$, $z = 0$$(2, 2, 0)$
$x = 2$, $y = 2$, $x + y - 2z = 2$$(2, 2, 1)$
$x = 2$, $z = 0$, $x + y - 2z = 2$$(2, 0, 0)$
$y = 2$, $z = 0$, $x + y - 2z = 2$$(0, 2, 0)$

The four points are distinct, and their list is $(2, 2, 0)$, $(2, 2, 1)$, $(2, 0, 0)$, and $(0, 2, 0)$. The count of the vertices is 4.

Result. The solid $E$ has 4 vertices: $(2, 2, 0)$, $(2, 2, 1)$, $(2, 0, 0)$, and $(0, 2, 0)$. The point $(1.5, 1.5, 0.25)$ lies inside $E$.

A common mistake. A reader may take the plane $x = 0$ or the plane $y = 0$ as a fifth face of $E$, but the four half-spaces give a single point on each of those planes, and the check in the plane x = 0 the largest z equals 0 prints 0.

Check. code/part1_verify.py solves the four triples and tests each point against the four half-spaces; it prints the vertex list and the interior point among the 24 checks of the part, and 24 of them pass.

Step 1.2 The coordinate inequalities and the closure of the solid

Reason. An order of integration needs one variable between two functions of the other two. The four conditions of Step 1.1 give the height $z$ between two functions of $x$ and $y$, and they give the range of $x$ and of $y$ on their own. A lower bound of an integral must come from a surface that belongs to the solid, so the bound $z \ge 0$ comes from the face $z = 0$ and the bound $x + y \ge 2$ comes from the face $x + y - 2z = 2$.

Computation. Divide the fourth condition by two and move the terms:

$$x + y - 2z \ge 2 \iff z \le \frac{x + y - 2}{2}.$$

The condition $z \ge 0$ together with this bound forces $x + y \ge 2$, because a height that is at least zero and at most $(x + y - 2)/2$ exists only when $(x + y - 2)/2 \ge 0$. The two conditions $x \le 2$ and $y \le 2$ need no partner from below, since the condition $x + y \ge 2$ with $x \le 2$ and $y \le 2$ already gives $x \ge 0$ and $y \ge 0$. Hence

$$E = \{(x, y, z) : 0 \le x \le 2,\ 0 \le y \le 2,\ 0 \le z \le \tfrac{1}{2}(x + y - 2)\}.$$

The lower bounds $x \ge 0$ and $y \ge 0$ are consequences and not faces: the plane $x = 0$ meets $E$ only at $(0, 2, 0)$, and the plane $y = 0$ meets $E$ only at $(2, 0, 0)$.

Result. The solid is the set of the points with $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le y \le 2$, and $0 \le z \le (x + y - 2)/2$. The four named planes are the four faces, and no fifth surface is needed.

A common mistake. A reader may take the box $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$ as the solid, but the slanted plane cuts that box along the diagonal, and the check base times height without the factor one third differs from the volume of E prints 2 against the volume $\frac{2}{3}$.

Check. code/part1_verify.py takes random points and boundary points, tests them against the four half-spaces, tests them against the inequalities above, and reports the number of disagreeing points; it also solves four linear programs for the extreme values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ and prints the largest height 1.

Step 1.3 The solid, its volume, and the areas of its faces

Reason. Three independent methods must agree on the volume before any order of integration can be trusted: the determinant of three edge vectors, the pyramid formula, and an iterated integral. The volume is also the value of every one of the six integrals of $f = 1$, so the volume is the common target of the whole exercise.

Computation. The edge vectors at the vertex $(2, 2, 0)$ are $(0, 0, 1)$, $(0, -2, 0)$, and $(-2, 0, 0)$. The determinant of the three edge vectors decides the volume. Its absolute value is $$\left| \det \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \right| = 4.$$

Hence the volume is one sixth of that absolute value, that is $4/6 = \frac{2}{3}$. The face $z = 0$ is the triangle with the corners $(2, 0)$, $(0, 2)$, and $(2, 2)$, whose area is 2; the height above that face is 1, and one third of the product of the two numbers is $\frac{2}{3}$.

The iterated integral of Step 1.2 with $f = 1$ is

$$\int_{0}^{2} \int_{2-y}^{2} \int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} 1 \, dz \, dx \, dy = 0.666666666667.$$

The areas of the four faces are 2 on the face $z = 0$, 1 on the face $x = 2$, 1 on the face $y = 2$, and 2.44948974278 on the face $x + y - 2z = 2$.

Figure 1 draws the solid with its four faces and its four vertices.

Result. The volume of $E$ is $\frac{2}{3}$; the checker prints 0.6666666666666666 by the determinant, by the pyramid formula, and by the iterated integral, and 0.668544 by a random sample of 4000000 points with the error 0.000746194498784. Figure 1 shows the solid.

A common mistake. A reader may take the mirror image of the slanted plane, that is the half space $x + y - 2z \le 2$, but the solid lies above that plane, and the check wrong side of the slanted plane: its volume differs from the volume of E prints 3.33333333333 against $\frac{2}{3}$.

Check. code/part1_verify.py prints the three volume values, the four face areas, and the volume of the mirror image; 24 of its 24 checks pass.

Step 1.4 The three projections

Reason. An order of integration is a description of the solid by three nested intervals, and the two outer intervals are the projection of the solid on a coordinate plane. A point lies in the projection on the plane $z = 0$ when some point of the solid has that pair $(x, y)$, so the projection is the set of the pairs that the inequalities of Step 1.2 admit for some $z$.

Computation. The pair $(x, y)$ is admissible when $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le y \le 2$, and $(x + y - 2)/2 \ge 0$, that is $x + y \ge 2$. So the projection on the plane $z = 0$ is the triangle with the corners $(2, 0)$, $(0, 2)$, and $(2, 2)$, and its area is 2.0.

The pair $(x, z)$ is admissible when $0 \le x \le 2$ and some $y$ in the interval $0 \le y \le 2$ obeys $y \ge 2 - x + 2z$. Such a $y$ exists exactly when $2 - x + 2z \le 2$, that is $z \le x/2$. So the projection on the plane $y = 0$ keeps the coordinates $x$ and $z$, and it is the triangle with the corners $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$, and $(2, 1)$, whose area is 1.0.

The pair $(y, z)$ is admissible when $0 \le y \le 2$ and $z \le y/2$, because the same argument with the roles of $x$ and $y$ exchanged applies. So the projection on the plane $x = 0$ keeps the coordinates $y$ and $z$, and it is the triangle with the corners $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$, and $(2, 1)$, whose area is 1.0.

Figure 2 draws the three projections.

Result. The projection on the plane $z = 0$ is the triangle of the corners (2, 0), (0, 2), (2, 2) with area 2.0. The projection on the plane $y = 0$ is the triangle of the corners (0, 0), (2, 0), (2, 1) with area 1.0. The projection on the plane $x = 0$ is the triangle of the corners (0, 0), (2, 0), (2, 1) with area 1.0. Figure 2 shows all three projections.

A common mistake. A reader may take the projection on the plane $y = 0$ for the rectangle $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$, but the slanted plane gives the upper bound $z \le x/2$, and the corner list of the check holds only three corners.

Check. code/part1_verify.py compares each projection area from its corner list with the fraction of random points that the projection admits; it prints 2.0, 1.0, and 1.0.

Part 2. The two orders with $z$ innermost

Step 2.1 The inner interval of $z$ over the projection $D_{xy}$

Reason. The order $dz\,dx\,dy$ fixes $x$ and $y$ first and then integrates along a vertical line. The two ends of that line are the two surfaces that the line meets, and the length of the line must be the difference of the two bounding surfaces of Step 1.2.

Computation. For a fixed pair $(x, y)$ of the projection $D_{xy}$, the line parallel to the $z$-axis enters the solid on the face $z = 0$ and leaves it on the face $x + y - 2z = 2$. The face equation gives $z = (x + y - 2)/2$, so the inner interval is

$$0 \le z \le \frac{x + y - 2}{2}.$$

The length of the interval is $(x + y - 2)/2$, and its integral over the projection $D_{xy}$ must be the volume. The length is negative outside $D_{xy}$, so the projection is the set where the length is not negative.

Result. The inner interval of the order $dz\,dx\,dy$ is $0 \le z \le (x + y - 2)/2$. The area of $D_{xy}$ is 2, and the integral of the length of the vertical segments over $D_{xy}$ is 0.666666666667, which is the volume of Step 1.3.

A common mistake. A reader may take the whole square $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le y \le 2$ as the projection, but the limits give the triangle $x + y \ge 2$, and the check full square [0, 2] x [0, 2] as the projection: its signed integral differs from the volume prints 0 against 0.666666666667.

Check. code/part2_verify.py integrates the length of the strips over the projection and compares the result with the area of the corner list; it prints 2 for both numbers.

Step 2.2 The order $dz\,dx\,dy$

Reason. The outer variable is $y$, so the outer interval is the range of $y$ over the projection $D_{xy}$. The middle variable is $x$, so the middle interval is the range of $x$ along one horizontal slice of $D_{xy}$ at a fixed $y$. The inner interval is the one of Step 2.1.

Computation. The projection $D_{xy}$ holds the pairs with $0 \le y \le 2$ and $2 - y \le x \le 2$, so the outer interval is $0 \le y \le 2$ and the middle interval is $2 - y \le x \le 2$. The inner interval is $0 \le z \le (x + y - 2)/2$. Hence

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-y}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy.$$

The value of this integral with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667, which is the volume of Step 1.3.

Figure 3 shows the projection with the three horizontal strips of this order.

Result. One of the six orders is $$\int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-y}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy$$ and its value with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667. Figure 3 shows its strips.

A common mistake. A reader may take the order as a split of the solid into two pieces, but the limits give one piece, because a single interval of $y$, a single interval of $x$, and a single interval of $z$ describe the whole solid.

Check. code/part2_verify.py compares the limits of this order with the four half-spaces of Step 1.2 on 300000 random points and on the boundary points; it prints the number of agreeing points. It also computes the value by a symbolic integration and by a nested Gauss rule with 12 points, and prints 0.666666666667 twice.

Step 2.3 The order $dz\,dy\,dx$

Reason. The exchange of $x$ and $y$ leaves the four half-spaces of Step 1.1 unchanged, because the conditions $x \le 2$ and $y \le 2$ exchange and the condition $x + y - 2z \ge 2$ is symmetric in $x$ and $y$. A symmetry of the solid exchanges the orders of integration that fix the same third variable, so the second order with $z$ innermost is the first order with $x$ and $y$ exchanged.

Computation. The outer interval is the range of $x$ over $D_{xy}$, that is $0 \le x \le 2$. The middle interval is the range of $y$ along one vertical slice of $D_{xy}$ at a fixed $x$, that is $2 - x \le y \le 2$. The inner interval is the one of Step 2.1. Hence

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-x}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx.$$

The value of this integral with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667. The right panel of Figure 3 draws the vertical strips of this order.

Result. A second order is $$\int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-x}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx$$ and its value with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667, equal to the value of Step 2.2 and to the volume of Step 1.3. Figure 3 shows its strips.

A common mistake. A reader may take the middle bound as $0 \le y \le 2$ inside the outer interval, but the limits give $2 - x \le y \le 2$, because a point with $y < 2 - x$ and a fixed $x$ leaves the projection $D_{xy}$.

Check. code/part2_verify.py reports the number of agreeing points of this order against the half-spaces, and it prints the value 0.666666666667 by a symbolic integration and again by a nested Gauss rule.

Part 3. The two orders with $y$ innermost

Step 3.1 The inner interval of $y$ over the projection $D_{xz}$

Reason. The order $dy\,dx\,dz$ fixes $x$ and $z$ first and then integrates along a line parallel to the $y$-axis. The two ends of that line are the two faces that the line meets, so the interval follows from the face equations of Step 1.1 and not from the drawing.

Computation. For a fixed pair $(x, z)$ of the projection on the plane $y = 0$, the line parallel to the $y$-axis enters the solid on the slanted face $x + y - 2z = 2$ and leaves it on the face $y = 2$. The slanted face gives $y = 2 - x + 2z$, so the inner interval is

$$2 - x + 2z \le y \le 2.$$

The length of the interval is $x - 2z$, which is not negative exactly when $z \le x/2$, that is exactly on the projection of Step 1.4.

Result. The inner interval of the order $dy\,dx\,dz$ is $2 - x + 2z \le y \le 2$, and the projection $D_{xz}$ has the area 1.

A common mistake. A reader may take the inner interval as $0 \le y \le 2$, but the limits give $2 - x + 2z \le y \le 2$, because the lower end is the slanted face and not the plane $y = 0$; the plane $y = 0$ meets the solid only at the point $(2, 0, 0)$.

Check. code/part3_verify.py integrates the length of the strips over the projection and compares the result with the area of the corner list; it prints 1 for both numbers and 9 passing checks out of 9.

Step 3.2 The order $dy\,dx\,dz$

Reason. The outer variable is $z$, so the outer interval is the range of $z$ over the projection on the plane $y = 0$. The middle variable is $x$, so the middle interval is the range of $x$ along one horizontal slice of that projection at a fixed $z$. The inner interval is the one of Step 3.1.

Computation. The projection $D_{xz}$ holds the pairs with $0 \le z \le 1$ and $2z \le x \le 2$. With the inner interval of Step 3.1,

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz.$$

The value of this integral with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667, which is the volume of Step 1.3.

Figure 4 shows the projection with the three horizontal strips of this order.

Result. A third order is $$\int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz$$ and its value with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667. Figure 4 shows its strips.

A common mistake. A reader may take the outer bound as $0 \le z \le 2$ because the picture of the solid reaches the height of the vertex $B$, but the limits give $0 \le z \le 1$, and the check outer bound z from 0 to 2 instead of 0 to 1: its signed integral differs from the volume prints 0 against 0.666666666667.

Check. code/part3_verify.py compares the limits with the four half-spaces on 300000 random points and on the boundary points, and it prints 0.666666666667 by a symbolic integration and again by a nested Gauss rule with 12 points.

Step 3.3 The order $dy\,dz\,dx$

Reason. The exchange of the roles of the middle and the outer variable must follow the shape of the projection and not the shape of the solid, because the inner interval of $y$ depends on $x$ and on $z$ and not on the order. On the projection $D_{xz}$ a vertical line at a fixed $x$ runs from $z = 0$ to $z = x/2$, so the middle interval is that height range.

Computation. The outer interval is the range of $x$ over $D_{xz}$, that is $0 \le x \le 2$. The middle interval is $0 \le z \le x/2$. With the inner interval of Step 3.1,

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx.$$

The value of this integral with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667. The right panel of Figure 4 draws the vertical strips of this order.

Result. A fourth order is $$\int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx$$ and its value with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667, equal to the volume of Step 1.3. Figure 4 shows its strips.

A common mistake. A reader may take the upper end of the height interval as the constant $1$ inside the outer interval, but the limits give $z \le x/2$, because the height of the solid above a fixed $x$ grows with $x$ and reaches $1$ only at $x = 2$.

Check. code/part3_verify.py reports the number of agreeing points of this order against the half-spaces and prints 0.666666666667 by both the symbolic integration and the nested Gauss rule.

Part 4. The two orders with $x$ innermost

Step 4.1 The inner interval of $x$ over the projection $D_{yz}$

Reason. The order $dx\,dy\,dz$ fixes $y$ and $z$ first and then integrates along a line parallel to the $x$-axis. As in Part 3, the two ends of the line are the two faces that the line meets, and these faces are read from the equations of Step 1.1.

Computation. For a fixed pair $(y, z)$ of the projection on the plane $x = 0$, the line parallel to the $x$-axis enters the solid on the slanted face $x + y - 2z = 2$ and leaves it on the face $x = 2$. The slanted face gives $x = 2 - y + 2z$, so the inner interval is

$$2 - y + 2z \le x \le 2.$$

The length of the interval is $y - 2z$, which is not negative exactly when $z \le y/2$, that is exactly on the projection of Step 1.4.

Result. The inner interval of the order $dx\,dy\,dz$ is $2 - y + 2z \le x \le 2$, and the projection $D_{yz}$ has the area 1.

A common mistake. A reader may take the inner interval as $0 \le x \le 2$, but the limits give $2 - y + 2z \le x \le 2$, because the plane $x = 0$ meets the solid only at the point $(0, 2, 0)$ and does not bound a face.

Check. code/part4_verify.py integrates the length of the strips over the projection and compares the result with the area of the corner list; it prints 1 for both numbers and 8 passing checks out of 8.

Step 4.2 The order $dx\,dy\,dz$

Reason. The outer variable is $z$, so the outer interval is the range of $z$ over the projection on the plane $x = 0$. The middle variable is $y$, so the middle interval is the range of $y$ along one horizontal slice of that projection at a fixed $z$. The inner interval is the one of Step 4.1.

Computation. The projection $D_{yz}$ holds the pairs with $0 \le z \le 1$ and $2z \le y \le 2$. With the inner interval of Step 4.1,

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz.$$

The value of this integral with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667, which is the volume of Step 1.3.

Figure 5 shows the projection with the three horizontal strips of this order.

Result. A fifth order is $$\int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz$$ and its value with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667. Figure 5 shows its strips.

A common mistake. A reader may take the middle bound as $0 \le y \le 2$ inside the outer interval, but the limits give $2z \le y \le 2$, because a point with $y < 2z$ and a fixed $z$ lies outside the projection $D_{yz}$.

Check. code/part4_verify.py compares the limits with the four half-spaces on 300000 random points and on the boundary points, and it prints 0.666666666667 by a symbolic integration and again by a nested Gauss rule with 12 points.

Step 4.3 The order $dx\,dz\,dy$

Reason. The last order fixes $y$ on the outside and $z$ in the middle. A vertical line at a fixed $y$ runs from the base $z = 0$ up to the slanted face, so the middle interval is the height range of the projection $D_{yz}$ at that $y$, and the inner interval stays the one of Step 4.1, because the inner variable $x$ is bounded by faces and not by the order.

Computation. The outer interval is the range of $y$ over $D_{yz}$, that is $0 \le y \le 2$. The middle interval is $0 \le z \le y/2$. With the inner interval of Step 4.1,

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{y}{2}}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy.$$

The value of this integral with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667. The right panel of Figure 5 draws the vertical strips of this order.

Result. A sixth order is $$\int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{y}{2}}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy$$ and its value with $f = 1$ is 0.666666666667, equal to the volume of Step 1.3. Figure 5 shows its strips.

A common mistake. A reader may take the middle interval as $0 \le z \le 1$ for every $y$, but the limits give $0 \le z \le y/2$, and the check middle bound z from 0 to 1 instead of 0 to y/2: its signed integral differs from the volume prints 0 against 0.666666666667.

Check. code/part4_verify.py reports the number of agreeing points of this order against the half-spaces and prints 0.666666666667 by both the symbolic integration and the nested Gauss rule.

Part 5. The checks and the six answers

Step 5.1 Six orders, one value

Reason. Six formulas that describe the same solid must give the same number for every function that is continuous on the solid. One polynomial is enough to test the six limits against each other, because a wrong limit changes the value of a polynomial and not only the value of the constant function.

Computation. Take $f(x, y, z) = 2 + xy - z$. The six orders give the value 2.63333333333, that is $\frac{79}{30}$, and the six symbolic integrations return one common result. The six formulas are laid out in Step 5.3.

The six formulas are also six different expressions: the count of distinct expressions is 6.

Result. All six orders agree on the polynomial 2 + xy - z, and the common value is 2.63333333333. The six formulas are 6 different expressions.

A common mistake. A reader may test the six formulas with the constant function alone, but the limits give one value for that function even when two orders differ, because a constant weight cannot see a wrong interval that moves nothing.

Check. code/part5_verify.py runs the six symbolic integrations of the polynomial, prints the count of distinct results, and prints the common value 2.63333333333.

Step 5.2 A numerical quadrature with no symbolic bound

Reason. A symbolic integration and its own limits cannot disagree, because both come from the same limits. An independent check must integrate the polynomial over the box that holds the solid and weight each point by one or by zero, so that no limit of integration of the six orders enters the computation.

Computation. The box $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$ holds the solid, and the six conditions of Step 1.2 decide whether a point of the box lies in $E$. A random sample of 4000000 points gives 2.64204586335 with the standard error 0.00299851423762. A midpoint rule on a grid of cells gives the values 2.6270825, 2.63177078125, 2.63294270508, 2.63323567688, and 2.63330891926 for 20, 40, 80, 160, and 320 cells on one axis; the last error is 2.44140752153e-05, and each refinement lowers the error by about four.

Figure 6 plots the error of the midpoint rule against the number of cells on one axis.

Result. Both quadratures agree with the symbolic value 2.63333333333: the box quadrature gives 2.64204586335 with the standard error 0.00299851423762, and the finest grid gives the error 2.44140752153e-05. Figure 6 shows the convergence.

A common mistake. A reader may integrate the polynomial over the whole box without the indicator, but the limits give a smaller solid inside the box, and the weight of the extra points changes the value of a polynomial that depends on the coordinates.

Check. code/part5_verify.py computes the box value with 4000000 random points, the grid values at the five resolutions, and the ratios of the successive errors; it prints 11 passing checks out of 11.

Step 5.3 The six answers

Reason. The exercise asks for six iterated integrals and for no number, so the answer is the six formulas together with the reason that each limit follows from a face of the solid. The six formulas come in three pairs, and each pair fixes the same innermost variable.

Computation. With $z$ innermost:

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-y}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-x}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx.$$

With $y$ innermost:

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx.$$

With $x$ innermost:

$$\iiint_E f \, dV = \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{y}{2}}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy.$$

Every formula gives the volume $\frac{2}{3}$ when $f = 1$, and every formula gives 2.63333333333 when $f = 2 + xy - z$.

Result. The six iterated integrals of the exercise are the six formulas of this step: $\int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-y}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy$, $\int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-x}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx$, $\int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz$, $\int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx$, $\int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz$, and $\int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{y}{2}}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy$, and each of them equals the triple integral of $f$ over $E$.
