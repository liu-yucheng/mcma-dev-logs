Problem 25: the mass, the center of mass, and the moment of inertia of a solid

A verified tutorial. Each numerical claim holds the name of the script that checks the claim.

Statement

If $E$ is the solid bounded by the cylinder $y^{2} + z^{2} = 9$ and the planes $x = 0$, $y = 3x$, and $z = 0$ in the first octant, with the density function $\rho(x, y, z) = x^{2} + y^{2}$, find the following quantities, correct to three decimal places.

(a) The mass. (b) The center of mass. (c) The moment of inertia about the $z$ axis.

The density has units of mass per unit volume. The three quantities are the integral of $\rho$ over $E$, the point $\left(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}\right)$ with $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{M}\int_{E} x\rho\,dV$ and similarly for the other two coordinates, and the integral of $\left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right)\rho$ over $E$.

Step 1.1 — Turn the four surfaces into three pairs of limits

Reason. A triple integral needs one pair of limits for each variable. The statement gives four surfaces: the cylinder $y^{2} + z^{2} = 9$, the plane $x = 0$, the plane $y = 3x$, and the plane $z = 0$, and it places the solid in the first octant. The cylinder speaks about $y$ and $z$ only, so it fixes the outer variables. The plane $y = 3x$ speaks about $x$ and $y$ only, so it fixes the inner variable once $y$ is known. A reader may take the solid for the whole quarter cylinder, but the plane $y = 3x$ gives the bound $x \le y / 3$, and that bound removes the part of the quarter cylinder where $x$ is larger. The first octant gives the lower bound $0$ of each variable.

Computation. The cylinder meets the plane $z = 0$ at $y = 3$, so $y$ runs from $0$ to $3$. For a fixed $y$ the cylinder gives $z$ from $0$ to $\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$. For a fixed $y$ the plane $y = 3x$ gives $x$ from $0$ to $y / 3$. The three pairs of limits are

$$ 0 \le y \le 3, \qquad 0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}, \qquad 0 \le x \le \frac{y}{3}. $$

The inner integral is a length along $x$, the middle integral is a length along $z$, and the outer integral is a length along $y$. The order is $dx\,dz\,dy$.

Verification. code/part1_verify.py integrates the constant $1$ over these limits in the order $dx\,dz\,dy$ and again in the order $dz\,dy\,dx$. The two orders agree to a relative difference of $2.96 \times 10^{-16}$, and each order agrees with a uniform sample of the box $0 \le x \le 1$, $0 \le y \le 3$, $0 \le z \le 3$. The sample route prints $2.999574$ inside the error $0.003000$, and the closed form prints $3.000000$.

Result. The volume of the solid is $3.000000$ cubic units, and the limits above are the limits of every integral in this tutorial. Figure 1 draws the four surfaces and the body that they close, and the anchor rows of code/part1_verify.py print the residual of the equation of each surface at the point that its label marks.

Step 1.2 — Read the section at a fixed $y$ and the projection on the plane $x = 0$

Reason. The middle and inner limits must describe a plane figure at a fixed $y$, because the outer integral adds these figures. A reader may take the section for a circular annulus, but the limits $0 \le x \le y / 3$ and $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$ give a rectangle, because $x$ and $z$ vary independently for a fixed $y$. The corners of that rectangle are the four points where the two bounds meet.

Computation. For a fixed $y$ the corner list is $(0, y, 0)$, $(y/3, y, 0)$, $(y/3, y, \sqrt{9 - y^{2}})$, and $(0, y, \sqrt{9 - y^{2}})$. The width is $y / 3$ and the height is $\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$, so the area is

$$ A(y) = \frac{y}{3}\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}. $$

The projection of the solid on the plane $x = 0$ drops $x$ and keeps the disc $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 9$ in the first quadrant, whose area is $9\pi/4$.

Step 2.1 — Integrate the two inner variables of the mass

Reason. The mass is the integral of the density $\rho = x^{2} + y^{2}$ over the solid. The inner integral runs along a line of constant $y$ and $z$, and the density is a quadratic in $x$ on that line, so the inner integral is a polynomial in $y$ and $z$ that we can write explicitly. A reader may take the inner integral for $x^{2} + y^{2}$ times the length $y/3$, but the limits $0 \le x \le y/3$ give $\int_{0}^{y/3} (x^{2} + y^{2}) \, dx = \frac{y^{3}}{81} + \frac{y^{3}}{3}$.

Computation. The inner integral is

$$ \int_{0}^{y/3} \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right) dx = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^{3} + y^{2}\frac{y}{3} = \frac{28 y^{3}}{81}. $$

The middle integral multiplies this value by the length of the $z$ interval, $\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$, because $28y^{3}/81$ does not depend on $z$:

$$ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9-y^{2}}} \frac{28 y^{3}}{81} \, dz = \frac{28 y^{3}}{81}\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}. $$

Step 2.2 — Integrate the outer variable and read the mass

Reason. After Step 2.1 the mass is one integral in $y$, and that integral has a Beta form because the factor $\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$ is the square root of a difference of squares. A reader may take the mass for the area of the projection times the mean density, but the projection has the area $7.068583$ and the density grows with $y$, so the limits $0 \le x \le y/3$ and $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$ give the single integral $\int_{0}^{3} \frac{28 y^{3}}{81}\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}\, dy$.

Computation. Substitute $y = 3t$, so $dy = 3\,dt$ and $\sqrt{9 - y^{2}} = 3\sqrt{1 - t^{2}}$. The integral becomes

$$ M = \frac{28}{81}\int_{0}^{3} y^{3}\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}\, dy = \frac{28}{81}\, 3^{5}\int_{0}^{1} t^{3}\sqrt{1 - t^{2}}\, dt . $$

The remaining integral is $\frac{1}{2}B(2, \frac{3}{2}) = \frac{2}{15}$, so

$$ M = \frac{28}{81}\cdot 243 \cdot \frac{2}{15} = \frac{56}{5} = 11.200000 . $$

Verification. code/part2_verify.py computes the mass by the Beta function, by nested quadrature in the order $dx\,dz\,dy$, by quadrature in the order $dz\,dy\,dx$, and by a uniform sample of the box. The four routes print $11.200000000$, $11.200000000$, $11.200000000$, and $11.193404$ with the error $0.014365$, which is $0.459$ standard errors from the quadrature.

Check of the limits against a wrong reading. code/part2_verify.py prints two wrong masses that a wrong limit set produces. If $x$ runs to $1$ instead of $y/3$, the print "mass with $x \le 1$ instead of $x \le y / 3$" gives $18.260507$, and that value exceeds the true mass. If the density is replaced by a constant over the projection, the print "mass of the double integral over the projection" gives $7.000000$. A reader may take either value for the mass, but the limits $0 \le x \le y/3$ and $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$ give $11.200000$.

Result. The mass of the solid is $M = 11.200000$, which is $56/5$ exactly, and the mean density is $M / V = 3.733333$.

Step 3.1 — Compute the numerators of $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$

Reason. The coordinate $\bar{x}$ is the integral of $x\rho$ over the solid divided by the mass, and $\bar{y}$ is the integral of $y\rho$ divided by the same mass. The numerator of $\bar{x}$ has the extra factor $x$ under the inner integral, so it is a quartic in $y$, and the numerator of $\bar{y}$ has the extra factor $y$, so it carries one more power of $y$ than the mass does. A reader may divide the numerator of $x\rho$ by the volume instead of the mass, but the mass $M = 11.200000$ is the divisor that the definition gives.

Computation. The inner integral of $x\rho$ is $\int_{0}^{y/3} x(x^{2} + y^{2})\,dx = \frac{y^{4}}{324} + \frac{y^{4}}{18} = \frac{19y^{4}}{324}$, and the inner integral of $y\rho$ is $y$ times the value of Step 2.1, $\frac{28y^{4}}{81}$. After the $z$ and $y$ integrals,

$$ \int_{E} x\rho \, dV = \frac{171\pi}{128} = 4.196971, \qquad \int_{E} y\rho \, dV = \frac{63\pi}{8} = 24.740042 . $$

Step 3.2 — Compute $\bar{z}$ with exact fractions and read the center of mass

Reason. The coordinate $\bar{z}$ is the integral of $z\rho$ over the solid divided by the mass. The density does not contain $z$, so the inner $z$ integral is $\int_{0}^{\sqrt{9-y^{2}}} z\,dz = \frac{9 - y^{2}}{2}$, and every power of $y$ that remains is even. A reader may take the mean height of the quarter disc for $\bar{z}$, but the limits $0 \le x \le y/3$ give a solid that is thinner near $y = 3$, and the value $0.937500$ follows from them.

Computation. The numerator is

$$ \int_{E} z\rho \, dV = \int_{0}^{3}\frac{28y^{3}}{81}\cdot\frac{9 - y^{2}}{2}\,dy . $$

Write $y = 3t$ again. The integral becomes $\frac{28}{81}\cdot\frac{81}{2}\cdot 243\int_{0}^{1} t^{3}(1 - t^{2})\,dt$, and $\int_{0}^{1} t^{3}(1 - t^{2})\,dt = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12}$. Hence the numerator is $\frac{21}{2}$ and

$$ \bar{z} = \frac{21/2}{56/5} = \frac{15}{16} = 0.937500 . $$

The division uses exact fractions, because the digits of the floating route near the third decimal cannot be trusted to round. code/part3_verify.py prints the exact fraction as $15/16$, and the third decimal of that value is $0.938$.

Verification. code/part3_verify.py computes the numerator of $z\rho$ from the closed form, from the order $dx\,dz\,dy$, from the order $dz\,dy\,dx$, and from exact fractions; the four routes print $10.500000$ and their largest spread is $1.78 \times 10^{-15}$. The check named "the centroid of the projection misses z bar" prints $1.273240$ for the mean height of the quarter disc and $0.937500$ for the correct value. The coordinate checks compare each closed route with a uniform sample of the solid, and the largest deviation is $0.289$ standard errors, below the factor four that the script allows.

Result. The center of mass is

$$ \left(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}\right) = \left(0.375,\ 2.209,\ 0.938\right) . $$

Step 4.1 — Compute the moment of inertia about the $z$ axis

Reason. The moment of inertia about the $z$ axis is the integral of $(x^{2} + y^{2})\rho$ over the solid, because the distance of a point from that axis is $\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}$ and the density multiplies the squared distance. The integrand is therefore $(x^{2} + y^{2})^{2}$, which is a quartic in $x$ and $y$. A reader may replace the solid by the cylinder of radius $3$, but the limits $0 \le x \le y/3$ give the smaller value $59.794286$, while the cylinder of radius $3$ gives $100.800000$.

Computation. The inner integral is

$$ \int_{0}^{y/3}\left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right)^{2} dx = \frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^{5} + \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^{3}y^{2} + \frac{y^{4}}{3}\cdot y = \frac{436\,y^{5}}{1215} . $$

The $z$ integral multiplies this value by $\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}$, and the substitution $y = 3t$ gives

$$ I_{z} = \frac{436}{1215}\cdot 3^{7}\int_{0}^{1} t^{5}\sqrt{1 - t^{2}}\,dt = \frac{436}{1215}\cdot 2187 \cdot \frac{1}{2}B\!\left(3, \frac{3}{2}\right) = 59.794286 . $$

Step 5.1 — Cross-check every quantity and read the three answers

Reason. The statement asks for three decimal places, so each answer must be safe in its third decimal. A single integration route cannot show that, because a wrong set of limits or a wrong power would pass through one route unnoticed. The lesson of Step 2.2 shows the danger: the wrong limit on $x$ gives a mass of $18.260507$, and only a second route, or a wrong-value check, shows that this value cannot be the mass. A reader may take the printed value of a script for the answer, but the answer needs a second route that uses different limits and a sample that uses no limits at all. The script code/part5_verify.py builds both.

Computation. The script reads the values that the earlier scripts wrote, recomputes every integral in the order $dz\,dy\,dx$, and draws a fresh uniform sample of the box.
