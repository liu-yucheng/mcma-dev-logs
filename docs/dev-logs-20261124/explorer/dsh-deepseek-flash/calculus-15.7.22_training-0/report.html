Volume of a Solid Bounded by a Cylinder and Two Planes

Every number on this page comes from a script in code/. Each script prints the computed value beside the expected value and stops when the two disagree.

The statement

Use a triple integral to find the volume of the given solid.

The solid enclosed by the cylinder $x^2+z^2=4$ and the planes $y=-1$ and $y+z=4$.

The three equations are the only data of the exercise. No length is given as a separate number, and the method is fixed: one triple integral.

Answer $V=20\pi\approx 62.831853071796$ cubic units

Plan of the solution

The solution has four parts. Each part ends with an answer, and the four answers agree.

  1. Part 1 reads the three surfaces and writes the solid as three inequalities. It also finds the vertical thickness of the solid.
  2. Part 2 writes one triple integral in the order $dy\,dz\,dx$ and evaluates it.
  3. Part 3 derives the area of the slice at a fixed height from the same inequalities, samples the area at three heights, and integrates the area.
  4. Part 4 evaluates the integral in polar coordinates and checks the value with two numerical methods.

Part 1: The region

Step 1.1: Read the three surfaces

ReasonWe must know the shape and the position of each surface before we write an integral. The form of each equation gives that information.

  1. $x^2+z^2=4$ contains no $y$. Thus every plane $y=c$ cuts the surface in the same circle of radius $2$. The surface is a circular cylinder, its axis is the $y$ axis, and its radius is $2$.
  2. $y=-1$ contains no $x$ and no $z$. Thus the surface is the horizontal plane through $y=-1$.
  3. $y+z=4$, that is $y=4-z$, contains no $x$. Thus the surface is a plane parallel to the $x$ axis, and it tilts in the $z$ direction.

CheckThe point $(2,0,0)$ satisfies $x^2+z^2=4$, and $(2.1,0,0)$ does not. The normal vector of $y=-1$ is $(0,1,0)$, and the normal vector of $y+z=4$ is $(0,1,1)$; the two vectors are not parallel, so the two planes are not parallel.

Step 1.2: Write the solid as three inequalities

ReasonThe word "enclosed" means the bounded region between the surfaces. A point belongs to the region when it is on the correct side of each surface, and one test point fixes each side.

The point $(0,1,0)$ satisfies the three equations with room to spare: $0\lt 4$, $1>-1$, $1+0\lt 4$. Thus $(0,1,0)$ is inside the solid.

$$x^2+z^2\le 4,\qquad y\ge -1,\qquad y+z\le 4.$$

CheckThe cylinder gives $-2\le x\le 2$ and $-2\le z\le 2$. The first plane gives $y\ge -1$, and the second plane gives $y\le 4-z\le 6$. The region is bounded in all three directions.

Verified by code/part1_verify.py: $500$ points built inside the disk with a height between $-1$ and $4-z$ all pass the three inequalities, and none of $500$ points built below $-1$ or above $4-z$ passes.

Result: $S=\{(x,y,z):x^2+z^2\le 4,\ -1\le y\le 4-z\}$.

Step 1.3: The vertical thickness of the solid

ReasonThe first integration will be in $y$. An inner integral in $y$ needs the length of the interval of $y$ above each point $(x,z)$ of the disk.

For a point $(x,z)$ with $x^2+z^2\le 4$, the two plane inequalities allow every $y$ with $-1\le y\le 4-z$. The interval is not empty exactly when $4-z\ge -1$, that is $z\le 5$. Every point of the disk has $z\le 2$, so the interval is never empty. Its length is

$$h(x,z)=(4-z)-(-1)=5-z.$$

On the disk $z$ runs from $-2$ to $2$. Thus $h$ runs from $5-2=3$ at $z=2$ to $5-(-2)=7$ at $z=-2$, and $3\le h\le 7$. The smallest thickness is positive, so no vertical line meets the solid in an empty set.

CheckThe disk has area $4\pi$, so $12\pi\le V\le 28\pi$. This bound checks the answer of Part 2; it is not the answer.

Answer of Part 1: $S=\{(x,y,z):x^2+z^2\le 4,\ -1\le y\le 4-z\}$, with thickness $h=5-z$ and $3\le h\le 7$.

Verified by code/part1_verify.py: the measured thickness at $200$ random points differs from $5-z$ by at most $0.000999$, and the accepted difference is $0.002000$.

Part 2: One triple integral

Step 2.1: Choose the order of integration

ReasonEach bound must come from the three inequalities. The variable $y$ appears alone in the two plane inequalities, and the cylinder restricts only $x$ and $z$. Thus for each $(x,z)$ of the disk the set of allowed $y$ is one interval, and its endpoints do not depend on $x$. An inner integral in $y$ needs no split of the disk.

The inequality $x^2+z^2\le 4$ needs $4-x^2\ge 0$, so $-2\le x\le 2$. For a fixed $x$ of that interval the same inequality gives $-\sqrt{4-x^2}\le z\le\sqrt{4-x^2}$.

We integrate in the order $dy\,dz\,dx$: $y$ first, then $z$, then $x$.

Result: $\displaystyle V=\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\int_{-1}^{4-z}dy\,dz\,dx$.

Step 2.2: Integrate the inner variable

ReasonThe inner integral has the constant integrand $1$, so its value is the length of the interval of $y$.

$$\int_{-1}^{4-z}dy=(4-z)-(-1)=5-z,$$ $$V=\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\sqrt{4-x^2}}(5-z)\,dz\,dx=\iint_{D}(5-z)\,dA,\qquad D=\{(x,z):x^2+z^2\le 4\}.$$

CheckThe integrand $5-z$ is exactly the thickness $h$ of Step 1.3. The triple integral became a double integral over the disk, and its integrand is the thickness. The script code/part2_verify.py compares this inner integral with $5-z$ at four values of $z$; the values agree to $10^{-12}$.

Step 2.3: Evaluate the double integral

ReasonThe integral of a difference is the difference of the integrals. The first integral has the constant integrand $5$ and needs only the area of the disk. The second integral has the integrand $z$.

$\iint_D 5\,dA=5\cdot\pi\cdot 2^2=20\pi$. The disk is symmetric about $z=0$, and the integrand $z$ changes sign under $z\mapsto -z$, so $\iint_D z\,dA=0$. Thus $V=20\pi$.

A second route integrates in $z$ first. With $a=\sqrt{4-x^2}$,

$$\int_{-a}^{a}(5-z)\,dz=\Big[5z-\frac{z^2}{2}\Big]_{-a}^{a}=10a,\qquad V=\int_{-2}^{2}10\sqrt{4-x^2}\,dx=10\cdot 2\pi=20\pi,$$

because the integral of $\sqrt{4-x^2}$ from $-2$ to $2$ is the area of the upper half of the disk.

Answer of Part 2: $V=20\pi\approx 62.831853071796$.

Verified by code/part2_verify.py: the double integral gives $62.831853071796$, the half disk area gives $6.283185307180$, and $10$ times that area gives $62.831853071796$.

Part 3: Slices at a fixed height

Step 3.1: The shape of the slice comes from the inequalities

ReasonA second computation checks the answer. We cut the solid with the plane $y=c$ and take the area $A(c)$ of the cut; then $V=\int A(c)\,dc$. The shape of the cut must come from the inequalities, and not from a figure.

A point $(x,c,z)$ belongs to the solid exactly when $x^2+z^2\le 4$ and $z\le 4-c$. Thus the slice is the part of the disk below the line $z=4-c$.

Because the disk has $-2\le z\le 2$, there are three cases. If $c\le 2$, then $4-c\ge 2$ and the line misses the disk, so the slice is the full disk. If $2\lt c\le 6$, then $-2\le 4-c\lt 2$ and the line cuts the disk, so the slice is a circular segment. If $c>6$, the slice is empty. The bottom plane gives $c\ge -1$, so the height of the solid covers $-1\le c\le 6$; the height $6$ occurs at the single point $(0,6,-2)$.

Result: the slice is the full disk for $-1\le c\le 2$ and a circular segment for $2\lt c\le 6$.

Step 3.2: The area of the slice

ReasonThe area of the full disk is known, and the area of a circular segment is the area of a sector minus the area of a triangle.

For $-1\le c\le 2$, $A(c)=4\pi$. For $2\lt c\le 6$ put $k=c-4$, so $0\lt k\le 2$. The line is $z=-k$ at the distance $k$ from the center. The sector has the angle $2\arccos(k/R)$ and the area $R^2\arccos(k/R)$, and the removed triangle has the area $k\sqrt{R^2-k^2}$. With $R=2$,

$$A(c)=4\arccos\frac{c-4}{2}-(c-4)\sqrt{4-(c-4)^2}.$$

CheckAt $k=0$, that is $c=4$: $A=4\cdot\pi/2=2\pi$, the half disk. At $k=2$, that is $c=6$: $A=0$. At $k=-1$, that is $c=3$: $A=8\pi/3+\sqrt{3}\approx 10.109631$.

Result: $A(c)=4\pi$ for $-1\le c\le 2$, and the segment formula for $2\lt c\le 6$.

Step 3.3: Integrate the area of the slice

ReasonThe volume is the integral of the areas of the slices that are perpendicular to the outer axis, here the $y$ axis.

$$V=\int_{-1}^{2}4\pi\,dc+\int_{2}^{6}A(c)\,dc=12\pi+\int_{2}^{6}A(c)\,dc.$$

With $u=c-4$,

$$\int_{2}^{6}A(c)\,dc=\int_{-2}^{2}\left(4\arccos\frac{u}{2}-u\sqrt{4-u^2}\right)du.$$

The term $u\sqrt{4-u^2}$ is odd, so its integral is $0$. With $u=2t$,

$$\int_{-2}^{2}\arccos\frac{u}{2}\,du=2\int_{-1}^{1}\arccos t\,dt=2\Big[t\arccos t-\sqrt{1-t^2}\Big]_{-1}^{1}=2\pi,$$

so the curved part is $8\pi$ and $V=12\pi+8\pi=20\pi$.

Answer of Part 3: $V=20\pi\approx 62.831853071796$, in agreement with Part 2.

Verified by code/part3_verify.py: the integral of the closed form area gives $62.831853071796$, the two pieces give $62.831853071796$, and the curved part gives $25.132741228718$, equal to $8\pi$.

Part 4: Polar coordinates and numerical checks

Step 4.1: Evaluate the double integral in polar coordinates

ReasonThe disk is a circle about the origin of the plane of $x$ and $z$, so polar coordinates give constant limits in the angle.

With $x=r\cos\theta$, $z=r\sin\theta$, and $dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$ for $0\le r\le 2$ and $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$,

$$V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}(5-r\sin\theta)\,r\,dr\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigl(10-\frac{8}{3}\sin\theta\Bigr)d\theta=10\cdot 2\pi-\frac{8}{3}\cdot 0=20\pi,$$

because the integral of $\sin\theta$ over one period is $0$. The constant term is the volume of a cylinder of radius $2$ and height $5$, and the symmetric term adds nothing.

Answer of Part 4, Step 1: $V=20\pi$.

Step 4.2: Independent numerical check

ReasonA closed form can hide an error in a bound. A numerical method that uses only the three inequalities can find such an error, because it never uses the algebraic form of the answer.

Adaptive triple quadrature over $x^2+z^2\le 4$ and $-1\le y\le 4-z$ gives $62.831853071796$, and the difference from $20\pi$ is $1.4\times10^{-14}$.

The bounding box $[-2,2]\times[-1,6]\times[-2,2]$ has volume $112$. With the seed $20261122$, $4\,000\,000$ random points give the estimate $62.777736$ and the standard error $0.027794$; the estimate lies $1.947$ standard errors from $20\pi$, below the accepted limit of $3$.

Answer of Part 4: $V=20\pi\approx 62.831853071796$. All four parts give the same value.

Verified by code/part4_verify.py: the polar integral gives $62.831853071796$ with the difference $0$, the triple quadrature gives $62.831853071796$ with the difference $1.4 imes10^{-14}$, and the Monte Carlo estimate $62.777736$ lies $1.947$ standard errors from $20\pi$, below the accepted limit of $3$.
