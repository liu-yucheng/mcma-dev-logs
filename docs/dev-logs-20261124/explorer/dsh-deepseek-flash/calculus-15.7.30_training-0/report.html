Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 30

Six orders for one triple integral

A tutorial that writes $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ in six ways, and checks every bound of every order with a script that the page cites.

The statement

Express the integral $\displaystyle\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ as an iterated integral in six different ways, where $E$ is the solid bounded by the given surfaces.

$$y^2+z^2=9,\qquad x=-2,\qquad x=2$$

The integrand $f$ is a general continuous function, so the answer is a list of six limit sets, not one number. The page therefore checks two things: the volume and the sections of $E$, which are numbers, and the agreement of the six orders on one chosen function.

How the solution is organised

Part 1 reads the region and measures it. Parts 2, 3, and 4 group the orders by the outer variable: $x$ outermost, $y$ outermost, and $z$ outermost. Part 5 puts one function through all six orders. Part 6 counts the orders and states the single rule that produces every bound.

Every computation of this page has a script under code/, and every table of this page is the printed output of one script. Each check compares a value that one method computes with a value that a different method computes.

Part 1. The solid and its volume

Step 1.1 — Read the three surfaces and write the condition on a point

Reason. Every later step uses one description of the region, so write that description first and read it from the three surfaces of the statement.

Computation. The surface $y^2+z^2=9$ is a circular cylinder of radius $3$ whose axis is the $x$-axis. The surface $x=-2$ is a plane, and the surface $x=2$ is a parallel plane. The solid is bounded by these three surfaces and by no other surface, so a point lies in $E$ exactly when

$$E=\{(x,y,z): -2\leq x\leq 2,\ y^2+z^2\leq 9\}.$$

Result. $E$ is the part of the cylinder of radius $3$ about the $x$-axis that lies between the planes $x=-2$ and $x=2$. It is a closed body, and it is symmetric about each of the three coordinate planes.

Verification. The script code/part1_verify.py measures the volume of $E$ by three routes, and every route agrees with the closed form $36\pi$.

Step 1.2 — Measure the volume, which is $36\pi$

Reason. The integrand $f$ is general, so no order has a numeric value that we can compare. The volume is the one number of $E$ that we can compare, and it checks the bounds that later steps print. The constant $36\pi$ also appears in every order when $f=1$.

Computation. The section at a fixed value of $x$ is the disk $y^2+z^2\leq 9$, and its area is $\pi\cdot 3^2=9\pi$. The area does not depend on $x$, so

$$V=\int_{-2}^{2}9\pi\,dx=9\pi\cdot 4=36\pi\approx 113.0973.$$

Result. The volume of $E$ is $36\pi$. The bounding box $[-2,2]\times[-3,3]\times[-3,3]$ has volume $144$, and $36\pi<144$, as a solid inside its box must satisfy.

Verification. The script code/part1_verify.py reports $113.097335529233$ from the slice quadrature against $36\pi$, with a difference of $1.99\times10^{-13}$, and $1.42\times10^{-14}$ from a triple quadrature over the whole solid.

Step 1.3 — Name the two projections

Reason. An integration order is a projection followed by a stack of sections. Each order needs the projection that drops the two inner variables, so name the two projections once.

Computation. Drop $x$: the set $\{(y,z): y^2+z^2\leq 9\}$ is the disk of radius $3$ in the plane $x=0$. Drop $z$: the set $\{(x,y): -2\leq x\leq 2,\ -3\leq y\leq 3\}$ is the rectangle $[-2,2]\times[-3,3]$ in the plane $z=0$.

Result. The projection on the plane $x=0$ is a disk. The projection on the plane $z=0$ is a rectangle. A projection that keeps the coordinates $x$ and $z$ drops $y$, so it lies on the plane $y=0$, and by symmetry it is the same rectangle.

Verification. The script code/part1_verify.py measures the disk area on a grid at five values of $x$ and reports $28.2754$ against $9\pi=28.2743$. The plane $x=2.2$ misses the solid, and the measured area there is $0$.

Part 2. The section at a fixed x: two orders

Step 2.1 — The section at a fixed $x$ is a disk

Reason. The outer integral of the first two orders runs over $x$, so the inner part must be the section at a fixed value of $x$.

Computation. Fix $x=t$ with $-2\leq t\leq 2$. The condition on the point is $-2\leq t\leq 2$ and $y^2+z^2\leq 9$. The first condition holds for the whole slice, so the section is $\{(t,y,z): y^2+z^2\leq 9\}$: a disk of radius $3$ in the plane $x=t$.

Result. Every section at a fixed $x$ is a disk of radius $3$ with area $9\pi$. The shape and the area do not change with $x$.

Verification. The script code/part2_verify.py measures the area of the section on a grid at five values of $x$ and prints each measured area against $9\pi=28.274333882308$.

Step 2.2 — First order: inner integral over $z$, then $y$, then $x$

Reason. Take the disk as the region of the inner two integrations. Cut the disk into strips of constant $y$: on the strip at $y$, the coordinate $z$ runs from the lower half of the circle to the upper half.

Computation. On the disk, $z^2\leq 9-y^2$ gives $-\sqrt{9-y^2}\leq z\leq\sqrt{9-y^2}$, and the strip exists when $-3\leq y\leq 3$. The outer variable $x$ runs over $[-2,2]$. Therefore

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx.$$

Result. In the order $dz\,dy\,dx$ the inner pair $(dz,dy)$ covers the disk, and the inner limits depend on $y$ only. The limits of the integral in $x$ are constants.

Verification. The script code/part2_verify.py evaluates this order for $f=1$ and reports $113.097335529233$ against $36\pi$.

Step 2.3 — Second order: inner integral over $y$, then $z$, then $x$

Reason. Keep the disk and change the direction of the strips. Cut the disk into strips of constant $z$: on the strip at $z$, the coordinate $y$ runs from one half of the circle to the other.

Computation. On the disk, $y^2\leq 9-z^2$ gives $-\sqrt{9-z^2}\leq y\leq\sqrt{9-z^2}$, and the strip exists when $-3\leq z\leq 3$. The outer variable $x$ runs over $[-2,2]$. Therefore

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx.$$

Result. The order $dy\,dz\,dx$ has the same outer limits as Step 2.2 and a different inner pair. The two orders differ only in the direction of the strips on the same disk, so the two integrals are equal.

Verification. The script code/part2_verify.py evaluates this order for $f=1$ and reports $113.097335529233$. The script also shows that the inner double integral depends on $x$: its value is $91.891585117501$ at $x=0$ and $120.165918999810$ at $x=1$, and the difference is $9\pi$.

Part 3. The section at a fixed y: two orders

Step 3.1 — The section at a fixed $y$ is a rectangle

Reason. The outer integral of the third and fourth orders runs over $y$, so the inner part must be the section at a fixed value of $y$. Derive the shape from the inequalities, and read the printed corner list before naming the shape.

Computation. Fix $y=b$. The condition on the point is $-2\leq x\leq 2$ and $z^2\leq 9-b^2$. The two conditions involve different variables, so the section is the product of two intervals:

$$\{(x,b,z): -2\leq x\leq 2,\ -\sqrt{9-b^2}\leq z\leq\sqrt{9-b^2}\}.$$

Result. The section at $y=b$ is the rectangle $[-2,2]\times[-\sqrt{9-b^2},\sqrt{9-b^2}]$ in the plane $y=b$, and its area is $8\sqrt{9-b^2}$. At $b=0$ the area is $24$. At $|b|=3$ the rectangle collapses to the segment $-2\leq x\leq 2$ on the line $z=0$, and the area is $0$.

Verification. The script code/part3_verify.py prints the corner list of the section at $b=-3,-2,0,2,3$. At $|b|=3$ the printed corners are $(-2,0)$ and $(2,0)$, which is one segment, and the printed area is $0$.

Step 3.2 — Third order: inner integral over $z$, then $x$, then $y$

Reason. Take the rectangle as the region of the inner two integrations. Cut the rectangle into strips of constant $x$: on the strip at $x$, the coordinate $z$ runs from the lower edge to the upper edge.

Computation. On the rectangle at $y=b$, the coordinate $z$ runs from $-\sqrt{9-b^2}$ to $\sqrt{9-b^2}$ for every $x$ in $[-2,2]$. The outer variable $y$ runs over $[-3,3]$. Therefore

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy.$$

Result. In the order $dz\,dx\,dy$ the inner limits depend on the outer variable $y$, and the middle limits are constants. The projection on the plane $y=0$ is the rectangle $[-2,2]\times[-3,3]$, and it gives this order.

Verification. The script code/part3_verify.py evaluates this order for $f=1$ and reports $113.097335529233$ against $36\pi$.

Step 3.3 — Fourth order: inner integral over $x$, then $z$, then $y$

Reason. Keep the rectangle and exchange the two inner integrations. Cut the rectangle into strips of constant $z$: on the strip at $z$, the coordinate $x$ runs from $-2$ to $2$.

Computation. On the rectangle at $y=b$, the middle limits are $-\sqrt{9-b^2}\leq z\leq \sqrt{9-b^2}$, and the inner limits are $-2\leq x\leq 2$. The outer variable $y$ runs over $[-3,3]$. Therefore

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}\int_{-2}^{2}f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy.$$

Result. In the order $dx\,dz\,dy$ the inner limits are constants, and the middle limits depend on the outer variable $y$. The two orders with $y$ outermost differ only in the order of the two inner integrations on the same rectangle, so their values agree.

Verification. The script code/part3_verify.py evaluates this order for $f=1$ and for $f=1+x+y^2$, and reports $113.097335529233$ and $367.566340470006$ against $36\pi$ and $117\pi$.

Part 4. The section at a fixed z: two orders

Step 4.1 — The section at a fixed $z$ is the same rectangle

Reason. The cylinder $y^2+z^2=9$ and the two planes treat $y$ and $z$ in the same way, so the section at a fixed $z$ follows from the section at a fixed $y$ with the two letters exchanged. Derive it again from the inequalities, because the figure must not decide the shape.

Computation. Fix $z=c$. The condition on the point is $-2\leq x\leq 2$ and $y^2\leq 9-c^2$. The two conditions involve different variables, so the section is

$$\{(x,y,c): -2\leq x\leq 2,\ -\sqrt{9-c^2}\leq y\leq\sqrt{9-c^2}\}.$$

Result. The section at $z=c$ is the rectangle $[-2,2]\times[-\sqrt{9-c^2},\sqrt{9-c^2}]$ in the plane $z=c$, and its area is $8\sqrt{9-c^2}$. That area equals the area of the section at $y=c$. At $|c|=3$ the rectangle collapses to the segment $-2\leq x\leq 2$ on the line $y=0$.

Verification. The script code/part4_verify.py measures the section area on a grid at $c=-3,-2,0,1.5,3$ and compares each measurement with $8\sqrt{9-c^2}$. It also compares the measured area at $z=c$ with the closed form of the area at $y=c$ for three values of $c$.

Step 4.2 — Fifth order: inner integral over $y$, then $x$, then $z$

Reason. Take the rectangle at a fixed $z$ as the region of the inner two integrations. Cut it into strips of constant $x$: on the strip at $x$, the coordinate $y$ runs from the lower edge to the upper edge.

Computation. On the rectangle at $z=c$, the coordinate $y$ runs from $-\sqrt{9-c^2}$ to $\sqrt{9-c^2}$ for every $x$ in $[-2,2]$. The outer variable $z$ runs over $[-3,3]$. Therefore

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz.$$

Result. In the order $dy\,dx\,dz$ the inner limits depend on the outer variable $z$, and the middle limits are constants.

Verification. The script code/part4_verify.py evaluates this order for $f=1$ and for $f=1+x+z^2$, and reports $113.097335529233$ and $367.566340470006$ against $36\pi$ and $117\pi$.

Step 4.3 — Sixth order: inner integral over $x$, then $y$, then $z$

Reason. Keep the rectangle at a fixed $z$ and exchange the two inner integrations. Cut the rectangle into strips of constant $y$: on the strip at $y$, the coordinate $x$ runs from $-2$ to $2$.

Computation. On the rectangle at $z=c$, the middle limits are $-\sqrt{9-c^2}\leq y\leq \sqrt{9-c^2}$, and the inner limits are $-2\leq x\leq 2$. The outer variable $z$ runs over $[-3,3]$. Therefore

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}\int_{-2}^{2}f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz.$$

Result. In the order $dx\,dy\,dz$ the inner limits are constants, and the middle limits depend on the outer variable $z$. The six orders are now complete: two with $x$ outermost, two with $y$ outermost, and two with $z$ outermost.

Verification. The script code/part4_verify.py evaluates this order for $f=1$ and reports $113.097335529233$. The two orders with $z$ outermost agree to the printed digits.

Part 5. The six orders agree on one function

Step 5.1 — One function that uses all three coordinates

Reason. A general $f$ has no numeric value, so the six orders cannot be compared by a number. Choose one function that depends on all three coordinates, and compare the six orders on that function. The closed form of its integral follows from Parts 1, 3, and 4.

Computation. Take $f(x,y,z)=1+x+y^2+z^2$. Then

$$\iiint_E f\,dV=V+\iiint_E x\,dV+\iiint_E y^2\,dV+\iiint_E z^2\,dV =36\pi+0+81\pi+81\pi=198\pi\approx 622.0353.$$

The term $\iiint_E x\,dV$ is $0$ because the solid is symmetric about the plane $x=0$ and the integrand is odd in $x$.

Result. The six orders must all give $198\pi$ for this function.

Verification. The script code/part5_verify.py evaluates the six orders with six separate nested quadratures and reports $622.035345410779$ six times against $198\pi=622.035345410779$. The largest pairwise difference of the six values is $3.4\times10^{-13}$.

Step 5.2 — Write the six answers in one table

Reason. The answer to the exercise is the list of six iterated integrals, so collect them in one place, and state which limits depend on which variable.

Computation. The six integrals are

$$\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}f\,dz\,dy\,dx,\qquad \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}f\,dy\,dz\,dx,$$
$$\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}f\,dz\,dx\,dy,\qquad \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}\int_{-2}^{2}f\,dx\,dz\,dy,$$
$$\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}f\,dy\,dx\,dz,\qquad \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}\int_{-2}^{2}f\,dx\,dy\,dz,$$

with $f=f(x,y,z)$ in every one of them.

Result. The list above is the final answer of the exercise.

Verification. The script code/part5_verify.py checks that the six printed limit sets give one value for $f=1+x+y^2+z^2$. The script code/part6_verify.py checks that each printed limit set accepts exactly the points of $E$ in a random sample of $20000$ points.

Part 6. Why there are exactly six orders

Step 6.1 — Count the orders: there are six

Reason. The exercise asks for the number of ways to write the integral, so count the ways before choosing them.

Computation. An order is a choice of one variable for the inner integral, one of the two remaining variables for the middle integral, and the last variable for the outer integral. There are $3$ choices for the inner variable, $2$ for the middle variable, and $1$ for the outer variable, so the number of orders is $3\cdot 2\cdot 1=3!=6$.

Result. There are exactly six orders, and they are the six permutations of the three coordinate names.

Verification. The script code/part6_verify.py counts the orders three ways and reports $6$ from the enumeration, $6$ from the factorial formula, and $6$ from three nested loops that skip a repeated name.

Step 6.2 — One bound rule that produces all six limit sets

Reason. Six separate derivations hide a mistake. One rule that covers every order shows why the six limit sets agree.

Computation. The solid has two kinds of surface. The planes fix $x$ between two constants, and the cylinder relates $y$ and $z$ only to each other. Hence the rule: the bounds of $x$ are always $-2$ and $2$; the bounds of $y$ are $-3$ and $3$, or the two halves of the cylinder at the fixed value of $z$; the bounds of $z$ are $-3$ and $3$, or the two halves of the cylinder at the fixed value of $y$. The variable of the pair $y,z$ that is integrated first takes the cylinder bound, and the other one takes the constant bound.

Result. Applied to the six permutations, the rule gives the six limit sets of Step 5.2. In the orders with $x$ outermost the inner limits depend on $y$ or on $z$. In the orders with $y$ or $z$ outermost the inner limits may depend on the outer variable.
