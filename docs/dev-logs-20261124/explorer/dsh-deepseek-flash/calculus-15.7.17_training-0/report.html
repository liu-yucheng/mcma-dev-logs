Problem Evaluate the triple integral

Statement, as given

Evaluate the triple integral

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV,$$

where $E$ is the solid region bounded by the paraboloid $x = 4y^{2} + 4z^{2}$ and the plane $x = 4$.

The problem gives no numerical answer and no intermediate value. The tutorial computes the value and checks it. The integrand is $f(x, y, z) = x$, and the volume element is $dV = dx \, dy \, dz$ in Cartesian order.

What you need before you start.
  • Cylindrical coordinates about an axis that is not the $z$ axis, and the Jacobian factor $r$.
  • Fubini's theorem for a region that is a graph over a plane region.
  • The area of a disk, $\pi r^{2}$, and the power rule for $r^{n}$.

Plan Four parts, eleven steps

The plan follows one idea. The region is a solid of revolution about the $x$ axis, and the integrand does not depend on the angle. Therefore the angle separates from the problem, and the work reduces to one integral in $r$.

Part 1 Set up the region

Goal: describe the solid as an inequality, find where the two surfaces meet, and choose a coordinate system in which both the region and the integrand are simple.

1.1Identify the two boundary surfaces

Why

The limits of the integral come from the geometry of the two surfaces. You must know which surface is the floor of the region and which surface is the roof. The direction in which the paraboloid opens decides this.

The first surface is a paraboloid of revolution:

$$x = 4y^{2} + 4z^{2} = 4\left(y^{2} + z^{2}\right).$$

Set $r^{2} = y^{2} + z^{2}$. The equation becomes $x = 4r^{2}$. The vertex is the origin, because $r = 0$ gives $x = 0$, and $x = 4r^{2}$ increases with $r$. The paraboloid opens along the positive $x$ axis. The second surface is the plane $x = 4$, parallel to the $yz$ plane.

At the axis point $(0, 0)$ of the $yz$ plane the paraboloid has $x = 0$ and the plane has $x = 4$. The paraboloid is therefore below the plane near the axis.

Result of step 1.1

$$E = \left\{(x, y, z) : 4\left(y^{2} + z^{2}\right) \le x \le 4\right\}.$$

The curved surface is the lower bound on $x$. The flat surface is the upper bound on $x$.

Checked by code/part1_verify.py: the paraboloid height is 4.000000 at $r = 1$ and 4.080400 at $r = 1.01$.

1.2Find the circle where the surfaces meet

Why

The region ends where the two surfaces meet. That curve is also the boundary of the shadow that $E$ casts on the $yz$ plane. The next step needs that shadow.

Set the two expressions for $x$ equal:

$$4\left(y^{2} + z^{2}\right) = 4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^{2} + z^{2} = 1.$$

This is a circle of radius $1$ in the plane $x = 4$. Two height checks confirm it: at $r = 0.5$ the paraboloid has $x = 1$, below the plane, and at $r = 1$ it has $x = 4$, on the plane.

Every point with $y^{2} + z^{2} \le 1$ gives a nonempty segment $4r^{2} \le x \le 4$. Every point with $y^{2} + z^{2} > 1$ gives an empty segment, because $4r^{2} > 4$.

Result of step 1.2

The surfaces meet on the circle $y^{2} + z^{2} = 1$ in the plane $x = 4$.

The projection of $E$ onto the $yz$ plane is the closed unit disk $D = \left\{y^{2} + z^{2} \le 1\right\}$.

Checked by code/part1_verify.py: membership of 200000 random points agrees with the rule $r \le 1$ at a rate of 1.000000. The counted volume is 6.2755 against the exact 6.2832.

1.3Write $E$ as an iterated region and choose the order

Why

A triple integral needs three one-variable integrals. One variable must run between two functions, and the other two run over a plane region. Choose the variable that already has two bounds as the inner variable.

Step 1.1 gives two bounds on $x$ and no bound on $y$ or $z$ alone. Step 1.2 gives the plane region for $(y, z)$. Thus

$$E = \left\{(x, y, z) : (y, z) \in D,\; 4\left(y^{2} + z^{2}\right) \le x \le 4\right\},$$

and the order $dx$ first, then the area element of the disk, is natural:

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \iint_{D} \left[\int_{4(y^{2}+z^{2})}^{4} x \, dx\right] dA.$$

The order is good for two reasons. The inner integrand $x$ has the elementary antiderivative $x^{2}/2$. The value of the inner integral depends only on $y^{2} + z^{2}$, which is the quantity that describes the disk $D$.

Result of step 1.3

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \iint_{D} \left(\int_{4(y^{2}+z^{2})}^{4} x \, dx\right) dA, \qquad D = \left\{y^{2} + z^{2} \le 1\right\}.$$

Checked by code/part1_verify.py: the segment above a point of the plane is nonempty exactly on the unit disk, with agreement 1.000000 over 200000 points.

1.4Use cylindrical coordinates about the $x$ axis

Why

The disk has a polar description, and the inner integral depends on $y^{2} + z^{2}$ only. Polar coordinates for $(y, z)$ turn the disk into a rectangle in $(r, \theta)$ and the integrand into a function of $r$ alone.

The axis of the solid is the $x$ axis, so the rotation is about $x$. Do not use the $z$ axis: the paraboloid $x = 4\left(y^{2}+z^{2}\right)$ is not a surface of revolution about $z$.

Put $y = r\cos\theta$, $z = r\sin\theta$ and keep $x$. Then $y^{2} + z^{2} = r^{2}$ and

$$dA = r \, dr \, d\theta, \qquad dV = r \, dx \, dr \, d\theta,$$

where the factor $r$ is the Jacobian of the map $(r, \theta) \mapsto (y, z)$. The bounds become

$$0 \le \theta \le 2\pi, \qquad 0 \le r \le 1, \qquad 4r^{2} \le x \le 4.$$

Result of step 1.4

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{4r^{2}}^{4} x \, r \; dx \, dr \, d\theta.$$

Checked by code/part2_verify.py: with the factor $r$ the volume of $E$ is 6.283185307180, which equals $2\pi$. Without the factor $r$ it is 16.755160819146, which is wrong.

Part 2 Integrate along the $x$ direction

Goal: remove the variable $x$ and leave a two-dimensional integral over the disk.

2.1Integrate $x$ from $4r^{2}$ to $4$

Why

In the inner integral the variables $r$ and $\theta$ are constant, so only $x$ changes. The antiderivative of $x$ is $x^{2}/2$, and the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the value at the two bounds.

$$\int_{4r^{2}}^{4} x \, dx = \left[\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right]_{4r^{2}}^{4} = \frac{16}{2} - \frac{16r^{4}}{2} = 8 - 8r^{4}.$$

Three values act as checks. At $r = 0$ the segment is $0 \le x \le 4$ and the integral is $8$. At $r = 0.5$ the segment is $1 \le x \le 4$ and the integral is $7.5$. At $r = 1$ the segment has zero length and the integral is $0$.

Result of step 2.1

$$\int_{4r^{2}}^{4} x \, dx = 8\left(1 - r^{4}\right).$$

The inner integral is largest at the axis and falls to zero at the rim of the cap.

Checked by code/part2_verify.py: closed form against adaptive quadrature at $r = 0,\; 0.25,\; 0.5,\; 0.75,\; 1$; every difference is 0.000e+00. At $r = 1/2$ the exact value is $15/2 = 7.5$.

2.2Assemble the two outer integrals

Why

The inner integral is done. The remaining work is its integral over the disk. The value $8\left(1 - r^{4}\right)$ depends on $r$ only, so after you insert the area element $r \, dr \, d\theta$ the disk integral becomes two one-variable integrals.

Insert the result of step 2.1 into the iterated integral of step 1.4 and collect the factors in $r$:

$$8\left(1 - r^{4}\right) r = 8r - 8r^{5},$$

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \left(8r - 8r^{5}\right) dr \, d\theta.$$

The integrand has no $\theta$, and both pairs of limits are constants. The double integral is therefore the product of two single integrals.

Result of step 2.2

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \left(\int_{0}^{2\pi} d\theta\right) \left(\int_{0}^{1} \left(8r - 8r^{5}\right) dr\right).$$

The first factor is the length of a full turn. The second is the area under the curve $8r - 8r^{5}$.

Checked by code/part2_verify.py: the inner integral evaluated from 20000 random points $(y, z)$ matches the form in $r$; the largest difference is 5.3e−15.

Part 3 Reduce to one dimension

Goal: evaluate the two single integrals and multiply them.

3.1Integrate over $\theta$

Why

The integrand has no $\theta$. The integral of a constant over an interval is the constant times the length of the interval, and a full turn has length $2\pi$.

$$\int_{0}^{2\pi} d\theta = \left[\theta\right]_{0}^{2\pi} = 2\pi.$$

This factor is the total angle of the solid. It does not depend on $r$ or on $x$.

Result of step 3.1

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = 2\pi \int_{0}^{1} \left(8r - 8r^{5}\right) dr.$$

The angle contributes $2\pi = 6.283185307180$.

Checked by code/part3_verify.py: quadrature gives 6.283185307180 against the expected $2\pi$, difference 0.000e+00.

3.2Integrate over $r$

Why

The integrand is a sum of two powers of $r$, and the power rule applies to each term separately: the antiderivative of $r^{n}$ is $r^{n+1}/(n+1)$ for $n \ne -1$.

$$\int_{0}^{1} \left(8r - 8r^{5}\right) dr = \left[4r^{2} - \frac{8r^{6}}{6}\right]_{0}^{1} = 4 - \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{3}.$$

The separate terms give the same value: $8 \cdot \frac{1}{2} - 8 \cdot \frac{1}{6} = 4 - \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{3}$.

Result of step 3.2

$$\int_{0}^{1} \left(8r - 8r^{5}\right) dr = \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.666666666667.$$

This is the value of the integral of $x$ per radian of angle.

Checked by code/part3_verify.py: quadrature gives 2.666666666667, and exact Fraction arithmetic gives 8/3.

3.3Multiply the two factors

Why

The double integral of step 2.2 is the product of a function of $\theta$ and a function of $r$ over a rectangle of limits, so it equals the product of the two single integrals. Both are known, and the final value is one multiplication.

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \left(2\pi\right)\left(\frac{8}{3}\right) = \frac{16\pi}{3}.$$

$$\frac{16\pi}{3} = \frac{16 \cdot 3.141592653589793}{3} = 16.755160819146\ldots$$

The value is positive, as it must be: $x \ge 0$ on $E$ and the volume of $E$ is positive.

Result of step 3.3 — the final answer

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \frac{16\pi}{3} \approx 16.7552.$$

Checked by code/part3_verify.py: the product is 16.755160819146, nested quadrature is 16.755160819146, and a sample of four million points gives 16.772078 with a standard error of 0.009369.

Part 4 Verify the value

Goal: obtain the same number by a route that shares no step with Part 3, and check it against two quantities that have their own formulas.

4.1Integrate in the order $dx\,dy\,dz$

Why

A limit error is the most common fault in a triple integral. A second order of integration uses different limits, so it exposes such a fault. Take $x$ as the outer variable: the slice of $E$ at a fixed $x$ is a disk, because the condition $4\left(y^{2}+z^{2}\right) \le x$ fixes the radius.

Fix $x$ with $0 \le x \le 4$. The slice obeys $y^{2} + z^{2} \le x/4$, so it is a disk of radius

$$r(x) = \frac{\sqrt{x}}{2}, \qquad A(x) = \pi r(x)^{2} = \frac{\pi x}{4}.$$

On the slice $x$ is constant, so the slice integral is $x \cdot A(x)$. Then

$$\int_{0}^{4} x \cdot \frac{\pi x}{4} \, dx = \frac{\pi}{4} \int_{0}^{4} x^{2} \, dx = \frac{\pi}{4} \cdot \frac{64}{3} = \frac{16\pi}{3}.$$

At $x = 2$ the radius is $\sqrt{2}/2 \approx 0.7071$ and the area is $\pi/2 \approx 1.5708$.

Result of step 4.1

$$\iiint_{E} x \, dV = \int_{0}^{4} \frac{\pi x^{2}}{4} \, dx = \frac{16\pi}{3}.$$

Two orders give the same value, so the limits of Part 1 are correct.

Checked by code/part4_verify.py: this order gives 16.755160819146 against the expected 16.755160819146, difference 0.000e+00. The counted slice area at $x = 2$ is 1.570488 against $\pi/2 = 1.570796$.

4.2Check the volume and the mean value

Why

Two numbers must agree with the integral: the volume of $E$ and the mean value of $x$ over $E$. Both have independent formulas. The mean value also gives a physical check. The region spans $0 \le x \le 4$, and it is thin near $x = 0$ and wide near $x = 4$, so the mean value must lie above the midpoint $2$ and below $4$.

$$V = \int_{0}^{4} \frac{\pi x}{4} \, dx = \frac{\pi}{4} \cdot 8 = 2\pi, \qquad \bar{x} = \frac{16\pi/3}{2\pi} = \frac{8}{3}.$$

A third route is the shell form. At radius $r$ the shell has circumference $2\pi r$, length $4 - 4r^{2}$, and mean $x$ equal to $\left(4 + 4r^{2}\right)/2$:

$$\int_{0}^{1} 2\pi r \left(4 - 4r^{2}\right)\frac{4 + 4r^{2}}{2}\, dr = 16\pi \int_{0}^{1} r\left(1 - r^{4}\right) dr = 16\pi\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{6}\right) = \frac{16\pi}{3}.$$

Result of step 4.2

$$V = 2\pi \approx 6.283185307180, \qquad \bar{x} = \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.666666666667, \qquad V\bar{x} = \frac{16\pi}{3}.$$

The mean value lies in $(0, 4)$ and above $2$, which fits the shape of $E$.

Checked by code/part4_verify.py: volume 6.283185307180 from the disk slices, mean value 2.666666666667, shell integral 16.755160819146. code/part1_verify.py counts the volume as 6.2755 against 6.2832.

Answer Summary

Value of the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint_{E} x \, dV = \frac{16\pi}{3}$
= 16.755160819146…

The solid is a paraboloid cap: $4\left(y^{2}+z^{2}\right) \le x \le 4$ over the unit disk. Cylindrical coordinates about the $x$ axis give the iterated integral $\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{4r^{2}}^{4} x \, r \, dx \, dr \, d\theta$, whose inner integral is $8\left(1-r^{4}\right)$ and whose outer integrals are $2\pi$ and $8/3$. Three other orders of integration and a point sample agree with the value. The volume is $2\pi$ and the mean value of $x$ is $8/3$.
