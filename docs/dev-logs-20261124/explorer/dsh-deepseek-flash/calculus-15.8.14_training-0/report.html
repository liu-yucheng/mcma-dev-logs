The statement

Use a graphing device to draw the solid enclosed by the paraboloids $z = x^{2} + y^{2}$ and $z = 5 - x^{2} - y^{2}$.

Step 1.1 - Read each surface from its own equation

Reason. Each equation gives the height $z$ of one surface above a point of the plane $z = 0$. The lower equation holds the sum $x^{2} + y^{2}$, and that sum is the squared distance from the vertical axis. So the lower surface is a surface of revolution about the vertical axis, it opens upward, and its lowest point is the origin. The upper equation holds $5$ minus the same sum, so the upper surface is the same shape turned over, and its highest point is $(0, 0, 5)$.

Computation. Write $r^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2}$ for the squared distance from the vertical axis. The two surfaces then read as follows.

$$ z_{\text{lower}} = r^{2}, \qquad z_{\text{upper}} = 5 - r^{2} $$

The lower height is never negative, so the lower surface stays at or above the plane $z = 0$. The upper height never passes $5$, so the upper surface stays at or below the plane $z = 5$.

Result. The check samples two hundred thousand points of the square $|x| \le 2$, $|y| \le 2$, so it covers all four quadrants, and it prints the largest gap at every sampled point [scan cylindrical_identity in code/part1_verify.py:38-73]. The gap between $x^{2} + y^{2}$ and $r^{2}$ is $8.882 \times 10^{-16}$ for the lower surface and $1.776 \times 10^{-15}$ for the upper surface, against the tolerance $1.0 \times 10^{-12}$. The check also compares a finite difference of each height along the radius with the slope $2 r$ and $-2 r$, and the largest gaps are $9.548 \times 10^{-14}$ and $1.199 \times 10^{-13}$, so the rewritten forms are exact rather than approximate.

Common mistake. A reader may take the upper equation for a bowl that opens upward, but the minus sign in front of $x^{2} + y^{2}$ makes it open downward from the height $5$.

Step 1.2 - Find the circle where the two surfaces meet

Reason. The two surfaces meet where they give the same height above the same point of the plane $z = 0$. Setting the two heights equal turns the pair of equations into one equation in the squared distance $r^{2}$. That equation decides the size of the circle, and the common height then follows from either surface.

Computation. Set the two heights equal and collect the squared distance on one side.

$$ r^{2} = 5 - r^{2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2 r^{2} = 5 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad r^{2} = \frac{5}{2} $$

The common height follows from either surface at $r^{2} = \frac{5}{2}$.

$$ z = r^{2} = \frac{5}{2}, \qquad z = 5 - r^{2} = 5 - \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{2} $$

So the meeting set is the circle $x^{2} + y^{2} = \frac{5}{2}$ in the plane $z = \frac{5}{2}$. Its radius is $\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$, which is about $1.5811$.

Result. A root search on the difference of the two heights returns the squared radius $2.500000000$ against the exact value $\frac{5}{2} = 2.5$ [scan meeting_curve_root in code/part1_verify.py:76-108]. The same check walks 72 points of the circle: the two heights differ by at most $1.776 \times 10^{-15}$, and every common height differs from $\frac{5}{2}$ by at most $8.882 \times 10^{-16}$. The check also prints the largest slope of the difference $5 - 2 r^{2}$ away from the axis, and that slope is $-0.030000$, which is negative, so the difference falls at every sampled point and the root is the only root.

Common mistake. A reader may take the radius of the circle for $\frac{5}{2}$, but the equation fixes the squared radius, so the radius is $\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$ and the squared radius is $\frac{5}{2}$.

Step 1.3 - Decide which surface is on top

Reason. The body is the set of points between the two surfaces, so the drawing needs to know which surface is above the other. The difference of the two heights answers that question at every squared distance, and its sign classifies each point of the plane $z = 0$. The circle of Step 1.2 is where the sign changes.

Computation. Subtract the lower height from the upper height.

$$ z_{\text{upper}} - z_{\text{lower}} = (5 - r^{2}) - r^{2} = 5 - 2 r^{2} $$

The difference is positive exactly when $r^{2} < \frac{5}{2}$, it is zero at $r^{2} = \frac{5}{2}$, and it is negative outside that circle. So the upper surface is above the lower surface inside the circle, the two surfaces meet on the circle, and the upper surface is below the lower surface outside the circle. No point outside the circle lies between the two surfaces.

Result. The check prints the difference at six squared radii: $5, 3, 1, 0, -1, -3$ at $r^{2} = 0, 1, 2, 2.5, 3, 4$ [scan above_below_order in code/part1_verify.py:111-143].
