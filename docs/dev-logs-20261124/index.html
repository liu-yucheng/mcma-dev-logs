Experiment Report · 2026-11-24

🧪 Learning to Plot, and Failing to Transfer It

🤖 A new recursive self-improvement (RSI) harness trained DeepSeek Flash at 200 tokens/s on 17 canonical solutions. 📈 It learned a plotting recipe: labelled 3D solids and labelled 2D projections. 🐢 The same instruction then ran on Qwen3.8-27B at 40 tokens/s on 24 problems. ❌ The math stayed correct, but the plot style did not transfer. ✅ DeepSeek Flash is acceptable as a math tutor. ⏱ Qwen3.8-27B is not.

← ⌂ 🔬 Overview 🤖 Harness 📊 Results Briefing 🔍 Results Explorer 🗺 Future Plans

⚡ Short answer

✅ The framework works. The MCMA DeepSeek Harness plugin trains an agent the way a classical model is trained: forward pass, feedback, backward pass, validate, and test. The operator trained 17 canonical solutions and tested 7 unseen problems.

🧪 The learning is real, but implicit. The instruction grew from 272 to 11,952 characters of learned experience. It now carries plotting rules that the v0 prompt did not have. The loss curve does not show this: the final pass had the worst single loss of the run (0.316), while the plot instruction was at its best.

🐢 The transfer failed. Qwen3.8-27B read the v17 instruction and solved 24 problems. It passed its own release gate 24 of 24 times. It did not reproduce the learned plot style: no labelled projection panel on 15.7.32, and overlapping surface labels on 15.7.18.

📌 The manual remarks assume that both models give correct math, because many earlier runs did. This log checks that assumption where it can: the DeepSeek test split has a canonical answer for each problem, and Qwen has a structural release gate. The one DeepSeek error is 15.7.23, a wedge volume given as 1/3 instead of π/4 − 1/3.

💡 Main finding

🔧 The harness learned through the prompt, not through the loss. Every pass added text distilled from that pass’s evaluator findings. The tutor prompt grew from 1,432 to 21,584 characters. The plotting rules in v17 are a record of what previous passes got wrong. The loss is a five-dimension rubric average; it cannot say which sentence caused the gain.

🎨 The learned recipe has two visible parts. On 15.7.32, DeepSeek draws a labelled tetrahedron with vertex letters and plane labels, then a second figure with three labelled 2D projections and their areas. The v0 output had no such projection panel. This is the clearest evidence that the instruction changed the plot.

❌ Words alone did not move the recipe to a slower model. Qwen3.8-27B received the same rules at 40 tokens/s. On 15.7.32 it drew one 3D panel with clipped, low-contrast labels and no projection panel. On 15.7.18 it drew one 3D panel whose surface labels collide on the curved wall, with no projection and no adjusted viewpoint.

⏱ The speed verdict is separate from the quality verdict. With RSI off, DeepSeek Flash finishes a problem in under 5 minutes on average. Qwen3.8-27B needs over 15 minutes on average. The manual remarks call Qwen unacceptable as a tutor regardless of harness improvements.

🔄 What changed since the last log

📋 ScopeThe last log compared four one-shot recipes. This log trains one recipe, then transfers the learned instruction to a second model.
🎯 GoalThe last log asked for correct solutions. This log asks whether a model can learn how to plot from canonical solutions, and whether it can teach that skill to another model.
🤖 HarnessA new MCMA plugin adds mcma_forward, mcma_feedback, mcma_backward, mcma_resume, and mcma_test to DeepSeek Harness.
📜 PromptThe instruction is a trained artifact. Version v0 is the old 64-line prompt. Version v17 is a 424-line prompt with a score model, a verification law, a page law, and a figure law.
🧠 ModelsDeepSeek Flash at 200 tokens/s does the learning. Qwen3.8-27B at 40 tokens/s receives the compiled experience.
🗃 DataTwo workspaces and two datasets: 17 training solutions, 7 test problems, and 24 transfer problems from Stewart 15.7, 15.8, and 15.9.
