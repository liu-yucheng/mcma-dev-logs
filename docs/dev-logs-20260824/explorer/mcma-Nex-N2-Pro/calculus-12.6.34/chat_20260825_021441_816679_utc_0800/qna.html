Answer
Complete the squares in the variables that appear quadratically: $$ 4y^2-16y=4\bigl((y-2)^2-4\bigr)=4(y-2)^2-16, $$ and $$ z^2-4z=(z-2)^2-4. $$ Substituting into $$ 4y^2+z^2-x-16y-4z+20=0 $$ gives $$ 4(y-2)^2-16+(z-2)^2-4-x+20=0, $$ so $$ 4(y-2)^2+(z-2)^2-x=0. $$ Thus the reduced standard form is $$ x=4(y-2)^2+(z-2)^2. $$ This is an **elliptic paraboloid**. It opens in the positive $x$-direction because $x$ is the sum of two nonnegative squared terms. Its vertex occurs when both squared terms are zero: $$ (y,z)=(2,2),\qquad x=0, $$ so the vertex is $(0,2,2)$. The axis is the line $y=2,\ z=2$. For $x=k>0$, the cross-section is the ellipse $$ 4(y-2)^2+(z-2)^2=k, $$ or $$ \frac{(y-2)^2}{k/4}+\frac{(z-2)^2}{k}=1. $$ The traces in the planes $z=2$ and $y=2$ are parabolas: $$ x=4(y-2)^2,\qquad x=(z-2)^2. $$ There are no real points with $x<0$. $$ \boxed{x=4(y-2)^2+(z-2)^2,\ \text{an elliptic paraboloid with vertex }(0,2,2)\text{ opening in the }+x\text{ direction}} $$ --- --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./20260825_021731_632122_utc_0800_pgfplots_0.tex)
