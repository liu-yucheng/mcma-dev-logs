## Drawing the Silo: Cylinder + Hemisphere ### 1. Set up the geometry I place the silo **standing on the ground plane $z=0$ with its axis along the $z$-axis**. Working through the description gives the natural placement: - **Cylinder:** radius $r=3$, height $h=10$, so it occupies $0\le z\le 10$. - **Hemisphere:** radius $3$, resting *flat-side down* on the top of the cylinder. Its center is therefore the point $(0,0,10)$ and it spans $10\le z\le 13$. The two parts share the **circle** $x^2+y^2=9$ at height $z=10$, which is exactly what makes them join smoothly (the hemisphere is tangent to the vertical cylinder-wall there, so the silhouette has no corner). A silo is a **solid of revolution**, so an efficient way to draw it is to sketch the *profile curve in a plane through the axis* (say the $xz$-plane) and rotate it about the $z$-axis. The profile is: - a **rectangle** (base disk edge + vertical wall): $x=3$ for $0\le z\le 10$; - a **semicircle** of radius $3$ centered at $(0,10)$: $x^2+(z-10)^2=9$ for $z\ge 10$, opened downward so it caps the cylinder. ### 2. Equations of the two surfaces **Cartesian coordinates** $$\text{Cylinder: } x^2+y^2=9,\quad 0\le z\le 10 \qquad\qquad \text{Hemisphere: } x^2+y^2+(z-10)^2=9,\quad z\ge 10$$ **Cylindrical coordinates** $(x=r\cos\theta,\ y=r\sin\theta,\ z)$ $$\text{Cylinder: } r=3,\ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\ 0\le z\le 10 \qquad \text{Dome: } z=10+\sqrt{9-r^2},\ 0\le r\le 3$$ **Spherical coordinates** measured from the **center of the hemisphere** $(0,0,10)$ — the description used in *Triple Integrals in Spherical Coordinates*. With $\rho$ = distance from $(0,0,10)$, $\phi$ the angle from the downward vertical, and $\theta$ the azimuth, $$\text{Dome: } \rho = 3,\quad 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\quad 0\le\phi\le \tfrac{\pi}{2}.$$ That nice constant bound $\rho=3$ is precisely why offsetting the origin to the dome's center is the convenient choice. ### 3. The drawing The figure below is the completed silo rendered as a surface: a translucent blue cylinder capped by a red hemispherical dome, with the shared circle $z=10$ drawn in black and a green segment marking $r=3$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ### 4. Cross-section that generates the silo Looking straight at the $xz$-plane shows the shape as a **rectangle with a semicircular cap** — the profile you revolve about the $z$-axis. This pinpoints the three key numbers: the wall stands at $x=\pm3$, the wall height is $h=10$, and the dome is the upper semicircle $x^2+(z-10)^2=9$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

Checks (bonus) Although this exercise only asks for the drawing, the same setup lets you check the picture numerically: - **Volume** $= \underbrace{\pi r^2 h}_{\text{cylinder}} + \underbrace{\tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3}_{\text{hemisphere}} = 90\pi + 18\pi = 108\pi \approx 339.29$. - **Exterior area** $= \underbrace{2\pi r h}_{\text{wall}} + \underbrace{\pi r^2}_{\text{base}} + \underbrace{2\pi r^2}_{\text{dome}} = 60\pi + 9\pi + 18\pi = 87\pi \approx 273.32$. **Answer (the picture):** a cylinder of radius $3$ and height $10$ on $0\le z\le 10$, smoothly capped by the hemisphere $x^2+y^2+(z-10)^2=9,\ z\ge10$, giving a silo whose outline is a rectangle topped by a semicircle of the same radius, reaching an apex at $z=13$. $$\boxed{\text{Silo} = \{x^2+y^2\le 9,\ 0\le z\le 10\}\ \cup\ \{x^2+y^2+(z-10)^2\le 9,\ z\ge 10\}}$$
