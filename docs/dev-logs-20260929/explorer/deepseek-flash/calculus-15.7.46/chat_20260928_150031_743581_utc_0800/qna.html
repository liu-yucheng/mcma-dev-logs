## Moment of inertia of a solid cone about its axis For a solid $E$ with density $k$, the moment of inertia about the $z$-axis is $$ I_z=\iiint_E k(x^2+y^2)\,dV. $$ The cone is $$ E=\{(x,y,z):\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\le z\le h\}. $$ At height $z$, its horizontal cross-section is a disk of radius $z$; hence the base radius is $h$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- Use cylindrical coordinates: $$ x=r\cos\theta,\qquad y=r\sin\theta,\qquad x^2+y^2=r^2,\qquad dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta. $$ Since $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=r$, the cone is described by $$ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\qquad 0\le r\le h,\qquad r\le z\le h. $$ Therefore $$ I_z=k\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^h\!\!\int_r^h r^2(r)\,dz\,dr\,d\theta =2\pi k\int_0^h r^3(h-r)\,dr. $$ Equivalently, slice the cone into thin cylindrical shells of radius $r$, thickness $dr$, and height $h-r$: the volume of such a shell is $dV=2\pi r(h-r)\,dr$, and its contribution to $I_z$ is $r^2\,dm$, giving the same integral. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- Now evaluate the remaining single integral: $$ \int_0^h r^3(h-r)\,dr =\int_0^h (h r^3-r^4)\,dr =\left[\frac{h r^4}{4}-\frac{r^5}{5}\right]_0^h =\frac{h^5}{4}-\frac{h^5}{5} =\frac{h^5}{20}. $$ Thus $$ I_z=2\pi k\cdot\frac{h^5}{20} =\boxed{\frac{\pi k h^5}{10}}. $$ For a check, the mass is $$ M=k\cdot\frac13\pi h^2\cdot h=\frac{\pi k h^3}{3}, $$ so $$ I_z=\frac{3}{10}M h^2, $$ which is the standard formula for a solid cone about its axis.
