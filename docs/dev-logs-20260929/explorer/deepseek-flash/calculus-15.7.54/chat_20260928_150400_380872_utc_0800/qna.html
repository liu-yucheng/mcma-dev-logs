## Average Value of a Function over a Solid Region The average value of $f(x,y,z)$ over a solid region $E$ is $$ f_{\mathrm{ave}}=\frac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV. $$ Here $$ f(x,y,z)=x^2z+y^2z=(x^2+y^2)z. $$ The region $E$ is bounded below by the plane $z=0$ and above by the paraboloid $$ z=1-x^2-y^2. $$ Thus the projection of $E$ onto the $xy$-plane is the unit disk $$ x^2+y^2\le 1, $$ and for each point in that disk, $0\le z\le 1-x^2-y^2$. The region is a solid paraboloid cap. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ## Cylindrical Coordinates Because the region is rotationally symmetric about the $z$-axis, use cylindrical coordinates: $$ x=r\cos\theta,\qquad y=r\sin\theta,\qquad z=z,\qquad dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta. $$ Then $$ x^2+y^2=r^2, $$ and the inequalities become $$ 0\le r\le 1,\qquad 0\le \theta\le 2\pi,\qquad 0\le z\le 1-r^2. $$ So $$ E=\{(r,\theta,z):0\le r\le 1,\ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\ 0\le z\le 1-r^2\}. $$ The cross-section in the $(r,z)$-plane shows exactly how $z$ ranges above each fixed radius $r$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- ## Volume of $E$ $$ \begin{aligned} V(E) &=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1\int_0^{1-r^2} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\\ &=2\pi\int_0^1 r(1-r^2)\,dr\\ &=2\pi\left[\frac{r^2}{2}-\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^1\\ &=2\pi\left(\frac12-\frac14\right)\\ &=\frac{\pi}{2}. \end{aligned} $$ ## Numerator Integral Since $f(x,y,z)=r^2z$, the numerator is $$ \begin{aligned} \iiint_E f\,dV &=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1\int_0^{1-r^2} r^2z\cdot r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\\ &=2\pi\int_0^1 r^3\left[\frac{z^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{1-r^2}dr\\ &=\pi\int_0^1 r^3(1-r^2)^2\,dr\\ &=\pi\int_0^1\left(r^3-2r^5+r^7\right)dr\\ &=\pi\left[\frac{r^4}{4}-\frac{2r^6}{6}+\frac{r^8}{8}\right]_0^1\\ &=\pi\left(\frac14-\frac13+\frac18\right)\\ &=\pi\left(\frac{6}{24}-\frac{8}{24}+\frac{3}{24}\right)\\ &=\frac{\pi}{24}. \end{aligned} $$ ## Average Value $$ \begin{aligned} f_{\mathrm{ave}} &=\frac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f\,dV\\ &=\frac{\pi/24}{\pi/2}\\ &=\frac{1}{12}. \end{aligned} $$ Thus the average value of $f(x,y,z)=x^2z+y^2z$ over the given solid paraboloid is $$ \boxed{\frac{1}{12}}. $$
