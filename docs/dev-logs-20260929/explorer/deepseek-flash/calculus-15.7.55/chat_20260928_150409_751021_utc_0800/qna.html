## Problem **(a)** Find the region $E$ that **maximizes** $\displaystyle\iiint_E \bigl(1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2\bigr)\,dV$. **(b)** Use a CAS to get the exact maximum value. --- ## Part (a) — The maximizing region Write the integrand as a scalar field $$ f(x,y,z)=1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2 . $$ **Key idea (why we keep only where $f\ge 0$).** For any region $E$, split it by the sign of the integrand: $$ E_+ = E\cap\{f\ge 0\},\qquad E_- = E\cap\{f<0\}. $$ Then $$ \iiint_E f\,dV = \underbrace{\iiint_{E_+} f\,dV}_{\ \ge\,0} + \underbrace{\iiint_{E_-} f\,dV}_{\ \le\,0}. $$ - **Remove every point with $f<0$:** the second term is $\le 0$, so deleting $E_-$ can only *increase* the integral. - **Keep every point with $f>0$:** the first term is positive, so adding such a point also *increases* the integral. Hence the integral is largest exactly when $E$ is the set where the integrand is non-negative: $$ E = \{(x,y,z): f(x,y,z)\ge 0\} = \{(x,y,z): x^2+2y^2+3z^2\le 1\}. $$ This is the **solid ellipsoid** centered at the origin with semi-axes $$ a=1\ \text{along }x,\qquad b=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\ \text{along }y,\qquad c=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\ \text{along }z. $$ (The boundary $x^2+2y^2+3z^2=1$ has measure zero, so including it or not does not affect the value.) --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- --- ## Part (b) — The exact maximum value We evaluate $$ V_{\max}=\iiint_E \bigl(1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2\bigr)\,dV,\qquad E:\ x^2+2y^2+3z^2\le 1. $$ **Change of variables to turn the ellipsoid into a ball.** Let $$ x=u,\qquad y=\frac{v}{\sqrt2},\qquad z=\frac{w}{\sqrt3}. $$ Then $x^2+2y^2+3z^2 = u^2+v^2+w^2$, so $E$ maps to the **unit ball** $B:\ u^2+v^2+w^2\le 1$, and the Jacobian is constant: $$ \left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}\right| =1\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt6}. $$ Therefore $$ V_{\max}=\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\iiint_B \bigl(1-u^2-v^2-w^2\bigr)\,dV. $$ **Spherical coordinates** with $u^2+v^2+w^2=r^2$ and $dV=r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$: $$ V_{\max}=\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{1}(1-r^2)\,r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi. $$ The three single integrals separate: $$ \int_0^{1}(r^2-r^4)\,dr=\frac13-\frac15=\frac{2}{15},\qquad \int_0^{\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta=2,\qquad \int_0^{2\pi}d\phi=2\pi. $$ Their product is $\dfrac{2}{15}\cdot 2\cdot 2\pi=\dfrac{8\pi}{15}$, hence $$ V_{\max}=\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\cdot\frac{8\pi}{15} =\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt6} =\frac{8\pi\sqrt6}{15\cdot 6} =\frac{4\pi\sqrt6}{45}\approx 0.684026. $$ **CAS verification.** In Mathematica, for example, 
Integrate[(1 - x^2 - 2 y^2 - 3 z^2), 
  {x, -1, 1}, {y, -Sqrt[1 - x^2]/Sqrt[2], Sqrt[1 - x^2]/Sqrt[2]},
  {z, -Sqrt[1 - x^2 - 2 y^2]/Sqrt[3], Sqrt[1 - x^2 - 2 y^2]/Sqrt[3]}]
returns exactly $\dfrac{4\pi\sqrt6}{45}$, agreeing with the hand computation (numerically $0.684026576\ldots$). --- ## Answers **(a)** $E$ is the ellipsoid $\;x^2+2y^2+3z^2\le 1$. **(b)** The exact maximum value is $$ \boxed{\dfrac{4\pi\sqrt6}{45}} $$
