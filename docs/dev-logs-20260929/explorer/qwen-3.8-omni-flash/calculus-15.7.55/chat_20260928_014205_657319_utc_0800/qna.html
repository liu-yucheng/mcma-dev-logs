# Triple Integral Maximization (Stewart, *Calculus* 15.7, Ex. 55) Choose the region $E$ so that $$ \iiint_E \left(1-x^{2}-2 y^{2}-3 z^{2}\right) d V $$ is as **large** as possible, then compute that largest value. --- ## Part (a) — Finding the region $E$ ### Step 1: The governing principle Write $f(x,y,z)=1-x^{2}-2 y^{2}-3 z^{2}$. Since $dV>0$, every point of $E$ contributes $f\,dV$ and contributions simply add. Hence, **point by point**: - $f(P)>0$: putting $P$ **in** $E$ raises the integral — leaving it out forfeits free positive mass. - $f(P)<0$: putting $P$ **in** $E$ lowers the integral — it must stay **out**. - $f(P)=0$: irrelevant, a surface has volume $0$. So the maximizer is forced, with no Lagrange multipliers or calculus of variations needed: > **Take $E$ to be exactly the set on which the integrand is non-negative.** Optimality here is *local* — the sign of $f$ at each point settles that point's fate. That is the whole trick of this exercise. ### Step 2: Solve the inequality $f\ge 0$ $$ 1-x^{2}-2 y^{2}-3 z^{2} \ge 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{2}+2 y^{2}+3 z^{2} \le 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{x^{2}}{1}+\frac{y^{2}}{1/2}+\frac{z^{2}}{1/3} \le 1 $$ a **solid ellipsoid** centered at the origin with semi-axes $$ a=1,\qquad b=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\approx0.7071,\qquad c=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}\approx0.5774 . $$ Pattern to remember: **the larger a variable's coefficient in $f$, the shorter the semi-axis in that direction**, since that term spends the budget of $1$ fastest. ### Step 3: The answer to (a), and its volume $$ E=\left\{(x, y, z) \mid x^{2}+2 y^{2}+3 z^{2} \leq 1\right\}, \qquad \operatorname{Vol}(E)=\frac{4\pi}{3}abc=\frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt6}=\frac{2\sqrt6\,\pi}{9}\approx 1.7101 . $$ The plot below exhibits the mechanism in the slice $z=0$: the red zone $f>0$ is exactly the interior of the ellipse $x^{2}+2y^{2}=1$ (which is $\partial E$ there), the blue zone $f<0$ is its exterior, and the dotted unit circle shows how the coefficients squeeze the figure toward the $z$-axis. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- --- ## Part (b) — The exact maximum value ### Step 1: Adapt coordinates to the ellipsoid (the key technique) Use **generalized spherical coordinates** matched to the level sets of $f$: $$ x=r\sin\phi\cos\theta,\qquad y=\frac{r}{\sqrt2}\sin\phi\sin\theta,\qquad z=\frac{r}{\sqrt3}\cos\phi,\qquad 0\le r\le1,\;0\le\phi\le\pi,\;0\le\theta\le2\pi . $$ Then $$ x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2} =r^{2}\sin^{2}\phi\cos^{2}\theta+r^{2}\sin^{2}\phi\sin^{2}\theta+r^{2}\cos^{2}\phi=r^{2}, $$ so $E$ becomes the rectangular box $[0,1]\times[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi]$ **and** the integrand collapses to $1-r^{2}$, a function of $r$ alone. That is the payoff of choosing coordinates aligned with the geometry. ### Step 2: The Jacobian This map is the ordinary spherical map composed with the diagonal scaling $\operatorname{diag}\!\big(1,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2},\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\big)$, whose determinant is $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt6}$. Therefore $$ \left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(r,\phi,\theta)}\right| =\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\,r^{2}\sin\phi =\frac{\sqrt6}{6}\,r^{2}\sin\phi $$ (confirmed symbolically: $|J|=\sqrt6\,r^{2}\sin\phi/6$). ### Step 3: The integral separates into three elementary factors $$ \iiint_E (1-x^{2}-2y^{2}-3z^{2})\,dV =\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\int_0^{2\pi}\!d\theta\int_0^{\pi}\!\sin\phi\,d\phi\int_0^{1}(1-r^{2})\,r^{2}\,dr $$ $$ \int_0^{2\pi}d\theta=2\pi,\qquad \int_0^{\pi}\sin\phi\,d\phi=\big[-\cos\phi\big]_0^{\pi}=2,\qquad \int_0^{1}(r^{2}-r^{4})\,dr=\Big[\tfrac{r^{3}}{3}-\tfrac{r^{5}}{5}\Big]_0^{1}=\frac13-\frac15=\frac{2}{15}. $$ Multiplying (angular product $2\pi\cdot2=4\pi$): $$ \iiint_E (1-x^{2}-2y^{2}-3z^{2})\,dV =\frac{1}{\sqrt6}\cdot4\pi\cdot\frac{2}{15} =\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt6} =\frac{8\sqrt6\,\pi}{90} =\boxed{\dfrac{4\sqrt6\,\pi}{45}}\approx0.68403 . $$ ### Step 4: Cross-checks (three independent routes agree) 1. **Shell method.** The sub-level set $\{x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}\le t^{2}\}$ has volume $V(t)=\frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt6}t^{3}$, so $dV=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt6}t^{2}\,dt$ and $$\int_0^1(1-t^{2})\,\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt6}t^{2}\,dt=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt6}\cdot\frac{2}{15}=\frac{4\sqrt6\,\pi}{45}.\ \checkmark$$ 2. **Brute-force Cartesian** iterated integral $\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-\sqrt{(1-x^2)/2}}^{\sqrt{(1-x^2)/2}}\!\int_{-\sqrt{(1-x^2-2y^2)/3}}^{\sqrt{(1-x^2-2y^2)/3}} f\,dz\,dy\,dx$ evaluated by Gauss–Legendre: $0.6840265742$, matching to $\sim2\times10^{-9}$. $\checkmark$ 3. **CAS** (SymPy `integrate` in the Step-1 coordinates) returns $4\sqrt6\,\pi/45$ exactly. $\checkmark$ ### Step 5: Confirming a maximum, not just a stationary value Scanning the one-parameter family $E_s=\{x^{2}+2y^{2}+3z^{2}\le s^{2}\}$ gives $$ I(s)=\int_0^{s}(1-t^{2})\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt6}t^{2}\,dt =\frac{2\sqrt6\,\pi}{9}s^{3}-\frac{2\sqrt6\,\pi}{15}s^{5}, \qquad I'(s)=-\frac{2\sqrt6\,\pi}{3}s^{2}(s-1)(s+1). $$ For $s>0$ the only critical point is $s=1$, with $I'>0$ on $(0,1)$ and $I'<0$ on $(1,\infty)$; indeed $I''(1)=-\frac{4\sqrt6\,\pi}{3}<0$. The peak sits exactly on the zero level set of $f$ — an independent confirmation of part (a). The graph below shows the trade-off: shrink $E$ and you discard positive shells; grow $E$ and you swallow negative ones (by $s\approx1.22$ the integral is already negative). --- ## Final answers **(a)** $\;E=\left\{(x, y, z) \mid x^{2}+2 y^{2}+3 z^{2} \leq 1\right\}$ — the solid ellipsoid with semi-axes $1,\;1/\sqrt2,\;1/\sqrt3$ and volume $2\sqrt6\,\pi/9$. **(b)** $\displaystyle\max\ \iiint_E (1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2)\,dV=\frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt6}=\frac{4\sqrt6\,\pi}{45}\approx0.68403$. ### Take-away for this problem type When a multiple integral asks you to *choose the region* that maximizes (or minimizes) its value, do not set up an optimization: inspect the **sign of the integrand**. Maximizing $\Rightarrow$ take $\{f\ge0\}$; minimizing $\Rightarrow$ take $\{f\le0\}$. The optimal boundary is always a level surface $f=0$ of the integrand. Then evaluate with coordinates adapted to that level surface (here, ellipsoidal / scaled-spherical), which typically makes the integral separate. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_2.tex)
