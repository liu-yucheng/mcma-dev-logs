## Problem Use cylindrical coordinates. **(a)** Find the volume of the solid that the cylinder $r=a\cos\theta$ cuts out of the sphere of radius $a$ centered at the origin. **(b)** Illustrate the solid by graphing the sphere and the cylinder on the same screen. --- ## Step 1 — Translate the two surfaces | surface | equation | role | |---|---|---| | sphere, radius $a$ centred at $O$ | $x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2\iff \rho^2=r^2+z^2=a^2$ | top/bottom: $z=\pm\sqrt{a^2-r^2}$ | | circular cylinder | $r=a\cos\theta\iff x^2+y^2=ax\iff \left(x-\tfrac a2\right)^2+y^2=\left(\tfrac a2\right)^2$ | lateral wall (radius $a/2$, axis through $(a/2,0)$) | Because $r=a\cos\theta$ is traced exactly once for $-\tfrac\pi2\le\theta\le\tfrac\pi2$ (and $r\ge0$ requires $\cos\theta\ge0$), the solid is $$ W=\left\{(r,\theta,z):\ -\tfrac{\pi}{2}\le\theta\le\tfrac{\pi}{2},\quad 0\le r\le a\cos\theta,\quad -\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\le z\le \sqrt{a^2-r^2}\right\} $$ **Key check:** the cylinder lies *entirely inside* the disk $r\le a$, since $a\cos\theta\le a$. So the radial upper limit $a\cos\theta$ never overshoots the sphere's equator (equality only at $\theta=0$) — no extra case splitting is needed. This is *Viviani's solid*. --- ## Step 2 — The base region $D$ (2-D picture) Projecting $W$ onto the $xy$-plane gives $D=\{\,0\le r\le a\cos\theta,\ -\tfrac\pi2\le\theta\le\tfrac\pi2\,\}$: the orange disk of radius $a/2$ centred at $(a/2,0)$, internally tangent to the circle $r=a$ (the sphere's shadow) at $(a,0)$. The red rays show the order of integration: for each fixed $\theta$, $r$ runs from the origin out to the cylinder wall $r=a\cos\theta$; then $\theta$ sweeps $-\tfrac\pi2\to\tfrac\pi2$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ## Step 3 — Set up the triple integral With $dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$ and the symmetry $z\mapsto -z$: $$ V=\iiint_W dV=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta}\!\int_{-\sqrt{a^2-r^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2-r^2}} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta =\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\;dr\,d\theta $$ ### Inner integral ($z$) $$\int_{-\sqrt{a^2-r^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2-r^2}}dz=2\sqrt{a^2-r^2}$$ ### Middle integral ($r$) — substitute $u=a^2-r^2$, $du=-2r\,dr$ $$ \int_{0}^{a\cos\theta}2r\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\,dr =\Big[-\tfrac{2}{3}\big(a^2-r^2\big)^{3/2}\Big]_{0}^{a\cos\theta} =\tfrac{2}{3}a^3-\tfrac{2}{3}\big(a^2-a^2\cos^2\theta\big)^{3/2} $$ **Careful with the $3/2$ power:** $\big(a^2\sin^2\theta\big)^{3/2}=a^3|\sin\theta|^3$, *not* $a^3\sin^3\theta$. This absolute value is the one trap in the problem. Hence $$ \int_{0}^{a\cos\theta}2r\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\,dr=\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\Big(1-|\sin\theta|^{3}\Big) $$ ### Outer integral ($\theta$) — the integrand is even $$ V=\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\bigl(1-|\sin\theta|^{3}\bigr)d\theta =\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\left[\pi-2\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{3}\theta\,d\theta\right] $$ using $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\theta\,d\theta=\Big[-\cos\theta+\tfrac{\cos^3\theta}{3}\Big]_0^{\pi/2}=\frac{2}{3}$ (from $\sin^3\theta=\sin\theta(1-\cos^2\theta)$, then $u=\cos\theta$). $$ V=\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\left(\pi-\frac{4}{3}\right)=\frac{2a^{3}}{9}\,(3\pi-4) $$ --- ## Step 4 — Verification * Symbolic (SymPy): $\ \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\frac{2a^3}{3}\bigl(1-|\sin\theta|^3\bigr)d\theta=\frac{2a^3(3\pi-4)}{9}$ ✔ (pieces: $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}d\theta=\pi$, $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}|\sin\theta|^3d\theta=\frac43$) * Independent nested quadrature at $a=1$: $V=1.2055036$ versus formula $\frac{2}{9}(3\pi-4)=1.2055062$ ✔ * Monte-Carlo on the raw solid $\{x^2+y^2+z^2\le a^2\}\cap\{x^2+y^2\le ax\}$, $3\times10^6$ points: $V=1.2062\pm0.0023$ ✔ * Sanity: $V\big/\bigl(\tfrac43\pi a^3\bigr)\approx0.288$, i.e. the cylinder removes just under $29\%$ of the ball — plausible for a cylinder of half the radius pushed against the boundary. * Trap check: dropping the absolute value would give $\frac{2\pi a^3}{3}\approx2.094\,a^3$, which is *exactly half* the ball's volume $\frac43\pi a^3$ — $73\%$ too large, and a red flag that the odd function $\sin^3\theta$ was integrated symmetrically to $0$ by accident. --- ## Step 5 — Part (b): the solid on screen The 3-D picture puts both surfaces in one frame: the transparent blue sphere $\rho=a$, the translucent orange cylinder $r=a\cos\theta$ (its vertical generators and its equatorial cross-section drawn darker), and — in green — the actual solid, bounded laterally by the cylinder wall and above/below by the spherical caps $z=\pm\sqrt{a^2-r^2}$. The two red curves are the **Viviani windows**: substitute $r=a\cos\theta$ into $z^2=a^2-r^2$ to get $$ x=a\cos^2\theta,\qquad y=a\sin\theta\cos\theta,\qquad z=\pm\,a|\sin\theta|,\qquad -\tfrac\pi2\le\theta\le\tfrac\pi2 $$ Landmarks (verified numerically, $a=1$): * $\theta=0$: $(a,0,0)$ — the point where the cylinder is tangent to the sphere's equator, and the windows dip to $z=0$ there. * $\theta=\pm\tfrac\pi2$: $(0,0,\pm a)$ — both branches of a window meet on the $z$-axis at the top/bottom of the sphere; this is the **double (self-intersection) point** of each figure-eight curve. * $\theta=\pm\tfrac\pi3$: $\bigl(\tfrac a4,\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3 a}{4},\pm\tfrac{\sqrt3 a}{2}\bigr)$, and every point satisfies $r^2+z^2=a^2$ exactly, confirming the curve lies on both surfaces. Each window is a figure eight on the cylinder, and the two windows (upper and lower) pinched at $(0,0,\pm a)$ are exactly the boundary of the green solid. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- ## Summary — how to attack this kind of problem 1. **Rewrite every surface in $(r,\theta,z)$.** $r=a\cos\theta$ is a *cylinder* (complete the square: $(x-a/2)^2+y^2=(a/2)^2$), not a sphere; $\rho=a$ gives $z=\pm\sqrt{a^2-r^2}$. 2. **Project to the plane to get the $\theta$- and $r$-limits.** Here $r\ge0$ forces $\cos\theta\ge0$, so $-\tfrac\pi2\le\theta\le\tfrac\pi2$ and $0\le r\le a\cos\theta$. Check that the inner wall really lies inside the outer surface ($a\cos\theta\le a$) so no case split is needed. 3. **Integrate in the order $dz$, then $dr$, then $d\theta$** — $z$ first uses the $\pm$ symmetry, $r$ then yields to $u=a^2-r^2$. 4. **Watch $(a^2\sin^2\theta)^{3/2}=a^3|\sin\theta|^3$.** Keep the absolute value, then split/even-out the $\theta$-integral. 5. **Use evenness** in $\theta$ to reduce to $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\theta\,d\theta=\tfrac23$. 6. **Sanity-check** against the enclosing ball's volume $\tfrac43\pi a^3$, and against a numerical or Monte-Carlo evaluation. ### Final answer, part (a) $$ \boxed{V=\dfrac{2a^{3}}{9}\,(3\pi-4)=\dfrac{2a^{3}}{3}\left(\pi-\dfrac{4}{3}\right)\approx 1.2055\,a^{3}} $$ Part (b) is illustrated by the graphs above: the sphere $\rho=a$ (blue, transparent) and the cylinder $r=a\cos\theta$ (orange) on the same screen, with Viviani's solid (green) trapped between the caps $z=\pm\sqrt{a^2-r^2}$ and the red Viviani windows.
