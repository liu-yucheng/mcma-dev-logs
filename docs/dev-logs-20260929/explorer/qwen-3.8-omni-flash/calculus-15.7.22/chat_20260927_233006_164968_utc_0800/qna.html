## Problem Use a triple integral to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the cylinder $x^{2}+z^{2}=4$ and the planes $y=-1$ and $y+z=4$. ## Step 1 — Understand the region The cylinder $x^{2}+z^{2}=4$ has its axis along the **$y$-axis** (the variable $y$ is missing), so it is a tube of radius $2$ running in the $y$-direction. The two planes $$y=-1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad y=4-z$$ cap the tube at its two ends. Since the tube bounds the solid "sideways", the natural projection is **onto the $xz$-plane**, not the $xy$-plane. So the solid is $$E=\Big\{(x,y,z)\;:\;(x,z)\in D,\ \ -1\le y\le 4-z\Big\},\qquad D=\{(x,z):\ x^{2}+z^{2}\le 4\}.$$ **Check that the caps do not cross inside the tube.** On $D$ we have $-2\le z\le 2$, hence $$(4-z)-(-1)=5-z\ \ge\ 5-2=3>0,$$ so $y=-1$ is genuinely the lower bound everywhere on $D$: the shortest column has length $3$ (at $z=2$) and the tallest has length $7$ (at $z=-2$). --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- The 3D picture above shows the flat circular face $y=-1$ (green), the slanted elliptical face $y=4-z$ (orange), and the tube wall joining them (blue). ## Step 2 — Set up the triple integral Integrating with respect to $y$ first, $$V=\iiint_E 1\,dV=\iint_{D}\left[\int_{-1}^{\,4-z} dy\right] dA=\iint_{D}(5-z)\,dA .$$ In Cartesian form, with $-2\le x\le 2$ and $-\sqrt{4-x^{2}}\le z\le\sqrt{4-x^{2}}$: $$V=\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}^{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}\int_{-1}^{\,4-z} dy\,dz\,dx .$$ ## Step 3 — Evaluate with polar coordinates in the $xz$-plane Set $x=r\cos\theta,\ z=r\sin\theta,\ dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$, with $0\le r\le 2,\ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi$: $$V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\int_{0}^{2}\bigl(5-r\sin\theta\bigr)\,r\,dr\,d\theta =\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\int_{0}^{2}\bigl(5r-r^{2}\sin\theta\bigr)\,dr\,d\theta .$$ Inner integral in $r$: $$\int_{0}^{2}\bigl(5r-r^{2}\sin\theta\bigr)dr =\left[\ \frac{5r^{2}}{2}-\frac{r^{3}}{3}\sin\theta\ \right]_{0}^{2} =10-\frac{8}{3}\sin\theta .$$ Outer integral in $\theta$ (the $\sin\theta$ term contributes nothing over a full period): $$V=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(10-\frac{8}{3}\sin\theta\right)d\theta =20\pi-\frac{8}{3}\Bigl[-\cos\theta\Bigr]_{0}^{2\pi} =20\pi-0=20\pi .$$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- ## Step 4 — Why the answer is so clean (symmetry sanity check) Split the integral: $$V=\iint_{D}5\,dA-\iint_{D}z\,dA .$$ The disk $D$ is symmetric about the line $z=0$ while $z$ is odd in $z$, so $\iint_{D} z\,dA=0$. Therefore $$V=5\cdot\mathrm{Area}(D)=5\cdot\pi(2)^{2}=20\pi .$$ Equivalently: the *average* column height over $D$ is $5-\bar z=5-0=5$, the height of the column through the centre — so the solid has the same volume as the right cylinder of radius $2$ and height $5$. **Numeric check.** Symbolic integration in Cartesian coordinates returns exactly $20\pi$; a Monte-Carlo estimate over the box $[-2,2]\times[-1,6]\times[-2,2]$ with $4\times10^{6}$ random points gives $62.80$, against $20\pi=62.8319$ — agreement within sampling error. ## Answer $$\boxed{20\pi}$$
