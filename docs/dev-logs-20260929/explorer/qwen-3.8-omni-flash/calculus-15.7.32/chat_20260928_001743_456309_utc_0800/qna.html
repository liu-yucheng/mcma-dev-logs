# Writing $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ in Six Orders **Surfaces:** $x=2,\quad y=2,\quad z=0,\quad x+y-2z=2$. ## Step 1 — Identify the solid $E$ The slanted plane is most useful solved for each variable in turn: $$ z=\frac{x+y-2}{2},\qquad x=2+2z-y,\qquad y=2+2z-x . $$ $E$ is cut out by four planes, so it is a **tetrahedron**. Its four vertices are the intersections of triples of planes: | planes used | vertex | |---|---| | $x=2,\ y=2,\ z=0$ | $(2,2,0)$ | | $x=2,\ y=2,\ x+y-2z=2\;\Rightarrow\;4-2z=2$ | $(2,2,1)$ | | $x=2,\ z=0,\ x+y-2z=2\;\Rightarrow\;y=0$ | $(2,0,0)$ | | $y=2,\ z=0,\ x+y-2z=2\;\Rightarrow\;x=0$ | $(0,2,0)$ | The solid lies **above** $z=0$, **below** the slant plane, and behind the planes $x=2$ and $y=2$; i.e. $$ E=\left\{(x,y,z)\;:\; z\ge 0,\ \ x\le 2,\ \ y\le 2,\ \ x+y-2z\ge 2\right\} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\le z\le \frac{x+y-2}{2}. $$ Note $z\ge 0$ together with $z\le (x+y-2)/2$ forces $x+y\ge 2$ — that single inequality is what makes every shadow a *triangle* rather than a square. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ## Step 2 — The general recipe For each of the $3! = 6$ orders $d\text{(1st)}\,d\text{(2nd)}\,d\text{(3rd)}$: 1. **Innermost** limits come from the two surfaces *parallel to the projection direction* — here one limit is always a coordinate plane ($x=2$, $y=2$ or $z=0$) and the other is the slant plane solved for that variable. 2. **Outer two** limits come from the *shadow* (projection) of $E$ on the corresponding coordinate plane — always a triangle here, so you write it twice (Type I and Type II description) to get the two orders. ## Step 3 — The three shadows * **On the $xy$-plane** (project along $z$): need $0\le \tfrac{x+y-2}{2}$, plus $x\le2,\ y\le2$ $$D_{xy}=\{\,2-x\le y\le 2,\ 0\le x\le 2\,\}=\{\,2-y\le x\le 2,\ 0\le y\le 2\,\}.$$ * **On the $xz$-plane** (project along $y$): need $2+2z-x\le 2\iff z\le x/2$, plus $x\le2,\ z\ge0$ $$D_{xz}=\{\,0\le z\le x/2,\ 0\le x\le 2\,\}=\{\,0\le x\le 2z,\ 0\le z\le 1\,\}.$$ * **On the $yz$-plane** (project along $x$): need $2+2z-y\le 2\iff z\le y/2$, plus $y\le2,\ z\ge0$ $$D_{yz}=\{\,0\le z\le y/2,\ 0\le y\le 2\,\}=\{\,0\le y\le 2z,\ 0\le z\le 1\,\}.$$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- ## Step 4 — Assemble the six iterated integrals **Order $dz\,dy\,dx$** (shadow $D_{xy}$, $x$ outer): $z$ runs from $0$ up to the slant plane, $$\int_0^2\!\int_{2-x}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx$$ **Order $dz\,dx\,dy$** (same shadow, $y$ outer): $$\int_0^2\!\int_{2-y}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy$$ **Order $dy\,dz\,dx$** (shadow $D_{xz}$, $x$ outer): $y$ runs from the slant plane up to $y=2$, $$\int_0^2\!\int_{0}^{x/2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx$$ **Order $dy\,dx\,dz$** (same shadow, $z$ outer): $$\int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz$$ **Order $dx\,dz\,dy$** (shadow $D_{yz}$, $y$ outer): $x$ runs from the slant plane up to $x=2$, $$\int_0^2\!\int_{0}^{y/2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy$$ **Order $dx\,dy\,dz$** (same shadow, $z$ outer): $$\int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz$$ ## Step 5 — Verification (done symbolically) * With $f\equiv 1$, **each** of the six integrals evaluates to $\tfrac23$, which equals the tetrahedron volume $\tfrac16\lvert\det(B-A,\,C-A,\,D-A)\rvert=\tfrac23$. ✔ * With a deliberately non-symmetric test integrand $f=x+2yz+3x^2y$, all six orders give the **same** value $\tfrac{127}{15}$, confirming the limits describe the same region. ✔ ## Final answer $$ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &\text{(i)}\ \ \int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{2-x}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f\,dz\,dy\,dx &&\text{(ii)}\ \ \int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{2-y}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f\,dz\,dx\,dy\\[9pt] &\text{(iii)}\ \ \int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f\,dy\,dz\,dx &&\text{(iv)}\ \ \int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f\,dy\,dx\,dz\\[9pt] &\text{(v)}\ \ \int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\frac{y}{2}}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f\,dx\,dz\,dy &&\text{(vi)}\ \ \int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f\,dx\,dy\,dz \end{aligned}} $$ *(Each of the six equals $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$; e.g. all give $2/3$ for $f=1$.)* **Take-away for this kind of problem:** sketch the solid and find its vertices, solve the oblique plane for each variable, project onto the three coordinate planes to get triangular shadows, then write each shadow in both Type-I and Type-II form — that yields all six orders systematically.
