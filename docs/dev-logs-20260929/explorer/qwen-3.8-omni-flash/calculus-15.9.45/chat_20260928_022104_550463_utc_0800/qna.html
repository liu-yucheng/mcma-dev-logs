## Tumor model: volume of the bumpy sphere $\rho=1+\tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi$ ### Step 1 — Describe the solid $E$ in spherical coordinates Because $\left|\tfrac15\sin 6\theta\sin 5\phi\right|\le \tfrac15$, the radius satisfies $0.8\le\rho\le 1.2>0$ everywhere: the surface is star-shaped about the origin, so every ray $\theta=\text{const}$, $\phi=\text{const}$ meets it exactly once. Hence $$ E=\left\{(\rho,\theta,\phi)\;\middle|\;0\le \rho\le 1+\tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi,\; 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\; 0\le\phi\le\pi\right\}. $$ With $dV=\rho^{2}\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi$, $$ V=\iiint_E dV=\int_0^{\pi}\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^{\,1+\frac15\sin 6\theta\sin 5\phi}\rho^{2}\sin\phi\;d\rho\,d\theta\,d\phi . $$ *Teaching point:* for a surface given as $\rho=f(\theta,\phi)$ with $f>0$, always integrate $\rho$ from $0$ to $f$ first — the Jacobian $\rho^2\sin\phi$ is what turns the radial profile into a volume. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ### Step 2 — The $\rho$-integration $$ \int_0^{R}\rho^{2}\sin\phi\,d\rho=\frac{R^{3}}{3}\,\sin\phi,\qquad R(\theta,\phi)=1+\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon=\tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi , $$ so that $$ V=\frac13\int_0^{\pi}\!\int_0^{2\pi}(1+\varepsilon)^{3}\sin\phi\;d\theta\,d\phi . $$ ### Step 3 — The $\theta$-integration (odd harmonics die) Expand $(1+\varepsilon)^3=1+3\varepsilon+3\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}$ and use, over a full period, $$ \int_0^{2\pi}\sin 6\theta\,d\theta=0,\qquad \int_0^{2\pi}\sin^{2}6\theta\,d\theta=\pi,\qquad \int_0^{2\pi}\sin^{3}6\theta\,d\theta=0 . $$ The linear and cubic terms vanish (they are odd in $\sin 6\theta$), leaving $$ \int_0^{2\pi}(1+\varepsilon)^{3}d\theta=2\pi+3\cdot\frac{1}{25}\sin^{2}5\phi\cdot\pi=2\pi\left(1+\frac{3}{50}\sin^{2}5\phi\right). $$ So **all** the $\theta$-bumpiness contributes only through the mean square $\langle \sin^2 6\theta\rangle=\tfrac12$; bumps and dents cancel at first order (see panel (a) of the next figure). --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- ### Step 4 — The $\phi$-integration $$ V=\frac{2\pi}{3}\int_0^{\pi}\left(1+\frac{3}{50}\sin^{2}5\phi\right)\sin\phi\;d\phi=\frac{2\pi}{3}\left[\,\underbrace{\int_0^{\pi}\sin\phi\,d\phi}_{=\,2}+\frac{3}{50}\underbrace{\int_0^{\pi}\sin^{2}5\phi\,\sin\phi\;d\phi}_{=\;I}\right]. $$ Evaluate $I$ with the power-reduction identity $\sin^{2}5\phi=\tfrac{1-\cos 10\phi}{2}$: $$ I=1-J,\qquad J=\int_0^{\pi}\cos 10\phi\,\sin\phi\;d\phi . $$ Product-to-sum, $\cos 10\phi\,\sin\phi=\tfrac12\left[\sin 11\phi-\sin 9\phi\right]$, and $\int_0^{\pi}\sin(k\phi)\,d\phi=\frac{2}{k}$ for odd $k$: $$ J=\frac12\left(\frac{2}{11}-\frac{2}{9}\right)=\frac{1}{11}-\frac{1}{9}=-\frac{2}{99}\;\Longrightarrow\; I=1+\frac{2}{99}=\frac{100}{99}. $$ *(The five dotted lines in panel (b) are the saddle rings $\sin 5\phi=0$; the small negative value of $J$ is why $I>1$.)* ### Step 5 — Assemble $$ V=\frac{2\pi}{3}\left(2+\frac{3}{50}\cdot\frac{100}{99}\right)=\frac{2\pi}{3}\left(2+\frac{2}{33}\right)=\frac{2\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{68}{33}=\frac{136\pi}{99}\approx 4.31572 . $$ Sanity check: the unit ball has volume $\frac{4}{3}\pi=\frac{132}{99}\pi$, so the tumor is bigger by exactly $\frac{4\pi}{99}\approx 0.1269$ — the shaded excess area in panel (b). The perturbation adds volume only at second order, which is why a lumpy surface with equal bumps and dents still encloses **more** than the sphere. ### CAS confirmation (Mathematica / SymPy)

Independent numerical quadrature of $\frac13\int_0^{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}R^3\sin\phi\,d\theta\,d\phi$ gives $4.3157230$ versus $\frac{136\pi}{99}=4.3157232$ (relative error $5\times10^{-8}$). $$ \boxed{V=\dfrac{136}{99}\,\pi\approx 4.3157} $$ --- ### Method summary for this kind of problem 1. **Check star-shapedness:** if $\rho=f(\theta,\phi)>0$ on the whole rectangle $[0,2\pi]\times[0,\pi]$, then $0\le\rho\le f$ describes the interior and no splitting of the region is needed. 2. **Order of integration:** $\rho$ first ($\Rightarrow f^3/3$), then the angular variables. Only $\sin\phi$ (never $\sin\theta$) belongs to the Jacobian. 3. **Harmonic bookkeeping:** over a full period all odd powers of $\sin(m\theta)$ or $\sin(n\phi)$ integrate to $0$; even powers reduce via $\sin^2 u=\tfrac{1-\cos 2u}{2}$. 4. **Last one-dimensional integral:** products $\cos(k\phi)\sin\phi$ are handled by product-to-sum, using $\int_0^{\pi}\sin(j\phi)\,d\phi=2/j$ for odd $j$, $0$ for even $j$. 5. **Estimate before trusting:** compare with $\frac43\pi$ (unit ball). The relative correction is of order $\tfrac32\langle\varepsilon^2\rangle/2\sim\tfrac{1}{50}$, exactly the $\frac{4\pi}{99}$ excess found here.
