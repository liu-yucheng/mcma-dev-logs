## Volume by a triple integral The solid is bounded by the parabolic cylinder $y=x^2$, the plane $z=0$ (bottom), and the plane $y+z=1$, i.e. $z=1-y$ (top). In the $xy$-plane, the relevant region is where the top surface is above the bottom surface: $$ 0 \le z \le 1-y,\qquad x^2 \le y \le 1. $$ The condition $x^2\le y\le 1$ forces $x^2\le 1$, so $-1\le x\le 1$. Thus the volume is $$ V=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{x^2}^{1}\int_{0}^{1-y}1\,dz\,dy\,dx =\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{x^2}^{1}(1-y)\,dy\,dx. $$ Evaluate the inner integral: $$ \int_{x^2}^{1}(1-y)\,dy = \left[y-\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_{x^2}^{1} = \frac12-x^2+\frac{x^4}{2}. $$ Therefore $$ V=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\frac12-x^2+\frac{x^4}{2}\right)dx. $$ The integrand is even, so $$ V=2\int_0^1\left(\frac12-x^2+\frac{x^4}{2}\right)dx =2\left[\frac{x}{2}-\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{x^5}{10}\right]_0^1. $$ Hence $$ V=2\left(\frac12-\frac13+\frac1{10}\right) =2\left(\frac{15-10+3}{30}\right) =2\cdot\frac{8}{30} =\frac{8}{15}. $$ Equivalently, integrating in the order $dz\,dx\,dy$ gives $$ V=\int_0^1\int_{-\sqrt y}^{\sqrt y}(1-y)\,dx\,dy =\int_0^12\sqrt y(1-y)\,dy =2\left(\frac23-\frac25\right) =\frac{8}{15}. $$ $$ \boxed{\dfrac{8}{15}} $$ --- No graphical results.
