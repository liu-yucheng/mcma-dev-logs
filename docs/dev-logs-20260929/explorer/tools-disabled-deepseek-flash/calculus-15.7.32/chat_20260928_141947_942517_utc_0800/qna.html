# Expressing $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ in six ways ## 1. Identify the solid $E$ The solid is bounded by the four planes $$x=2,\qquad y=2,\qquad z=0,\qquad x+y-2z=2.$$ Intersecting **triples** of these planes gives the vertices (each vertex lies on 3 of the 4 planes): - $x=2,\ y=2,\ z=0$ $\Rightarrow$ $(2,2,0)$ - $x=2,\ y=2,\ x+y-2z=2$ $\Rightarrow$ $(2,2,1)$ - $x=2,\ z=0,\ x+y-2z=2$ $\Rightarrow$ $(2,0,0)$ - $y=2,\ z=0,\ x+y-2z=2$ $\Rightarrow$ $(0,2,0)$ So $E$ is the **tetrahedron** with vertices $(2,2,0),(2,2,1),(2,0,0),(0,2,0)$. Testing the centroid $\left(\tfrac32,\tfrac32,\tfrac14\right)$ against each plane fixes the inequalities: $$x\le 2,\qquad y\le 2,\qquad z\ge 0,\qquad x+y-2z\ge 2.$$ Therefore $$E=\Big\{(x,y,z):x\le2,\ y\le2,\ z\ge0,\ x+y-2z\ge2\Big\} =\Big\{(x,y,z):0\le z\le \tfrac{x+y-2}{2},\ 0\le x\le2,\ 0\le y\le2,\ x+y\ge2\Big\}.$$ ## 2. Useful projections (the key to switching order) | project onto | shadow region | two descriptions | |---|---|---| | $xy$-plane | triangle $(2,2),(2,0),(0,2)$ | $0\le x\le2,\ 2-x\le y\le2$ or $0\le y\le2,\ 2-y\le x\le2$ | | $xz$-plane | triangle $(0,0),(2,0),(2,1)$ | $0\le x\le2,\ 0\le z\le \tfrac{x}{2}$ or $0\le z\le1,\ 2z\le x\le2$ | | $yz$-plane | triangle $(0,0),(2,0),(2,1)$ | $0\le y\le2,\ 0\le z\le \tfrac{y}{2}$ or $0\le z\le1,\ 2z\le y\le2$ | Whenever $z$ is the **innermost** variable, it runs from the floor $z=0$ up to the slanted wall: $$0\le z\le \frac{x+y-2}{2}.$$ Whenever $z$ is **innermost with $x,y$ given**, and when $x$ or $y$ is innermost, the other lower limit comes from solving the slant plane $x+y-2z=2$: $$x\ge 2+2z-y,\qquad y\ge 2+2z-x.$$ ## 3. Worked example (order $dz\,dy\,dx$) Keep $x$ outermost. A vertical line at fixed $x\in[0,2]$ enters the $xy$-shadow at $y=2-x$ and leaves at $y=2$; then $z$ runs $0\to\tfrac{x+y-2}{2}$: $$\int_0^2\!\int_{2-x}^{2}\!\int_0^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx.$$ ## 4. The six iterated integrals **$(i)$ order $dz\,dy\,dx$** $$\int_0^2\!\int_{2-x}^{2}\!\int_0^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx$$ **$(ii)$ order $dz\,dx\,dy$** $$\int_0^2\!\int_{2-y}^{2}\!\int_0^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy$$ **$(iii)$ order $dy\,dz\,dx$** $$\int_0^2\!\int_0^{x/2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx$$ **$(iv)$ order $dx\,dz\,dy$** $$\int_0^2\!\int_0^{y/2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy$$ **$(v)$ order $dy\,dx\,dz$** $$\int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz$$ **$(vi)$ order $dx\,dy\,dz$** $$\int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz$$ ## 5. Verification Setting $f\equiv1$ must give the tetrahedron's volume for **every** order. Using the edge vectors $(0,0,1),(0,-2,0),(-2,0,0)$ at the vertex $(2,2,0)$, the volume is $\tfrac16|{\det}|=\tfrac16(4)=\tfrac23$. Direct evaluation confirms this for all six: - (i): $\int_0^2\!\int_{2-x}^2 \tfrac{x+y-2}{2}\,dy\,dx=\int_0^2\tfrac{x^2}{4}\,dx=\tfrac23$ - (iii): $\int_0^2\!\int_0^{x/2}(x-2z)\,dz\,dx=\int_0^2\tfrac{x^2}{4}\,dx=\tfrac23$ - (v): $\int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}(x-2z)\,dx\,dz=\int_0^1(2-4z+2z^2)\,dz=\tfrac23$ - (ii), (iv), (vi) follow by the $x\leftrightarrow y$ symmetry and identical algebra. All six agree, so the bounds are correct. $$\boxed{ \begin{aligned} &\int_0^2\!\int_{2-x}^{2}\!\int_0^{(x+y-2)/2} f\,dz\,dy\,dx,\qquad \int_0^2\!\int_{2-y}^{2}\!\int_0^{(x+y-2)/2} f\,dz\,dx\,dy,\\[4pt] &\int_0^2\!\int_0^{x/2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f\,dy\,dz\,dx,\qquad \int_0^2\!\int_0^{y/2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f\,dx\,dz\,dy,\\[4pt] &\int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f\,dy\,dx\,dz,\qquad \int_0^1\!\int_{2z}^{2}\!\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f\,dx\,dy\,dz. \end{aligned}}$$ ## How to think about this type of problem 1. **Corner-find:** intersect triples of bounding planes to get the vertices — the solid is the tetrahedron they span. 2. **Orient the inequalities:** pick one interior point (e.g. the centroid) and test it against each plane to fix $\le$ vs. $\ge$. 3. **Choose a projection:** the outer two integrals live on a *shadow* of $E$ on a coordinate plane; read its boundary from the vertices. 4. **Innermost variable:** enter through one face and exit through another — the limits are functions of the outer variables (often the slanted plane solved for the inner variable). 5. **Check:** put $f=1$; every valid ordering must return the same volume. --- No graphical results.
