## Problem Sketch the solid whose volume is given by the iterated integral $$ \int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1-x}\int_{0}^{2-2z} dy\,dz\,dx. $$ ## Step 1 — Read the region off the limits of integration An iterated integral is a set of nested inequalities, one per variable, read from the **outside in**: $$ 0\le x\le 1,\qquad 0\le z\le 1-x,\qquad 0\le y\le 2-2z. $$ Rewriting the last two as inequalities among the coordinates, $$ x\ge 0,\quad y\ge 0,\quad z\ge 0,\quad x+z\le 1,\quad y+2z\le 2. $$ So the solid $\mathcal S$ is the part of space cut out by the three coordinate planes and the two planes $$ x+z=1\ \ (\text{parallel to the }y\text{-axis}),\qquad y+2z=2\ \ (\text{parallel to the }x\text{-axis}). $$ ## Step 2 — Slice horizontally, perpendicular to the $z$-axis Fix $0\le z\le 1$. Notice that the $x$-condition and the $y$-condition then decouple: $$ 0\le x\le 1-z,\qquad 0\le y\le 2-2z. $$ Thus the horizontal cross-section is a **rectangle** of side lengths $(1-z)$ and $2(1-z)$, keeping a fixed aspect ratio $1:2$, with area $$ A(z)=(1-z)\cdot 2(1-z)=2(1-z)^{2}. $$ - At $z=0$: the rectangle $[0,1]\times[0,2]$ with corners $(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(1,2,0),(0,2,0)$. - As $z$ runs from $0$ to $1$ the rectangle shrinks linearly and collapses to the single point $(0,0,1)$. ## Step 3 — Identify and sketch the solid Because every horizontal slice is a scaled copy of the *same* rectangle shrinking to a point, the solid is a **pyramid**, and since the apex is not above the centre of the base, it is an **oblique rectangular pyramid**. - **Base** (in the plane $z=0$): the rectangle with vertices $(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(1,2,0),(0,2,0)$. - **Apex**: $E=(0,0,1)$ on the $z$-axis (directly above the base corner at the origin). - **Lateral faces**, each a triangle joining $E$ to one base edge: - base edge along the $x$-axis $\Rightarrow$ face $y=0$: triangle $E,(0,0,0),(1,0,0)$; - base edge along the $y$-axis $\Rightarrow$ face $x=0$: triangle $E,(0,0,0),(0,2,0)$; - base edge $(1,0,0)\!-\!(1,2,0)$ lies in the plane $x+z=1$; - base edge $(0,2,0)\!-\!(1,2,0)$ lies in the plane $y+2z=2$. The surface therefore has **five faces** — the rectangle $z=0$ plus the four planes $x=0,\ y=0,\ x+z=1,\ y+2z=2$ — with the **five vertices** $$ (0,0,0),\ (1,0,0),\ (1,2,0),\ (0,2,0),\ (0,0,1), $$ and **eight edges** (Euler check: $V-E+F=5-8+5=2$). $\checkmark$ --- Image `pyplot_0.png` not found.
