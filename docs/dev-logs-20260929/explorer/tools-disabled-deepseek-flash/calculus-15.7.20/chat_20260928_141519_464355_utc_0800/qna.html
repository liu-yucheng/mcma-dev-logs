## Solution The solid is enclosed by two paraboloids: $$ y=x^2+z^2 \qquad \text{(lower surface)} $$ $$ y=8-x^2-z^2 \qquad \text{(upper surface)} $$ First find the intersection curve by equating the two surfaces: $$ x^2+z^2=8-x^2-z^2 $$ $$ 2(x^2+z^2)=8 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2+z^2=4 $$ Thus the projection of the solid onto the \(xz\)-plane is the disk $$ D:\ x^2+z^2\le 4 $$ Use cylindrical coordinates in the \(xz\)-plane: $$ x=r\cos\theta,\qquad z=r\sin\theta,\qquad dV=r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta $$ The triple integral for the volume is therefore $$ V=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2\int_{r^2}^{\,8-r^2} r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta $$ Evaluate the inner integral with respect to \(y\): $$ \int_{r^2}^{\,8-r^2} r\,dy = r\bigl[(8-r^2)-r^2\bigr] = r(8-2r^2) = 8r-2r^3 $$ Now integrate with respect to \(r\): $$ \int_0^2 (8r-2r^3)\,dr = \left[4r^2-\frac12 r^4\right]_0^2 = (16-8)-0 = 8 $$ Finally integrate with respect to \(\theta\): $$ V=\int_0^{2\pi} 8\,d\theta=16\pi $$ **Check by horizontal slicing.** For \(0\le y\le 4\), the cross-section is a disk of radius \(\sqrt{y}\), area \(\pi y\). For \(4\le y\le 8\), the cross-section is a disk of radius \(\sqrt{8-y}\), area \(\pi(8-y)\). Thus $$ V=\int_0^4 \pi y\,dy+\int_4^8 \pi(8-y)\,dy =8\pi+8\pi=16\pi $$ which agrees with the triple-integral result. $$ \boxed{16\pi} $$ --- No graphical results.
