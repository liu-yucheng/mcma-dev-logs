## Moment of inertia of a solid cone about the $z$-axis ### The physical definition For a solid with density $k$, the **moment of inertia about the $z$-axis** is the integral of the density times the squared distance to the $z$-axis: $$ I_z=\iiint_D (x^2+y^2)\,k\,dV, $$ since $x^2+y^2$ is exactly the squared distance from $(x,y,z)$ to the $z$-axis. ### Describing the region Here $D$ is the solid cone $$ \sqrt{x^2+y^2}\;\le\;z\;\le\;h . $$ At a fixed height $z$ the cross-section is the disk $x^2+y^2\le z^2$ of radius $z$. Thus the vertex sits at the origin, and the top at $z=h$ is a disk of radius $h$. It is natural to use **cylindrical coordinates**, where $x^2+y^2=r^2$ and $dV=r\,dr\,dz\,d\theta$: $$ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\qquad 0\le z\le h,\qquad 0\le r\le z . $$ ### Setting up and evaluating the integral Substituting $x^2+y^2=r^2$ and $dV=r\,dr\,dz\,d\theta$, so the integrand becomes $r^2\cdot r=r^3$: $$ I_z=k\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{h}\int_0^{z} r^3\,dr\,dz\,d\theta . $$ Evaluate inside–out. The inner integral: $$ \int_0^z r^3\,dr=\frac{z^4}{4}. $$ The middle integral: $$ \int_0^h \frac{z^4}{4}\,dz=\frac{h^5}{20}. $$ The outer integral just contributes the factor $2\pi$. Hence $$ I_z=k\cdot 2\pi\cdot \frac{h^5}{20}=\frac{k\pi h^5}{10}. $$ ### Sanity check A solid right circular cone of mass $M$ and base radius $R$ satisfies the classic result $I_z=\tfrac{3}{10}MR^2$. Here $R=h$ and the volume is $\tfrac13\pi h^3$, so $M=k\cdot\tfrac13\pi h^3$ and $$ I_z=\frac{3}{10}\Big(k\tfrac{\pi h^3}{3}\Big)h^2=\frac{k\pi h^5}{10}, $$ which matches. (One can also integrate in spherical coordinates using $\phi=\pi/4$ for the cone and $x^2+y^2=\rho^2\sin^2\phi$, obtaining the same value.) ### Final answer $$ \boxed{I_z=\frac{\pi k h^5}{10}} $$ **Key technique:** the distance-squared weighting $x^2+y^2$ makes cylindrical coordinates the natural choice — the bounds become the simple box $0\le r\le z$, $0\le z\le h$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$ — and the integration reduces to integrating $z^4$. --- No graphical results.
