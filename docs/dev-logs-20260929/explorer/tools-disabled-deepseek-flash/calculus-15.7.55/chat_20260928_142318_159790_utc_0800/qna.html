## Part (a): region that maximizes the triple integral Let $$ f(x,y,z)=1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2. $$ For any region $E$ of finite volume, write it as the disjoint union of the parts where $f>0$, where $f<0$, and where $f=0$. Since the integral is additive, $$ \iiint_E f\,dV = \iiint_{E\cap\{f>0\}} f\,dV + \iiint_{E\cap\{f<0\}} f\,dV. $$ The second term is $\le 0$ because $f<0$ there, while the first term is maximized precisely when $E$ contains the entire set $\{f>0\}$. Thus the maximum occurs for $$ E=\{f\ge 0\} =\{(x,y,z):x^2+2y^2+3z^2\le 1\}. $$ The boundary $f=0$ can be included or excluded because it has zero volume and does not change the integral. This is an ellipsoid with semi-axes $1$, $1/\sqrt2$, and $1/\sqrt3$. --- Image `pyplot_0.png` not found. [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

Image `pgfplots_0.png` not found. --- ## Part (b): exact maximum value The maximum is therefore $$ I=\iiint_{x^2+2y^2+3z^2\le 1}(1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2)\,dV. $$ Use the substitution $$ u=x,\qquad v=\sqrt2\,y,\qquad w=\sqrt3\,z. $$ Then $x^2+2y^2+3z^2=u^2+v^2+w^2$, and the Jacobian is $$ \left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}\right| = \frac{1}{\sqrt2\sqrt3}=\frac1{\sqrt6}. $$ So $$ I=\frac1{\sqrt6}\iiint_{u^2+v^2+w^2\le 1}(1-u^2-v^2-w^2)\,du\,dv\,dw. $$ In spherical coordinates the unit ball gives $$ \iiint_B(1-\rho^2)\,dV = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^{\pi}\!\int_0^1(1-\rho^2)\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta = (2\pi)(2)\left(\frac13-\frac15\right) = \frac{8\pi}{15}. $$ Hence $$ I=\frac1{\sqrt6}\cdot\frac{8\pi}{15} = \frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt6} = \frac{4\pi\sqrt6}{45} \approx 0.6840265775. $$ A CAS confirms this exactly. For example, in Mathematica, 
Integrate[1 - x^2 - 2 y^2 - 3 z^2,
  {x, y, z} \[Element] Ellipsoid[{0, 0, 0}, {1, 1/Sqrt[2], 1/Sqrt[3]}]]
returns $(4\pi\sqrt6)/45$. --- $$ \boxed{\text{(a) }E:\ x^2+2y^2+3z^2\le 1,\qquad \text{(b) } \iiint_E(1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2)\,dV=\frac{4\pi\sqrt6}{45}\approx 0.6840266.} $$
