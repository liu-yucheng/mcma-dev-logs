## Problem Use **cylindrical coordinates**. (a) Find the volume of the solid that the cylinder $r=a\cos\theta$ cuts out of the sphere of radius $a$ centered at the origin. (b) Illustrate the solid by graphing the sphere and the cylinder on the same screen. --- ## Part (a): Volume ### 1. Describe the two surfaces in cylindrical coordinates In cylindrical coordinates $(r,\theta,z)$ with $x=r\cos\theta,\;y=r\sin\theta,\;z=z$, the volume element is $$ dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta . $$ - **Sphere** of radius $a$ centered at the origin: $x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2$, i.e. $$ r^2+z^2=a^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z=\pm\sqrt{a^2-r^2} . $$ - **Cylinder** $r=a\cos\theta$: multiplying by $r$ gives $r^2=ar\cos\theta$, i.e. $x^2+y^2=ax$, or $$ \left(x-\tfrac{a}{2}\right)^2+y^2=\left(\tfrac{a}{2}\right)^2 . $$ So the "cylinder" is a circular cylinder of **radius $\tfrac{a}{2}$** whose axis is the vertical line $x=\tfrac{a}{2},\,y=0$ (it passes through the origin and is tangent to the sphere's equator at $(a,0)$). ### 2. Set up the region The solid cut out is the set of points lying **inside both** surfaces. From the cylinder, since $r\ge 0$ we need $\cos\theta\ge 0$, i.e. $$ -\tfrac{\pi}{2}\le\theta\le\tfrac{\pi}{2},\qquad 0\le r\le a\cos\theta . $$ (Note $a\cos\theta\le a$, so the cylinder stays within the sphere's equatorial radius $r=a$ automatically.) For fixed $(r,\theta)$, the sphere caps the solid at $z=\pm\sqrt{a^2-r^2}$. Hence $$ R:\quad -\tfrac{\pi}{2}\le\theta\le\tfrac{\pi}{2},\qquad 0\le r\le a\cos\theta,\qquad -\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\le z\le \sqrt{a^2-r^2} . $$ ### 3. Evaluate the triple integral $$ V=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta}\!\!\int_{-\sqrt{a^2-r^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2-r^2}} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta =\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\,dr\,d\theta . $$ **The $r$-integral.** Substitute $u=a^2-r^2$, so $du=-2r\,dr$; when $r=0$, $u=a^2$, and when $r=a\cos\theta$, $u=a^2-a^2\cos^2\theta=a^2\sin^2\theta$: $$ \int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{a^2-r^2}\,dr =\int_{a^2\sin^2\theta}^{a^2}\sqrt{u}\,du =\frac{2}{3}\Big[a^{3}-\big(a^2\sin^2\theta\big)^{3/2}\Big] =\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\Big(1-|\sin\theta|^{3}\Big). $$ > **Key point (a common error).** Because $\sqrt{\sin^2\theta}=|\sin\theta|$, we get $\big(a^2\sin^2\theta\big)^{3/2}=a^3|\sin\theta|^{3}$, **not** $a^3\sin^3\theta$. Keeping the absolute value is essential. **The $\theta$-integral.** Now $$ V=\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\Big(1-|\sin\theta|^{3}\Big)\,d\theta =\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\left[\pi-\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}|\sin\theta|^{3}\,d\theta\right]. $$ Using the evenness of $|\sin\theta|^3$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\theta\,d\theta=\frac{2}{3}$: $$ \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}|\sin\theta|^{3}\,d\theta=2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\theta\,d\theta=2\cdot\frac{2}{3}=\frac{4}{3}. $$ Therefore $$ V=\frac{2a^{3}}{3}\left(\pi-\frac{4}{3}\right)=\boxed{\;\dfrac{2a^{3}}{9}\,(3\pi-4)\;}. $$ Numerically $V\approx 1.2055\,a^{3}$. > If one *forgets* the absolute value and integrates the odd function $\sin^3\theta$ over the symmetric interval (getting $0$), the incorrect answer $V=\frac{2\pi a^3}{3}\approx 2.094\,a^3$ results. The $|\sin\theta|^3$ correction reduces the volume, which is consistent with the cross-sectional disk having radius only $a/2$. As a bound check, the enclosing cylinder column of radius $a/2$ has cross-sectional area $\pi a^2/4$, and $V=(2/9)(3\pi-4)a^3<\frac{\pi a^2}{4}\cdot(2a)=\frac{\pi a^3}{2}$. ✔ ### 4. Indifferent check in Cartesian coordinates Equivalently $V=\iint_{D} 2\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}\,dA$ where $D$ is the disk $(x-\tfrac{a}{2})^2+y^2\le (\tfrac{a}{2})^2$. Evaluating this disk integral reproduces $\frac{2a^3}{9}(3\pi-4)$. --- Image `pyplot_0.png` not found. [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

import numpy as np, matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from mpl_toolkits.mplot3d import Axes3D

a = 1.0
fig = plt.figure(figsize=(9,9)); ax = fig.add_subplot(111, projection="3d")

# sphere (both hemispheres, grey wireframe)
u = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 49); v = np.linspace(0, np.pi/2, 25)
U, V = np.meshgrid(u, v, indexing="ij")
X, Y, Z = a*np.cos(V)*np.cos(U), a*np.cos(V)*np.sin(U), a*np.sin(V)
for s in (1, -1):
    ax.plot_wireframe(X, Y, s*Z, rstride=2, cstride=2, color="grey", alpha=0.45)

# cylinder (x-a/2)^2+y^2=(a/2)^2

                color="tab:red", alpha=0.45)
