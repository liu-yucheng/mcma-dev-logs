## Solution ### (a) Express the volume as a triple integral The solid lies in the first octant, so $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$, $z\ge0$. From the cylinder $$y^2+z^2=1$$ we get, in the first octant, $$0\le z\le \sqrt{1-y^2}.$$ The planes $y=x$ and $x=1$ cut the cylinder in the $xy$-plane. The wedge that is actually cut by both planes is the region satisfying $$0\le y\le x\le 1.$$ Therefore the wedge is $$E=\{(x,y,z):0\le x\le1,\ 0\le y\le x,\ 0\le z\le\sqrt{1-y^2}\},$$ and its volume is $$V=\iiint_E dV=\int_0^1\int_0^x\int_0^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz\,dy\,dx.$$ Equivalently, reversing the order of integration in the triangular base, $$V=\int_0^1\int_y^1\int_0^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz\,dx\,dy.$$ ### (b) Exact value of the integral Use the second form: $$V=\int_0^1\int_y^1 \sqrt{1-y^2}\,dx\,dy =\int_0^1(1-y)\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy.$$ Split the integral: $$V=\int_0^1\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy-\int_0^1 y\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy.$$ The first integral is the area of a quarter unit disk: $$\int_0^1\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$ For the second integral, let $u=1-y^2$, so $du=-2y\,dy$: $$\int_0^1 y\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy =\frac12\int_0^1 u^{1/2}\,du =\frac12\cdot\frac23=\frac13.$$ Therefore $$V=\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac13=\frac{3\pi-4}{12}\approx 0.4521.$$ $$\boxed{V=\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac13=\frac{3\pi-4}{12}}$$ --- No graphical results.
