Answer
## Evaluate $\iiint_E z\,dV$ **Region $E$.** The cylinder $y^2+z^2=9$ is vertical along the $x$-axis. In the first octant, $z\ge 0$, so the upper $z$-surface is $$ z=\sqrt{9-y^2},\qquad 0\le y\le 3. $$ The planes $x=0$ and $y=3x$ bound a wedge in the $xy$-plane. The bounded side is $$ 0\le x\le \frac{y}{3}, $$ because the other side $y\le 3x$ is unbounded in $x$. Hence $$ E=\left\{(x,y,z):0\le y\le 3,\;0\le x\le \frac{y}{3},\;0\le z\le \sqrt{9-y^2}\right\}. $$ **Iterated integral.** $$ \iiint_E z\,dV =\int_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{y/3}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} z\,dz\,dx\,dy. $$ Evaluate from the inside out: $$ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} z\,dz =\frac{9-y^2}{2}. $$ Then $$ \int_{0}^{y/3}\frac{9-y^2}{2}\,dx =\frac{y(9-y^2)}{6}. $$ Finally, $$ \iiint_E z\,dV =\frac16\int_{0}^{3}\left(9y-y^3\right)dy =\frac16\left[\frac{9y^2}{2}-\frac{y^4}{4}\right]_{0}^{3} =\frac16\left(\frac{81}{2}-\frac{81}{4}\right) =\frac16\cdot\frac{81}{4} =\frac{27}{8}. $$ **Check with another order.** Using $0\le x\le 1$ and $3x\le y\le 3$, $$ \iiint_E z\,dV =\int_{0}^{1}\int_{3x}^{3}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} z\,dz\,dy\,dx =\frac{27}{8}, $$ and cylindrical coordinates give the same value. $$ \boxed{\iiint_E z\,dV=\frac{27}{8}} $$ --- No graphical results.
