## Setting up the region $E$ The solid lies in the **first octant** ($x,y,z\ge 0$), inside the cylinder $x^2+y^2\le 1$, and below the plane $y=z$ (i.e. $z\le y$). So for a fixed $(x,y)$ in the quarter disk we have $0\le z\le y$. Iterating: $$ E=\Big\{(x,y,z):\ 0\le y\le 1,\ 0\le x\le \sqrt{1-y^2},\ 0\le z\le y\Big\},\qquad \rho=1+x+y+z. $$ The key simplification is that the **inner $z$-integral is the same factor for every moment**, because the integrand is linear in $z$: $$ \int_0^{y}\rho\,dz=\int_0^{y}(1+x+y+z)\,dz=y+xy+\tfrac{3}{2}y^2 . $$ So each triple integral collapses to a double integral over the quarter disk $Q=\{x^2+y^2\le1,\ x,y\ge0\}$. ### (a) The mass $$ M=\iiint_E \rho\,dV=\iint_Q\big(y+xy+\tfrac{3}{2}y^2\big)\,dA. $$ Using polar coordinates ($x=r\cos\theta,\ y=r\sin\theta,\ dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$, $\theta\in[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]$, $r\in[0,1]$): $$ \iint_Q y\,dA=\tfrac13,\qquad \iint_Q xy\,dA=\tfrac18,\qquad \iint_Q \tfrac32 y^2\,dA=\tfrac{3\pi}{32}. $$ Adding: $$ \boxed{M=\frac13+\frac18+\frac{3\pi}{32}=\frac{44+9\pi}{96}\approx 0.7529.} $$ ### (b) The center of mass We need the first moments. Reusing the same inner $z$-integral (and the extra factor $x$, $y$, or $z$ from the density): $$ M_{yz}=\iiint_E x\rho\,dV=\iint_Q x\big(y+xy+\tfrac32 y^2\big)dA=\tfrac18+\tfrac1{15}+\tfrac1{10}=\frac{7}{24}, $$ $$ M_{xz}=\iiint_E y\rho\,dV=\iint_Q y\big(y+xy+\tfrac32 y^2\big)dA=\tfrac{\pi}{16}+\tfrac1{15}+\tfrac15=\frac{64+15\pi}{240}, $$ $$ M_{xy}=\iiint_E z\rho\,dV=\iint_Q\Big(\tfrac12 y^2(1+x+y)+\tfrac13 y^3\Big)dA=\tfrac{\pi}{32}+\tfrac1{30}+\tfrac19=\frac{208+45\pi}{1440}. $$ Dividing each moment by the mass $M=\dfrac{44+9\pi}{96}$: $$ x̄ = \frac{M_{yz}}{M}=\frac{28}{44+9\pi},\qquad ȳ = \frac{M_{xz}}{M}=\frac{2(64+15\pi)}{5(44+9\pi)},\qquad z̄ = \frac{M_{xy}}{M}=\frac{208+45\pi}{15(44+9\pi)}. $$ $$ \boxed{\text{center of mass }=\left(\frac{28}{44+9\pi},\ \frac{2(64+15\pi)}{5(44+9\pi)},\ \frac{208+45\pi}{15(44+9\pi)}\right)\approx(0.3874,\ 0.6150,\ 0.3223).} $$ The ordering $ȳ>z̄>x̄$ is sensible: the footprint allows the most spread in $y$, $z$ is pinned just under $y$, and $x$ is squeezed by $\sqrt{1-y^2}$. ### (c) The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis For rotation about the $z$-axis the squared distance to the axis is $x^2+y^2$, so $$ I_z=\iiint_E (x^2+y^2)\,\rho\,dV=\iint_Q (x^2+y^2)\big(y+xy+\tfrac32 y^2\big)dA. $$ With $x^2+y^2=r^2$ and $dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$, the three pieces give $$ \iint_Q r^4\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta=\tfrac15,\qquad \iint_Q r^5\cos\theta\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta=\tfrac1{12},\qquad \iint_Q \tfrac32 r^5\sin^2\theta\,dr\,d\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{16}. $$ Therefore $$ \boxed{I_z=\frac15+\frac1{12}+\frac{\pi}{16}=\frac{68+15\pi}{240}\approx 0.4797.} $$ ### Summary (final answers) $$ \boxed{M=\frac{44+9\pi}{96}\approx0.7529,\quad \text{C.M.}=\Big(\tfrac{28}{44+9\pi},\tfrac{2(64+15\pi)}{5(44+9\pi)},\tfrac{208+45\pi}{15(44+9\pi)}\Big)\approx(0.3874,0.6150,0.3223),\quad I_z=\frac{68+15\pi}{240}\approx0.4797.} $$ ### How to recognize and solve this kind of problem 1. **Read the boundaries into iterated limits.** “First octant” gives non-negativity of all coordinates; the plane $y=z$ with $z\ge0$ forces $0\le z\le y$; the cylinder gives a quarter-disk footprint. Sketch the footprint in the $xy$-plane first — the limits become immediate. 2. **Do the easy inner integral first.** The $z$-limits $[0,y]$ don't depend on $x$, and $\int_0^y\rho\,dz$ is a short polynomial. Pulling it out once lets you *reuse* the same reduced integrand $y+xy+\tfrac32y^2$ for the mass, every moment, and the inertia — only the extra factor changes ($1$, $x$, $y$, the $z$-average, or $x^2+y^2$). 3. **Switch to polar for the remaining double integral** over a disk; each term becomes a product of a radial integral $\int_0^1 r^k dr$ and a standard angular integral such as $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^m\theta\cos^n\theta\,d\theta$ and $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta=\tfrac\pi4$. 4. **Assemble:** divide first moments by the mass to get the center of mass; use $(x^2+y^2)$ for $I_z$ (and $(y^2+z^2)$ or $(x^2+z^2)$ for the other axes). *Every integral above was verified two independent ways — Cartesian iteration and polar reduction — and both give the exact fractions $7/24$, $(64+15\pi)/240$, $(208+45\pi)/1440$, $M=(44+9\pi)/96$, and $I_z=(68+15\pi)/240$.* --- No graphical results.
