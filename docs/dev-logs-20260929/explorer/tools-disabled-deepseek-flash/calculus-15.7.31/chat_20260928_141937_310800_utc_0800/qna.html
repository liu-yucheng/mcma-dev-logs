# Expressing $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ in six ways **Given surfaces:** the parabolic cylinder $\;y=x^{2},\;$ the plane $\;z=0,\;$ and the plane $\;y+2z=4$ (i.e. $z=2-\tfrac{y}{2}$). --- ## Step 1 — Understand the solid $E$ The floor is $z=0$ and the roof is the slanted plane $z=2-\tfrac{y}{2}$, so for a point of $E$ we need $$0\le z\le 2-\tfrac{y}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\le 4 .$$ The parabolic cylinder $y=x^{2}$ gives the remaining wall; combining it with $y\le 4$ forces $x^{2}\le 4$, i.e. $|x|\le 2$. Hence $$E=\{(x,y,z):\ -2\le x\le 2,\ x^{2}\le y\le 4,\ 0\le z\le 2-\tfrac{y}{2}\}.$$ A quick sanity check: slicing at fixed $x=c$ gives a triangle in the $yz$-plane with vertices $(c^2,0),(4,0),\big(c^2,2-\tfrac{c^2}{2}\big)$, which is non-degenerate exactly when $|c|\le 2$. ✔ ## Step 2 — The three coordinate-plane projections Every iterated integral is built by choosing the **outermost** variable, projecting the solid onto that variable's coordinate plane, and then working inward. So we record the three projections: $$D_{xy}:\ x^{2}\le y\le 4 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \begin{cases}-2\le x\le 2\\ x^{2}\le y\le 4\end{cases} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \begin{cases}0\le y\le 4\\ -\sqrt{y}\le x\le \sqrt{y}\end{cases}$$ $$D_{xz}:\ -2\le x\le 2,\qquad 0\le z\le 2-\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}$$ $$D_{yz}:\ 0\le y\le 4,\qquad 0\le z\le 2-\tfrac{y}{2}$$ The $xz$-projection comes from requiring a $y$ with $x^{2}\le y\le 4-2z$, which is possible iff $x^{2}\le 4-2z$. ## Step 3 — The six orderings Choosing which variable is integrated first/second/last gives the six orders. Here is each, written with the **inner** integral first in the differential list. **(1) $dz\,dy\,dx$** — use $D_{xy}$, integrate $z$ innermost: $$\int_{-2}^{2}\!\int_{x^{2}}^{4}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-y/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx$$ **(2) $dz\,dx\,dy$** — same projection, but $x$ before $y$: $$\int_{0}^{4}\!\int_{-\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{y}}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-y/2} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy$$ **(3) $dy\,dz\,dx$** — use $D_{xz}$, integrate $y$ innermost (from the cylinder to the plane): $$\int_{-2}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-x^{2}/2}\!\int_{x^{2}}^{\,4-2z} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx$$ **(4) $dy\,dx\,dz$** — same projection, but $x$ before $z$: $$\int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{-\sqrt{4-2z}}^{\sqrt{4-2z}}\!\int_{x^{2}}^{\,4-2z} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz$$ **(5) $dx\,dz\,dy$** — use $D_{yz}$, integrate $x$ innermost (across the cylinder): $$\int_{0}^{4}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-y/2}\!\int_{-\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{y}} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy$$ **(6) $dx\,dy\,dz$** — same projection, but $y$ before $z$: $$\int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\,4-2z}\!\int_{-\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{y}} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz$$ --- ## Verification Setting $f\equiv 1$, all six integrals must return the same volume. Using the slice $V=\int_{-2}^{2}\frac{(4-x^{2})^{2}}{4}\,dx$ we get $$V=\frac14\int_{-2}^{2}\big(16-8x^{2}+x^{4}\big)\,dx=\frac12\Big(32-\tfrac{64}{3}+\tfrac{32}{5}\Big)=\frac{128}{15}\approx 8.5333,$$ and each of (1)–(6) independently reduces to $\tfrac{128}{15}$ (e.g. (2) gives $\int_0^4 2\sqrt y\,(2-\tfrac y2)\,dy=\tfrac{64}{3}-\tfrac{64}{5}=\tfrac{128}{15}$, and (4) gives $\tfrac43\int_0^2(4-2z)^{3/2}dz=\tfrac{128}{15}$). This confirms the bounds are mutually consistent. --- ## Final answer $$\boxed{ \begin{aligned} &\text{(1) }\int_{-2}^{2}\!\int_{x^{2}}^{4}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-y/2} f\,dz\,dy\,dx &\qquad \text{(2) }\int_{0}^{4}\!\int_{-\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{y}}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-y/2} f\,dz\,dx\,dy\\[6pt] &\text{(3) }\int_{-2}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-x^{2}/2}\!\int_{x^{2}}^{\,4-2z} f\,dy\,dz\,dx &\qquad \text{(4) }\int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{-\sqrt{4-2z}}^{\sqrt{4-2z}}\!\int_{x^{2}}^{\,4-2z} f\,dy\,dx\,dz\\[6pt] &\text{(5) }\int_{0}^{4}\!\int_{0}^{\,2-y/2}\!\int_{-\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{y}} f\,dx\,dz\,dy &\qquad \text{(6) }\int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\,4-2z}\!\int_{-\sqrt{y}}^{\sqrt{y}} f\,dx\,dy\,dz \end{aligned} }$$ where $f=f(x,y,z)$ in every integral. --- No graphical results.
