Answer
## Volume enclosed by the "bumpy sphere" $\rho = 1 + \tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi$ ### 1. Set up the triple integral In **spherical coordinates** the volume element is $$ dV = \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho\, d\theta\, d\phi, $$ where $\rho\ge 0$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$ is the azimuthal angle and $\phi\in[0,\pi]$ is the polar angle. The region $E$ is the solid enclosed by the surface, so for every $(\theta,\phi)$ the radius runs from $0$ up to the surface: $$ E = \left\{ (\rho,\theta,\phi) : 0 \le \rho \le 1 + \tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi,\ \ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\ \ 0\le\phi\le\pi \right\}. $$ Hence $$ V = \iiint_E dV = \int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{1+\frac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi} \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\theta \, d\phi. $$ ### 2. Do the $\rho$-integral (this is where the cube comes from) $$ \int_0^{R}\rho^2\,d\rho = \frac{R^3}{3}, \qquad R = 1 + \tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi . $$ So $$ V = \frac13\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi} \Bigl(1 + \tfrac15\sin 6\theta\,\sin 5\phi\Bigr)^{3}\sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta. $$ ### 3. Expand the cube and use orthogonality Write $u=\sin 6\theta$, $v=\sin 5\phi$, so $R^3 = 1 + \tfrac35 uv + \tfrac{3}{25}u^2v^2 + \tfrac{1}{125}u^3v^3$. The mixed terms all **average to zero** over a full period: $$ \int_0^{2\pi}\sin 6\theta\,d\theta = 0, \quad \int_0^{2\pi}\sin^3 6\theta\,d\theta = 0, $$ $$ \int_0^{\pi}\sin 5\phi\,\sin\phi\,d\phi = 0, \quad \int_0^{\pi}\sin^3 5\phi\,\sin\phi\,d\phi = 0 $$ (the $\phi$-integrals reduce to $\int_0^\pi \cos(k\phi)\,d\phi = 0$ for even integers $k$). Only the **constant term** and the **$u^2v^2$ term** survive. ### 4. Evaluate the two surviving integrals $$ \int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2 6\theta\,d\theta = \pi . $$ For the polar one, use $\sin^2 5\phi = \tfrac{1-\cos 10\phi}{2}$ and $\int_0^{\pi}\cos 10\phi\,\sin\phi\,d\phi = -\tfrac{2}{99}$: $$ \int_0^{\pi}\sin^2 5\phi\,\sin\phi\,d\phi = \frac12\int_0^{\pi}\sin\phi\,d\phi - \frac12\int_0^{\pi}\cos 10\phi\,\sin\phi\,d\phi = 1 + \frac{1}{99} = \frac{100}{99}. $$ ### 5. Combine $$\begin{aligned} \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi} R^3\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta &= \underbrace{1}_{}\cdot(2\pi)(2) + \tfrac{3}{25}\cdot(\pi)\Bigl(\tfrac{100}{99}\Bigr) \\[2pt] &= 4\pi + \tfrac{300\pi}{25\cdot 99} = 4\pi + \tfrac{4\pi}{33} = \tfrac{136\pi}{33}. \end{aligned}$$ Therefore $$ V = \frac13\cdot\frac{136\pi}{33} = \frac{136\pi}{99} \approx 4.3157. $$ ### Answer $$ \boxed{V = \dfrac{136}{99}\,\pi} $$ --- ### Bonus: the general pattern For a surface $\rho = 1 + c\,\sin m\theta\,\sin n\phi$ with integers $m,n\ge 1$, every cross term still averages to zero and the whole $\theta$-dependence collapses. The general result is $$ V = \frac13\left[4\pi + 3c^2\pi\Bigl(1 + \frac{1}{4n^2-1}\Bigr)\right], $$ which is **independent of $m$** and depends on $n$ only through the factor $1+\frac{1}{4n^2-1}$. For $c=\tfrac15,\ n=5$ this gives $\frac13\left[4\pi + \frac{3}{25}\pi\cdot\frac{100}{99}\right] = \frac{136\pi}{99}$, confirming the boxed answer. --- No graphical results.
