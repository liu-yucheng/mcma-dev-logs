# Average value of $f(x,y,z)=x^2z+y^2z$ over a solid region ## 1. The region $E$ The solid is bounded **below** by the plane $z=0$ and **above** by the paraboloid $z=1-x^2-y^2$. The two surfaces meet where $$ 1-x^2-y^2=0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; x^2+y^2=1, $$ so the projection of $E$ onto the $xy$-plane is the unit disk $D:\;x^2+y^2\le 1$. Thus $$ E=\{(x,y,z):\;x^2+y^2\le 1,\;\;0\le z\le 1-x^2-y^2\}. $$ ## 2. The average-value formula By definition $$ f_{\text{ave}}=\frac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV, $$ so we need two quantities: the volume $V(E)$ and the triple integral of $f$. ## 3. Volume of $E$ The volume is the integral of height over the base disk: $$ V(E)=\iint_D (1-x^2-y^2)\,dA. $$ Switching to polar coordinates ($x=r\cos\theta,\;y=r\sin\theta,\;dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$): $$ V(E)=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 (1-r^2)\,r\,dr\,d\theta =2\pi\int_0^1 (r-r^3)\,dr =2\pi\Big[\tfrac{r^2}{2}-\tfrac{r^4}{4}\Big]_0^1 =2\pi\cdot\tfrac14=\frac{\pi}{2}. $$ ## 4. Triple integral of $f$ Begin by simplifying the integrand. Since $f=x^2z+y^2z$, $$ f=(x^2+y^2)z=r^2z, $$ which is tailor-made for cylindrical coordinates ($dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$): $$ \iiint_E f\,dV=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{1-r^2} r^2 z\;\, r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta. $$ Evaluate the innermost integral first: $$ \int_0^{1-r^2} z\,dz=\frac{(1-r^2)^2}{2}. $$ Then $$ \iiint_E f\,dV =2\pi\int_0^1 r^3\cdot\frac{(1-r^2)^2}{2}\,dr =\pi\int_0^1 r^3(1-2r^2+r^4)\,dr. $$ Expand and integrate term by term: $$ \pi\int_0^1 (r^3-2r^5+r^7)\,dr =\pi\Big[\tfrac{r^4}{4}-\tfrac{2r^6}{6}+\tfrac{r^8}{8}\Big]_0^1 =\pi\Big(\tfrac14-\tfrac13+\tfrac18\Big). $$ With a common denominator $24$, $$ \tfrac14-\tfrac13+\tfrac18=\tfrac{6-8+3}{24}=\tfrac{1}{24}, $$ so $$ \iiint_E f\,dV=\frac{\pi}{24}. $$ ## 5. The average value Divide the integral by the volume: $$ f_{\text{ave}}=\frac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f\,dV =\frac{\pi/24}{\pi/2} =\frac{\pi}{24}\cdot\frac{2}{\pi} =\frac{1}{12}. $$ ## Answer $$ \boxed{f_{\text{ave}}=\dfrac{1}{12}} $$ ### Key takeaways - The average value is $\dfrac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f\,dV$: a "density-weighted" average where the volume plays the role of the total weight. - When the region is a solid of revolution about the $z$-axis (here, a paraboloid over a disk), **cylindrical coordinates** simplify both the volume and the integral; use $dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$. - Always simplify the integrand *before* integrating — recognizing $x^2z+y^2z=r^2z$ reduced a messy integrand to a single radial factor. --- No graphical results.
