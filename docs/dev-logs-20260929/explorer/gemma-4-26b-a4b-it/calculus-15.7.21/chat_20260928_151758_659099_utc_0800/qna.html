# Volume of the Solid The problem asks us to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the cylinder $y = x^2$ and the planes $z = 0$ and $y + z = 1$. ## 1. Identify the Boundaries of the Solid The solid is bounded by: 1. The cylinder: $y = x^2$ 2. The bottom plane: $z = 0$ 3. The top plane: $y + z = 1 \implies z = 1 - y$ To find the extent of the solid in the $xy$-plane, we look at the projection of the intersection of the planes. The planes $z = 0$ and $z = 1 - y$ intersect when $0 = 1 - y$, which means $y = 1$. Since the cylinder is $y = x^2$, the solid is constrained by the curve $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 1$ in the $xy$-plane. The intersection of $y = x^2$ and $y = 1$ gives $x^2 = 1$, so $x = -1$ and $x = 1$. Thus, the limits for the variables are: - For a fixed $x$ and $y$, $z$ ranges from $0$ to $1 - y$. - For a fixed $x$, $y$ ranges from $x^2$ to $1$. - $x$ ranges from $-1$ to $1$. ## 2. Set up the Triple Integral The volume $V$ of a solid $E$ is given by the triple integral: $$ V = \iiint_E dV $$ Using the limits derived above: $$ V = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^2}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-y} dz \, dy \, dx $$ ## 3. Evaluate the Integral First, we evaluate the innermost integral with respect to $z$: $$ \int_{0}^{1-y} dz = [z]_0^{1-y} = 1 - y $$ Next, we evaluate the integral with respect to $y$: $$ \int_{x^2}^{1} (1 - y) \, dy = \left[ y - \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_{x^2}^{1} $$ Evaluating at the boundaries: $$ = \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2} \right) - \left( x^2 - \frac{(x^2)^2}{2} \right) = \frac{1}{2} - x^2 + \frac{x^4}{2} $$ Finally, we evaluate the integral with respect to $x$: $$ V = \int_{-1}^{1} \left( \frac{1}{2} - x^2 + \frac{x^4}{2} \right) \, dx $$ Since the integrand is an even function, we can integrate from $0$ to $1$ and multiply by $2$: $$ V = 2 \int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{1}{2} - x^2 + \frac{x^4}{2} \right) \, dx $$ $$ V = 2 \left[ \frac{1}{2}x - \frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{x^5}{10} \right]_0^1 $$ $$ V = 2 \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{10} \right) $$ Calculating the fraction: $$ \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{15 - 10 + 3}{30} = \frac{8}{30} = \frac{4}{15} $$ So, $$ V = 2 \times \frac{4}{15} = \frac{8}{15} $$ ## Conclusion The volume of the solid is: $$ \\boxed{\frac{8}{15}} $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
