# Problem Solution: Triple Integrals and Mass/Moment of Inertia The goal is to set up integral expressions for the mass $M$, the center of mass $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})$, and the moment of inertia $I_z$ for a solid $E$ with density $\rho(x, y, z) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. The solid is bounded by the cylinder $y = x^2$ and the planes $z = 0$ and $y + z = 1$. ## 1. Geometric Analysis First, let's identify the boundaries of the solid $E$: - **Cylinder:** $y = x^2$. This parabolic cylinder opens along the $z$-axis. In the $xy$-plane, it defines the region between the parabola $y = x^2$ and some boundary. - **Plane 1:** $z = 0$. This is the $xy$-plane, forming the bottom boundary. - **Plane 2:** $y + z = 1 \implies z = 1 - y$. This plane forms the top boundary. To find the projection of the solid onto the $xy$-plane, we look at the intersection of the boundaries. The top plane $z = 1 - y$ meets the bottom plane $z = 0$ when $1 - y = 0$, which means $y = 1$. Thus, in the $xy$-plane, the region is bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 1$. The intersection points of $y = x^2$ and $y = 1$ are $x^2 = 1 \implies x = \pm 1$. **Limits of Integration:** For a given $(x, y)$ in the projection $D$, $z$ ranges from $0$ to $1 - y$. The projection $D$ in the $xy$-plane is: $$D = \{ (x, y) \mid -1 \le x \le 1, \text{ and } x^2 \le y \le 1 \}$$ Therefore, the solid $E$ can be described as: $$E = \{ (x, y, z) \mid -1 \le x \le 1, x^2 \le y \le 1, 0 \le z \le 1 - y \}$$ ## 2. Setup of Integrals The density function is given by $\rho(x, y, z) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. ### (a) Mass $M$ The mass is the integral of the density over the volume of the solid: $$M = \iiint_E \rho(x, y, z) \, dV$$ Substituting the limits and density: $$M = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^2}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-y} \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \, dz \, dy \, dx$$ ### (b) Center of Mass $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})$ The coordinates of the center of mass are given by: $$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{M} \iiint_E x \rho(x, y, z) \, dV, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{1}{M} \iiint_E y \rho(x, y, z) \, dV, \quad \bar{z} = \frac{1}{M} \iiint_E z \rho(x, y, z) \, dV$$ Due to the symmetry of the region $D$ across the $yz$-plane (the region is symmetric with respect to $x \to -x$) and the density function (which is symmetric with respect to $x \to -x$), the integral for $\bar{x}$ will be zero: $$\iiint_E x \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \, dV = 0 \implies \bar{x} = 0$$ The non-zero expressions are: $$\bar{x} = 0$$ $$\bar{y} = \frac{1}{M} \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^2}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-y} y \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \, dz \, dy \, dx$$ $$\bar{z} = \frac{1}{M} \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^2}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-y} z \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \, dz \, dy \, dx$$ ### (c) Moment of Inertia about the $z$-axis $I_z$ The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis is: $$I_z = \iiint_E (x^2 + y^2) \rho(x, y, z) \, dV$$ Substituting the density $\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$: $$I_z = \iiint_E (x^2 + y^2)^{3/2} \, dV$$ $$I_z = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^2}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-y} (x^2 + y^2)^{3/2} \, dz \, dy \, dx$$ ##
