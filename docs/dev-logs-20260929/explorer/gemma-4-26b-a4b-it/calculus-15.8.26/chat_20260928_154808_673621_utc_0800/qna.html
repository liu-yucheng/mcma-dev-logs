# Problem Solution ## Problem Statement (a) Find the volume of the solid that the cylinder $r = a \cos \theta$ cuts out of the sphere of radius $a$ centered at the origin using cylindrical coordinates. (b) Illustrate the solid of part (a) by graphing the sphere and the cylinder on the same screen. --- ## Part (a): Finding the Volume To find the volume of the solid using cylindrical coordinates, we first describe the surfaces. 1. **The Sphere:** The sphere of radius $a$ centered at the origin is given by $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = a^2$. In cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$, this is $r^2 + z^2 = a^2$, which implies: $$z = \pm \sqrt{a^2 - r^2}$$ 2. **The Cylinder:** The cylinder is given by $r = a \cos \theta$. Note that for $r$ to be non-negative, we must have $\cos \theta \ge 0$, which means $\theta \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$. ### Setting up the Triple Integral The volume $V$ is given by the triple integral: $$V = \iiint_E dV = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \int_0^{a \cos \theta} \int_{-\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$$ ### Step 1: Integrate with respect to $z$ $$\int_{-\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}} r \, dz = r \left[ z \right]_{-\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}}^{\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}} = 2r \sqrt{a^2 - r^2}$$ ### Step 2: Integrate with respect to $r$ Now we evaluate: $$\int_0^{a \cos \theta} 2r \sqrt{a^2 - r^2} \, dr$$ Let $u = a^2 - r^2$, then $du = -2r \, dr$. When $r = 0$, $u = a^2$. When $r = a \cos \theta$, $u = a^2 - a^2 \cos^2 \theta = a^2 \sin^2 \theta$. The integral becomes: $$\int_{a^2}^{a^2 \sin^2 \theta} -\sqrt{u} \, du = \int_{a^2 \sin^2 \theta}^{a^2} u^{1/2} \, du$$ $$= \left[ \frac{2}{3} u^{3/2} \right]_{a^2 \sin^2 \theta}^{a^2} = \frac{2}{3} \left( (a^2)^{3/2} - (a^2 \sin^2 \theta)^{3/2} \right)$$ $$= \frac{2}{3} (a^3 - a^3 |\sin \theta|^3) = \frac{2}{3} a^3 (1 - |\sin \theta|^3)$$ ### Step 3: Integrate with respect to $\theta$ $$V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{2}{3} a^3 (1 - |\sin \theta|^3) \, d\theta$$ Since the integrand is even, we can simplify: $$V = 2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} a^3 \int_0^{\pi/2} (1 - \sin^3 \theta) \, d\theta = \frac{4}{3} a^3 \left( \int_0^{\pi/2} 1 \, d\theta - \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^3 \theta \, d\theta \right)$$ Evaluate the components: 1. $\int_0^{\pi/2} 1 \, d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ 2. For $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^3 \theta \, d\theta$, use the identity $\sin^2 \theta = 1 - \cos^2 \theta$: $$\int_0^{\pi/2} (1 - \cos^2 \theta) \sin \theta \, d\theta$$ Let $v = \cos \theta$, $dv = -\sin \theta \, d\theta$: $$\int_1^0 -(1 - v^2) \, dv = \int_0^1 (1 - v^2) \, dv = \left[ v - \frac{v^3}{3} \right]_0^1 = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$$ Plugging these back: $$V = \frac{4}{3} a^3 \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{2}{3} \right) = \frac{4}{3} a^3 \frac{3\pi - 4}{6} = \frac{2}{3} a^3 \left( \pi - \frac{4}{3} \right) = \frac{2}{3} \pi a^3 - \frac{8}{9} a^3$$ Wait, let me re-check the simplification: $$V = \frac{4}{3} a^3 \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{2}{3} \right) = \frac{2}{3} \pi a^3 - \frac{8}{9} a^3$$ This matches my previous Python verification: $-8/9 a^3 + 2/3 \pi a^3$. Final result for volume: $$\boxed{V = \left( \frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{8}{9} \right) a^3}$$ --- ## Part (b): Illustration Below is the visualization of the sphere and the cylinder $r = a \cos \theta$.
