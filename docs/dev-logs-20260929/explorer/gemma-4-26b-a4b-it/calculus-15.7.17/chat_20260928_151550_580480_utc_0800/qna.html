# Solution: Evaluating the Triple Integral The problem asks us to evaluate the triple integral: $$ \iiint_E x \, dV $$ where $E$ is the region bounded by the paraboloid $x = 4y^2 + 4z^2$ and the plane $x = 4$. ## 1. Understanding the Region $E$ The region $E$ is bounded by two surfaces: 1. **A paraboloid**: $x = 4(y^2 + z^2)$. This is a surface that opens along the positive $x$-axis with its vertex at the origin $(0,0,0)$. 2. **A plane**: $x = 4$. This is a plane perpendicular to the $x$-axis. To find the intersection of these two surfaces, we set $x = 4$ in the equation for the paraboloid: $$ 4 = 4(y^2 + z^2) \implies y^2 + z^2 = 1 $$ The intersection is a circle of radius 1 in the plane $x = 4$, centered at $(4,0,0)$. The region $E$ consists of all points $(x, y, z)$ such that: - The projection onto the $yz$-plane is the disk $D = \{(y, z) \mid y^2 + z^2 \le 1\}$. - For a given $(y, z)$ in $D$, $x$ ranges from the paraboloid to the plane: $4(y^2 + z^2) \le x \le 4$. ## 2. Setting up the Integral in Cylindrical Coordinates Since the region has rotational symmetry about the $x$-axis, it is natural to use a modified version of cylindrical coordinates. Let's define: - $x = x$ - $y = r \cos \theta$ - $z = r \sin \theta$ The differential volume element in these coordinates is $dV = r \, dx \, dr \, d\theta$. The bounds for the variables are: - $\theta$ goes from $0$ to $2\pi$ (the full rotation around the $x$-axis). - $r$ goes from $0$ to $1$ (the radius of the disk $D$). - $x$ goes from the paraboloid to the plane: $x = 4(y^2 + z^2) = 4r^2$ to $x = 4$. The integral becomes: $$ \iiint_E x \, dV = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 \int_{4r^2}^4 x \cdot r \, dx \, dr \, d\theta $$ ## 3. Evaluating the Integral First, evaluate the innermost integral with respect to $x$: $$ \int_{4r^2}^4 x \cdot r \, dx = r \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_{4r^2}^4 = r \left( \frac{4^2}{2} - \frac{(4r^2)^2}{2} \right) = r \left( 8 - 8r^4 \right) = 8r - 8r^5 $$ Next, evaluate the integral with respect to $r$: $$ \int_0^1 (8r - 8r^5) \, dr = \left[ 4r^2 - \frac{8r^6}{6} \right]_0^1 = \left[ 4r^2 - \frac{4}{3}r^6 \right]_0^1 = 4 - \frac{4}{3} = \frac{12-4}{3} = \frac{8}{3} $$ Finally, evaluate the integral with respect to $\theta$: $$ \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{8}{3} \, d\theta = \frac{8}{3} [\theta]_0^{2\pi} = \frac{8}{3} \cdot 2\pi = \frac{16\pi}{3} $$ ## Final Answer The value of the triple integral is: $$ \boxed{\frac{16\pi}{3}} $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
