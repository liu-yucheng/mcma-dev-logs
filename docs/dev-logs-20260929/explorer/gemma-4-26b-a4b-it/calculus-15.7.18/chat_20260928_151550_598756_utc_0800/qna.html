# Solution to the Triple Integral Problem ## Problem Statement Evaluate the triple integral: $$ \iiint_E z \, dV $$ where $E$ is the region in the first octant bounded by the cylinder $y^2 + z^2 = 9$ and the planes $x = 0$, $y = 3x$, and $z = 0$. ## Step 1: Analyze the region $E$ First, we identify the bounding surfaces in the first octant ($x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0$): 1. **Cylinder**: $y^2 + z^2 = 9$. This can be written as $z = \sqrt{9 - y^2}$ since $z \ge 0$. 2. **Plane 1**: $x = 0$ (the $yz$-plane). 3. **Plane 2**: $y = 3x$, which can be rewritten as $x = \frac{y}{3}$. 4. **Plane 3**: $z = 0$ (the $xy$-plane). The region is in the **first octant**, so all variables are non-negative. ### Projection onto the $xy$-plane The boundaries in the $xy$-plane are $x = 0$ and $x = \frac{y}{3}$. From the cylinder equation $y^2 + z^2 = 9$, we see that for $z$ to be a real number, $y^2 \le 9$, which means $0 \le y \le 3$ in the first octant. Thus, for a fixed $y$, $x$ ranges from $0$ to $\frac{y}{3}$. ### Bounds for $z$ For a fixed $(x, y)$ in the $xy$-projection, $z$ starts from the plane $z = 0$ and goes up to the cylinder $z = \sqrt{9 - y^2}$. Therefore, the limits of integration are: - $0 \le y \le 3$ - $0 \le x \le \frac{y}{3}$ - $0 \le z \le \sqrt{9 - y^2}$ ## Step 2: Set up the triple integral The triple integral is given by: $$ \iiint_E z \, dV = \int_0^3 \int_0^{y/3} \int_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} z \, dz \, dx \, dy $$ ## Step 3: Evaluate the integral We evaluate the integral step-by-step. ### 1. Inner integral (with respect to $z$) $$ \int_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} z \, dz = \left[ \frac{z^2}{2} \right]_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} = \frac{(\sqrt{9-y^2})^2}{2} - 0 = \frac{9 - y^2}{2} $$ ### 2. Middle integral (with respect to $x$) $$ \int_0^{y/3} \frac{9 - y^2}{2} \, dx = \frac{9 - y^2}{2} \int_0^{y/3} 1 \, dx = \frac{9 - y^2}{2} \left[ x \right]_0^{y/3} = \frac{9 - y^2}{2} \cdot \frac{y}{3} = \frac{9y - y^3}{6} $$ ### 3. Outer integral (with respect to $y$) $$ \int_0^3 \frac{9y - y^3}{6} \, dy = \frac{1}{6} \int_0^3 (9y - y^3) \, dy $$ $$ = \frac{1}{6} \left[ \frac{9y^2}{2} - \frac{y^4}{4} \right]_0^3 $$ $$ = \frac{1}{6} \left( \left( \frac{9(3^2)}{2} - \frac{3^4}{4} \right) - (0) \right) $$ $$ = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{81}{2} - \frac{81}{4} \right) $$ $$ = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{162 - 81}{4} \right) = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{81}{4} \right) $$ $$ = \frac{81}{24} $$ Simplify the fraction: Divide numerator and denominator by 3: $$ \frac{81 \div 3}{24 \div 3} = \frac{27}{8} $$ ## Final Answer The value of the triple integral is: $$ \boxed{\frac{27}{8}} $$ --- No graphical results.
