# Problem Solution The problem asks us to: (a) Find the region $E$ for which the triple integral $\iiint_E (1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2) \, dV$ is a maximum. (b) Calculate the exact maximum value of the triple integral. ## Part (a): Finding the Region $E$ We want to maximize the integral $$ I(E) = \iiint_E f(x, y, z) \, dV $$ where $f(x, y, z) = 1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2$. To maximize the integral, we should include all points $(x, y, z)$ where the integrand $f(x, y, z)$ is positive. If we were to include points where $f(x, y, z) < 0$, the integral's value would decrease. If we were to exclude points where $f(x, y, z) > 0$, the integral's value would also be smaller than its potential maximum. Therefore, the optimal region $E$ is the set of all points such that: $$ 1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2 \ge 0 $$ Rearranging this inequality, we get: $$ x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 \le 1 $$ This inequality describes the interior and boundary of an ellipsoid centered at the origin. Specifically, it is an ellipsoid with semi-axes of lengths $1$, $1/\sqrt{2}$, and $1/\sqrt{3}$ along the $x, y,$ and $z$ axes, respectively. **Answer (a):** The region $E$ is the ellipsoid defined by $x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 \le 1$. ## Part (b): Calculating the Maximum Value To calculate the integral $I = \iiint_E (1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2) \, dV$ over the region $E: x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 \le 1$, we use a change of variables to simplify the region of integration. Let: $$ u = x, \quad v = \sqrt{2}y, \quad w = \sqrt{3}z $$ Then the region $E$ in $(x, y, z)$ coordinates transforms into the unit sphere $S$ in $(u, v, w)$ coordinates: $$ u^2 + v^2 + w^2 \le 1 $$ The Jacobian of this transformation is: $$ \frac{\partial(x, y, z)}{\partial(u, v, w)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1/\sqrt{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1/\sqrt{3} \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} $$ The integrand transforms as follows: $$ 1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2 = 1 - u^2 - v^2 - w^2 $$ The integral becomes: $$ I = \iiint_S (1 - u^2 - v^2 - w^2) \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \, du \, dv \, dw $$ Switching to spherical coordinates $(r, \phi, \theta)$ for the $(u, v, w)$ space: $$ u = r \sin \phi \cos \theta, \quad v = r \sin \phi \sin \theta, \quad w = r \cos \phi $$ The volume element is $du \, dv \, dw = r^2 \sin \phi \, dr \, d\phi \, d\theta$. The bounds for the unit sphere are $0 \le r \le 1$, $0 \le \phi \le \pi$, and $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$. The integral is: $$ I = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\pi \int_0^1 (1 - r^2) r^2 \sin \phi \, dr \, d\phi \, d\theta $$ First, compute the $r$-integral: $$ \int_0^1 (r^2 - r^4) \, dr = \left[ \frac{r^3}{3} - \frac{r^5}{5} \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5} = \frac{2}{15} $$ Next, the $\phi$-integral: $$ \int_0^\pi \sin \phi \, d\phi = [-\cos \phi]_0^\pi = -(-1) - (-1) = 2 $$ Finally, the $\theta$-integral: $$ \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta = 2\pi $$ Multiplying everything together: $$ I = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \cdot \frac{2}{15} \cdot 2 \cdot 2\pi = \frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}} $$ To simplify the expression: $$ I = \frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{8\sqrt{6}\pi}{15 \cdot 6} = \frac{8\sqrt{6}\pi}{90} = \frac{4\sqrt{6}\pi}{45} $$ **Answer (b):** The exact maximum value is $\frac{4\sqrt{6}\pi}{45}$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
