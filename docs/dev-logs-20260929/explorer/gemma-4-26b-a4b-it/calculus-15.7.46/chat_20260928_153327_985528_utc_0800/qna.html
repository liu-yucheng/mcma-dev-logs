# Problem Statement Assume that the solid has constant density $k$. Find the moment of inertia about the $z$-axis of the solid cone defined by: $$ \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \leq z \leq h $$ # Solution To find the moment of inertia $I_z$ about the $z$-axis, we use the triple integral: $$ I_z = \iiint_E (x^2 + y^2) \rho(x, y, z) \, dV $$ where $\rho(x, y, z) = k$ is the constant density. ## 1. Coordinate System and Bounds The region $E$ is described by $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \leq z \leq h$. This region is best represented using **cylindrical coordinates** $(r, \theta, z)$, where: - $x = r\cos\theta$ - $y = r\sin\theta$ - $z = z$ - $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ - $dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$ The inequalities become: - $r \leq z \leq h$ - Since $r \leq z \leq h$, it follows that $0 \leq r \leq h$. - The angle $\theta$ covers the full circle: $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$. The bounds for the triple integral are: - $0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi$ - $0 \leq r \leq h$ - $r \leq z \leq h$ ## 2. Setting up the Integral The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis is: $$ I_z = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{h} \int_{r}^{h} k \cdot r^2 \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta $$ $$ I_z = k \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{h} \int_{r}^{h} r^3 \, dz \, dr \, d\theta $$ ## 3. Evaluating the Integral First, integrate with respect to $z$: $$ \int_{r}^{h} r^3 \, dz = r^3 [z]_{r}^{h} = r^3 (h - r) = hr^3 - r^4 $$ Next, integrate with respect to $r$: $$ \int_{0}^{h} (hr^3 - r^4) \, dr = \left[ h \frac{r^4}{4} - \frac{r^5}{5} \right]_{0}^{h} $$ $$ = \left( \frac{h^5}{4} - \frac{h^5}{5} \right) = \frac{5h^5 - 4h^5}{20} = \frac{h^5}{20} $$ Finally, integrate with respect to $\theta$: $$ I_z = k \int_{0}^{2\pi} \frac{h^5}{20} \, d\theta = k \cdot \frac{h^5}{20} \cdot [\theta]_{0}^{2\pi} $$ $$ I_z = k \cdot \frac{h^5}{20} \cdot 2\pi = \frac{\pi k h^5}{10} $$ ## Final Answer The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis of the solid cone is: $$ \boxed{I_z = \frac{\pi k h^5}{10}} $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
