## Problem Statement Find the mass and center of mass of the solid $E$ with the given density function $\rho(x, y, z) = 4$. The solid $E$ is bounded by: 1. The parabolic cylinder $z = 1 - y^2$ 2. The plane $x + z = 1$ (which can be written as $z = 1 - x$) 3. The plane $x = 0$ 4. The plane $z = 0$ ## Solution ### 1. Understanding the Region $E$ The solid $E$ is bounded by several surfaces. Let's identify the bounds for each variable. - **The $z$ bounds:** The solid is bounded below by $z = 0$ and above by two surfaces: $z = 1 - y^2$ and $z = 1 - x$. For a given $(x, y)$, the upper limit of $z$ is the minimum of these two surfaces: $z_{\text{max}} = \min(1 - y^2, 1 - x)$. - **The $x$ bounds:** The plane $x = 0$ is a boundary. The intersection of $z = 1 - x$ and $z = 0$ gives $x = 1$. Thus, $0 \le x \le 1$. - **The $y$ bounds:** The parabolic cylinder $z = 1 - y^2$ meets $z = 0$ at $y = \pm 1$. For any $z \in [0, 1]$, the $y$ values are bounded by $- \sqrt{1-z} \le y \le \sqrt{1-z}$. A more convenient way to set up the triple integral is to integrate with respect to $y$ first, then $z$, then $x$, or to use the projection on the $xz$-plane. Let's consider the projection of the solid on the $xz$-plane. The boundaries in the $xz$-plane are $x = 0$, $z = 0$, and $x + z = 1$ (or $z = 1 - x$). This forms a triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$. For each point $(x, z)$ in this triangle, the $y$-limits are determined by the cylinder $z = 1 - y^2$, which implies $y^2 = 1 - z$, or $y = \pm \sqrt{1-z}$. So, the region $E$ can be described as: $$ E = \{ (x, y, z) \mid 0 \le x \le 1, \ 0 \le z \le 1 - x, \ -\sqrt{1-z} \le y \le \sqrt{1-z} \} $$ ### 2. Calculating the Mass $M$ The mass $M$ is given by the triple integral of the density function $\rho(x, y, z) = 4$ over the volume $E$: $$ M = \iiint_E \rho(x, y, z) \, dV = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4 \, dy \, dz \, dx $$ First, evaluate the inner integral with respect to $y$: $$ \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4 \, dy = 4 [y]_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} = 4(2\sqrt{1-z}) = 8\sqrt{1-z} $$ Next, evaluate the integral with respect to $z$: $$ \int_0^{1-x} 8\sqrt{1-z} \, dz $$ Let $u = 1 - z$, then $du = -dz$. When $z=0, u=1$. When $z=1-x, u=x$. $$ \int_1^x 8\sqrt{u} (-du) = \int_x^1 8u^{1/2} \, du = 8 \left[ \frac{2}{3} u^{3/2} \right]_x^1 = \frac{16}{3} (1 - x^{3/2}) $$ Finally, evaluate the integral with respect to $x$: $$ M = \int_0^1 \frac{16}{3} (1 - x^{3/2}) \, dx = \frac{16}{3} \left[ x - \frac{2}{5} x^{5/2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{16}{3} \left( 1 - \frac{2}{5} \right) = \frac{16}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = \frac{16}{5} $$ Thus, the mass is $M = \frac{16}{5} = 3.2$. ### 3. Calculating the Center of Mass $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})$ The coordinates of the center of mass are given by: $$ \bar{x} = \frac{M_{yz}}{M}, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{M_{xz}}{M}, \quad \bar{z} = \frac{M_{xy}}{M} $$ where $M_{yz}$, $M_{xz}$, and $M_{xy}$ are the moments about the $yz$, $xz$, and $xy$ planes, respectively. **Moment $M_{yz}$:** $$ M_{yz} = \iiint_E x \rho \, dV = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4x \, dy \, dz \, dx $$ Integrating $y$: $$ \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4x \, dy = 8x\sqrt{1-z} $$ Integrating $z$: $$ \int_0^{1-x} 8x\sqrt{1-z} \, dz = 8x \left[ -\frac{2}{3}(1-z)^{3/2} \right]_0^{1-x} = 8x \left( -\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2} + \frac{2}{3} \right) = \frac{16}{3} (x - x^{5/2}) $$ Integrating $x$: $$ M_{yz} = \int_0^1 \frac{16}{3} (x - x^{5/2}) \, dx = \frac{16}{3} \left[ \frac{1}{2}x^2 - \frac{2}{7}x^{7/2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{16}{3} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{2}{7} \right) = \frac{16}{3} \left( \frac{7-4}{14} \right) = \frac{16}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{14} = \frac{16}{14} = \frac{8}{7} $$ So, $\bar{x} = \frac{8/7}{16/5} = \frac{8}{7} \cdot \frac{5}{16} = \frac{5}{14}$. **Moment $M_{xz}$:** $$ M_{xz} = \iiint_E y \rho \, dV = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4y \, dy \, dz \, dx $$ Since the integrand $4y$ is an odd function of $y$ and the integration limits for $y$ are symmetric about $y=0$, the integral is zero: $$ M_{xz} = 0 \implies \bar{y} = 0 $$ **Moment $M_{xy}$:** $$ M_{xy} = \iiint_E z \rho \, dV = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4z \, dy \, dz \, dx $$ Integrating $y$: $$ \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} 4z \, dy = 8z\sqrt{1-z} $$ Integrating $z$: $$ \int_0^{1-x} 8z\sqrt{1-z} \, dz $$ Let $u = 1-z \implies z = 1-u, dz = -du$. $$ \int_1^x 8(1-u)\sqrt{u} (-du) = 8 \int_x^1 (u^{1/2} - u^{3/2}) \, du = 8 \left[ \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} - \frac{2}{5}u^{5/2} \right]_x^1 $$ $$ = 8 \left[ \left(\frac{2}{3} - \frac{2}{5}\right) - \left(\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2} - \frac{2}{5}x^{5/2}\right) \right] = 8 \left[ \frac{4}{15} - \frac{2}{3}x^{3/2} + \frac{2}{5}x^{5/2} \right] = \frac{32}{15} - \frac{16}{3}x^{3/2} + \frac{16}{5}x^{5/2} $$ Integrating $x$: $$ M_{xy} = \int_0^1 \left( \frac{32}{15} - \frac{16}{3}x^{3/2} + \frac{16}{5}x^{5/2} \right) \, dx = \left[ \frac{32}{15}x - \frac{16}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{5}x^{5/2} + \frac{16}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{7}x^{7/2} \right]_0^1 $$ $$ = \frac{32}{15} - \frac{32}{15} + \frac{32}{35} = \frac{32}{35} $$ So, $\bar{z} = \frac{32/35}{16/5} = \frac{32}{35} \cdot \frac{5}{16} = \frac{2}{7}$. ### Final Answer The mass of the solid is: $$ \boxed{M = \frac{16}{5}} $$ The center of mass is: $$ \boxed{(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}) = \left(\frac{5}{14}, 0, \frac{2}{7}\right)} $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
