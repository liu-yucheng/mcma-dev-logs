# Problem Solution The problem asks for the volume of a wedge in the first octant cut from the cylinder $y^2 + z^2 = 1$ by the planes $y = x$ and $x = 1$. ## Part (a): Express the volume as a triple integral ### 1. Identify the bounding surfaces The volume is bounded by: - The cylinder: $y^2 + z^2 = 1$. In the first octant, this is $z = \sqrt{1 - y^2}$. - The plane: $y = x$. - The plane: $x = 1$. - The first octant constraints: $x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0$. ### 2. Determine the region of integration Let's analyze the projection of the solid onto the $xy$-plane. The boundaries in the $xy$-plane are: - $x = 1$ - $y = x$ - $y = 0$ (since it's in the first octant and $y=x$ and $x=1$ intersect at $(1,1)$) The region $D$ in the $xy$-plane is a triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,1)$. For a fixed $x$ in the interval $[0, 1]$, the variable $y$ ranges from $0$ to $x$. For each $(x, y) \in D$, the variable $z$ ranges from the $xy$-plane ($z=0$) up to the cylinder $z = \sqrt{1 - y^2}$. ### 3. Set up the triple integral The volume $V$ is given by the triple integral: $$ V = \iiint_E dV = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{x} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz \, dy \, dx $$ Alternatively, we can integrate in the order $dz \, dx \, dy$. If we fix $y$, then $x$ ranges from $y$ to $1$ (since the region is between $y=x$ and $x=1$). The variable $y$ ranges from $0$ to $1$. Thus, another valid representation is: $$ V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{y}^{1} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz \, dx \, dy $$ ## Part (b): Find the exact value of the triple integral We will evaluate the first integral: $$ V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{x} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz \, dy \, dx $$ ### Step 1: Evaluate the innermost integral (with respect to $z$) $$ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz = [z]_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} = \sqrt{1 - y^2} $$ ### Step 2: Evaluate the middle integral (with respect to $y$) We need to compute: $$ I_y = \int_{0}^{x} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy $$ Using the standard integral formula $\int \sqrt{a^2 - u^2} \, du = \frac{u}{2}\sqrt{a^2 - u^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\arcsin(\frac{u}{a}) + C$: $$ I_y = \left[ \frac{y}{2}\sqrt{1 - y^2} + \frac{1}{2}\arcsin(y) \right]_{0}^{x} $$ $$ I_y = \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{1 - x^2} + \frac{1}{2}\arcsin(x) - (0 + 0) = \frac{x\sqrt{1 - x^2}}{2} + \frac{\arcsin(x)}{2} $$ ### Step 3: Evaluate the outermost integral (with respect to $x$) Now we compute: $$ V = \int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{x\sqrt{1 - x^2}}{2} + \frac{\arcsin(x)}{2} \right) dx $$ We can split this into two integrals: $$ V = \frac{1}{2} \underbrace{\int_{0}^{1} x\sqrt{1 - x^2} \, dx}_{I_1} + \frac{1}{2} \underbrace{\int_{0}^{1} \arcsin(x) \, dx}_{I_2} $$ **Calculating $I_1$:** Let $u = 1 - x^2$. Then $du = -2x \, dx$, so $x \, dx = -\frac{1}{2} du$. When $x=0, u=1$; when $x=1, u=0$. $$ I_1 = \int_{1}^{0} \sqrt{u} \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) du = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{1} u^{1/2} du = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{3} $$ **Calculating $I_2$:** Use integration by parts for $\int \arcsin(x) \, dx$. Let $u = \arcsin(x)$ and $dv = dx$. Then $du = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} dx$ and $v = x$. $$ \int \arcsin(x) \, dx = x\arcsin(x) - \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} dx $$ For $\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} dx$, let $w = 1 - x^2$, $dw = -2x \, dx$. $$ \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} dx = -\frac{1}{2} \int w^{-1/2} dw = -\frac{1}{2} (2w^{1/2}) = -\sqrt{1 - x^2} $$ So, $$ I_2 = [x\arcsin(x) + \sqrt{1 - x^2}]_{0}^{1} $$ $$ I_2 = (1 \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} + 0) - (0 \cdot 0 + 1) = \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 $$ ### Final Calculation: $$ V = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{3} \right) + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \right) $$ $$ V = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{1}{6} - \frac{3}{6} = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{3} $$ **Final Answer:** The volume of the wedge is: $$ \boxed{\frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{3}} $$ --- No graphical results.
