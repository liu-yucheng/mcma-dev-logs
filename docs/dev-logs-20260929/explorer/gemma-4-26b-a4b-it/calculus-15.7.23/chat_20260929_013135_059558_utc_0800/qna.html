# Problem Solution The problem asks us to find the volume of a wedge in the first octant cut from the cylinder $y^2 + z^2 = 1$ by the planes $y = x$ and $x = 1$. ## Part (a): Expressing the volume as a triple integral ### 1. Analyze the Region $E$ To set up the triple integral $\iiint_E dV$, we first define the boundaries of the region $E$ in the first octant ($x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0$): * **The Cylinder:** $y^2 + z^2 = 1 \implies z = \sqrt{1 - y^2}$. Since we are in the first octant, $z$ ranges from $0$ to $\sqrt{1 - y^2}$. * **The Planes:** The region is bounded by $y = x$ and $x = 1$. * **The Base Region (in the $xy$-plane):** The region in the $xy$-plane is bounded by the lines $y = x$, $x = 1$, and the $x$-axis ($y = 0$, because we are in the first octant). Looking at the $xy$-plane: * The vertices of this triangular base are $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,1)$. * For a fixed $y$, $x$ ranges from $y$ to $1$. * The range for $y$ is from $0$ to $1$. ### 2. Set up the Triple Integral Using the bounds identified: * $0 \le z \le \sqrt{1 - y^2}$ * $y \le x \le 1$ * $0 \le y \le 1$ The volume $V$ is expressed as the triple integral: $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{y}^{1} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1 - y^2}} dz \, dx \, dy$$ --- ## Part (b): Finding the exact value We now evaluate the integral step-by-step. ### 1. Evaluate the Inner Integral (with respect to $z$) $$ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1 - y^2}} dz = \Big[ z \Big]_{0}^{\sqrt{1 - y^2}} = \sqrt{1 - y^2} $$ ### 2. Evaluate the Middle Integral (with respect to $x$) $$ \int_{y}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dx = \sqrt{1 - y^2} \Big[ x \Big]_{y}^{1} = \sqrt{1 - y^2} (1 - y) $$ ### 3. Evaluate the Outer Integral (with respect to $y$) The volume $V$ is: $$ V = \int_{0}^{1} (1 - y) \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy $$ We can split this into two separate integrals: $$ V = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy - \int_{0}^{1} y \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy $$ **First Integral:** $\int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy$ This represents the area of a quarter circle with radius $R = 1$ in the $y$-$z$ plane. $$ \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy = \frac{1}{4} \pi (1)^2 = \frac{\pi}{4} $$ **Second Integral:** $\int_{0}^{1} y \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy$ Use $u$-substitution: let $u = 1 - y^2$, then $du = -2y \, dy \implies y \, dy = -\frac{1}{2} du$. When $y = 0, u = 1$. When $y = 1, u = 0$. $$ \int_{0}^{1} y \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy = \int_{1}^{0} \sqrt{u} \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right) du = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{1} u^{1/2} \, du $$ $$ = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{u^{3/2}}{3/2} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} [1^{3/2} - 0^{3/2}] = \frac{1}{3} $$ ### 4.
