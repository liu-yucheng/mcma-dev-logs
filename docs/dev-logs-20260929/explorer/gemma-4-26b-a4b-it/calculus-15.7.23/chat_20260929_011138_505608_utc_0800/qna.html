# Solution to the Triple Integral Problem ## Problem Statement (a) Express the volume of the wedge in the first octant that is cut from the cylinder $y^2 + z^2 = 1$ by the planes $y = x$ and $x = 1$ as a triple integral. (b) Use either the Table of Integrals or a computer algebra system to find the exact value of the triple integral in part (a). ## Analysis ### 1. Identify the region of integration The region is in the **first octant**, which means: $$x \ge 0, \quad y \ge 0, \quad z \ge 0$$ The boundaries of the region are: 1. The cylinder: $y^2 + z^2 = 1 \implies z = \sqrt{1 - y^2}$ (since $z \ge 0$). 2. The plane $y = x$. 3. The plane $x = 1$. The projection of this region onto the $xy$-plane is bounded by the lines $y = x$, $x = 1$, and the coordinate axes in the first octant. Specifically, since we are in the first octant ($y \ge 0$) and the region is bounded by $y=x$ and $x=1$, the $y$ values range from $0$ to $1$. For a fixed $y$, $x$ ranges from $y$ up to $1$. Thus, the projection in the $xy$-plane is the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,1)$. ### 2. Setup the Triple Integral To find the volume $V$, we integrate the constant function $f(x, y, z) = 1$ over the volume $D$: $$V = \iiint_D dV$$ We can set up the integral using the order $dz \, dx \, dy$ or $dz \, dy \, dx$. **Order $dz \, dx \, dy$:** - The bounds for $z$ are from the $xy$-plane ($z=0$) to the cylinder $z = \sqrt{1 - y^2}$. - The bounds for $x$ are from the plane $y=x$ to the plane $x=1$. - The bounds for $y$ are from $0$ to $1$. Therefore: $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{y}^{1} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz \, dx \, dy$$ Alternatively, using the order $dz \, dy \, dx$: - The bounds for $z$ are $0 \le z \le \sqrt{1 - y^2}$. - For a fixed $x$, $y$ ranges from $0$ to $x$. - $x$ ranges from $0$ to $1$. $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{x} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz \, dy \, dx$$ ### 3. Evaluate the Integral Let's calculate the volume using the first setup: $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{y}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dx \, dy$$ Since $\sqrt{1 - y^2}$ is independent of $x$: $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \left( \int_{y}^{1} dx \right) dy$$ $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} (1 - y) \, dy$$ $$V = \int_{0}^{1} \left( \sqrt{1 - y^2} - y\sqrt{1 - y^2} \right) dy$$ We split this into two integrals: $$I_1 = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy$$ $$I_2 = \int_{0}^{1} y\sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy$$ **Calculating $I_1$:** $I_1$ represents the area of a quarter-circle with radius $1$. $$I_1 = \frac{1}{4} \pi (1)^2 = \frac{\pi}{4}$$ **Calculating $I_2$:** Use substitution $u = 1 - y^2$, $du = -2y \, dy \implies y \, dy = -\frac{1}{2} du$. When $y=0, u=1$. When $y=1, u=0$. $$I_2 = \int_{1}^{0} \sqrt{u} \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right) du = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{1} u^{1/2} du$$ $$I_2 = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{u^{3/2}}{3/2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} [1 - 0] = \frac{1}{3}$$ **Final Volume:** $$V = I_1 - I_2 = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{3}$$ ## Final Answer (a) The triple integral is: $$\int_{0}^{1} \int_{y}^{1} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} dz \, dx \, dy$$ (b) The exact value of the volume is: $$\boxed{\frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{3}}$$ --- Image `pyplot_0.png` not found.
