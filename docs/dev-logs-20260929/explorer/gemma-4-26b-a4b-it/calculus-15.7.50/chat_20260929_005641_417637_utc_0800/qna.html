## Problem Analysis The problem asks us to find the mass, center of mass, and moment of inertia about the $z$-axis of a solid $E$ in the first octant. **Given:** - Solid $E$ is bounded by the cylinder $y^2 + x^2 = 9$ and the planes $x = 0$, $y = 3x$, and $z = 0$. - Density function $\rho(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2$. **Critical Observation:** As stated, a cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ and the planes $x=0, y=3x, z=0$ in the first octant do not enclose a finite volume in the $z$-direction. For a "solid" to have finite mass and moments, there must be an upper bound for $z$. Based on standard calculus textbook problems (e.g., Stewart), the term "cylinder" in this context is likely a typo for the **sphere** $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$, or there is an implicit $z$-limit. However, the most mathematically consistent interpretation that makes this a standard triple integral problem is that the boundary is the **sphere** $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$. We will proceed with the assumption that the boundary is the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$, which means the top surface is $z = \sqrt{9 - x^2 - y^2}$. ### 1. Setup in Cylindrical Coordinates In cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$: - $x = r \cos \theta$ - $y = r \sin \theta$ - $z = z$ - $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ - $dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta$ **Boundaries:** - The cylinder/sphere radius: $r = 3$. - The planes $x = 0$ and $y = 3x$: - $x = 0 \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$. - $y = 3x \implies \tan \theta = 3 \implies \theta = \arctan(3)$. - Since we are in the first octant and bounded by these, $\theta$ ranges from $\arctan(3)$ to $\frac{\pi}{2}$. - The plane $z = 0$ and the sphere top: $z \in [0, \sqrt{9 - r^2}]$. **Density:** $$\rho(r, \theta, z) = r^2$$ ### 2.
