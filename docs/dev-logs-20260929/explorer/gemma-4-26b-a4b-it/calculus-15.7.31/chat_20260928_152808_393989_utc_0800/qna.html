# Problem Solution: Expressing a Triple Integral in Six Different Ways The problem asks us to express the triple integral $\iiint_E f(x, y, z) \, dV$ as an iterated integral in six different ways, where $E$ is the solid bounded by the following surfaces: 1. $y = x^2$ 2. $z = 0$ 3. $y + 2z = 4$ ## 1. Analysis of the Region $E$ To find the limits of integration, we first identify the bounding surfaces and their intersections. - **Surface 1 (Parabolic Cylinder):** $y = x^2$. This surface extends along the $z$-axis. - **Surface 2 (Base Plane):** $z = 0$. This is the $xy$-plane. - **Surface 3 (Inclined Plane):** $y + 2z = 4$, which can be rewritten as $z = \frac{4 - y}{2}$. ### Intersections: - The intersection of $z = 0$ and $y + 2z = 4$ occurs when $0 = \frac{4 - y}{2} \implies y = 4$. - The intersection of $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$ occurs when $x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2$. - The intersection of $y = x^2$ and $y + 2z = 4$ is a curve where $z = \frac{4 - x^2}{2}$. Thus, the solid $E$ is bounded: - Below by $z = 0$. - Above by $z = \frac{4 - y}{2}$. - On the "sides" by the parabolic cylinder $y = x^2$. - In the $y$-direction, $x^2 \le y \le 4$ (since $z=0 \implies y=4$). The projection of the solid onto the $xy$-plane is the region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$. ## 2. Determining the Six Iterated Integrals We consider all six possible orders of integration: $dz\,dy\,dx$, $dz\,dx\,dy$, $dy\,dz\,dx$, $dy\,dx\,dz$, $dx\,dy\,dz$, and $dx\,dz\,dy$. ### Order 1: $dz \, dy \, dx$ - $z$ varies from the bottom surface $z = 0$ to the top surface $z = \frac{4 - y}{2}$. - For a fixed $x$, $y$ varies from the parabola $y = x^2$ to the line $y = 4$. - $x$ varies from the left intersection $x = -2$ to the right intersection $x = 2$. $$\int_{-2}^{2} \int_{x^2}^{4} \int_{0}^{\frac{4-y}{2}} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx$$ ### Order 2: $dz \, dx \, dy$ - For a fixed $x$, $z$ goes from $0$ to $\frac{4 - x^2}{2}$. - $x$ varies from $-2$ to $2$. $$\int_{-2}^{2} \int_{0}^{\frac{4-x^2}{2}} \int_{x^2}^{4-2z} f(x,y,z) \, dy \, dz \, dx$$ ### Order 4: $dy \, dx \, dz$ -
