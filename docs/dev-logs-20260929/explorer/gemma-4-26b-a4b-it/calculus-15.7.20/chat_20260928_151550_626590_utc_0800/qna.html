# Problem Solution: Volume of a Solid Enclosed by Two Paraboloids The problem asks us to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the two paraboloids: 1. $y = x^2 + z^2$ 2. $y = 8 - x^2 - z^2$ ## 1. Understanding the Geometry The equations describe two paraboloids that open along the $y$-axis. - $y = x^2 + z^2$ is a paraboloid opening in the positive $y$-direction, with its vertex at $(0,0,0)$. - $y = 8 - (x^2 + z^2)$ is a paraboloid opening in the negative $y$-direction, with its vertex at $(0,8,0)$. The solid is the region trapped between these two surfaces. ## 2. Finding the Intersection To find where the two surfaces intersect, we set the expressions for $y$ equal to each other: $$x^2 + z^2 = 8 - x^2 - z^2$$ $$2(x^2 + z^2) = 8$$ $$x^2 + z^2 = 4$$ The intersection is a circle in the $xz$-plane with radius $R = \sqrt{4} = 2$, located at the height $y = 4$. ## 3. Setting up the Triple Integral Since the boundary of the intersection is a circle in the $xz$-plane, it is most efficient to use **cylindrical coordinates**. In this problem, the "vertical" axis is the $y$-axis. We define our cylindrical coordinates as: - $x = r \cos \theta$ - $z = r \sin \theta$ - $y = y$ - $dV = r \, dy \, dr \, d\theta$ The bounds for the coordinates are: - $\theta$ ranges from $0$ to $2\pi$. - $r$ ranges from $0$ to $2$ (the radius of the intersection). - $y$ ranges from the bottom surface to the top surface. - Bottom surface: $y = x^2 + z^2 = r^2$ - Top surface: $y = 8 - (x^2 + z^2) = 8 - r^2$ The volume $V$ is given by: $$V = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{r^2}^{8-r^2} r \, dy \, dr \, d\theta$$ ## 4. Evaluating the Integral First, we integrate with respect to $y$: $$\int_{r^2}^{8-r^2} r \, dy = r [y]_{r^2}^{8-r^2} = r \left( (8 - r^2) - r^2 \right) = r(8 - 2r^2) = 8r - 2r^3$$ Next, we integrate with respect to $r$: $$\int_{0}^{2} (8r - 2r^3) \, dr = \left[ 4r^2 - \frac{2r^4}{4} \right]_{0}^{2} = \left[ 4r^2 - \frac{r^4}{2} \right]_{0}^{2}$$ $$= \left( 4(2)^2 - \frac{2^4}{2} \right) - 0 = (16 - 8) = 8$$ Finally, we integrate with respect to $\theta$: $$V = \int_{0}^{2\pi} 8 \, d\theta = [8\theta]_{0}^{2\pi} = 16\pi$$ ## Final Answer The volume of the solid is: $$\boxed{16\pi}$$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
