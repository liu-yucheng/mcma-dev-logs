## Setting Up the Problem The solid is bounded by: - The cylinder $x^2+z^2=4$ (axis along the $y$-axis, radius $2$) - The plane $y=-1$ - The plane $y+z=4$, i.e. $y=4-z$ Because the cylinder is independent of $y$, every horizontal slice of the solid in a plane $y=\text{const}$ is the same disk $D=\{(x,z):x^2+z^2\le 4\}$. The lower and upper $y$-boundaries are constant and linear in $z$ respectively, so it is most natural to integrate **with $y$ on the inside**, projecting the solid onto the $xz$-plane. For each $(x,z)$ inside the disk $D$, the variable $y$ ranges from $-1$ up to $4-z$. ## The Triple Integral $$ V=\iiint_E dV=\iint_{D}\int_{-1}^{\,4-z}dy\,dA=\iint_{D}(5-z)\,dA . $$ Use polar coordinates in the $xz$-plane: $x=r\cos\theta,\ z=r\sin\theta$, with $0\le r\le 2,\ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi$ (and Jacobian $r$): $$ V=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^2\bigl(5-r\sin\theta\bigr)\,r\,dr\,d\theta . $$ ## Evaluating the Inner Integral $$ \int_0^2 (5r-r^2\sin\theta)\,dr=\left[\tfrac{5r^2}{2}-\tfrac{r^3\sin\theta}{3}\right]_0^2=10-\tfrac{8}{3}\sin\theta . $$ ## Evaluating the Outer Integral $$ V=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\left(10-\tfrac{8}{3}\sin\theta\right)d\theta=10\cdot 2\pi-\tfrac{8}{3}\bigl[-\cos\theta\bigr]_0^{2\pi}=20\pi-0 . $$ The $\sin\theta$ term integrates to $0$ — this is the "slanted" plane contributing an extra half-volume wedge above $y=5$ on one side and a missing wedge on the other; they exactly cancel. ## Result $$ \boxed{\,V=20\pi\,} $$ (Equivalent viewpoint: the volume equals $\text{(base area)}\times\text{(average height)}=(\pi\cdot 2^2)\cdot 5=20\pi$, because the slanted top $y=4-z$ has average value $5$ over the disk centered at $z=0$.) ## Visualizations The 3D picture shows the cylindrical lateral surface with the flat bottom $y=-1$ and the tilted top $y=4-z$ clipped to the disk. The 2D cross-sections make the geometry explicit: in the $xz$-plane the base is a disk of radius $2$; in the $yz$-plane the solid is the trapezoidal region between $y=-1$ and $y=4-z$ over $|z|\le 2$, whose height equals $5-z$.
