# Mass and Center of Mass of Solid $E$ ## Setting Up the Region The solid $E$ is bounded by four surfaces: - **Parabolic cylinder:** $z = 1 - y^2$ (parallel to the $x$-axis) - **Plane:** $x + z = 1$, i.e. $x = 1 - z$ - **Plane:** $x = 0$ (the $yz$-plane) - **Plane:** $z = 0$ (the $xy$-plane) The parabolic cylinder meets the plane $z=0$ along the lines $y = \pm 1$, and reaches its maximum $z = 1$ at $y = 0$. For each fixed $y$, the cross-section in the $xz$-plane is a triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$. This gives the bounds: $$-1 \le y \le 1, \qquad 0 \le z \le 1 - y^2, \qquad 0 \le x \le 1 - z.$$ ## Computing the Mass With constant density $\rho = 4$, the mass is $$ m \;=\; \iiint_E \rho\, dV \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-y^2}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-z} dx\,dz\,dy. $$ **Integrate in $x$:** $$ \int_{0}^{1-z} dx \;=\; 1 - z. $$ **Integrate in $z$:** Set $u = 1 - z$, so $z = 0 \Rightarrow u = 1$ and $z = 1-y^2 \Rightarrow u = y^2$: $$ \int_{0}^{1-y^2}(1-z)\,dz \;=\; \int_{y^2}^{1} u\,du \;=\; \frac{1 - y^4}{2}. $$ **Integrate in $y$:** $$ m \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-y^4}{2}\,dy \;=\; 2\left[y - \frac{y^5}{5}\right]_{-1}^{1} \;=\; 2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right) \;=\; \boxed{\frac{16}{5}}. $$ (Image `pyplot_1.png`. Alt text: 3D Solid with center of mass) *Figure 1: Solid $E$ — a wedge whose top is the parabolic cylinder $z=1-y^2$, with the purple dot showing the center of mass at $(5/14,\,0,\,2/7)$.* ## Computing the Center of Mass Since $E$ and $\rho$ are symmetric about the plane $y=0$, we immediately get $$ \bar y \;=\; 0. $$ **Moment $M_{yz}$ (for $\bar x$):** $$ M_{yz} \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-y^2}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-z} x\,dx\,dz\,dy \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-y^2}\frac{(1-z)^2}{2}\,dz\,dy. $$ Substituting $u = 1 - z$: $$ \int_{0}^{1-y^2}(1-z)^2\,dz \;=\; \int_{y^2}^{1} u^2\,du \;=\; \frac{1-y^6}{3}. $$ $$ M_{yz} \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-y^6}{6}\,dy \;=\; \frac{2}{3}\!\left(2-\frac{2}{7}\right) \;=\; \frac{8}{7}. $$ $$ \bar x \;=\; \frac{M_{yz}}{m} \;=\; \frac{8/7}{16/5} \;=\; \frac{5}{14}. $$ **Moment $M_{xy}$ (for $\bar z$):** $$ M_{xy} \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-y^2}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-z} z\,dx\,dz\,dy \;=\; 4\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1-y^2} z(1-z)\,dz\,dy. $$ Let $u = 1 - y^2$. Then $$ \int_{0}^{u} z(1-z)\,dz \;=\; \frac{u^2}{2} - \frac{u^3}{3} \;=\; \frac{u^2(3-2u)}{6}. $$ With $u = 1-y^2$ and $3 - 2u = 1 + 2y^2$: $$ M_{xy} \;=\; \frac{2}{3}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-y^2)^2(1+2y^2)\,dy \;=\; \frac{2}{3}\int_{-1}^{1}(1 - 3y^4 + 2y^6)\,dy. $$ $$ M_{xy} \;=\; \frac{2}{3}\cdot 2\left(1 - \frac{3}{5} + \frac{2}{7}\right) \;=\; \frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{24}{35} \;=\; \frac{32}{35}. $$ $$ \bar z \;=\; \frac{M_{xy}}{m} \;=\; \frac{32/35}{16/5} \;=\; \frac{2}{7}. $$ Image `pyplot_2.png` not found. *Figure 2: Projections of $E$ — left: $yz$-plane (parabolic); middle: $xz$-plane slice at $y=0$ (triangle); right: $xy$-plane at $z=0$ (rectangle). Each shows the corresponding coordinate of the center of mass.* ## Final Answer $$ m \;=\; \frac{16}{5}, \qquad (\bar x, \bar y, \bar z) \;=\; \left(\frac{5}{14},\; 0,\; \frac{2}{7}\right). $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
