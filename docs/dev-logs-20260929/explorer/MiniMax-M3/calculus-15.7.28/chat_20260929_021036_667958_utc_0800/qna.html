## Identifying the Solid Reading the iterated integral from inside out, $$\int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{2}\!\int_{0}^{4-y^{2}} dx\,dz\,dy,$$ the three variables are bounded by $$0\le y\le 2,\qquad 0\le z\le 2,\qquad 0\le x\le 4-y^{2}.$$ So the solid $E$ is the set of points $$E=\{(x,y,z):\;0\le y\le 2,\;0\le z\le 2,\;0\le x\le 4-y^{2}\}.$$ ### Bounding surfaces | Surface | Equation | Type | |---|---|---| | $y=0$ and $y=2$ | two planes | parallel sides | | $z=0$ and $z=2$ | two planes | bottom & top | | $x=0$ | plane | flat vertical face | | $x=4-y^{2}$ | parabolic cylinder (parallel to $z$) | curved vertical face | ### Geometric picture The cross-section at height $z$ (and hence also at the base $z=0$) is the parabolic region in the $xy$-plane bounded by the parabola $x=4-y^{2}$, the line $y=0$, and the line $y=2$. This region extends vertically from $z=0$ to $z=2$. The solid is therefore a **wedge-like block** standing on the parabolic base, with the parabolic cylinder $x=4-y^{2}$ as one face and the flat plane $x=0$ as the opposite face. It has six corners: $$A(0,0,0),\ B(4,0,0),\ C(0,2,0),\ D(0,0,2),\ E(4,0,2),\ F(0,2,2).$$ (Because $y=2$ lies exactly at the tip of the parabola, $C$ and $F$ are also the points where the parabolic face meets the back plane $x=0$.) ### Volume (sanity check) $$V=\int_0^2\!\int_0^2(4-y^{2})\,dz\,dy=2\!\int_0^2(4-y^{2})\,dy=2\Big[4y-\tfrac{y^{3}}{3}\Big]_0^2=2\!\left(8-\tfrac{8}{3}\right)=\tfrac{32}{3}.$$ ### Projections (useful when sketching by hand) - **Onto the $xy$-plane** ($z=0$ face): the parabolic region bounded by $y=0$, $y=2$, and the curve $x=4-y^{2}$. - **Onto the $yz$-plane** ($x=0$ face): the square $0\le y\le 2,\ 0\le z\le 2$. - **Onto the $xz$-plane** ($y=0$ face): the rectangle $0\le x\le 4,\ 0\le z\le 2$.
