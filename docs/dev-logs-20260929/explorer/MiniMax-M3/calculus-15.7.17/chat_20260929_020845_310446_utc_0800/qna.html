## Problem Evaluate the triple integral $$\iiint_E x\,dV$$ where $E$ is bounded by the paraboloid $x = 4y^2 + 4z^2$ and the plane $x = 4$. ## Setting up the region The paraboloid opens along the positive $x$-axis. For a point inside $E$, the $x$-coordinate is sandwiched between the paraboloid and the plane: $$4y^2 + 4z^2 \;\le\; x \;\le\; 4.$$ The shadow (projection) of $E$ on the $yz$-plane is obtained by intersecting the two surfaces, $4y^2+4z^2 = 4$, which is the **unit disk** $y^2+z^2 \le 1$. (Image `pyplot_1.png`. Alt text: 3D view of region E) The 3D figure shows the paraboloid (blue) capped by the plane $x=4$ (orange disk). A red line segment shows the typical $x$-integration interval $[4y^2+4z^2,\,4]$ at a fixed $(y,z)$. ## Reducing to a 2D integral Using $x$ as the innermost variable: $$\iiint_E x\,dV = \iint_{y^2+z^2\le 1}\;\left[\int_{4y^2+4z^2}^{4} x\,dx\right] dA.$$ **Inner integral in $x$:** $$\int_{4y^2+4z^2}^{4} x\,dx = \frac{16}{2} - \frac{(4y^2+4z^2)^2}{2} = 8 - 8(y^2+z^2)^2.$$ So the integral becomes $$I = \iint_{y^2+z^2\le 1}\bigl[\,8 - 8(y^2+z^2)^2\,\bigr]\,dA.$$ ## Switching to polar coordinates in the $yz$-plane Let $y = r\cos\theta$, $z = r\sin\theta$, with $r\in[0,1]$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$. Then $y^2+z^2 = r^2$ and $dA = r\,dr\,d\theta$: Image `pyplot_2.png` not found. **Left:** the unit disk in the $yz$-plane with concentric rings illustrating the polar shells $r=$ const. **Right:** the result of the inner $x$-integration, $8(1-r^4)$, evaluated as a function of $r$ — this is exactly the function whose (weighted) area we need. $$I = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 \bigl[8 - 8r^4\bigr]\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\!\int_0^1 \bigl(8r - 8r^5\bigr)dr.$$ ## Final evaluation $$\int_0^1 (8r - 8r^5)\,dr = \left[4r^2 - \tfrac{4}{3}r^6\right]_0^1 = 4 - \frac{4}{3} = \frac{8}{3}.$$ Multiplying by the $2\pi$ from $\theta$: $$I = 2\pi\cdot \frac{8}{3} = \boxed{\dfrac{16\pi}{3}}.$$ This was confirmed symbolically with SymPy and numerically (≈ 16.7552), and the inner integral reduces the problem cleanly to polar coordinates because the paraboloid cross-section $x = 4r^2$ depends only on the radius $r$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
