# Volume of the Solid Enclosed by Two Paraboloids ## Setting Up the Problem We need to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the two paraboloids: $$y = x^2 + z^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad y = 8 - x^2 - z^2$$ **Step 1 — Find where the paraboloids meet.** Setting them equal: $$x^2 + z^2 = 8 - x^2 - z^2 \implies 2(x^2+z^2) = 8 \implies x^2 + z^2 = 4$$ The two paraboloids intersect in a **circle of radius 2** lying in the plane $y = 4$. **Step 2 — Choose coordinates.** Both paraboloids are circular and share the **y-axis** as their axis of symmetry, so cylindrical coordinates with the y-axis as the polar axis are natural. Set $$x = r\cos\theta,\quad z = r\sin\theta,\quad \text{so } x^2+z^2 = r^2,\quad dV = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dy$$ In these coordinates the two paraboloids simplify to $$y = r^2 \quad(\text{lower})\qquad\text{and}\qquad y = 8 - r^2 \quad(\text{upper})$$ ## Setting Up the Triple Integral The bounds are: | Variable | Range | |----------|-------| | $\theta$ | $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$ | | $r$ | $0 \le r \le 2$ (the intersection radius) | | $y$ | $r^2 \le y \le 8-r^2$ | $$V = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\!\int_{0}^{2}\!\!\int_{r^2}^{8-r^2} r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta$$ ## Evaluating the Integral **Inner integral** (over $y$): $$\int_{r^2}^{8-r^2} r\,dy = r\bigl[(8-r^2)-r^2\bigr] = 8r - 2r^3$$ **Middle integral** (over $r$): $$\int_{0}^{2}(8r-2r^3)\,dr = \left[4r^2 - \tfrac{r^4}{2}\right]_{0}^{2} = 16 - 8 = 8$$ **Outer integral** (over $\theta$): $$\int_{0}^{2\pi} 8\,d\theta = 16\pi$$ ## Result $$\boxed{\,V = 16\pi\,}$$ ## Why Cylindrical Coordinates Are the Right Choice A typical horizontal slice through the solid at height $y$ (with $0 \le y \le 4$) is a **washer** whose - outer radius is $r = \sqrt{8-y}$ (from the upper paraboloid $y = 8-r^2$), - inner radius is $r = \sqrt{y}$ (from the lower paraboloid $y = r^2$), with cross-sectional area $$A(y) = \pi\bigl[(8-y) - y\bigr] = \pi(8-2y).$$ Equivalently, slicing perpendicular to the y-axis gives $$V = \int_{0}^{4} A(y)\,dy = \pi\int_{0}^{4}(8-2y)\,dy = \pi\bigl[8y - y^2\bigr]_{0}^{4} = 16\pi,$$ confirming the same answer.
