## Step 1 — Identify the solid $E$ $E$ is bounded below by the plane $y = 0$ and above by the parabolic cylinder $y = 4 - x^2 - 4z^2$. They meet when $y = 0$, giving the curve $$x^2 + 4z^2 = 4 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{x^2}{2^2} + \frac{z^2}{1^2} = 1,$$ an **ellipse** in the $xz$-plane with semi-axes $2$ (in $x$) and $1$ (in $z$). So $$E=\bigl\{(x,y,z):\;x^2+4z^2\le 4,\;\ 0\le y\le 4-x^2-4z^2\bigr\}.$$ The projection onto the three coordinate planes is therefore: | plane | projection $D$ | |------|------| | $xz$ | $\dfrac{x^2}{4}+z^2\le 1$ (ellipse, area $2\pi$) | | $xy$ | $0\le y\le 4-x^2$, $|x|\le 2$ (parabolic) | | $yz$ | $0\le y\le 4-4z^2$, $|z|\le 1$ (parabolic) | A 3D picture of $E$ (parabolic cylinder on top, plane on the bottom) is shown below. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ## Step 3 — Strategy for finding bounds For each order of integration, the bounds of the *innermost* variable depend on the next variables out, the bounds of the middle variable depend on the outermost, and the outermost variable has *constant* bounds coming from the projection of $E$ onto the corresponding plane. Because the upper surface is given explicitly as $y = 4 - x^2 - 4z^2$ and the lower surface as $y = 0$, the simplest descriptions arise when **$y$ is the innermost** variable (its bounds are $0$ to $4 - x^2 - 4z^2$). When $y$ is in the middle or outermost, we must solve $0 \le y \le 4 - x^2 - 4z^2$ for the variable we want to be the innermost, producing expressions like $\pm\sqrt{4-x^2-4z^2}$, $\pm 2\sqrt{1-z^2}$, etc. I illustrate the derivation for **order $dz\,dy\,dx$** in the figure below, then list all six orders. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_2.tex)

--- ## Step 4 — The six iterated integrals Let $\displaystyle D_z(x)=\frac12\sqrt{4-x^2},\quad D_y(x,y)=\frac12\sqrt{4-x^2-y},\quad D_y(z)=\frac12\sqrt{4-4z^2},\quad D_x(y)=\sqrt{4-y},\quad D_z(y,x)=\frac12\sqrt{4-y-x^2},\quad D_x(z)=2\sqrt{1-z^2}$. --- **(1) $dz\,dy\,dx$ — $x$ outermost; $y$ innermost in last step ($y$ bounds are clean):** $$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-2}^{2}\!\!\int_{0}^{4-x^2}\!\!\int_{-\tfrac12\sqrt{4-x^2-y}}^{\tfrac12\sqrt{4-x^2-y}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx.$$ --- **(2) $dy\,dz\,dx$ — $x$ outermost; $y$ innermost (cleanest of all six):** $$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-2}^{2}\!\!\int_{-\tfrac12\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\tfrac12\sqrt{4-x^2}}\!\!\int_{0}^{4-x^2-4z^2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx.$$ --- **(3) $dz\,dy\,dx$ with $y$ outermost — $y \in [0,4]$, then $x$, then $z$:** $$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{0}^{4}\!\!\int_{-\sqrt{4-y}}^{\sqrt{4-y}}\!\!\int_{-\tfrac12\sqrt{4-y-x^2}}^{\tfrac12\sqrt{4-y-x^2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy.$$ --- **(4) $dx\,dz\,dy$ — $y$ outermost; $z$ middle; $x$ innermost:** $$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{0}^{4}\!\!\int_{-\tfrac12\sqrt{4-y}}^{\tfrac12\sqrt{4-y}}\!\!\int_{-\sqrt{4-y-4z^2}}^{\sqrt{4-y-4z^2}} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy.$$ --- **(5) $dy\,dx\,dz$ — $z$ outermost; $y$ innermost (clean):** $$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{-2\sqrt{1-z^2}}^{2\sqrt{1-z^2}}\!\!\int_{0}^{4-4z^2-x^2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz.$$ --- **(6) $dx\,dy\,dz$ — $z$ outermost; $y$ middle; $x$ innermost:** $$\iiint_E f\,dV=\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{4-4z^2}\!\!\int_{-\sqrt{4-4z^2-y}}^{\sqrt{4-4z^2-y}} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz.$$ --- **Verification.** Setting $f\equiv 1$, every one of the six integrals equals the volume of $E$. Using the parametrization $x=2r\cos\theta,\ z=r\sin\theta,\ 0\le r\le 1,\ 0\le\theta\le 2\pi$ of the base ellipse (Jacobian $2r$): $$\mathrm{Vol}(E)=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 4(1-r^2)\cdot 2r\,dr\,d\theta=8\!\int_0^1 r(1-r^2)\,dr\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!d\theta=8\cdot\tfrac14\cdot 2\pi=4\pi.$$ Symbolic integration confirms $\iiint_E 1\,dV = 4\pi$ for all six orders, and numerical integration with $f=x^2+y^2+z^2$ gives $\iiint_E f\,dV = 14\pi \approx 43.98$ for every order, as expected. $$\boxed{\,\iiint_E f\,dV = \int_{-2}^{2}\!\!\int_{0}^{4-x^2}\!\!\int_{-\frac12\sqrt{4-x^2-y}}^{\frac12\sqrt{4-x^2-y}} f\,dz\,dy\,dx \;=\; \int_{-2}^{2}\!\!\int_{-\frac12\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\frac12\sqrt{4-x^2}}\!\!\int_{0}^{4-x^2-4z^2} f\,dy\,dz\,dx\,}$$
