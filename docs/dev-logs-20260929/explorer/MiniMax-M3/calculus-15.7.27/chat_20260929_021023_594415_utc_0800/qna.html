# Sketching the Solid for $\displaystyle \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{1-x}\!\!\int_0^{2-2x} dy\,dz\,dx$ ## Step 1 — Translate the limits into inequalities Reading the bounds from the inside out: | Variable | Lower bound | Upper bound | Equivalent inequality | |----------|-------------|-------------|-----------------------| | $y$ | $0$ | $2-2x$ | $0 \le y \le 2-2x$ | | $z$ | $0$ | $1-x$ | $0 \le z \le 1-x$ | | $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0 \le x \le 1$ | So the solid is the set of points $(x,y,z)$ satisfying all of $$y\ge 0,\qquad y+2x\le 2,\qquad z\ge 0,\qquad x+z\le 1,\qquad x\ge 0.$$ ## Step 2 — Identify the five bounding surfaces The five faces of the solid are the portions of the following planes inside the region: | Face | Equation | Description | |------|----------|-------------| | Bottom | $y = 0$ | the $xz$-plane | | Top | $y = 2-2x$ | a slanted plane | | Back | $x = 0$ | the $yz$-plane | | Front‑left | $z = 0$ | the $xy$-plane | | Slanted side | $x+z = 1$ | a plane through $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,0,1)$ | ## Step 3 — Vertices of the solid Intersecting the planes three at a time gives five vertices: $$A(0,0,0),\quad B(1,0,0),\quad C(0,0,1),\quad D(0,2,0),\quad E(0,2,1).$$ (Image `pyplot_1.png`. Alt text: 3D solid) (Image `pgfplots_1.png`. Alt text: 3D solid (PGFplots)) The solid looks like a triangular wedge: the base is the right triangle $ABC$ in the $xz$-plane, the back face $ACED$ is a $1\times 2$ rectangle standing in the $yz$-plane, the front‑left face $ABD$ is a triangle, and the slanted faces $CBE$ (top) and $BDE$ (side $x+z=1$) complete the figure. ## Step 4 — Two‑dimensional views **Base triangle (the $xz$-plane, $y=0$).** The two inner integrals tell us the projection of the solid onto $y=0$ is the triangle bounded by $x=0$, $z=0$ and $x+z=1$. **Cross‑section (the $xy$-plane, $z=0$).** At any fixed $x\in[0,1]$ the variable $y$ runs from $0$ to $2-2x$, independent of $z$. So slicing the solid with the plane $z=0$ produces the right triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,2)$. Image `pyplot_2.png` not found. Image `pgfplots_2.png` not found. ## Step 5 — Volume (as a sanity check) Carrying out the integration gives $$ V \;=\; \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{1-x}(2-2x)\,dz\,dx \;=\; \int_0^1 (2-2x)(1-x)\,dx \;=\; \int_0^1 (2-4x+2x^2)\,dx \;=\; \Bigl[2x-2x^2+\tfrac{2}{3}x^3\Bigr]_0^1 \;=\; \boxed{\dfrac{2}{3}}. $$ In particular, the solid has volume $\dfrac{2}{3}$ — consistent with viewing it as a "wedge" whose base triangle has area $\tfrac12$ and whose vertical height tapers linearly from $2$ (at $x=0$) to $0$ (at $x=1$): $$ V \;=\; \int_0^1 \underbrace{(1-x)}_{\text{length in }z}\cdot\underbrace{(2-2x)}_{\text{height in }y}\,dx \;=\; \tfrac{2}{3}. $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
