## Setup The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis for a body of constant density $k$ is $$ I_z \;=\; \iiint_E k\,(x^2+y^2)\,dV . $$ The cone $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\le z\le h$ is rotationally symmetric, so cylindrical coordinates are ideal: $$ x = r\cos\theta,\quad y = r\sin\theta,\quad z = z, \quad\text{with}\quad x^2+y^2 = r^2,\;\; dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta . $$ The bounds become $0\le\theta\le 2\pi,\; 0\le r\le h,\; r\le z\le h$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- Visualization of the solid cone (vertex at origin, apex at top $z=h$, base radius $h$). ## Evaluation $$ I_z \;=\; \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{h}\!\!\int_r^{h} k\,r^2\cdot r\;dz\,dr\,d\theta \;=\; k\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!d\theta\!\int_0^{h}\! r^3\bigl(z\bigr)\big|_{r}^{h}dr \;=\; 2\pi k\!\int_0^{h}\! r^3(h-r)\,dr . $$ Computing the $r$-integral, $$ \int_0^{h}\! r^3(h-r)\,dr \;=\; h\!\int_0^{h}\!r^3\,dr - \int_0^{h}\!r^4\,dr \;=\; \frac{h^5}{4} - \frac{h^5}{5} \;=\; \frac{h^5}{20}. $$ Therefore $$ I_z \;=\; 2\pi k\cdot\frac{h^5}{20} \;=\; \frac{\pi k h^5}{10}. $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)

--- The 2D cross-section in the $(r,z)$-plane shows the triangular integration region $r\le z\le h$ that we integrate over. ## Verification The mass of the cone is $$ M \;=\; k\cdot \frac{1}{3}\pi h^2\cdot h \;=\; \frac{\pi k h^3}{3}. $$ The standard textbook formula for a solid cone of mass $M$ with base radius $R=h$ gives $I_z = \dfrac{3MR^2}{10}$. Plugging in: $$ \frac{3}{10}\cdot\frac{\pi k h^3}{3}\cdot h^2 \;=\; \frac{\pi k h^5}{10}. \quad\checkmark $$ A direct symbolic computation with sympy also returns $\pi k h^5 / 10$, confirming the result. ## Final Answer $$ \boxed{\,I_z \;=\; \dfrac{\pi k h^5}{10}\,} $$
