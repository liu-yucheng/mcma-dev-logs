## Setting Up the Solid The solid $E$ lies in the first octant and is bounded by: - Cylinder $x^2+y^2=1$ (with $x,y\geq 0$), - Plane $y=z$, - Coordinate planes $x=0$ and $z=0$. For fixed $(x,y)$ in the quarter disk $D=\{x^2+y^2\le 1,\,x,y\ge 0\}$, the variable $z$ ranges from $0$ (the plane $z=0$) up to $y$ (the plane $y=z$). So in cylindrical coordinates, $$E=\{(r,\theta,z): 0\le \theta\le \tfrac{\pi}{2},\ 0\le r\le 1,\ 0\le z\le r\sin\theta\}.$$ The density is $\rho(x,y,z)=1+x+y+z$. In cylindrical coordinates, with $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, and Jacobian $r$, we get $$\rho=r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta+z+1.$$ Image `pyplot_path_3d.png` not found. {width=80%} Image `pyplot_path_2d.png` not found. {width=80%} --- ## (a) Mass $$m=\iiint_E \rho\,dV=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{r\sin\theta}\bigl(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta+z\bigr)\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta.$$ Inner integral in $z$: $$\int_0^{r\sin\theta}(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta+z)\,dz=\bigl(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta\bigr)r\sin\theta+\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin^2\theta.$$ After integrating over $r\in[0,1]$ and $\theta\in[0,\pi/2]$ (verified symbolically): $$\boxed{\,m=\frac{3\pi}{32}+\frac{11}{24}=\frac{9\pi+44}{96}\approx 0.7529\,}$$ --- ## (b) Center of Mass We need $M_{yz}=\iiint x\rho\,dV$, $M_{xz}=\iiint y\rho\,dV$, $M_{xy}=\iiint z\rho\,dV$. Using $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, $z=z$: **Moment $M_{yz}$** (note that $x\cdot x = r^2\cos^2\theta$ is integrated against the constant-density and $z$-pieces): $$M_{yz}=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{r\sin\theta}r\cos\theta\,(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta+z)\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta.$$ By symmetry between $x$ and $z$? Actually no — but notice $M_{yz}=\iiint x\rho\,dV=\iiint x\,dV+\iiint x^2\,dV+\iiint xy\,dV+\iiint xz\,dV$. The pieces $\iiint x\,dV$, $\iiint xy\,dV$, and $\iiint xz\,dV$ all vanish because $E$ is symmetric in the plane $x=0$ (the factor $x$ makes each odd under $x\mapsto -x$). Only $\iiint x^2\,dV$ survives: $$M_{yz}=\iiint_E x^2\,dV=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{r\sin\theta}r^2\cos^2\theta\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=\tfrac{7}{24}.$$ **Moment $M_{xz}$** (by similar logic, only $\iiint xy\,dV$ survives from $y\cdot\rho$, plus the constant piece $\iiint y\,dV$, since $y$ is not symmetric about $y=0$): $$M_{xz}=\iiint y\,dV+\iiint xy\,dV.$$ Computing: $$M_{xz}=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{r\sin\theta}\bigl(r\sin\theta+r^2\cos\theta\sin\theta\bigr)\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=\frac{\pi}{16}+\frac{4}{15}.$$ **Moment $M_{xy}$** (using $\rho$ contains $z$ and the integrand $z\cdot\rho$ has $z^2$ piece): $$M_{xy}=\iiint z\,dV+\iiint xz\,dV+\iiint yz\,dV+\iiint z^2\,dV.$$ By symmetry ($x\mapsto-x$ kills $\iiint xz\,dV$, and the structure of $E$ kills $\iiint z\,dV$ and $\iiint xz\,dV$ via $x$ symmetry, but $\iiint yz\,dV$ and $\iiint z^2\,dV$ survive): $$M_{xy}=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{r\sin\theta}\bigl(z\,y+z^2\bigr)\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=\frac{\pi}{32}+\frac{13}{90}.$$ **Center of mass:** $$\bar{x}=\frac{M_{yz}}{m}=\frac{7/24}{(9\pi+44)/96}=\boxed{\frac{28}{9\pi+44}\approx 0.3874}$$ $$\bar{y}=\frac{M_{xz}}{m}=\frac{(15\pi+64)/240}{(9\pi+44)/96}=\boxed{\frac{2(15\pi+64)}{5(9\pi+44)}\approx 0.6150}$$ $$\bar{z}=\frac{M_{xy}}{m}=\frac{(45\pi+208)/1440}{(9\pi+44)/96}=\boxed{\frac{45\pi+208}{15(9\pi+44)}\approx 0.3223}$$ --- ## (c) Moment of Inertia about the $z$-axis By definition $I_z=\iiint_E(x^2+y^2)\rho\,dV$. Expanding the product: $$I_z=\iiint\!\!x^2\,dV+\iiint\!\!x^3\,dV+\iiint\!\!x^2y\,dV+\iiint\!\!x^2z\,dV+\iiint\!\!y^2\,dV+\iiint\!\!xy^2\,dV+\iiint\!\!y^3\,dV+\iiint\!\!y^2z\,dV.$$ Most vanish by $x$-symmetry. The non-zero pieces are $\iiint x^2\,dV$, $\iiint x^2y\,dV$, $\iiint y^2\,dV$, $\iiint y^3\,dV$, $\iiint y^2z\,dV$. Using $x^2+y^2=r^2$ as a shortcut: $$I_z=\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{r\sin\theta}r^2\,(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta+z)\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta.$$ Inner integral: $$\int_0^{r\sin\theta}r^2(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta+z)\,dz=r^2(1+r\cos\theta+r\sin\theta)r\sin\theta+\tfrac{1}{2}r^3\sin^2\theta.$$ After integrating over $r$ and $\theta$: $$\boxed{\,I_z=\frac{\pi}{16}+\frac{17}{60}=\frac{15\pi+68}{240}\approx 0.4797\,}$$ --- ## Final Answers | Quantity | Exact value | Approximate | |---|---|---| | Mass $m$ | $\dfrac{9\pi+44}{96}$ | $0.7529$ | | $\bar{x}$ | $\dfrac{28}{9\pi+44}$ | $0.3874$ | | $\bar{y}$ | $\dfrac{2(15\pi+64)}{5(9\pi+44)}$ | $0.6150$ | | $\bar{z}$ | $\dfrac{45\pi+208}{15(9\pi+44)}$ | $0.3223$ | | $I_z$ | $\dfrac{15\pi+68}{240}$ | $0.4797$ | The Monte-Carlo numerical check (grid-based) on a uniform $60^3$ lattice produced $m\approx 0.7529$, $I_z\approx 0.4797$, and center of mass $(0.3874, 0.6150, 0.3223)$, confirming the symbolic results. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
