# Mass and Center of Mass of the Solid $E$ ## Setting Up the Region The solid $E$ lies **above** the planar region $D$ bounded by $y=\sqrt{x}$, $y=0$, and $x=1$, and **below** the plane $z = 1+x+y$, with constant density $\rho(x,y,z)=2$. Reading the region $D$ from the picture (parabola on top, $x$-axis below, $x=1$ on the right), the natural description is a **type I (vertically simple) region**: $$ D = \big\{(x,y) : 0 \le x \le 1,\; 0 \le y \le \sqrt{x}\big\}, \qquad E = \big\{(x,y,z) : (x,y)\in D,\; 0 \le z \le 1+x+y \big\}. $$ ## Step 1: The Mass Since $\rho = 2$ is constant, the mass is $$ m = \iiint_E \rho\, dV = \int_0^1 \int_0^{\sqrt{x}} \int_0^{1+x+y} 2 \, dz\, dy\, dx. $$ The innermost integral gives the height of the solid above $(x,y)$: $$ \int_0^{\sqrt{x}} 2(1+x+y)\, dy = 2\Big[(1+x)y + \tfrac{y^2}{2}\Big]_0^{\sqrt{x}} = 2\sqrt{x}(1+x) + x. $$ Then $$ m = \int_0^1 \left(2\sqrt{x} + 2x^{3/2} + x\right) dx = \left[\tfrac{4}{3}x^{3/2} + \tfrac{4}{5}x^{5/2} + \tfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1 = \frac{4}{3} + \frac{4}{5} + \frac{1}{2} = \boxed{\frac{79}{30}}. $$ ## Step 2: The Moments With constant density $\rho=2$, the moments are $$ M_y = \iiint_E 2x\, dV, \qquad M_x = \iiint_E 2y\, dV, \qquad M_z = \iiint_E 2z\, dV. $$ **Moment about the $yz$-plane** (using the height integral $H(x) = \sqrt{x}(1+x) + \tfrac{x}{2}$ from Step 1, since $2\int_0^{\sqrt x}\!\int_0^{1+x+y} dz\,dy = 2H(x)$... more directly, $\int_0^{\sqrt{x}}\!\int_0^{1+x+y} 2x\, dz\, dy = 2x\,H(x)$... carrying out the integrals): $$ M_y = \int_0^1 \int_0^{\sqrt{x}} 2x(1+x+y)\, dy\, dx = \int_0^1 \left(2x^{3/2} + 2x^{5/2} + x^2\right) dx = \frac{4}{5} + \frac{4}{7} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{179}{105}. $$ **Moment about the $xz$-plane:** $$ M_x = \int_0^1 \int_0^{\sqrt{x}} 2y(1+x+y)\, dy\, dx = \int_0^1 \left(\sqrt{x} + \tfrac{4}{3}x^{3/2} + \tfrac{x^2}{2}\right) dx = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{8}{15} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{11}{10}. $$ **Moment about the $xy$-plane** (using $2\int_0^{z_{\text{top}}} z\, dz = (1+x+y)^2$): $$ M_z = \int_0^1 \int_0^{\sqrt{x}} (1+x+y)^2\, dy\, dx = \frac{571}{210}. $$ ## Step 3: The Center of Mass $$ \bar{x} = \frac{M_y}{m} = \frac{179/105}{79/30} = \frac{358}{553}, \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{M_x}{m} = \frac{11/10}{79/30} = \frac{33}{79}, \qquad \bar{z} = \frac{M_z}{m} = \frac{571/210}{79/30} = \frac{571}{553}. $$ So the center of mass is $$ (\bar{x},\,\bar{y},\,\bar{z}) = \left(\frac{358}{553},\; \frac{33}{79},\; \frac{571}{553}\right) \approx (0.647,\; 0.418,\; 1.033). $$ **Sanity checks:** $0 < \bar{x} < 1$, $0 < \bar{y} < 1$, and $0 < \bar{z} < 3$ — the point lies inside the solid (marked with a star in the figure above). Also $\bar z \approx \tfrac{1}{2}(1+\bar x + \bar y)$-ish behavior is expected since the solid's height grows linearly; the computed value $\bar z \approx 1.03$ sits comfortably between $0$ and the mean top height, as it should. ## Key Takeaways - For a constant density, the mass equals $\rho \times$ (volume), and the moments $M_y, M_x, M_z$ use $x, y, z$ as weights. - Reduce each triple integral to iterated single integrals: integrate over $z$ first (the height of the solid), then over the 2D region $D$. - Always sketch $D$ first: the boundary curves dictate the limits, and here $y=\sqrt{x}$ being the *upper* curve makes the region type I with $0 \le y \le \sqrt{x}$. - Verify the answer lies within the solid's bounding box: $(\bar x, \bar y, \bar z) \approx (0.65, 0.42, 1.03) \in [0,1]\times[0,1]\times[0,3]$. ✓ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
