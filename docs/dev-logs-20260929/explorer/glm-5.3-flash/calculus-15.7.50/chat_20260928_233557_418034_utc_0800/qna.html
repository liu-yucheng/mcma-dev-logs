# Solid $E$ with density $\rho(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2$ **The solid.** $E$ is bounded by the cylinder $y^2+z^2=9$, and the planes $x=0$, $y=3x$ (i.e. $x=y/3$), and $z=0$, all in the first octant. For a fixed $y\in[0,3]$, a vertical slice runs over $0\le z\le\sqrt{9-y^2}$ (inside the cylinder, above $z=0$) and $0\le x\le y/3$ (between the plane $x=0$ and the plane $y=3x$). Hence $$ E=\Bigl\{(x,y,z):\ 0\le y\le 3,\quad 0\le z\le\sqrt{9-y^2},\quad 0\le x\le \frac{y}{3}\Bigr\}. $$ The 3D picture below shows the solid (curved quarter-cylinder side, bottom disk $z=0$, back face $x=0$, slanted face $x=y/3$) with the computed center of mass marked in red. **(a) The mass.** Integrate in the order $z$, then $x$, then $y$: $$ m=\iiint_E \rho\,dV=\int_0^3\!\!\int_0^{y/3}\!\!\int_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}(x^2+y^2)\,dz\,dx\,dy. $$ Inner $z$-integral: $(x^2+y^2)\sqrt{9-y^2}$. Inner $x$-integral: $$ \int_0^{y/3}(x^2+y^2)\,dx=\frac{1}{3}\Bigl(\frac{y}{3}\Bigr)^{\!3}+y^2\cdot\frac{y}{3}=\frac{y^3}{81}+\frac{y^3}{3}=\frac{28y^3}{81}. $$ So $m=\dfrac{28}{81}\displaystyle\int_0^3 y^3\sqrt{9-y^2}\,dy$. With $y=3\sin\theta$, $dy=3\cos\theta\,d\theta$, $\sqrt{9-y^2}=3\cos\theta$: $$ \int_0^3 y^3\sqrt{9-y^2}\,dy=243\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=243\cdot\frac{2}{15}=\frac{162}{5}, $$ using $\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\tfrac12 B(2,\tfrac32)=\tfrac{2}{15}$. Therefore $$ m=\frac{28}{81}\cdot\frac{162}{5}=\frac{56}{5}=11.200. $$ **(b) The center of mass.** The moments are $M_{yz}=\iiint_E x\rho\,dV$, $M_{xz}=\iiint_E y\rho\,dV$, $M_{xy}=\iiint_E z\rho\,dV$. *About $x$* ($M_{yz}$): $\displaystyle\int_0^{y/3}(x^3+y^2x)\,dx=\frac{y^4}{324}+\frac{y^4}{18}=\frac{19y^4}{324}$, so $$ M_{yz}=\frac{19}{324}\int_0^3 y^4\sqrt{9-y^2}\,dy=\frac{19}{324}\cdot 729\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^4\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta =\frac{19}{324}\cdot 729\cdot\frac{\pi}{32}=\frac{171\pi}{128}. $$ *About $y$* ($M_{xz}$): $\displaystyle\int_0^{y/3}(x^2y+y^3)\,dx=\frac{y^4}{81}+\frac{y^4}{3}=\frac{28y^4}{81}$, so $$ M_{xz}=\frac{28}{81}\int_0^3 y^4\sqrt{9-y^2}\,dy=\frac{28}{81}\cdot\frac{729\pi}{32}=\frac{63\pi}{8}. $$ *About $z$* ($M_{xy}$): the $z$-integral contributes $\tfrac12(9-y^2)$, so $$ M_{xy}=\int_0^3\frac{28y^3}{81}\cdot\frac{9-y^2}{2}\,dy=\frac{14}{81}\int_0^3 y^3(9-y^2)\,dy=\frac{14}{81}\cdot\frac{729}{12}=\frac{21}{2}. $$ Hence $$ \bar{x}=\frac{M_{yz}}{m}=\frac{171\pi/128}{56/5}=\frac{855\pi}{7168}\approx 0.375,\qquad \bar{y}=\frac{M_{xz}}{m}=\frac{63\pi/8}{56/5}=\frac{315\pi}{448}\approx 2.209,\qquad \bar{z}=\frac{M_{xy}}{m}=\frac{21/2}{56/5}=\frac{15}{16}=0.938. $$ **(c) The moment of inertia about the $z$-axis.** Here $r^2=x^2+y^2$ equals the density itself, so $$ I_z=\iiint_E \underbrace{(x^2+y^2)}_{r^2}\rho\,dV=\int_0^3\!\!\int_0^{y/3}\!\!\int_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}(x^2+y^2)^2\,dz\,dx\,dy. $$ Inner $x$-integral: $$ \int_0^{y/3}(x^4+2x^2y^2+y^4)\,dx=\frac{y^5}{1215}+\frac{2y^5}{81}+\frac{y^5}{3}=\frac{436\,y^5}{1215}. $$ With the same substitution $y=3\sin\theta$ ($\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^5\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\tfrac12B(3,\tfrac32)=\tfrac{8}{105}$): $$ I_z=\frac{436}{1215}\int_0^3 y^5\sqrt{9-y^2}\,dy=\frac{436}{1215}\cdot 2187\cdot\frac{8}{105}=\frac{436}{1215}\cdot\frac{5832}{35}=\frac{10464}{175}\approx 59.794. $$ **Why the projection helps.** The second figure shows the projection of $E$ onto the $yz$-plane: a quarter disk of radius 3. Every $y$-integral above is of the form $\int_0^3 y^k\sqrt{9-y^2}\,dy$, which is exactly an integral over this quarter disk — this is why the substitution $y=3\sin\theta$ (a polar ray in the $yz$-plane) reduces everything to elementary trigonometric integrals. **Answers (correct to three decimal places):** $$ \boxed{\text{(a) } m=\frac{56}{5}=11.200 \qquad \text{(b) } (\bar x,\bar y,\bar z)=\left(\frac{855\pi}{7168},\ \frac{315\pi}{448},\ \frac{15}{16}\right)\approx(0.375,\ 2.209,\ 0.938) \qquad \text{(c) } I_z=\frac{10464}{175}=59.794} $$ *Verification:* all five integrals ($m$, $M_{yz}$, $M_{xz}$, $M_{xy}$, $I_z$) were cross-checked by high-order Gauss–Legendre quadrature of the reduced $y$-integrands (agreement to $\sim10^{-6}$), by Monte-Carlo sampling of the original triple integrals, and symbolically with a computer algebra system. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
