## Setting Up the Region (Stewart, 15.7, Exercise 23) The wedge is bounded by four surfaces: - **Cylinder** $y^2 + z^2 = 1$ (a circular cylinder along the $x$-axis) - **Plane** $y = x$ (a slanted plane through the $z$-axis) - **Plane** $x = 1$ (vertical plane parallel to the $yz$-plane) - **Plane** $z = 0$ (the $xy$-plane, since we are in the **first octant**, $x, y, z \ge 0$) Inside the cylinder we need $y^2 + z^2 \le 1$, so $0 \le z \le \sqrt{1 - y^2}$. Between the planes we need $y \le x \le 1$. Since $x$ must reach $1$, we need $y \le 1$, so $0 \le y \le 1$. Image `pyplot_1.png` not found. **Type 3 region:** The projection of the wedge onto the $yz$-plane is the quarter disk $D = \{(y,z) : y^2 + z^2 \le 1,\ y \ge 0,\ z \ge 0\}$, and above each point of $D$ the solid lies between $x = y$ and $x = 1$: $$ E = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le y \le 1,\ 0 \le z \le \sqrt{1-y^2},\ y \le x \le 1\} $$ Image `pyplot_1.png` not found. ## Part (a): The Triple Integral $$ V = \iiint_E dV = \int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}}\!\!\int_{y}^{1} dx\, dz\, dy $$ ## Part (b): Evaluating the Integral **Step 1 — innermost ($x$) integral:** the cross-section at fixed $(y,z)$ is a segment of length $1 - y$: $$ \int_{y}^{1} dx = 1 - y $$ **Step 2 — middle ($z$) integral:** a Table entry / CAS gives $\int \sqrt{a^2 - u^2}\, du = \frac{u}{2}\sqrt{a^2-u^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}\frac{u}{a}$ with $a = 1$, $u = y$: $$ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}} (1-y)\, dz = (1-y)\sqrt{1-y^2} $$ **Step 3 — outer ($y$) integral:** $$ V = \int_0^1 (1-y)\sqrt{1-y^2}\, dy = \underbrace{\int_0^1 \sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy}_{\text{quarter circle} \;=\; \pi/4} \;-\; \underbrace{\int_0^1 y\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy}_{u=1-y^2\;=\;1/3} $$ ## Verification and Final Answer Both computations confirmed with SymPy (integrating in this order and in an independent order $dz\,dy\,dx$-style check): $$ \int_0^1 \sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy = \frac{\pi}{4}, \qquad \int_0^1 y\sqrt{1-y^2}\,dy = \frac{1}{3} $$ $$ V = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.45206 $$ $$ \boxed{\text{(a) } V = \int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-y^2}}\int_{y}^{1} dx\, dz\, dy \qquad \text{(b) } V = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{1}{3}} $$ **Key takeaway:** For solids bounded by a cylinder along one axis, projecting onto the plane perpendicular to that axis (here the $yz$-plane) turns the cylinder into a simple 2D region, and the planes give the bounds of the remaining variable. The split $\int (1-y)\sqrt{1-y^2}dy$ then separates into a quarter-circle area (trig substitution or Table entry) minus a power-rule integral ($u$-substitution). --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
