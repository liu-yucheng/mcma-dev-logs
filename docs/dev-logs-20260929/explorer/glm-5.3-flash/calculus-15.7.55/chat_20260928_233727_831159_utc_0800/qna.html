# Triple Integral over the Best Region — Stewart 15.7, Exercise 55 ## Part (a): Find the region $E$ that maximizes the integral Let $f(x,y,z) = 1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2$. The key principle: > **To maximize $\iiint_D f\,dV$ over all regions $D$, take $D$ to be exactly the set where $f \ge 0$.** Adding any point where $f<0$ decreases the integral, and omitting any point where $f>0$ throws away positive contribution. Find where $f \ge 0$: $$ 1 - x^2 - 2y^2 - 3z^2 \ge 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 \le 1. $$ This is the **solid ellipsoid** centered at the origin with semi-axes $a = 1$, $b = 1/\sqrt{2}$, $c = 1/\sqrt{3}$ (the boundary ellipsoid passes through $(\pm1,0,0)$, $(0,\pm 1/\sqrt2,0)$, $(0,0,\pm 1/\sqrt3)$). $$ \boxed{E = \{(x,y,z)\;:\; x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 \le 1\}} $$ On this ellipsoid the integrand is $\ge 0$ (maximum $f=1$ at the origin), and it vanishes on the boundary — see the $y=0$ cross-section below. ## Part (b): Exact maximum value of the integral **Ellipsoidal (stretched-spherical) coordinates.** Map the unit ball $B$ onto $E$ by $$ x = u, \qquad y = \frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad z = \frac{w}{\sqrt{3}}. $$ Then $x^2+2y^2+3z^2 = u^2+v^2+w^2$, so $E$ corresponds exactly to $B: u^2+v^2+w^2\le 1$, and the Jacobian is $$ \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 1/\sqrt2 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1/\sqrt3 \end{vmatrix} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}. $$ Therefore $$ \iiint_E (1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2)\,dV = \frac{1}{\sqrt6}\iiint_B (1-u^2-v^2-w^2)\,dV. $$ **Spherical coordinates on $B$** ($u = \rho\sin\varphi\cos\theta$, etc., $dV = \rho^2\sin\varphi\,d\rho\,d\varphi\,d\theta$): $$ \iiint_B (1-\rho^2)\,dV = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^{\pi}\!\int_0^1 (1-\rho^2)\rho^2\sin\varphi\,d\rho\,d\varphi\,d\theta = 2\pi\cdot 2\int_0^1 (\rho^2-\rho^4)\,d\rho = 4\pi\left(\frac13-\frac15\right) = \frac{8\pi}{15}. $$ Hence $$ \iiint_E (1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2)\,dV = \frac{1}{\sqrt6}\cdot\frac{8\pi}{15} = \frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt6} = \boxed{\frac{4\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{45} \approx 0.68403} $$ **Cross-check (Cartesian):** slicing perpendicular to $z$, the inner integral over $z$ gives $\tfrac{4}{3\sqrt3}(1-x^2-2y^2)^{3/2}$, and integrating over the ellipse $x^2+2y^2\le 1$ with $x = r\cos t$, $y = \tfrac{r\sin t}{\sqrt2}$ gives the same $\tfrac{4\sqrt6\pi}{45}$. A Monte-Carlo simulation also agrees to $\sim 10^{-4}$. ## How to solve problems of this type 1. **Sign test:** the maximizing region is always ${(x,y,z) : f(x,y,z)\ge 0}$ — include positive integrand, exclude negative. 2. **Recognize the quadric:** $f = 1 - x^2-2y^2-3z^2 \ge 0$ is an ellipsoid; its semi-axes come from the coefficients ($a=1,\ b=1/\sqrt2,\ c=1/\sqrt3$). 3. **Stretch to a ball:** substitution $x = au,\ y = bv,\ z = cw$ turns ellipsoidal integrals into ball integrals, at the cost of the Jacobian $abc$. 4. **Finish with spherical coordinates:** $\iiint_B(1-\rho^2)dV = 8\pi/15$; scale by $abc = 1/\sqrt6$. ## Plots **Step 1 — The region $E$ in 3D (integrand is positive exactly on $E$):** --- Left panel: interior points of $E$ colored by integrand value (max $=1$ at origin, $0$ on boundary). Right panel: $E$ is the unit ball scaled by $(1,\,1/\sqrt2,\,1/\sqrt3)$ — shown by the $y=0$ cross-sections (dashed circle vs. red ellipse). **Step 2 — Why the boundary of $E$ is where $f=0$ ($y=0$ slice):** --- Left: the integrand (at $y=0$) is positive inside the black contour $x^2+3z^2=1$ and negative outside — any point outside the ellipsoid would subtract from the integral. Right: horizontal slices of the positive region $x^2+2y^2\le 1-3z^2$ shrink as $|z|$ grows toward $1/\sqrt3$. ## Final Answers **(a)** $E$ is the solid ellipsoid $$ E = \{(x,y,z)\;:\; x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 \le 1\}, $$ with semi-axes $1$, $1/\sqrt{2}$, $1/\sqrt{3}$ along the $x$, $y$, $z$ axes. **(b)** The maximum value of the triple integral is $$ \boxed{\iiint_E (1-x^2-2y^2-3z^2)\,dV = \frac{4\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{45} \approx 0.68403} $$ --- No graphical results.
