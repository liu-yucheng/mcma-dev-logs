# Volume of the "Bumpy Sphere" (Stewart 15.9, Exercise 45) **Problem.** The surfaces $\rho = 1 + \frac{1}{5}\sin(m\theta)\sin(n\phi)$ model tumors. For $m = 6$, $n = 5$, use a CAS to find the volume enclosed by $$ \rho = 1 + \tfrac{1}{5}\sin(6\theta)\sin(5\phi). $$ ## Step 1 — Set up the spherical triple integral In spherical coordinates the volume element is $dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\, d\rho\, d\phi\, d\theta$, and the solid is the set of points with $0 \le \rho \le f(\theta,\phi)$, where $f(\theta,\phi) = 1 + \frac{1}{5}\sin(6\theta)\sin(5\phi)$. Since $f > 0$ everywhere ($\frac45 \le f \le \frac65$), the volume is $$ V = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{f(\theta,\phi)} \rho^2\sin\phi\, d\rho\, d\phi\, d\theta = \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi} f(\theta,\phi)^3 \sin\phi \, d\phi\, d\theta . $$ The bumpy surface itself is shown below (it matches the textbook figure). --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- ## Step 2 — What the CAS computes (and why it collapses to a clean answer) Expanding the cube with $a = \frac{1}{5}\sin(6\theta)\sin(5\phi)$: $$ f^3 = 1 + 3a + 3a^2 + a^3. $$ The key point: every term containing an **odd power of $\sin(6\theta)$ integrates to zero** over $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$ (verified symbolically: $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin(6\theta)\,d\theta = 0$ and $\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^3(6\theta)\,d\theta = 0$). So the $3a$ and $a^3$ terms vanish, leaving $$ V = \frac{1}{3}\left[ \underbrace{\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi\, d\phi\, d\theta}_{4\pi} + 3\cdot\frac{1}{25}\underbrace{\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2(6\theta)\,d\theta}_{=\,\pi}\cdot\underbrace{\int_0^{\pi}\sin^2(5\phi)\sin\phi\, d\phi}_{=\,100/99} \right]. $$ The $\phi$-integral evaluates (by parts, twice, or by CAS) to $$ \int_0^{\pi} \sin^2(5\phi)\sin\phi\, d\phi = \frac{100}{99}. $$ Therefore $$ V = \frac{1}{3}\left(4\pi + \frac{3}{25}\cdot\pi\cdot\frac{100}{99}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\left(4\pi + \frac{4\pi}{33}\right) = \frac{4\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{34}{33} = \frac{136\pi}{99}. $$ **Numerical check (CAS / fine grid):** direct grid quadrature of $\frac13\int f^3\sin\phi$ gives $4.31572$, and $\frac{136\pi}{99} = 4.31572\ldots$ — exact agreement. Note the bumps **increase** the volume relative to the unit sphere $V = \frac{4\pi}{3} \approx 4.1888$: since $\mathbb{E}[a^2] > 0$ while odd terms average out, convexity of $\rho \mapsto \rho^3$ means outward bumps contribute more than inward dents. ## Step 3 — Geometric picture A slice at $\theta = \pi/12$ (where $\sin 6\theta = 1$, maximal bumps) versus the slice at $\theta = 0$ (a perfect circle) shows the $5$-lobed structure in $\phi$ that drives the correction term $\int \sin^2(5\phi)\sin\phi\, d\phi$. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)
