# Problem: Volume of the "Bumpy Sphere" (Stewart 15.9.45) The tumor model surface is $$ \rho = 1 + \tfrac{1}{5}\sin 6\theta \,\sin 5\phi , $$ and we must find the volume it encloses (using a CAS as the book instructs). ## Step 1 — Describe the solid in spherical coordinates The surface gives the radius as a function of the direction $(\theta,\phi)$, so every ray from the origin enters the solid at $\rho=0$ and exits at that radius. Hence $$ E = \left\{(\rho,\theta,\phi) \;\middle|\; 0 \le \rho \le 1 + \tfrac{1}{5}\sin 6\theta \sin 5\phi,\; 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi,\; 0 \le \phi \le \pi \right\}. $$ ## Step 2 — Set up the volume integral In spherical coordinates the volume element is $dV = \rho^2 \sin\phi\, d\rho\, d\theta\, d\phi$, so $$ V = \iiint_E dV = \int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{1+\frac15\sin 6\theta \sin 5\phi} \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho\, d\theta\, d\phi . $$ ## Step 3 — What a CAS (e.g. Mathematica) computes Integrating in $\rho$ first (with `Integrate`), $$ \int_0^{\rho_{\max}} \rho^2\, d\rho = \frac{\rho_{\max}^3}{3}, \qquad \rho_{\max} = 1 + \tfrac15 \sin 6\theta \sin 5\phi, $$ so $$ V = \frac13 \int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} \left(1 + \tfrac15 \sin 6\theta \sin 5\phi\right)^3 \sin\phi \, d\theta\, d\phi \;=\; \boxed{\dfrac{136\pi}{99} \approx 4.3157}. $$ **Why the answer is so clean (sanity check by hand).** Expanding with $s = \sin 6\theta\sin 5\phi$, $a = \tfrac15$: $$ (1+as)^3 = 1 + 3as + 3a^2 s^2 + a^3 s^3 . $$ - The linear term $\propto\sin 6\theta$ and cubic term $\propto \sin^3 6\theta$ integrate to $0$ over $\theta \in [0,2\pi]$. - The constant term contributes $\frac13 \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^{\pi} \sin\phi\, d\phi = \frac13(2\pi)(2) = \frac{4\pi}{3}$. - The $a^2$ term contributes $\frac13\cdot 3a^2 \left(\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2 6\theta\, d\theta\right)\left(\int_0^{\pi} \sin^2 5\phi \sin\phi\, d\phi\right) = a^2 \cdot \pi \cdot \frac{100}{99} = \frac{4\pi}{99}$. Sum: $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} + \dfrac{4\pi}{99} = \dfrac{132\pi + 4\pi}{99} = \dfrac{136\pi}{99}$. ✓ (Verified with SymPy: exactly $136\pi/99 \approx 4.31572$.) ## Teaching notes — how to attack this kind of problem 1. **Radial-surface solids in spherical coordinates:** whenever the boundary is given as $\rho = f(\theta,\phi)$ and $f > 0$, the solid is a "star-shaped" ball and $$V = \frac13 \int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta,\phi)^3 \sin\phi \, d\theta\, d\phi .$$ 2. **Oscillating perturbations average out:** for $\rho = 1 + a\sin m\theta \sin n\phi$, only the constant and $a^2$ terms survive integration — bumps and dents do not cancel in volume because of the $\rho^2$ weighting, but they *do* nearly cancel in a symmetric way. 3. **CAS usage:** in Mathematica, `Integrate[rho^2 Sin[phi], {phi,0,Pi}, {theta,0,2 Pi}, {rho,0,1+Sin[6 theta] Sin[5 phi]/5}]` returns $136\pi/99$ directly. ## Figures The 3D bumpy sphere (matches the textbook figure) and the 2D "radius map" $\rho(\theta,\phi)$ — the checkerboard of $m\times n = 6\times 5$ bumps that gets integrated over the parameter rectangle $[0,2\pi]\times[0,\pi]$: see the graphical results below (3D pyplot, 3D PGFplots, 2D pyplot, 2D PGFplots). ## Final answer $$ V = \iiint_E dV = \int_0^{\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{1+\frac15\sin 6\theta\sin 5\phi} \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho\, d\theta\, d\phi = \frac{136}{99}\pi \approx 4.3157 . $$ $$ \boxed{V = \dfrac{136\pi}{99} \approx 4.3157} $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
