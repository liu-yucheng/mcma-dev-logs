# Average Value of $f(x,y,z) = x^2z + y^2z$ over the Solid $E$ **Problem.** Find $$ f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV, \qquad f(x,y,z)=x^2z+y^2z, $$ where $E$ is enclosed by the paraboloid $z = 1 - x^2 - y^2$ and the plane $z=0$. ## Step 1: Understand the region (use cylindrical coordinates) The paraboloid opens downward from its vertex at $(0,0,1)$ and meets the plane $z=0$ where $$ 1-x^2-y^2 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^2+y^2 = 1, $$ so $E$ is a round paraboloid "dome" over the unit disk $D: x^2+y^2\le 1$. Since both the region and the integrand are symmetric about the $z$-axis, **cylindrical coordinates** are ideal: $$ x = r\cos\theta,\quad y = r\sin\theta,\quad z=z,\qquad dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta, $$ with the key facts $x^2+y^2 = r^2$ and $0 \le z \le 1-r^2$, $0\le r\le 1$, $0\le\theta\le 2\pi$. ## Step 2: Compute the volume $V(E)$ $$ V(E) = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{1-r^2} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 r(1-r^2)\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi\left[\frac{r^2}{2}-\frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^1 = 2\pi\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2}. $$ ## Step 3: Compute the triple integral of $f$ Factor the integrand and convert with the Jacobian: $$ f = x^2z + y^2z = (x^2+y^2)z = r^2 z, \qquad \text{so } f\,dV = r^2z\cdot r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = r^3 z\,dz\,dr\,d\theta. $$ Then $$ \iiint_E (x^2z+y^2z)\,dV = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{1-r^2} r^3 z\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^1 \frac{r^3(1-r^2)^2}{2}\,dr\,d\theta. $$ The $\theta$-integral gives $2\pi$, and by expansion: $$ \int_0^1 \frac{r^3(1-2r^2+r^4)}{2}\,dr = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{2}{6}+\frac{1}{8}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{6-8+3}{24} = \frac{1}{48}. $$ Therefore $$ \iiint_E f\,dV = 2\pi\cdot\frac{1}{48} = \frac{\pi}{24}. $$ ## Step 4: Average value $$ f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{V(E)}\iiint_E f\,dV = \frac{\pi/24}{\pi/2} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{24}{24}\cdot\frac{1}{12}\cdot 1 = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{12}} $$ **Verification (SymPy):** the volume integral gives $\pi/2$, the mass-type integral gives $\pi/24$, and their ratio is exactly $1/12$. ✔ **Takeaway for this problem type:** average value problems over solids reduce to *two* triple integrals (one for $\iiint f\,dV$, one for volume with integrand $1$). Always look for a coordinate system matching the region's symmetry — here $x^2+y^2$ appears in both the surface $z = 1-(x^2+y^2)$ and the integrand $f=(x^2+y^2)z$, so cylindrical coordinates make every step a simple one-variable power integral. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
