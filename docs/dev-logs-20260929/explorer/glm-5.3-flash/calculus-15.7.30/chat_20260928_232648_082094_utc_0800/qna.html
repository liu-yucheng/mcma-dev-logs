# Problem Express $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ as an iterated integral in **six different ways**, where $E$ is the solid bounded by $$ y^2+z^2 = 9, \qquad x = -2, \qquad x = 2. $$ # Step 1: Understand the solid $E$ The equation $y^2+z^2=9$ involves only $y$ and $z$, so it is a **circular cylinder of radius 3 whose axis is the $x$-axis**. The planes $x=-2$ and $x=2$ cap it on the ends. Hence $$ E = \big\{(x,y,z) : -2 \le x \le 2,\; y^2+z^2 \le 9 \big\}. $$ Every cross-section at a fixed $x$ is the same disk $y^2+z^2\le 9$ in the $yz$-plane. Solving $y^2+z^2=9$ for either variable: $$ y = \pm\sqrt{9-z^2} \qquad \text{or} \qquad z = \pm\sqrt{9-y^2}, \qquad -3 \le y,z \le 3. $$ The 3D figure shows the solid; the 2D figure shows the $yz$-cross-section disk together with the horizontal strip ($y$ inner limits) and vertical strip ($z$ inner limits) used to build the iterated integrals. # Step 2: The strategy for all six orders - If the **outer** variables are $y$ and $z$ (in either order), the disk projects onto a square $[-3,3]\times[-3,3]$ in those variables, and the inner limit for the remaining one of $y,z$ is $\pm\sqrt{9-(\text{other})^2}$, while $x$ runs over the full interval $-2\le x\le 2$. - If the **outer** variable is $x$, then $-2\le x\le 2$ and the two inner integrals sweep the disk $y^2+z^2\le 9$. # Step 3: The six iterated integrals **Orders with $z$ innermost** (integrate $z$ first): $$ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx $$ $$ \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy $$ **Orders with $y$ innermost:** $$ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx $$ $$ \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz $$ **Orders with $x$ innermost:** $$ \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}\int_{-2}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy $$ $$ \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}\int_{-2}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz $$ # Step 4: Verification Taking $f=1$, all six orders must give the volume $V = \pi r^2 h = \pi\cdot 3^2\cdot 4 = 36\pi$. Symbolic integration of each of the six iterated integrals above confirms $V = 36\pi$ for every ordering, so all six descriptions of $E$ are correct. $$ \boxed{\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} f\,dz\,dy\,dx,\quad \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} f\,dz\,dx\,dy,\quad \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}} f\,dy\,dz\,dx,\quad \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}} f\,dy\,dx\,dz,\quad \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-y^2}}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}}\int_{-2}^{2} f\,dx\,dz\,dy,\quad \int_{-3}^{3}\int_{-\sqrt{9-z^2}}^{\sqrt{9-z^2}}\int_{-2}^{2} f\,dx\,dy\,dz} $$ # Key takeaways 1. **Identify the surface first.** An equation missing one variable (here $x$) describes a cylinder parallel to that variable's axis — so $y^2+z^2=9$ is a cylinder along the $x$-axis, not along the $z$-axis. 2. **Only one inner limit pair involves a square root.** Because $E$ is bounded by flat planes in $x$ and a circular cylinder in the $yz$-direction, whichever of $y,z$ is integrated innermost gets the $\pm\sqrt{9-(\text{other})^2}$ limits; $x$ always gets constant limits $-2$ and $2$. 3. **Sanity-check with $f=1$.** For a good description of $E$, every ordering should yield the same volume $36\pi$ — a cheap, powerful check for these "express in six ways" problems. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
