# Expressing $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ in Six Orders **Problem.** Express $\iiint_E f(x,y,z)\,dV$ as an iterated integral in six different ways, where $E$ is the solid bounded by $x=2$, $y=2$, $z=0$, and $x+y-2z=2$. ## Step 1 — Identify the solid Write the slanted plane as $z = \dfrac{x+y-2}{2}$. Since $z \ge 0$ on $E$, we need $x+y \ge 2$; since $x \le 2$ and $y \le 2$, the solid lives in the box $0 \le x \le 2$, $0 \le y \le 2$, $0 \le z \le 1$, above $z=0$ and below $z = \frac{x+y-2}{2}$. **Vertices** (found by intersecting planes three at a time): $$ A=(2,0,0),\qquad B=(0,2,0),\qquad C=(2,2,0),\qquad D=(2,2,1) $$ So $E$ is a **tetrahedron**. Check: $\text{Vol}(E) = \tfrac{1}{6}|\det(B-A,\,C-A,\,D-A)| = \tfrac{2}{3}$. Image `pyplot_1.png` not found. A horizontal slice at height $z$ is the triangle $x+y \ge 2+2z$, $x\le2$, $y\le2$, i.e. $2z \le y \le 2$ and $2+2z-y \le x \le 2$: $$ \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz $$ **Order 6: $dy\,dx\,dz$** — same slices with $x$ and $y$ swapped: $$ \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz $$ ## Step 4 — Verification Setting $f \equiv 1$, every one of the six integrals must equal the volume of the tetrahedron: $$ \int_0^2\int_{2-x}^2 \frac{x+y-2}{2}\,dy\,dx = \int_0^2 \frac{1}{4}\big(x+y-2\big)^2\Big|_{y=2-x}^{y=2} dx = \int_0^2 \frac{x^2}{4}\,dx = \frac{x^3}{12}\Big|_0^2 = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}\;\checkmark $$ This matches $\text{Vol}(E) = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}(2)(2)(1) = \frac{2}{3}$ (area of base $\triangle ABC$ in $z=0$ is $\tfrac12(2)(2)=2$, height 1). Computer verification (sympy) confirms all six iterated integrals evaluate to $\frac{2}{3}$. ## Key takeaways 1. **Sketch first.** Find the vertices by intersecting the surfaces three at a time — this tells you the solid's shape (here a tetrahedron) and the range of each variable. 2. **Each order corresponds to a shadow.** Outer two integrals always describe the projection of $E$ onto the plane of those two variables; the innermost limits come from the two surfaces bounding $E$ in that direction. 3. **Solve the slanted plane for each variable.** The same plane $x+y-2z=2$ plays a different role in each order: it gives $z=\frac{x+y-2}{2}$, $y = 2-x+2z$, or $x = 2-y+2z$. 4. **Check your answer.** With $f=1$ all six orders must give the same number, the volume $\frac{2}{3}$ — a built-in correctness check for the limits. $\boxed{\begin{aligned} \iiint_E f\,dV &= \int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-x}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f\,dz\,dy\,dx \\ &= \int_{0}^{2}\int_{2-y}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x+y-2}{2}} f\,dz\,dx\,dy \\ &= \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\int_{2-x+2z}^{2} f\,dy\,dz\,dx \\ &= \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{y}{2}}\int_{2-y+2z}^{2} f\,dx\,dz\,dy \\ &= \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f\,dx\,dy\,dz \\ &= \int_{0}^{1}\int_{2z}^{2}\int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f\,dy\,dx\,dz \end{aligned}}$ --- No graphical results.
