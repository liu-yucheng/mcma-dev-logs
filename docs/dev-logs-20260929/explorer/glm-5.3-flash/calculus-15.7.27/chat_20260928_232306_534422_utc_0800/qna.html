# Sketching the Solid from an Iterated Integral **Problem.** Sketch the solid whose volume is given by $$ \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{2-2z} dy\,dz\,dx. $$ ## Step 1 — Read the limits outward-in The integral is written in the order $dy\,dz\,dx$, so we read from the *innermost* variable outward: - $y$ runs from $y=0$ up to $y = 2-2z$ — this is a **slanted plane** $y + 2z = 2$; - $z$ runs from $z=0$ up to $z = 1-x$ — this is a **slanted plane** $z + x = 1$; - $x$ runs from $x=0$ to $x=1$. So the solid $E$ is $$ E = \{(x,y,z) : 0 \le x \le 1,\; 0 \le z \le 1-x,\; 0 \le y \le 2-2z\}. $$ ## Step 2 — Identify the bounding surfaces $$ x = 0, \qquad x = 1, \qquad y = 0, \qquad z = 0, \qquad y = 2-2z. $$ Since $y \le 2-2z$ depends only on $z$, it is useful to project onto the $yz$-plane: for each $z \in [0,1]$, $y$ ranges over $0 \le y \le 2-2z$. This projection is the **triangle** with vertices $(z,y) = (0,0)$, $(0,2)$, $(1,0)$, shown below. The line $y = 2-2z$ descends from $2$ to $0$ as $z$ goes from $0$ to $1$. ## Step 3 — Find the vertices (corners of the solid) Plugging the corner values of $x$ and $z$ into $y = 2-2z$: - At $(x,z)=(0,0)$: $y = 2 \Rightarrow$ the point $(0,2,0)$; - At $(x,z)=(1,0)$: $y = 2 \Rightarrow$ the point $(1,2,0)$; - At $(x,z)=(0,1)$: $y = 0 \Rightarrow$ the point $(0,0,1)$; - At $(x,z)=(1,1)$ — excluded since $z \le 1-x$ collapses to $0$. Together with the origin $(0,0,0)$, the solid is the **tetrahedron** with vertices $$ (0,0,0), \qquad (1,0,0), \qquad (1,2,0), \qquad (0,0,1). $$ Intuitively: for each fixed $x$, the cross-section is a triangle in the $yz$-plane of the shape $0 \le z \le 1-x$, $0 \le y \le 2-2z$; as $x$ increases from $0$ to $1$, that triangle shrinks and finally degenerates, sweeping out a tetrahedron. ## Step 4 — Sketch The 3D figure below shows the tetrahedron: a flat bottom face on $z=0$ (the triangle with vertices $(0,0,0)$, $(1,0,0)$, $(1,2,0)$), a vertical side face on $y=0$, a side face on $x=0$, and the slanted top face $y = 2-2z$ through $(1,0,0)$, $(1,2,0)$, $(0,0,1)$. ## Step 5 — Verify the volume $$ V = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{2-2z} dy\,dz\,dx = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} (2-2z)\,dz\,dx = \int_0^1 \left[2z - z^2\right]_0^{1-x} dx = \int_0^1 (1-x^2)\,dx, $$ $$ V = \left[x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}. $$ Cross-check with the tetrahedron volume formula: taking the base as the triangle in $z=0$ with area $\tfrac12(1)(2) = 1$ and height $1$ (in the $z$-direction), $V = \tfrac13 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = \tfrac23$. ✔ Both methods agree with the numerical computation. $\boxed{\text{The solid is the tetrahedron with vertices }(0,0,0),\,(1,0,0),\,(1,2,0),\,(0,0,1),\text{ of volume } \tfrac{2}{3}.}$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
