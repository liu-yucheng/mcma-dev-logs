# Volume of the Solid Enclosed by $x^2+z^2=4$, $y=-1$, and $y+z=4$ ## Step 1: Understand the geometry The cylinder $x^2+z^2=4$ has its axis along the **$y$-axis** (the variables in the equation are $x$ and $z$, not $x$ and $y$). The solid consists of all points inside this cylinder, bounded: - **below** by the plane $y = -1$ (a flat cap perpendicular to the $y$-axis), - **above** by the plane $y+z=4$, i.e. $y = 4-z$ (a slanted cap). The slanted plane cuts the cylinder obliquely, so the top cap is a slanted disk, while the bottom cap is a flat circular disk at $y=-1$. ## Step 2: Describe the solid as a $z$-simple region **Projection:** The shadow of the solid on the $xz$-plane is the disk $$ D = \{(x,z) : x^2 + z^2 \le 4\}. $$ **Vertical bounds:** For each $(x,z)$ inside $D$, the $y$-coordinate runs from the bottom plane to the top plane: $$ -1 \le y \le 4 - z. $$ (Note that $4-z \ge 2 > -1$ on $D$, since $|z|\le 2$ — so these bounds are consistent everywhere on $D$.) ## Step 3: Set up the triple integral $$ V = \iiint_E dV = \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\int_{-1}^{4-z} dy\, dz\, dx $$ ## Step 4: Evaluate the inner integral The innermost integral simply gives the height of the solid above each point of $D$: $$ \int_{-1}^{4-z} dy = (4-z) - (-1) = 5 - z. $$ So the volume reduces to a double integral: $$ V = \iint_D (5 - z)\, dA = \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\sqrt{4-x^2}} (5 - z)\, dz\, dx. $$ ## Step 5: Evaluate the double integral (polar coordinates in the $xz$-plane) Since $D$ is a disk of radius $2$ centered at the origin, polar-like coordinates are natural: $$ x = r\cos\theta, \qquad z = r\sin\theta, \qquad dA = r\, dr\, d\theta, \qquad 0 \le r \le 2,\; 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi. $$ $$ V = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2 (5 - r\sin\theta)\, r\, dr\, d\theta $$ **Integrate in $r$:** $$ \int_0^2 (5r - r^2 \sin\theta)\, dr = \left[\frac{5r^2}{2} - \frac{r^3}{3}\sin\theta\right]_0^2 = 10 - \frac{8}{3}\sin\theta. $$ **Integrate in $\theta$:** the sine term vanishes by symmetry over a full period: $$ V = \int_0^{2\pi} \left(10 - \frac{8}{3}\sin\theta\right) d\theta = 10\cdot 2\pi - \frac{8}{3}\underbrace{\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\, d\theta}_{=\,0} = 20\pi. $$ **Symmetry insight:** the term $-z$ integrates to $0$ over the symmetric disk $D$ because $D$ is symmetric under $z \to -z$. So $V = 5\cdot\text{area}(D) = 5 \cdot \pi(2)^2 = 20\pi$ — the slanted top plane averages out to a flat top at height $y = 4$ (the plane's value at $z=0$, the center of the disk). ## Verification (via SymPy) Both the Cartesian triple integral and the polar-form integral evaluate to $20\pi \approx 62.83$, confirming the result. ## Final Answer $$ \boxed{V = 20\pi} $$ --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)

--- **Figure 1** shows the solid: a cylinder with axis along the $y$-axis, capped flat at $y=-1$ (orange) and cut obliquely by the plane $y=4-z$ (green). Notice how the green slanted cap makes the cylinder taller on the $z<0$ side and shorter on the $z>0$ side — this asymmetry is exactly what the $-z$ term in the integrand $5-z$ accounts for, and its net contribution cancels by symmetry. ## Where the region $D$ comes from Before setting up the iterated integral, we project the solid onto the $xz$-plane. Since the cylinder is $x^2+z^2=4$, the projection is simply the disk of radius $2$. The figure below shows $D$ together with a representative vertical slice at fixed $x$, which determines the middle limits of integration. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_1.tex)
