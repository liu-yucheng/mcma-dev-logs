# Evaluating $\iiint_E z\,dV$ **Region $E$:** bounded by the cylinder $y^2+z^2=9$, the planes $x=0$, $y=3x$, and $z=0$, in the first octant ($x\ge 0$, $y\ge 0$, $z\ge 0$). ## Step 1: Understand the geometry - The cylinder $y^2+z^2=9$ has its **axis along the $x$-axis** with radius $3$ (note: no $x$ in the equation). Inside the cylinder means $y^2+z^2\le 9$. - The plane $y = 3x$ gives $x = y/3$. Since $x\ge 0$ and $y\ge 0$, the solid lies in the wedge $0 \le x \le y/3$. - The floor is $z=0$; the first octant keeps $z\ge 0$. ## Step 2: Describe $E$ with iterated integral bounds Project $E$ onto the $yz$-plane: the projection $D_{yz}$ is the **quarter disk** $$ 0 \le y \le 3, \qquad 0 \le z \le \sqrt{9-y^2}. $$ For each point $(y,z)$ in $D_{yz}$, $x$ runs from the plane $x=0$ to the plane $x=y/3$. Hence $$ E = \Big\{ (x,y,z) : 0\le y\le 3,\; 0\le z\le \sqrt{9-y^2},\; 0\le x \le \tfrac{y}{3} \Big\}. $$ The $z$-simple description with $y$ outermost is the cleanest choice here. **Graphical aid — the solid $E$ and its projection:** *(3D view of the solid and 2D projection are shown in the plots below.)* ## Step 3: Set up and evaluate the integral $$ \iiint_E z\,dV = \int_{y=0}^{3} \int_{z=0}^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} \int_{x=0}^{y/3} z\,dx\,dz\,dy. $$ **Innermost ($x$):** $$ \int_0^{y/3} z\,dx = \frac{y\,z}{3}. $$ **Middle ($z$):** $$ \int_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} \frac{y\,z}{3}\,dz = \frac{y}{3}\left[\frac{z^2}{2}\right]_0^{\sqrt{9-y^2}} = \frac{y(9-y^2)}{6}. $$ **Outer ($y$):** $$ \int_0^3 \frac{9y - y^3}{6}\,dy = \frac{1}{6}\left[ \frac{9y^2}{2} - \frac{y^4}{4} \right]_0^3 = \frac{1}{6}\left( \frac{81}{2} - \frac{81}{4} \right) = \frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{81}{4}. $$ ## Step 4: Verification - **Symbolic computation (SymPy)** evaluates the iterated integral to exactly $\dfrac{27}{8}$, matching each intermediate step: inner $= \tfrac{y}{3}\left(\tfrac{9-y^2}{2}\right)$, outer $= \tfrac{27}{8}$. - **Monte Carlo check:** sampling a bounding box $[0,1]\times[0,3]\times[0,3]$ with $3\times 10^6$ points gives $\approx 3.376$, consistent with the exact value $27/8 = 3.375$. ## Final Answer $$ \iiint_E z\,dV = \boxed{\dfrac{27}{8}} $$ **Key takeaway:** when the bounding cylinder is $y^2+z^2=R^2$ (no $x$), its axis is the $x$-axis. Choosing the order $dx\,dz\,dy$ (integrate along the cylinder's axis first, then over the quarter-disk cross-section) turns the problem into three elementary one-variable integrals — no trig substitution ever needed. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
