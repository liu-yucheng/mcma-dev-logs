# Problem Find the mass and center of mass of the solid $E$ bounded by the parabolic cylinder $z = 1 - y^2$ and the planes $x + z = 1$, $x = 0$, and $z = 0$, with density $\rho(x,y,z) = 4$. # Step 1: Understand the region $E$ The bounding surfaces give us the inequalities describing $E$: - $z = 0$ (bottom) and $z = 1 - y^2$ (top): so $0 \le z \le 1 - y^2$. - $x = 0$ (front) and $x + z = 1$, i.e. $x = 1 - z$ (back): so $0 \le x \le 1 - z$. - The parabola $z = 1 - y^2$ hits $z=0$ when $y = \pm 1$: so $-1 \le y \le 1$. Hence $$ E = \{(x,y,z) : -1 \le y \le 1,\; 0 \le z \le 1 - y^2,\; 0 \le x \le 1 - z\}. $$ The **shadow** of $E$ on the $yz$-plane is the parabolic region $D$ shown below. [Graphical: 2D shadow region D in yz-plane] The full 3D solid $E$ is shown below. The symmetry in $y$ (the region depends on $y$ only through $y^2$) means $\bar y = 0$ immediately. [Graphical: 3D solid E] # Step 2: Mass Since the density is constant, $\rho = 4$: $$ m = \iiint_E \rho\, dV = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \int_0^{1-y^2} \int_0^{1-z} dx\, dz\, dy $$ Inner integral: $\int_0^{1-z} dx = 1 - z$. $$ m = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \int_0^{1-y^2} (1 - z)\, dz\, dy = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \left[ z - \frac{z^2}{2} \right]_0^{1-y^2} dy = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \left( (1-y^2) - \frac{(1-y^2)^2}{2} \right) dy $$ $$ = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1 - y^4}{2}\, dy = 2 \left[ y - \frac{y^5}{5} \right]_{-1}^{1} = 2 \cdot \frac{8}{5} = \frac{16}{5} $$ $$ \boxed{m = \frac{16}{5}} $$ # Step 3: Moments and center of mass **Moment about the $yz$-plane ($M_{yz}$, for $\bar x$):** $$ M_{yz} = \iiint_E \rho\, x\, dV = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \int_0^{1-y^2} \int_0^{1-z} x\, dx\, dz\, dy $$ Inner: $\int_0^{1-z} x\, dx = \frac{(1-z)^2}{2}$. $$ M_{yz} = 2 \int_{-1}^{1} \int_0^{1-y^2} (1-z)^2 dz\, dy = 2 \int_{-1}^{1} \left[ -\frac{(1-z)^3}{3} \right]_0^{1-y^2} dy = \frac{2}{3} \int_{-1}^{1} \left[ 1 - (y^2)^3 \right] dy $$ using $(1-z)^3 = 1 - 3y^2 + 3y^4 - y^6$ at $z = 1-y^2$, so $(1-z)^2 \cdot$ ... carefully: $(1-z)^2$ with $z=1-y^2$ gives $y^4$; the antiderivative evaluates to $\frac{1 - y^6}{3}$. Then $$ M_{yz} = \frac{2}{3} \left( 2 - \frac{2}{7} \right) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{12}{7} = \frac{8}{7} $$ $$ \bar x = \frac{M_{yz}}{m} = \frac{8/7}{16/5} = \frac{8}{7} \cdot \frac{5}{16} = \frac{5}{14} $$ **Moment for $\bar y$:** By the symmetry of $E$ and $\rho$ under $y \to -y$, the integrand $\rho\, y$ is odd in $y$, so $M_{xy} = 0$ and $$ \bar y = 0. $$ **Moment about the $xy$-plane ($M_{xy}$, for $\bar z$):** $$ M_{xy} = \iiint_E \rho\, z\, dV = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \int_0^{1-y^2} \int_0^{1-z} z\, dx\, dz\, dy = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \int_0^{1-y^2} z(1-z)\, dz\, dy $$ Inner: $\int_0^{u} (z - z^2) dz = \frac{u^2}{2} - \frac{u^3}{3}$ with $u = 1 - y^2$: $$ M_{xy} = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \left[ \frac{(1-y^2)^2}{2} - \frac{(1-y^2)^3}{3} \right] dy = 4 \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{(1-y^2)^2 (1 + 2y^2)}{6}\, dy $$ Expanding $(1 - 2y^2 + y^4)(1 + 2y^2) = 1 - 3y^4 + 2y^6$: $$ M_{xy} = \frac{2}{3} \int_{-1}^{1} (1 - 3y^4 + 2y^6)\, dy = \frac{2}{3} \left( 2 - \frac{6}{5} + \frac{4}{7} \right) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{70 - 21 + 10}{35} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{59}{35} = \frac{118}{105}? $$ No — recompute carefully: $\int_{-1}^1 y^4 dy = 2/5$, $\int_{-1}^1 y^6 dy = 2/7$, so the integral is $2 - 3\cdot\frac{2}{5} + 2\cdot\frac{2}{7} = 2 - \frac{6}{5} + \frac{4}{7} = \frac{70 - 42 + 20}{35} = \frac{48}{35}$. Then $$ M_{xy} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{48}{35} = \frac{32}{35}, \qquad \bar z = \frac{M_{xy}}{m} = \frac{32/35}{16/5} = \frac{32}{35} \cdot \frac{5}{16} = \frac{2}{7}. $$ # Step 4: Verification (SymPy) All integrals were verified symbolically with SymPy: $m = 16/5$, $M_{yz} = 8/7$, $M_{xy\text{-}z} = 32/35$, $y$-moment $= 0$. # Final Answer $$ \boxed{m = \frac{16}{5}, \qquad (\bar x, \bar y, \bar z) = \left( \frac{5}{14},\; 0,\; \frac{2}{7} \right)} $$ The center of mass (red star in the 3D plot above) lies inside $E$, on the symmetry plane $y = 0$, closer to the face $x=0$ than to $x+z=1$, and closer to the bottom than the top — as expected since the solid is denser in cross-section near $z = 0$ and widens (in $x$) as $z$ decreases. # Key Takeaways 1. **Set up the region first.** Convert bounding surfaces into inequalities; project onto a coordinate plane (here the $yz$-plane) to get the limits $-1 \le y \le 1$, $0 \le z \le 1 - y^2$, then $0 \le x \le 1-z$. 2. **Use symmetry to save work:** $\rho$ and $E$ are even in $y$, so $\bar y = 0$ without computing the integral. 3. **Choose the integration order wisely:** integrating $x$ first (limits $0$ to $1-z$) keeps every later integrand polynomial in $y$ and $z$. 4. **General formulas:** $m = \iiint_E \rho\,dV$, $\bar x = \frac{1}{m}\iiint_E \rho x\, dV$, etc. --- [PGFplots / tikz plot.](./pgfplots_0.tex)
