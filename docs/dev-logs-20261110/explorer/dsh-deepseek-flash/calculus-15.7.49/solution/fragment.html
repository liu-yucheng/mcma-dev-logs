Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 49

Mass, Center of Mass, and Moment of Inertia of a Cylindrical Wedge

A solid in the first octant is cut from a quarter cylinder by the slanted plane \(y = z\). The density grows with all three coordinates.

Problem

Let \(E\) be the solid in the first octant bounded by the cylinder \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 1\) and the planes \(y = z\), \(x = 0\), and \(z = 0\). The density function is

\[ \rho(x, y, z) = 1 + x + y + z . \]

Use exact values to find (a) the mass of \(E\), (b) the center of mass of \(E\), and (c) the moment of inertia of \(E\) about the \(z\)-axis.

Reading the solid

The cylinder \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 1\) cuts the first quadrant of the \(xy\)-plane in a quarter disk. The two planes \(x = 0\) and \(z = 0\) hold the solid against the coordinate planes. The plane \(y = z\) holds it below the slanted roof \(z = y\).

The roof tells the height of the solid at every point of the quarter disk. Above a fixed point \((x, y)\) with \(y > 0\), the solid runs from \(z = 0\) up to \(z = y\). The height therefore grows with \(y\) and never exceeds the radius of the disk. This gives the three sets of bounds

\[ 0 \leq y \leq 1, \qquad 0 \leq x \leq \sqrt{1 - y^{2}}, \qquad 0 \leq z \leq y . \]

The order \(z\), then \(x\), then \(y\) matches this shape: the inner integral covers one vertical segment, the middle integral covers one horizontal strip, and the outer integral sweeps the quarter disk from the \(x\)-axis to the point \((0, 1)\).

Solution

Step 1. Write the mass integral

The mass is the integral of the density over the solid. With the bounds above,

\[ M = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{1 - y^{2}}} \int_{0}^{y} \left( 1 + x + y + z \right) \, dz \, dx \, dy . \]

Step 2. Integrate in \(z\)

The inner integral runs over a segment of height \(y\). At fixed \(x\) and \(y\),

\[ \int_{0}^{y} \left( 1 + x + y + z \right) dz = \left( 1 + x + y \right) y + \frac{y^{2}}{2} = y + xy + \frac{3}{2} y^{2} . \]

The constant term \(1 + x + y\) gives the rectangle \(y \left( 1 + x + y \right)\), and the term \(z\) gives the triangle \(y^{2} / 2\).

Step 3. Integrate in \(x\)

The upper limit is \(s = \sqrt{1 - y^{2}}\), so the middle integral is

\[ \int_{0}^{s} \left( y + xy + \frac{3}{2} y^{2} \right) dx = y s + \frac{1}{2} y s^{2} + \frac{3}{2} y^{2} s . \]

Replace each even power of \(s\) by a power of \(1 - y^{2}\). The mass then reduces to three radial integrals over \(0 \leq y \leq 1\):

\[ M = \int_{0}^{1} y s \, dy + \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{1} y \left( 1 - y^{2} \right) dy + \frac{3}{2} \int_{0}^{1} y^{2} s \, dy . \]

Step 4. Evaluate the radial integrals

The substitution \(y = \sin t\), with \(dy = \cos t \, dt\) and \(s = \cos t\), turns each integral into a trigonometric integral on \(0 \leq t \leq \pi / 2\). For example,

\[ \int_{0}^{1} y^{2} \sqrt{1 - y^{2}} \, dy = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{2} t \, \cos^{2} t \, dt = \frac{\pi}{16} . \]

The integrals that the moments need are listed below. Every entry follows the same substitution.

IntegralValueIntegralValue
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} y s \, dy\)\(1/3\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} y s^{2} \, dy\)\(1/4\)
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} y^{2} s \, dy\)\(\pi/16\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} y^{2} s^{2} \, dy\)\(2/15\)
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} y^{3} s \, dy\)\(2/15\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} y s^{3} \, dy\)\(1/5\)

Here \(s = \sqrt{1 - y^{2}}\). These six integrals cover every radial integral on the page.

Step 5. The mass

Insert the three values:

\[ M = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{8} + \frac{3\pi}{32} = \frac{11}{24} + \frac{3\pi}{32} = \frac{44 + 9\pi}{96} \approx 0.752858 . \]

The middle term is \(\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{1}{8}\).

Step 6. The moment about the \(yz\)-plane

The first moment of mass about the plane \(x = 0\) weights each point by its distance \(x\) from that plane:

\[ M_{yz} = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{s} \int_{0}^{y} x \left( 1 + x + y + z \right) dz \, dx \, dy . \]

The inner integral gives \(xy + x^{2} y + \tfrac{3}{2} x y^{2}\). The integral in \(x\) then gives

\[ \frac{1}{2} y s^{2} + \frac{1}{3} y s^{3} + \frac{3}{4} y^{2} s^{2} . \]
\[ M_{yz} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{5} + \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{15} = \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{7}{24} \approx 0.291667 . \]

Step 7. The moment about the \(xz\)-plane

Repeat with the weight \(y\). The inner integral gives \(y^{2} + x y^{2} + \tfrac{3}{2} y^{3}\), and the integral in \(x\) gives

\[ y^{2} s + \frac{1}{2} y^{2} s^{2} + \frac{3}{2} y^{3} s . \]
\[ M_{xz} = \frac{\pi}{16} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{15} + \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{15} = \frac{\pi}{16} + \frac{4}{15} \approx 0.463016 . \]

Step 8. The moment about the \(xy\)-plane

Repeat with the weight \(z\). The inner integral gives \(\tfrac{1}{2} y^{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} x y^{2} + \tfrac{5}{6} y^{3}\), and the integral in \(x\) gives

\[ \frac{1}{2} y^{2} s + \frac{1}{4} y^{2} s^{2} + \frac{5}{6} y^{3} s . \]
\[ M_{xy} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\pi}{16} + \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{15} + \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{2}{15} = \frac{\pi}{32} + \frac{1}{30} + \frac{1}{9} = \frac{\pi}{32} + \frac{13}{90} \approx 0.242619 . \]

Step 9. The center of mass

Divide each first moment by the mass:

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{M_{yz}}{M} = \frac{7/24}{11/24 + 3\pi/32} = \frac{28}{44 + 9\pi} \approx 0.387 . \]
\[ \bar{y} = \frac{M_{xz}}{M} = \frac{\pi/16 + 4/15}{11/24 + 3\pi/32} = \frac{30\pi + 128}{5 \left( 44 + 9\pi \right)} \approx 0.615 . \]
\[ \bar{z} = \frac{M_{xy}}{M} = \frac{\pi/32 + 13/90}{11/24 + 3\pi/32} = \frac{90\pi + 416}{30 \left( 44 + 9\pi \right)} \approx 0.322 . \]

All three coordinates lie in the interval \((0, 1)\), as they must, because the solid sits inside the unit cube. The centroid lies nearer the \(y\)-axis than the \(x\)-axis, because the solid is tallest at \(y = 1\).

Step 10. The moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis

The squared distance from a point to the \(z\)-axis is \(x^{2} + y^{2}\), so

\[ I_{z} = \iiint_{E} \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) \left( 1 + x + y + z \right) dV . \]

Expanding the product gives eight monomial terms. Each one is an elementary integral of the same three-step form, and the script evaluates all eight:

\[ \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) \left( 1 + x + y + z \right) = x^{2} + y^{2} + x^{3} + x^{2} y + x^{2} z + x y^{2} + y^{3} + y^{2} z . \]
Term\(\displaystyle \iiint_{E} (\cdot)\, dV\)Term\(\displaystyle \iiint_{E} (\cdot)\, dV\)
\(x^{2}\)\(1/15\)\(x^{2} y\)\(\pi/96\)
\(y^{2}\)\(2/15\)\(x^{2} z\)\(\pi/192\)
\(x^{3}\)\(1/24\)\(x y^{2}\)\(1/24\)
\(y^{3}\)\(\pi/32\)\(y^{2} z\)\(\pi/64\)

The squares collect to \(\tfrac{1}{5}\), the cubes and mixed products collect to \(\tfrac{1}{12}\), and the four terms with \(\pi\) collect to \(\tfrac{\pi}{16}\):

\[ I_{z} = \frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{12} + \frac{\pi}{16} = \frac{17}{60} + \frac{\pi}{16} \approx 0.479683 . \]

Answer

(a) The mass is

\[ M = \frac{11}{24} + \frac{3\pi}{32} = \frac{44 + 9\pi}{96} \approx 0.753 . \]

(b) The center of mass is

\[ \left( \bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z} \right) = \left( \frac{28}{44 + 9\pi},\ \frac{30\pi + 128}{5 \left( 44 + 9\pi \right)},\ \frac{90\pi + 416}{30 \left( 44 + 9\pi \right)} \right) \approx \left( 0.387,\ 0.615,\ 0.322 \right) . \]

(c) The moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis is

\[ I_{z} = \frac{17}{60} + \frac{\pi}{16} \approx 0.480 . \]
