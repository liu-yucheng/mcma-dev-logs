Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 55

The Region That Maximizes a Triple Integral

Problem

Part (a) asks for the region \(E\) that makes a triple integral as large as possible. Part (b) asks for the exact value of that maximum. Write the integrand and the integral as

\[ g(x,y,z) = 1 - x^{2} - 2y^{2} - 3z^{2}, \qquad I(E) = \iiint_{E} g(x,y,z) \, dV. \]

The region \(E\) is not given. A larger region adds more volume, but the added volume can contribute a negative amount, so size alone does not decide the answer.

Reading the solid

This exercise does not fix the solid. It asks which solid is best, so the shape of the integrand \(g\) decides the answer. The function \(g\) has a fixed sign pattern: it is positive near the origin, zero on one surface, and negative far from the origin. The zero surface is the level set \(g = 0\):

\[ 1 - x^{2} - 2y^{2} - 3z^{2} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} = 1. \]

Divide the constant term into the three squares:

\[ \frac{x^{2}}{1^{2}} + \frac{y^{2}}{\left(1/\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}} + \frac{z^{2}}{\left(1/\sqrt{3}\right)^{2}} = 1. \]

This is an ellipsoid centered at the origin. Its semi-axes are \(a = 1\) along the \(x\)-axis, \(b = 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.707\) along the \(y\)-axis, and \(c = 1/\sqrt{3} \approx 0.577\) along the \(z\)-axis. Let \(E_{0}\) denote the closed region inside this ellipsoid:

\[ E_{0} = \{(x,y,z) : x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} \le 1\}. \]

Every point of \(E_{0}\) satisfies \(x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} \le 1\), so \(g \ge 0\) on \(E_{0}\). Every point outside \(E_{0}\) satisfies \(x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} > 1\), so \(g < 0\) there. The sign test in Step 1 checks this statement, and Step 2 turns it into a proof.

The change of variables in Step 3 removes the coefficients \(2\) and \(3\). Set \(u = x\), \(v = \sqrt{2}\,y\), and \(w = \sqrt{3}\,z\). Then \(x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} = u^{2} + v^{2} + w^{2}\), so the ellipsoid \(E_{0}\) becomes the unit ball \(B\), and the integrand becomes \(1 - \left(u^{2} + v^{2} + w^{2}\right)\).

Solution

Step 1 — Locate the sign change

Solve the inequality \(g > 0\):

\[ 1 - x^{2} - 2y^{2} - 3z^{2} > 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} < 1. \]

The solution set is the open ellipsoid \(E_{0}\). On the surface of \(E_{0}\) the integrand is zero, and outside \(E_{0}\) the integrand is negative. A random test confirms the sign pattern: of four million points in the cube \(-1 \le x,y,z \le 1\), no point had a positive integrand outside \(E_{0}\) and no point had a negative integrand inside \(E_{0}\), so the number of disagreements is \(0\).

Step 2 — Prove that the ellipsoid gives the maximum

Let \(E\) be any bounded region. Split \(E\) into the part inside \(E_{0}\) and the part outside it:

\[ \iiint_{E} g \, dV = \iiint_{E \cap E_{0}} g \, dV + \iiint_{E \setminus E_{0}} g \, dV. \]

On \(E \setminus E_{0}\) the integrand is negative, so that term is at most zero. On \(E \cap E_{0}\) the integrand is non-negative and \(E \cap E_{0}\) is a subset of \(E_{0}\), so that term is at most the integral over the whole ellipsoid. Together:

\[ \iiint_{E} g \, dV \le \iiint_{E \cap E_{0}} g \, dV \le \iiint_{E_{0}} g \, dV. \]

Both inequalities become equalities for the choice \(E = E_{0}\). Any other region either adds points where \(g < 0\) or omits points of \(E_{0}\) where \(g > 0\), and both changes lower the value. A region that differs from \(E_{0}\) only on a surface, which has volume zero, gives the same maximum. Hence \(E_{0}\) is the maximizing region.

A one-parameter check supports the argument. Scale the ellipsoid by the factor \(s\):

\[ E_{s} = \{(x,y,z) : x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} \le s^{2}\}, \qquad I(s) = \iiint_{E_{s}} g \, dV = \frac{2\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{45}\, s^{3} \left(5 - 3s^{2}\right). \]

Differentiate with respect to \(s\):

\[ I'(s) = \frac{2\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{3}\, s^{2}\left(1 - s^{2}\right). \]

On \(0 < s < 1\) the derivative is positive, and on \(s > 1\) it is negative, so \(s = 1\) is the only critical point in the range \(s > 0\) and it is the global maximum. The second derivative at that point is \(I''(1) = -4\sqrt{6}\,\pi/3 < 0\), which confirms a maximum. The computed values of \(I(s)\) show the same picture:

\[ \begin{array}{c|cccc} s & 0.4 & 0.6 & 0.8 & 1.0 \\ \hline I(s) & 0.098937604 & 0.289589491 & 0.539341275 & 0.684026576 \end{array} \]
\[ \begin{array}{c|cccc} s & 1.2 & 1.4 & 1.6 & 2.0 \\ \hline I(s) & 0.401879294 & -0.825866327 & -3.754375627 & -19.152744130 \end{array} \]

The largest tabulated value is at \(s = 1\). Past that point the extra shell lies entirely in the region where \(g < 0\), so the integral falls, and it soon becomes negative. A shift of the ellipsoid has the same effect: moving the center to \((0.1,0,0)\) lowers the value to \(0.66591 \pm 0.00039\), and the value keeps falling as the shift grows.

Step 3 — Change to scaled coordinates

Use the substitution

\[ u = x, \qquad v = \sqrt{2}\,y, \qquad w = \sqrt{3}\,z. \]

The inverse map is \(x = u\), \(y = v/\sqrt{2}\), \(z = w/\sqrt{3}\). Its Jacobian is the product of the three scale factors:

\[ \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{6}. \]

Therefore \(dV = du\,dv\,dw/\sqrt{6}\), the ellipsoid \(E_{0}\) becomes the unit ball \(B = \{(u,v,w) : u^{2} + v^{2} + w^{2} \le 1\}\), and the integrand becomes \(1 - \left(u^{2} + v^{2} + w^{2}\right)\). In spherical coordinates \(u = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta\), \(v = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta\), and \(w = \rho\cos\phi\), with \(dV = \rho^{2}\sin\phi \, d\rho\, d\phi\, d\theta\). The unit ball has the constant bounds

\[ 0 \le \rho \le 1, \qquad 0 \le \phi \le \pi, \qquad 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi. \]

Step 4 — Compute the volume of the region

Integrate the volume element over the ball, then divide by \(\sqrt{6}\):

\[ \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \rho^{2}\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta = 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3}. \]
\[ V(E_{0}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \cdot \frac{4\pi}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt{6}} = \frac{2\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{9} \approx 1.7101. \]

The result agrees with the product of the three semi-axes times \(4\pi/3\): the coefficient \(1/\sqrt{6}\) is exactly \(abc = 1 \cdot \left(1/\sqrt{2}\right) \cdot \left(1/\sqrt{3}\right)\).

Step 5 — Compute the exact maximum

Integrate \(1 - \rho^{2}\) over the ball in the same order:

\[ \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \left(1 - \rho^{2}\right)\rho^{2}\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta = 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \left[ \frac{\rho^{3}}{3} - \frac{\rho^{5}}{5} \right]_{0}^{1} = 4\pi \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5} \right) = \frac{8\pi}{15}. \]

Divide by \(\sqrt{6}\) and rationalize the denominator:

\[ \iiint_{E_{0}} g \, dV = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \cdot \frac{8\pi}{15} = \frac{8\pi}{15\sqrt{6}} = \frac{8\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{90} = \frac{4\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{45} \approx 0.68403. \]

The value is between \(0\) and the volume \(1.7101\), as it must be, because the integrand lies between \(0\) and \(1\) on \(E_{0}\). Its mean value on \(E_{0}\) is the quotient of the two results,

\[ \frac{1}{V(E_{0})} \iiint_{E_{0}} g \, dV = \frac{2}{5} = 0.4. \]

An independent quadrature of the same integral with mpmath reproduces \(0.68402657608632751762\), and the closed form gives the same digits.

Answer

\[ \text{(a)}\quad E = \{(x,y,z) : x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} \le 1\}. \]
\[ \text{(b)}\quad \iiint_{E} \left(1 - x^{2} - 2y^{2} - 3z^{2}\right) dV = \frac{4\sqrt{6}\,\pi}{45} \approx 0.6840. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(g(x,y,z)\)The integrand \(1 - x^{2} - 2y^{2} - 3z^{2}\).
\(I(E)\)The value of the triple integral of \(g\) over the region \(E\).
\(\iiint_{E} g \, dV\)The triple integral of \(g\) over \(E\); it is the limit of sums of \(g\) times small volumes.
\(dV\)The volume element. In Cartesian coordinates \(dV = dx\,dy\,dz\); after the substitution it is \(du\,dv\,dw/\sqrt{6}\).
\(E_{0}\)The closed ellipsoid \(x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} \le 1\), the answer to part (a).
\(a, b, c\)The semi-axes of the ellipsoid: \(1\) along \(x\), \(1/\sqrt{2}\) along \(y\), and \(1/\sqrt{3}\) along \(z\).
\(u, v, w\)The scaled coordinates \(u = x\), \(v = \sqrt{2}\,y\), \(w = \sqrt{3}\,z\) that turn the ellipsoid into the unit ball.
\(\dfrac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}\)The Jacobian of the inverse substitution, here \(1/\sqrt{6}\). It converts the volume element of the ball into the volume element of the ellipsoid.
\(B\)The unit ball \(u^{2} + v^{2} + w^{2} \le 1\) in the scaled coordinates.
\(\rho, \phi, \theta\)Spherical coordinates in the \(uvw\)-space: \(\rho\) is the distance from the origin, \(\phi\) is the angle from the \(w\)-axis, and \(\theta\) is the angle in the \(uv\)-plane.
\(V(E_{0})\)The volume of the ellipsoid, \(2\sqrt{6}\,\pi/9 \approx 1.7101\).
\(I(s)\)The integral over the scaled ellipsoid \(E_{s}\) with \(x^{2} + 2y^{2} + 3z^{2} \le s^{2}\), used to test maximality.
\(\pi\)The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, \(\pi \approx 3.14159\).

Verification

Three scripts check the result; each one runs from inside calculus-15.7.55/solution.

  • part1_step1.py reports the three semi-axes \(1.000000\), \(0.707107\), and \(0.577350\), and tests the sign of the integrand on four million random points. The number of disagreements is \(0\). The Monte Carlo volume is \(1.70857\) with standard error \(0.00164\), and the Monte Carlo integral is \(0.68367\) with standard error \(0.00082\), which is \(0.44\) standard errors below \(4\sqrt{6}\,\pi/45\).
  • part1_step2.py repeats the computation with SymPy. It returns the unit ball volume \(4\pi/3\), the Jacobian \(\sqrt{6}/6\), the ellipsoid volume \(2\sqrt{6}\,\pi/9 = 1.7100664402\), the ball integrals \(4\pi/5\) and \(8\pi/15\), the maximum \(4\sqrt{6}\,\pi/45 = 0.6840265761\), and the mean value \(2/5\).
  • part1_step3.py checks maximality. It derives \(I(s)\), its derivative, and the critical point \(s = 1\), and it tabulates \(I(s)\) from \(s = 0.4\) to \(s = 2.0\). It also evaluates the maximum with mpmath quadrature (\(0.68402657608632751762\), difference \(0.0\) from the closed form) and shows by Monte Carlo sampling that a shifted copy of the ellipsoid gives a smaller integral.
