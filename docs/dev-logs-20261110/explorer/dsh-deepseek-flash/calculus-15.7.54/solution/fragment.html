Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 54

The Average Value of a Function over a Paraboloid Cap

Problem

The average value of a function \(f(x,y,z)\) over a solid region \(E\) is

\[ f_{\mathrm{ave}} = \frac{1}{V(E)} \iiint_{E} f(x,y,z) \, dV, \]

where \(V(E)\) is the volume of \(E\). Find the average value of \(f(x,y,z) = x^{2}z + y^{2}z\) over the region enclosed by the paraboloid \(z = 1 - x^{2} - y^{2}\) and the plane \(z = 0\).

Reading the solid

The paraboloid opens downward from its vertex \((0,0,1)\). The two surfaces meet on the rim where both equations hold, that is, where \(1 - x^{2} - y^{2} = 0\). This equation describes the unit circle \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 1\). The solid therefore projects onto the unit disk

\[ D = \{(x,y) : x^{2} + y^{2} \le 1\}. \]

Fix a point \((x,y)\) in \(D\). The vertical line through that point enters \(E\) on the floor \(z = 0\) and leaves \(E\) on the paraboloid. The solid is the set of points between these two surfaces:

\[ E = \{(x,y,z) : x^{2} + y^{2} \le 1,\ 0 \le z \le 1 - x^{2} - y^{2}\}. \]

Introduce polar coordinates in the \(xy\)-plane, \(x = r\cos\theta\) and \(y = r\sin\theta\), so that \(x^{2} + y^{2} = r^{2}\). A small polar rectangle has area \(r \, dr \, d\theta\), hence the volume element is

\[ dV = dz \, r \, dr \, d\theta = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta. \]

In these coordinates the cap has constant bounds in two of the three variables:

\[ 0 \le z \le 1 - r^{2}, \qquad 0 \le r \le 1, \qquad 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi. \]

Solution

Step 1 — Compute the volume \(V(E)\)

The denominator of the average value is the volume of the cap. Integrate the volume element over the three ranges of the polar description:

\[ V(E) = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-r^{2}} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{1} r\left(1 - r^{2}\right) dr \, d\theta. \]

The radial integral is elementary:

\[ \int_{0}^{1} \left(r - r^{3}\right) dr = \left[ \frac{r^{2}}{2} - \frac{r^{4}}{4} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}. \]

The result does not depend on \(\theta\), so the outer integral multiplies it by \(2\pi\):

\[ V(E) = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708. \]

Step 2 — Simplify the integrand

Both terms of \(f\) carry the factor \(z\), and the two squared terms add to \(x^{2} + y^{2} = r^{2}\):

\[ f(x,y,z) = x^{2}z + y^{2}z = \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right) z = r^{2}z. \]

This step matters: after the substitution the integrand no longer contains \(x\) or \(y\) separately, so no trigonometric term survives the integration.

Step 3 — Integrate \(f\) over the cap

Multiply the integrand by the volume element and integrate in the order \(z\), then \(r\), then \(\theta\):

\[ \iiint_{E} \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right) z \, dV = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-r^{2}} r^{2} z \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta. \]

With respect to \(z\), the factor \(r^{3}\) is constant:

\[ \int_{0}^{1-r^{2}} r^{3} z \, dz = r^{3} \left[ \frac{z^{2}}{2} \right]_{0}^{1-r^{2}} = \frac{r^{3}\left(1 - r^{2}\right)^{2}}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(r^{3} - 2r^{5} + r^{7}\right). \]

Integrate this polynomial over \(0 \le r \le 1\):

\[ \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{2}\left(r^{3} - 2r^{5} + r^{7}\right) dr = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{2}{6} + \frac{1}{8} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{24} = \frac{1}{48}. \]

The outer integral adds the factor \(2\pi\):

\[ \iiint_{E} \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right) z \, dV = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{48} = \frac{\pi}{24} \approx 0.1309. \]

Step 4 — Divide by the volume

Insert the two results into the definition of the average value:

\[ f_{\mathrm{ave}} = \frac{1}{V(E)} \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \frac{\pi/24}{\pi/2} = \frac{\pi}{24} \cdot \frac{2}{\pi} = \frac{1}{12} \approx 0.0833. \]

The two factors of \(\pi\) cancel, so the average value is a rational number even though both the volume and the integral contain \(\pi\). The value is small because \(f\) vanishes on the rim and stays small near the axis, where the cap is high but \(r^{2}\) is small.

Answer

\[ f_{\mathrm{ave}} = \frac{1}{12} \approx 0.0833. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(f_{\mathrm{ave}}\)The average value of the function \(f\) over the solid \(E\), that is, the integral of \(f\) divided by the volume of \(E\).
\(V(E)\)The volume of the solid region \(E\), in cubic units.
\(\iiint_{E} f \, dV\)The triple integral of \(f\) over \(E\); it is the limit of sums of \(f\) times small volumes.
\(dV\)The volume element. In Cartesian coordinates \(dV = dx\,dy\,dz\); in the polar coordinates used here \(dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\).
\(E\)The solid region of integration, here the cap between the paraboloid and the plane \(z = 0\).
\(D\)The projection of \(E\) onto the \(xy\)-plane, here the unit disk.
\(z = 1 - x^{2} - y^{2}\)A downward paraboloid with vertex \((0,0,1)\); it is the upper boundary of \(E\).
\(r, \theta\)Polar coordinates in the \(xy\)-plane: \(r\) is the distance from the \(z\)-axis and \(\theta\) is the angle in the plane.
the factor \(r\)The Jacobian factor of polar coordinates. A small polar rectangle has area \(r\,dr\,d\theta\), not \(dr\,d\theta\).
\(\pi\)The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, \(\pi \approx 3.14159\).

Verification

Three scripts check the result; each one runs from inside calculus-15.7.54/solution.

  • part1_step1.py confirms the projection disk, tabulates the cap height \(1 - r^{2}\), and estimates the average value by Monte Carlo sampling of four million points. The estimate is \(0.08341\) with standard error \(0.00005\), that is \(1.5\) standard errors from \(1/12\).
  • part1_step2.py repeats the whole computation with SymPy and returns \(V(E) = \pi/2\), \(\iiint_{E} f \, dV = \pi/24\), and \(f_{\mathrm{ave}} = 1/12\).
  • part1_step3.py evaluates the integral with mpmath in polar order and in Cartesian order. Both values agree with \(\pi/24\) to fifteen digits, and the quotient with the volume reproduces \(0.0833333333333\).
