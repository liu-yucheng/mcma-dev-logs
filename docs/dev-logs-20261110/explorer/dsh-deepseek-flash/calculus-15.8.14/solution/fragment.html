Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.8 · Exercise 14

Drawing the Solid Enclosed by Two Paraboloids

Problem

Use a graphing device to draw the solid enclosed by the paraboloids \(z = x^{2} + y^{2}\) and \(z = 5 - x^{2} - y^{2}\).

Reading the solid

Both surfaces are paraboloids of revolution about the \(z\)-axis. The first opens upward with its vertex at the origin. The second opens downward with its vertex at \((0,0,5)\). Write \(r^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2}\). Then the two surfaces have the equations \(z = r^{2}\) and \(z = 5 - r^{2}\).

They meet where the two heights are equal:

\[ r^{2} = 5 - r^{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2r^{2} = 5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = \sqrt{\frac{5}{2}} \approx 1.5811. \]

At that radius both surfaces have the height \(z = 5/2\). The solid is therefore the closed region

\[ E = \left\{ (x,y,z) : x^{2} + y^{2} \le \frac{5}{2}, \ x^{2} + y^{2} \le z \le 5 - x^{2} - y^{2} \right\}. \]

It is a lens with a pointed rim: a lower bowl from \(z = 0\) up to \(z = 5/2\), capped by an upper bowl back down to the rim. The solid is symmetric about the plane \(z = 5/2\), so the two bowls enclose equal volumes.

Drawing the solid

A graphing device needs parametric surfaces, not inequalities. Use polar coordinates in the \(xy\)-plane, with \(0 \le r \le \sqrt{5/2}\) and \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\):

\[ \text{lower bowl: } (r\cos\theta,\ r\sin\theta,\ r^{2}), \qquad \text{upper bowl: } (r\cos\theta,\ r\sin\theta,\ 5 - r^{2}). \]

Draw both surfaces for the same range of \(r\). Their common boundary is the circle of radius \(\sqrt{5/2}\) at height \(5/2\). The figure shows the two bowls, the dashed rim circle, and the vertex of each bowl.

A vertical cross-section through the \(z\)-axis shows why the solid looks like a lens. It is the plane region between the two parabolas \(z = r^{2}\) and \(z = 5 - r^{2}\), rotated about the \(z\)-axis.

Checking the drawing

Three numbers fix the shape, and each one is easy to read from the figures. The rim radius is \(\sqrt{5/2} \approx 1.5811\). The solid reaches \(z = 5\) on the axis and \(z = 0\) on the axis. The widest horizontal circle is the rim.

The volume gives a further check that the two bowls are drawn at the correct scale. Integrate the vertical thickness over the disk of radius \(\sqrt{5/2}\):

\[ V = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{5/2}} \left(5 - 2r^{2}\right) r \, dr \, d\theta = 2\pi\left[\frac{5r^{2}}{2} - \frac{r^{4}}{2}\right]_{0}^{\sqrt{5/2}} = \frac{25\pi}{4} \approx 19.635. \]

Each bowl therefore holds half of that volume, \(25\pi/8\). A solid of revolution drawn with an incorrect rim radius would not reproduce this value.

Answer

The solid is the lens-shaped region between the two bowls,

\[ x^{2} + y^{2} \le \frac{5}{2}, \qquad x^{2} + y^{2} \le z \le 5 - x^{2} - y^{2}, \]

bounded below by \(z = x^{2} + y^{2}\) and above by \(z = 5 - x^{2} - y^{2}\). The two surfaces meet on the circle \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 5/2\) at height \(z = 5/2\). The drawing is produced by plotting the two parametric surfaces for \(0 \le r \le \sqrt{5/2}\) and \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\).

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(z = x^{2} + y^{2}\)A paraboloid of revolution: rotate the parabola \(z = r^{2}\) about the \(z\)-axis. Its vertex is at the origin.
\(z = 5 - x^{2} - y^{2}\)The same bowl turned upside down and lifted: vertex at \((0,0,5)\), opening downward.
\(r\)The distance from the \(z\)-axis, \(r = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\). Each surface is a function of \(r\) alone.
\(\theta\)The polar angle in the \(xy\)-plane, measured from the positive \(x\)-axis.
parameterizationA formula that gives a point of a surface as a function of two parameters, here \(r\) and \(\theta\). A graphing device plots a parameterized surface as a grid of points.
rimThe circle where the two surfaces meet, \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 5/2\) at \(z = 5/2\).
solid of revolutionA solid obtained by rotating a plane region about an axis. Here the region is the lens in the \((r,z)\)-plane and the axis is the \(z\)-axis.
