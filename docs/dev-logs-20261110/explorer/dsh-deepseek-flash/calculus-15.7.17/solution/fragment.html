Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 17

A Triple Integral over the Solid between a Paraboloid and a Plane

Problem

Evaluate the triple integral

\[ \iiint_{E} x \, dV, \]

where the solid \(E\) is bounded by the paraboloid \(x = 4y^{2} + 4z^{2}\) and the plane \(x = 4\).

Reading the solid

The two surfaces meet on the curve where both equations hold, that is, where \(4y^{2} + 4z^{2} = 4\). Divide by 4 to obtain the circle \(y^{2} + z^{2} = 1\). The solid therefore projects onto the unit disk \(D = \{(y,z) : y^{2} + z^{2} \le 1\}\) in the \(yz\)-plane.

At a fixed point \((y,z)\) of \(D\), the \(x\)-coordinate runs from the paraboloid to the plane:

\[ 4y^{2} + 4z^{2} \le x \le 4. \]

Use polar coordinates in the \(yz\)-plane, \(y = r\cos\theta\) and \(z = r\sin\theta\), so that \(y^{2} + z^{2} = r^{2}\). The area element of the disk is \(dA = r \, dr \, d\theta\), hence

\[ dV = dx \, dA = r \, dx \, dr \, d\theta. \]

In these coordinates the solid is described by the simple inequalities

\[ 4r^{2} \le x \le 4, \qquad 0 \le r \le 1, \qquad 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi. \]

Solution

Step 1 — Set up the iterated integral

Insert the description of \(E\) and the volume element into the triple integral:

\[ \iiint_{E} x \, dV = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{4r^{2}}^{4} x \cdot r \, dx \, dr \, d\theta. \]

Step 2 — Integrate with respect to \(x\)

The variable \(r\) is constant during the innermost integration, so

\[ \int_{4r^{2}}^{4} x r \, dx = r \left[ \frac{x^{2}}{2} \right]_{4r^{2}}^{4} = \frac{r}{2}\left(16 - 16r^{4}\right) = 8r\left(1 - r^{4}\right). \]

Step 3 — Integrate over the disk

The inner result depends only on \(r\). Integrate it over \(0 \le r \le 1\):

\[ \int_{0}^{1} 8r\left(1 - r^{4}\right) dr = 8 \int_{0}^{1} \left(r - r^{5}\right) dr = 8 \left[ \frac{r^{2}}{2} - \frac{r^{6}}{6} \right]_{0}^{1} = 8\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{6}\right) = 8 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{8}{3}. \]

Step 4 — Integrate with respect to \(\theta\)

The integrand no longer depends on \(\theta\), so the outer integral only multiplies the result by \(2\pi\):

\[ \iiint_{E} x \, dV = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \frac{8}{3} \, d\theta = \frac{8}{3} \cdot 2\pi = \frac{16\pi}{3}. \]

Answer

\[ \iiint_{E} x \, dV = \frac{16\pi}{3} \approx 16.755. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(\iiint_{E} f \, dV\)The triple integral of the function \(f\) over the three-dimensional region \(E\); it is the limit of sums of \(f\) times small volumes.
\(E\)The solid region of integration, that is, the set of points that satisfy the stated inequalities.
\(dV\)The volume element. In Cartesian coordinates \(dV = dx\,dy\,dz\); in the polar coordinates used here \(dV = r\,dx\,dr\,d\theta\).
\(x = 4y^{2} + 4z^{2}\)A parabolic bowl (an elliptic paraboloid) that opens along the positive \(x\)-axis.
\(r, \theta\)Polar coordinates in the \(yz\)-plane: \(r\) is the distance from the \(x\)-axis and \(\theta\) is the angle around it.
the factor \(r\)The Jacobian factor of polar coordinates. A small polar rectangle has area \(r\,dr\,d\theta\), not \(dr\,d\theta\).
\(D\)The projection of \(E\) onto the \(yz\)-plane, here the unit disk.
\(\pi\)The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, \(\pi \approx 3.14159\).

Verification

Three independent computations support the result.

  • part1_step1.py checks the region description and estimates the integral by Monte Carlo sampling; the estimate is \(16.746\), within one standard error of \(16\pi/3 = 16.755\).
  • part1_step2.py repeats the symbolic evaluation with SymPy and obtains \(16\pi/3\).
  • part1_step3.py evaluates the integral numerically with mpmath in two different orders of integration, and both values agree with \(16\pi/3\) to twelve digits.
