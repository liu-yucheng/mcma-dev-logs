Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.9 · Exercise 43

Drawing a Silo with a Hemispherical Roof

Problem

Use a graphing device to draw a silo that consists of a cylinder with radius \(3\) and height \(10\), with a hemisphere on top.

Reading the shape

Place the base of the silo on the \(xy\)-plane and put its axis on the \(z\)-axis. Three pieces describe the surface.

  • The flat base is the disk of radius \(3\) at height \(0\): \(x^{2} + y^{2} \le 9\), \(z = 0\).
  • The wall is the cylinder of radius \(3\) from \(z = 0\) to \(z = 10\): \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 9\), \(0 \le z \le 10\).
  • The roof is the upper half of the sphere of radius \(3\) whose center is \((0,0,10)\): \(x^{2} + y^{2} + (z - 10)^{2} = 9\), \(z \ge 10\).

The roof meets the wall on the circle \(x^{2} + y^{2} = 9\) at height \(z = 10\), so the two pieces fit without a gap. The dome rises \(3\) units above the wall, and the top of the silo is at \(z = 10 + 3 = 13\).

Drawing the shape

A graphing device draws parameterized surfaces. Use \(r\) for the distance from the \(z\)-axis and \(\theta\) for the angle around it.

\[ \text{wall: } (3\cos\theta,\ 3\sin\theta,\ z), \quad 0 \le z \le 10, \qquad \text{roof: } (3\sin\phi\cos\theta,\ 3\sin\phi\sin\theta,\ 10 + 3\cos\phi), \quad 0 \le \phi \le \frac{\pi}{2}. \]

Here \(\phi\) is measured from the positive \(z\)-axis, so \(\phi = 0\) gives the top of the dome and \(\phi = \pi/2\) gives its rim.

Checking the drawing

Two measures confirm that the drawing has the right size. The capacity of the silo is the sum of the volume of the cylinder and the volume of the half sphere:

\[ V = \pi r^{2} h + \frac{2}{3}\pi r^{3} = \pi \cdot 9 \cdot 10 + \frac{2}{3}\pi \cdot 27 = 90\pi + 18\pi = 108\pi \approx 339.292. \]

The exposed surface area is the lateral area of the wall plus the area of the dome:

\[ A = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^{2} = 2\pi \cdot 3 \cdot 10 + 2\pi \cdot 9 = 60\pi + 18\pi = 78\pi \approx 245.044. \]

A drawing with radius \(3\) has a footprint of diameter \(6\), and its highest point sits at \(z = 13\). Both are marked in panel (b).

Answer

The silo is the solid

\[ x^{2} + y^{2} \le 9, \qquad 0 \le z \le 10 + \sqrt{9 - x^{2} - y^{2}}, \]

built from a cylinder of radius \(3\) and height \(10\) with a hemisphere of radius \(3\) on top. The wall is drawn for \(0 \le z \le 10\), the dome for \(z \ge 10\) with center \((0,0,10)\), and the top of the silo is at height \(13\). The capacity is \(108\pi \approx 339.29\) cubic units.

Notation

SymbolMeaning
cylinderThe surface \(x^{2} + y^{2} = r^{2}\) together with its flat end disks; here the wall of the silo.
hemisphereOne half of a sphere. A hemisphere of radius \(r\) has area \(2\pi r^{2}\) and volume \(\frac{2}{3}\pi r^{3}\).
\(r\)The radius: the distance from the axis of the silo to its wall, here \(3\).
\(\theta\)The angle around the \(z\)-axis, \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\).
\(\phi\)The polar angle of spherical coordinates, measured from the positive \(z\)-axis. On the dome, \(\phi = 0\) is the top and \(\phi = \pi/2\) is the rim.
parameterizationA formula that gives every point of a surface from two parameters. A graphing device plots a grid of these points and joins them.
elevationA drawing of the object as seen from the side, with the vertical dimension in the plane of the page.
