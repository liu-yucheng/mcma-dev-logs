Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.9 · Exercise 43

Drawing a Silo with a Hemispherical Roof

Problem

Use a graphing device to draw a silo that consists of a cylinder with radius 3 and height 10, with a hemisphere on top.

Reading the shape

Place the base of the silo on the xy-plane and put its axis on the z-axis. Three pieces describe the surface.

  • The flat base is the disk of radius 3 at height 0: x2+y2≤9, z=0.
  • The wall is the cylinder of radius 3 from z=0 to z=10: x2+y2=9, 0≤z≤10.
  • The roof is the upper half of the sphere of radius 3 whose center is (0,0,10): x2+y2+(z−10)2=9, z≥10.

The roof meets the wall on the circle x2+y2=9 at height z=10, so the two pieces fit without a gap. The dome rises 3 units above the wall, and the top of the silo is at z=10+3=13.

Drawing the shape

A graphing device draws parameterized surfaces. Use r for the distance from the z-axis and θ for the angle around it.

wall: (3cosθ, 3sinθ, z),0≤z≤10,roof: (3sinϕcosθ, 3sinϕsinθ, 10+3cosϕ),0≤ϕ≤2π​.

Here ϕ is measured from the positive z-axis, so ϕ=0 gives the top of the dome and ϕ=π/2 gives its rim.

Checking the drawing

Two measures confirm that the drawing has the right size. The capacity of the silo is the sum of the volume of the cylinder and the volume of the half sphere:

V=πr2h+32​πr3=π⋅9⋅10+32​π⋅27=90π+18π=108π≈339.292.

The exposed surface area is the lateral area of the wall plus the area of the dome:

A=2πrh+2πr2=2π⋅3⋅10+2π⋅9=60π+18π=78π≈245.044.

A drawing with radius 3 has a footprint of diameter 6, and its highest point sits at z=13. Both are marked in panel (b).

Answer

The silo is the solid

x2+y2≤9,0≤z≤10+9−x2−y2​,

built from a cylinder of radius 3 and height 10 with a hemisphere of radius 3 on top. The wall is drawn for 0≤z≤10, the dome for z≥10 with center (0,0,10), and the top of the silo is at height 13. The capacity is 108π≈339.29 cubic units.

Notation

SymbolMeaning
