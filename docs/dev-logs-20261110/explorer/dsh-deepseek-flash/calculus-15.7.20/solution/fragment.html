Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 20

The Volume between Two Paraboloids

Problem

Use a triple integral to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the paraboloids

\[ y = x^{2} + z^{2} \qquad \text{and} \qquad y = 8 - x^{2} - z^{2}. \]

Reading the solid

Both surfaces are paraboloids of revolution about the \(y\)-axis. The first one, \(y = x^{2} + z^{2}\), opens toward \(+y\) with its vertex at the origin. The second one, \(y = 8 - x^{2} - z^{2}\), opens toward \(-y\) with its vertex at \((0,8,0)\). The solid lies between them, so it is the set of points whose \(y\)-coordinate is at least \(x^{2} + z^{2}\) and at most \(8 - x^{2} - z^{2}\).

The two surfaces meet where both formulas give the same height:

\[ x^{2} + z^{2} = 8 - x^{2} - z^{2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2\left(x^{2} + z^{2}\right) = 8 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x^{2} + z^{2} = 4. \]

So the rim of the solid is the circle of radius \(2\) in the plane \(y = 4\). Every point of the solid lies above the disk

\[ D = \{(x,z) : x^{2} + z^{2} \le 4\} \]

of the \(xz\)-plane, and above a point of this disk the solid runs from the lower paraboloid \(y = x^{2} + z^{2}\) up to the upper paraboloid \(y = 8 - x^{2} - z^{2}\). The vertical extent depends only on the distance from the \(y\)-axis, so cylindrical coordinates about that axis are natural.

Write \(x = r\cos\theta\) and \(z = r\sin\theta\); the coordinate \(y\) does not change. A point has distance \(r\) from the \(y\)-axis, and the two paraboloids become \(y = r^{2}\) and \(y = 8 - r^{2}\). A horizontal section at height \(y\) is a disk, so it has area \(A(y) = \pi r^{2}\). The solid is therefore

\[ E = \{(r,\theta,y) : 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi,\; 0 \le r \le 2,\; r^{2} \le y \le 8 - r^{2}\}. \]

The two cross-sections below make the limits visible. The left panel shows the solid cut by the plane \(z = 0\), where the distance \(r\) runs from \(0\) to \(2\). The right panel marks the chords that give the radius of the horizontal section at three heights. The chord grows from \(0\) at \(y = 0\) to \(2\) at \(y = 4\), then shrinks back to \(0\) at \(y = 8\).

Solution

Step 1 — Set up the iterated integral

The volume is the triple integral of \(1\) over \(E\). The description fixes the order \(dy\,dr\,d\theta\): the inner variable is \(y\), the middle variable is \(r\), and the outer variable is \(\theta\). With the volume element \(dV = r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta\),

\[ V = \iiint_{E} 1 \, dV = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{r^{2}}^{8-r^{2}} r \, dy \, dr \, d\theta. \]

The factor \(r\) is the Jacobian of cylindrical coordinates. It appears because a small piece of the disk of radius \(r\) and angle increment \(d\theta\) has area \(r\,dr\,d\theta\).

Step 2 — Integrate with respect to \(y\)

The integrand does not depend on \(y\), so the inner integral is the height of the solid at radius \(r\):

\[ \int_{r^{2}}^{8-r^{2}} dy = \left[ y \right]_{r^{2}}^{8-r^{2}} = 8 - 2r^{2}. \]

At \(r = 0\) this height is \(8\), and at \(r = 2\) it is \(0\). The height falls to zero exactly at the rim, where the two paraboloids meet.

Step 3 — Integrate with respect to \(r\)

Insert the height and the Jacobian \(r\):

\[ \int_{0}^{2} \left(8 - 2r^{2}\right) r \, dr = \int_{0}^{2} \left(8r - 2r^{3}\right) dr = \left[ 4r^{2} - \frac{r^{4}}{2} \right]_{0}^{2}. \]

At \(r = 2\) the bracket is

\[ 4 \cdot 4 - \frac{16}{2} = 16 - 8 = 8, \]

and at \(r = 0\) it is zero. The radial integral therefore equals \(8\). It is the area under the curve \(r\left(8 - 2r^{2}\right)\) from \(r = 0\) to \(r = 2\).

Step 4 — Integrate with respect to \(\theta\)

Nothing in the integrand depends on \(\theta\), so the outer integral multiplies by the angle \(2\pi\):

\[ V = \int_{0}^{2\pi} 8 \, d\theta = 8 \cdot 2\pi = 16\pi. \]

The numerical value is \(16\pi \approx 50.265482\).

Step 5 — Check with the horizontal sections

The solid is a solid of revolution, so each horizontal section is a disk. Below the meeting circle, at \(0 \le y \le 4\), the section reaches the lower paraboloid, and \(r^{2} \le y\) gives \(r = \sqrt{y}\). Above the meeting circle, at \(4 \le y \le 8\), the section reaches the upper paraboloid, and \(y \le 8 - r^{2}\) gives \(r = \sqrt{8 - y}\). Hence

\[ A(y) = \pi y \quad (0 \le y \le 4), \qquad A(y) = \pi\left(8 - y\right) \quad (4 \le y \le 8). \]

The upper paraboloid is the mirror image of the lower one in the plane \(y = 4\), so the two halves of the solid are congruent and have equal volume. Each half contributes half of the total, and the sum of the sections returns \(16\pi\), the same value as Step 4. The section check confirms the answer with a different order of integration, because it integrates in \(y\) last instead of first.

Answer

\[ V = 16\pi \approx 50.265482. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(\iiint_{E} 1 \, dV\)The triple integral of the constant \(1\) over \(E\); its value is the volume of \(E\).
\(E\)The solid between the two paraboloids, the set of points with \(r^{2} \le y \le 8 - r^{2}\) and \(r \le 2\).
\(y = x^{2} + z^{2}\)The lower paraboloid: a bowl that opens toward \(+y\) with its vertex at the origin.
\(y = 8 - x^{2} - z^{2}\)The upper paraboloid: a bowl that opens toward \(-y\) with its vertex at \((0,8,0)\).
\(r\), \(\theta\)Cylindrical coordinates about the \(y\)-axis: \(r = \sqrt{x^{2} + z^{2}}\) is the distance from that axis, and \(\theta\) is the angle around it.
\(dV = r\,dy\,dr\,d\theta\)The volume element in cylindrical coordinates about the \(y\)-axis; the factor \(r\) is the Jacobian.
\(8 - 2r^{2}\)The height of the solid above the point at distance \(r\) from the \(y\)-axis.
\(D\)The projection of \(E\) onto the \(xz\)-plane, the disk \(x^{2} + z^{2} \le 4\) of radius \(2\).
\(A(y)\)The area of the horizontal section of \(E\) at height \(y\); the section is a disk, so \(A(y) = \pi r^{2}\).

Verification

Three independent computations support the result.

  • part1_step1.py checks the two bounding surfaces and estimates the volume by Monte Carlo sampling in the box \(-2 \le x \le 2\), \(-2 \le z \le 2\), \(0 \le y \le 8\), whose volume is \(128.0\). The estimate is \(50.24278\) against \(50.26548\), a deviation of \(-0.73\) standard errors with standard error \(0.03125\). The sampled points fall inside the solid with fraction \(0.39252\), close to the ratio \(16\pi/128\). The same script reports the heights \(1.0000\) and \(7.0000\) at \(r = 1\), the equal values \(4.0000\) at the rim \(r = 2\), and the greatest separation \(8.0\) at \(r = 0\).
  • part1_step2.py evaluates the integral with SymPy. It returns the height \(8 - 2r^{2}\), the integrand \(2r\left(4 - r^{2}\right)\), the radial antiderivative \(r^{2}\left(8 - r^{2}\right)/2\), the radial integral \(8\), and the volume \(16\pi = 50.265482\). The section check returns the areas \(\pi y\) and \(\pi\left(8 - y\right)\), the total \(16\pi\), and the heights \(8\) and \(0\) at \(r = 0\) and \(r = 2\).
  • part1_step3.py evaluates the volume with mpmath quadrature at \(25\) digits in two orders, height first and sections first. Both values are \(50.2654824574367\), with a deviation of \(0.0\) from \(16\pi\).
