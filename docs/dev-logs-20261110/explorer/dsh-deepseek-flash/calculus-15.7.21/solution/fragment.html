Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 21

The Volume above a Parabola and below a Slanted Plane

Problem

Use a triple integral to find the volume of the solid enclosed by the parabolic cylinder \(y = x^{2}\) and the planes \(z = 0\) and \(y + z = 1\).

Reading the solid

The surface \(y = x^{2}\) does not mention \(z\): every vertical line through a point of the parabola \(y = x^{2}\) in the \(xy\)-plane belongs to it. So it is a parabolic cylinder that rises from the floor \(z = 0\) in the \(z\)-direction. The solid is on the concave side of this wall, where \(y \ge x^{2}\).

The plane \(z = 0\) is the floor. The plane \(y + z = 1\) is the roof. Solve the roof equation for \(z\):

\[ y + z = 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad z = 1 - y. \]

The roof is highest over the \(x\)-axis, where \(y = 0\) gives \(z = 1\), and it comes down to the floor at the line \(y = 1\). Above the floor the solid exists only where the roof is at or above the floor, that is where \(1 - y \ge 0\), or \(y \le 1\).

The wall and the roof meet where both conditions hold, so the solid projects onto

\[ D_{xy} = \{(x,y) : -1 \le x \le 1,\; x^{2} \le y \le 1\}, \]

the region between the parabola \(y = x^{2}\) and the line \(y = 1\). The two curves meet at \((-1,1)\) and \((1,1)\), because \(x^{2} = 1\) at those two values of \(x\) and at no other point.

Above a point of this region the solid runs from the floor \(z = 0\) up to the roof \(z = 1 - y\).

Therefore

\[ E = \{(x,y,z) : -1 \le x \le 1,\; x^{2} \le y \le 1,\; 0 \le z \le 1 - y\}. \]

Two views make the limits visible. The left panel below shows the projection \(D_{xy}\), in which \(x\) runs from \(-1\) to \(1\) and \(y\) runs from the parabola to the line \(y = 1\). The right panel shows the section at \(x = 0.5\), which is a triangle in the \(yz\)-plane.

Solution

Step 1 — Set up the iterated integral

The volume is the triple integral of \(1\) over \(E\). The description fixes the order \(dz\,dy\,dx\): the inner variable is \(z\), the middle variable is \(y\), and the outer variable is \(x\):

\[ V = \iiint_{E} 1 \, dV = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{x^{2}}^{1} \int_{0}^{1-y} dz \, dy \, dx. \]

Step 2 — Integrate with respect to \(z\)

The integrand does not depend on \(z\), so the inner integral is the height of the solid above the point \((x,y)\):

\[ \int_{0}^{1-y} dz = \left[ z \right]_{0}^{1-y} = 1 - y. \]

The height does not depend on \(x\). It equals \(1\) on the \(x\)-axis and falls to \(0\) at the line \(y = 1\).

Step 3 — Integrate with respect to \(y\)

Now integrate \(1 - y\) from the wall \(y = x^{2}\) up to the ridge \(y = 1\):

\[ \int_{x^{2}}^{1} \left(1 - y\right) dy = \left[ y - \frac{y^{2}}{2} \right]_{x^{2}}^{1} = \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) - \left(x^{2} - \frac{x^{4}}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2} - x^{2} + \frac{x^{4}}{2}. \]

The result is the area of the triangular section at \(x\), and it depends on \(x\) only through \(x^{2}\). At \(x = 0\) the section has area \(1/2\); at \(x = 1\) the area is \(0\), because the triangle has shrunk to a point.

Step 4 — Integrate with respect to \(x\)

The outer integral is a polynomial integral:

\[ V = \int_{-1}^{1} \left(\frac{1}{2} - x^{2} + \frac{x^{4}}{2}\right) dx = \left[ \frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^{3}}{3} + \frac{x^{5}}{10} \right]_{-1}^{1}. \]

At \(x = 1\) the bracket is

\[ \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{15}{30} - \frac{10}{30} + \frac{3}{30} = \frac{8}{30} = \frac{4}{15}, \]

and at \(x = -1\) it is the negative of this value, because every term has an odd power of \(x\). The difference is twice \(4/15\):

\[ V = \frac{4}{15} - \left(-\frac{4}{15}\right) = \frac{8}{15}. \]

The numerical value is \(8/15 \approx 0.533333\).

Step 5 — Check with three families of sections

A section at a fixed \(x\) is the triangle of Step 3, and its area integrates to \(8/15\). Two more families give the same value. A section at a fixed height \(z\) is the parabolic segment between \(x = -\sqrt{1 - z}\) and \(x = \sqrt{1 - z}\), with height \(1 - z - x^{2}\). Its area is

\[ A(z) = \int_{-\sqrt{1-z}}^{\sqrt{1-z}} \left(1 - z - x^{2}\right) dx = \frac{4}{3}\left(1 - z\right)^{3/2}, \]

and \(\int_{0}^{1} A(z)\,dz = 8/15\). A section at a fixed \(y\) is a rectangle of width \(2\sqrt{y}\) and height \(1 - y\), so its area is \(2\sqrt{y}\left(1 - y\right)\), and \(\int_{0}^{1} 2\sqrt{y}\left(1 - y\right) dy = 8/15\) as well. The three families slice the same solid in three directions, and each sum returns \(8/15\).

Answer

\[ V = \frac{8}{15} \approx 0.533333. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(\iiint_{E} 1 \, dV\)The triple integral of the constant \(1\) over \(E\); its value is the volume of \(E\).
\(E\)The solid of integration: the points with \(-1 \le x \le 1\), \(x^{2} \le y \le 1\), and \(0 \le z \le 1 - y\).
\(dV = dz\,dy\,dx\)The volume element in Cartesian coordinates, the product of the three coordinate differentials in the chosen order.
parabolic cylinder \(y = x^{2}\)The surface made by moving the parabola \(y = x^{2}\) parallel to the \(z\)-axis; it is the wall of the solid.
\(z = 0\)The floor of the solid, the \(xy\)-plane.
\(y + z = 1\)The roof of the solid; solved for \(z\) it gives the height \(z = 1 - y\).
\(D_{xy}\)The projection of \(E\) onto the \(xy\)-plane, the region between the parabola \(y = x^{2}\) and the line \(y = 1\).
\(1 - y\)The height of the solid above the point \((x,y)\); it does not depend on \(x\).
\(A(z)\)The area of the section at a fixed height \(z\), a parabolic segment of area \(\frac{4}{3}\left(1 - z\right)^{3/2}\).
\(\Delta x\)The thickness of one slice in the figure; the slices approximate \(E\), and the sum of their volumes becomes the integral as \(\Delta x \to 0\).

Verification

Three independent computations support the result.

  • part1_step1.py checks the three bounding surfaces and estimates the volume by Monte Carlo sampling in the box \(-1 \le x \le 1\), \(0 \le y \le 1\), \(0 \le z \le 1\), whose volume is \(2.0\). The estimate is \(0.53293\) against \(0.53333\), a deviation of \(-0.92\) standard errors with standard error \(0.00044\). The sampled points fall inside the solid with fraction \(0.26646\), close to the ratio \((8/15)/2\). The script also reports the wall values \(x^{2}\) at five values of \(x\) and the roof heights \(1.0000\), \(0.5000\), and \(0.0000\) at \(y = 0\), \(y = 0.5\), and \(y = 1\).
  • part1_step2.py evaluates the integral with SymPy. It returns the inner integral \(x^{4}/2 - x^{2} + 1/2\), the antiderivative \(x^{5}/10 - x^{3}/3 + x/2\), the values \(4/15\) and \(-4/15\) at \(x = 1\) and \(x = -1\), and the volume \(8/15 = 0.533333\). The section checks return the areas \(x^{4}/2 - x^{2} + 1/2\), \(4\left(1 - z\right)^{3/2}/3\), and \(2\sqrt{y}\left(1 - y\right)\), and each of the three integrates to \(8/15\). At \(x = 0.5\) the section area is \(0.28125\), with edges \(y = 0.25000\) and \(z = 0.75000\).
  • part1_step3.py evaluates the volume with mpmath quadrature at \(25\) digits in two orders, projection first and sections first. Both values are \(0.533333333333333\), with a deviation of \(0.0\) from \(8/15\).
