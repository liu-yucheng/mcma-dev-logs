Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 50

Mass, Center of Mass, and Moment of Inertia of a Quarter-Cylinder Wedge

A tall quarter cylinder is cut by the plane \(y = 3x\). The density depends on \(x\) and \(y\) only, so the height of the solid enters through one integral in \(z\).

Problem

Let \(E\) be the solid bounded by the cylinder \(y^{2} + z^{2} = 9\) and the planes \(x = 0\), \(y = 3x\), and \(z = 0\) in the first octant. The density function is

\[ \rho(x, y, z) = x^{2} + y^{2} . \]

Find (a) the mass of \(E\), (b) the center of mass of \(E\), and (c) the moment of inertia of \(E\) about the \(z\)-axis, correct to three decimal places. An exact form is also given for each value.

Reading the solid

The cylinder \(y^{2} + z^{2} = 9\) has its axis on the \(x\)-axis and radius \(3\). The plane \(x = 0\) caps the solid at the back, the plane \(z = 0\) holds it on the floor, and the plane \(y = 3x\) cuts it at the front.

On the floor \(z = 0\) the cylinder gives \(y \leq 3\). The plane \(y = 3x\) gives \(x \leq y / 3\), so the floor is the triangle with corners \((0, 0)\), \((0, 3)\), and \((1, 3)\). Above a point \((x, y)\) of that triangle the solid runs from \(z = 0\) up to the cylinder surface \(z = \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}\). The height therefore depends on \(y\) alone. This gives the three sets of bounds

\[ 0 \leq y \leq 3, \qquad 0 \leq x \leq \frac{y}{3}, \qquad 0 \leq z \leq \sqrt{9 - y^{2}} . \]

The order \(z\), then \(x\), then \(y\) matches this shape: the inner integral covers one vertical segment, the middle integral covers one horizontal strip of the triangle from \(x = 0\) to the plane \(y = 3x\), and the outer integral sweeps the triangle from the \(x\)-axis to the far corner \((1, 3)\).

Solution

Step 1. Write the mass integral

The mass is the integral of the density over the solid. With the bounds above,

\[ M = \int_{0}^{3} \int_{0}^{y/3} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) dz \, dx \, dy . \]

Step 2. Integrate in \(z\)

The density does not depend on \(z\). The inner integral therefore gives only the height of the solid at the fixed point \((x, y)\):

\[ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) dz = \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) \sqrt{9 - y^{2}} . \]

The mass becomes a double integral over the triangular base:

\[ M = \int_{0}^{3} \int_{0}^{y/3} \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) \sqrt{9 - y^{2}} \, dx \, dy . \]

Step 3. Integrate in \(x\)

At fixed \(y\) the inner factor \(x^{2} + y^{2}\) is a polynomial in \(x\), so

\[ \int_{0}^{y/3} \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) dx = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{y}{3} \right)^{3} + y^{2} \left( \frac{y}{3} \right) = \frac{y^{3}}{81} + \frac{y^{3}}{3} = \frac{28 y^{3}}{81} . \]

The cube term contributes \(y^{3} / 81\) and the constant term contributes \(y^{3} / 3\). The mass is then a single integral over \(y\):

\[ M = \frac{28}{81} \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} \sqrt{9 - y^{2}} \, dy . \]

Step 4. Substitute \(y = 3 \sin t\)

Set \(y = 3 \sin t\). Then \(dy = 3 \cos t \, dt\) and \(\sqrt{9 - y^{2}} = 3 \cos t\) on \(0 \leq t \leq \pi / 2\), so the square root disappears and two extra factors of \(3\) enter:

\[ \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} \sqrt{9 - y^{2}} \, dy = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \left( 27 \sin^{3} t \right) \left( 3 \cos t \right) \left( 3 \cos t \, dt \right) = 243 \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{3} t \, \cos^{2} t \, dt . \]

The factor \(3 \cos t\) from \(dy\) belongs to the substitution. Together with \(\sqrt{9 - y^{2}} = 3 \cos t\) it gives the two cosine factors of \(243 \sin^{3} t \, \cos^{2} t\). The trigonometric integral evaluates to \(2 / 15\), so

\[ \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} \sqrt{9 - y^{2}} \, dy = 243 \cdot \frac{2}{15} = \frac{486}{15} = \frac{162}{5} . \]

Step 5. Collect the radial and trigonometric integrals

Every remaining integral follows the same substitution \(y = 3 \sin t\), with \(dy = 3 \cos t \, dt\). Here \(s\) is shorthand for the height \(\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}\).

IntegralValueIntegralValue
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} s \, dy\)\(162/5\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{3} t \, \cos^{2} t \, dt\)\(2/15\)
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{3} y^{4} s \, dy\)\(729\pi/32\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{4} t \, \cos^{2} t \, dt\)\(\pi/32\)
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{3} y^{5} s \, dy\)\(5832/35\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^{5} t \, \cos^{2} t \, dt\)\(8/105\)
\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} \, dy\)\(81/4\)\(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} \left( 9 - y^{2} \right) dy\)\(243/4\)

The trigonometric values come from the substitution \(u = \cos t\) for the odd powers of \(\sin t\) and from the half-angle formulas for \(\sin^{4} t \cos^{2} t\). The last row is elementary. These eight integrals cover every step below.

Step 6. The mass

Insert the first value of the table:

\[ M = \frac{28}{81} \cdot \frac{162}{5} = \frac{28 \cdot 2}{5} = \frac{56}{5} = 11.2 . \]

To three decimal places the mass is \(11.200\).

Step 7. The moment about the \(yz\)-plane

The first moment of mass about the plane \(x = 0\) weights each point by its distance \(x\) from that plane:

\[ M_{yz} = \int_{0}^{3} \int_{0}^{y/3} \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} x \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) dz \, dx \, dy . \]

The inner integral again gives the height \(s = \sqrt{9 - y^{2}}\), and the integral in \(x\) is elementary:

\[ \int_{0}^{y/3} \left( x^{3} + x y^{2} \right) dx = \frac{1}{4} \left( \frac{y}{3} \right)^{4} + \frac{y^{2}}{2} \left( \frac{y}{3} \right)^{2} = \frac{y^{4}}{324} + \frac{y^{4}}{18} = \frac{19 y^{4}}{324} . \]

Insert the second value of the table:

\[ M_{yz} = \frac{19}{324} \cdot \frac{729 \pi}{32} = \frac{171 \pi}{128} \approx 4.196971 . \]

Step 8. The moment about the \(xz\)-plane

Repeat with the weight \(y\). The density factor is the same, so the integral in \(x\) gains one factor \(y\):

\[ \int_{0}^{y/3} y \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) dx = y \left( \frac{y^{3}}{81} + \frac{y^{3}}{3} \right) = \frac{28 y^{4}}{81} . \]
\[ M_{xz} = \frac{28}{81} \cdot \frac{729 \pi}{32} = \frac{63 \pi}{8} \approx 24.740042 . \]

Step 9. The moment about the \(xy\)-plane

The weight is \(z\), so the inner integral is elementary in \(z\) and the height enters as its square:

\[ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}} z \, dz = \frac{9 - y^{2}}{2} . \]

Multiply by the integral in \(x\) from Step 3 and use the last value of the table:

\[ M_{xy} = \frac{28}{81} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{3} y^{3} \left( 9 - y^{2} \right) dy = \frac{14}{81} \cdot \frac{243}{4} = \frac{21}{2} = 10.5 . \]

Step 10. The center of mass

Divide each first moment by the mass:

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{M_{yz}}{M} = \frac{171 \pi / 128}{56 / 5} = \frac{855 \pi}{7168} \approx 0.374730 , \]
\[ \bar{y} = \frac{M_{xz}}{M} = \frac{63 \pi / 8}{56 / 5} = \frac{315 \pi}{448} = \frac{45 \pi}{64} \approx 2.208932 , \]
\[ \bar{z} = \frac{M_{xy}}{M} = \frac{21 / 2}{56 / 5} = \frac{105}{112} = \frac{15}{16} = 0.9375 . \]

The center of mass lies inside the solid: \(0 < \bar{x} < 1\), \(0 < \bar{y} < 3\), and \(0 < \bar{z} < 3\). The value \(\bar{y} \approx 2.209\) lies close to the radius \(3\) because the density \(x^{2} + y^{2}\) grows with \(y\). The value \(\bar{z} \approx 0.938\) stays small because the height of the solid falls to zero as \(y\) grows.

Step 11. The moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis

The squared distance from a point to the \(z\)-axis is \(x^{2} + y^{2}\), so the integrand is \(\rho \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right) = \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right)^{2}\):

\[ I_{z} = \iiint_{E} \left( x^{2} + y^{2} \right)^{2} dV = \iiint_{E} \left( x^{4} + 2 x^{2} y^{2} + y^{4} \right) dV . \]

The integral in \(z\) gives the height \(s\). The integral in \(x\) then gives

\[ \int_{0}^{y/3} \left( x^{4} + 2 x^{2} y^{2} + y^{4} \right) dx = \frac{y^{5}}{1215} + \frac{2 y^{5}}{81} + \frac{y^{5}}{3} = \frac{436 y^{5}}{1215} . \]

Insert the third value of the table:

\[ I_{z} = \frac{436}{1215} \cdot \frac{5832}{35} = \frac{436 \cdot 24}{5 \cdot 35} = \frac{10464}{175} \approx 59.794286 . \]

Answer

(a) The mass is

\[ M = \frac{56}{5} = 11.200 . \]

(b) The center of mass is

\[ \left( \bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z} \right) = \left( \frac{855 \pi}{7168}, \frac{45 \pi}{64}, \frac{15}{16} \right) \approx \left( 0.375, 2.209, 0.938 \right) . \]

(c) The moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis is

\[ I_{z} = \frac{10464}{175} \approx 59.794 . \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(E\)The solid inside the cylinder, above the floor, between the two planes.
\(\rho(x, y, z)\)Mass density at the point \((x, y, z)\), in mass per unit volume.
\(dV\)Volume element, equal to \(dz \, dx \, dy\) in the order used here.
First octantThe region where \(x \geq 0\), \(y \geq 0\), and \(z \geq 0\).
\(s\)Shorthand for \(\sqrt{9 - y^{2}}\), the height of the solid at the level \(y\).
\(M\)Mass of the solid, the integral of \(\rho\) over \(E\).
\(M_{yz}, M_{xz}, M_{xy}\)First moments of mass about the planes \(x = 0\), \(y = 0\), and \(z = 0\).
\(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}\)Coordinates of the center of mass, for example \(\bar{x} = M_{yz} / M\).
\(I_{z}\)Moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis, the integral of \(\rho\) times the squared distance \(x^{2} + y^{2}\).
\(y = 3 \sin t\)Substitution for the radial integrals, with \(dy = 3 \cos t \, dt\) and \(0 \leq t \leq \pi / 2\).
\(dy = 3 \cos t \, dt\)Jacobian of that substitution. It supplies one factor \(3 \cos t\) in every transformed integral.
