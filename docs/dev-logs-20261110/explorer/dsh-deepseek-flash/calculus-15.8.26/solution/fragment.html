Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.8 · Exercise 26

Volume Cut from a Sphere by an Off-Centre Cylinder

Problem

Use cylindrical coordinates.

(a) Find the volume of the solid that the cylinder \(r = a\cos\theta\) cuts out of the sphere of radius \(a\) centred at the origin.

(b) Illustrate the solid of part (a) by graphing the sphere and the cylinder on the same screen.

Reading the solid

The sphere has the equation \(x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2} = a^{2}\). The cylinder has the polar equation \(r = a\cos\theta\). Multiply by \(r\) to see what this circle is:

\[ r = a\cos\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r^{2} = ar \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{2} + y^{2} = ax \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(x - \frac{a}{2}\right)^{2} + y^{2} = \left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^{2}. \]

So the cylinder has radius \(a/2\) and its axis is the vertical line through \((a/2, 0, 0)\). The axis is off centre: it lies inside the sphere, so the solid is the part of the sphere that also lies inside the cylinder.

The solid is therefore

\[ E = \left\{ (r,\theta,z) : -\frac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2}, \ 0 \le r \le a\cos\theta, \ -\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}} \le z \le \sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}} \right\}. \]

The range \(-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2\) is part of the description, not an extra assumption. The radius \(r\) is a distance, so \(r \ge 0\), and \(r \le a\cos\theta\) can hold only where \(\cos\theta \ge 0\). The angle \(\theta\) must stay in that half of the plane.

The two surfaces meet on a closed curve, the intersection of the sphere and the cylinder. On that curve \(x^{2} + y^{2} = ax\), so the sphere gives \(ax + z^{2} = a^{2}\), that is \(z = \pm\sqrt{a(a - x)}\). The curve has two branches, one above and one below the \(xy\)-plane.

Look at the solid from above. Its shadow in the \(xy\)-plane is the disk that the cylinder cuts there, and that disk is the region \(r \le a\cos\theta\).

Setting up the integral

In cylindrical coordinates the volume element is \(dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\). The height of the solid above the point \((r,\theta)\) is the distance between the lower and upper sheets of the sphere:

\[ 2\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}. \]

Insert this height and the limits of \(E\):

\[ V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} \int_{-\sqrt{a^{2}-r^{2}}}^{\sqrt{a^{2}-r^{2}}} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}\,dr\,d\theta. \]

Evaluating the integral

Step 1. Integrate with respect to \(r\). Put \(u = a^{2} - r^{2}\), so \(du = -2r\,dr\). Then

\[ \int 2r\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}\,dr = -\int u^{1/2}\,du = -\frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} = -\frac{2}{3}\left(a^{2} - r^{2}\right)^{3/2}. \]

At the upper limit \(r = a\cos\theta\),

\[ a^{2} - r^{2} = a^{2} - a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta = a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta, \qquad \left(a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right)^{3/2} = a^{3}\left|\sin\theta\right|^{3}. \]

The absolute value is needed because the square root is a distance. So the integral over \(r\) gives the slice volume

\[ \int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} 2r\sqrt{a^{2} - r^{2}}\,dr = \frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(1 - \left|\sin\theta\right|^{3}\right). \]

Step 2. Integrate with respect to \(\theta\). The slice depends on \(\left|\sin\theta\right|\), and the two halves of the interval give equal values. Double the integral over \(0 \le \theta \le \pi/2\), where \(\sin\theta \ge 0\) and the absolute value can be dropped:

\[ V = 2\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(1 - \sin^{3}\theta\right)d\theta = \frac{4}{3}a^{3}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(1 - \sin^{3}\theta\right)d\theta. \]

Reduce \(\sin^{3}\theta\) with \(\sin^{2}\theta = 1 - \cos^{2}\theta\) and the substitution \(u = \cos\theta\):

\[ \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{3}\theta\,d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(1 - \cos^{2}\theta\right)\sin\theta\,d\theta = \left[-\cos\theta + \frac{\cos^{3}\theta}{3}\right]_{0}^{\pi/2} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}. \]

Hence

\[ V = \frac{4}{3}a^{3}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{2}{3}\right) = \frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(\pi - \frac{4}{3}\right) = \frac{2a^{3}(3\pi - 4)}{9}. \]

Checking the value

The answer must lie between two known volumes. The sphere holds \(4\pi a^{3}/3\), and the cylinder holds \(\pi (a/2)^{2}(2a) = \pi a^{3}/2\). The solid is the overlap of the two, so its volume must not exceed the smaller value:

\[ \frac{2a^{3}(3\pi - 4)}{9} \approx 1.2055\,a^{3} \quad<\quad \frac{\pi a^{3}}{2} \approx 1.5708\,a^{3} \quad<\quad \frac{4\pi a^{3}}{3} \approx 4.1888\,a^{3}. \]

The overlap is about three quarters of the cylinder, namely \(1.2055/1.5708 = 0.767\) of it, as it should be, because the cylinder lies well inside the sphere. As a fraction of the sphere the volume is

\[ \frac{V}{4\pi a^{3}/3} = \frac{\pi - 4/3}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{2}{3\pi} \approx 0.2878. \]

Three independent computations give the same number for \(a = 1\), namely \(1.205506\). They are the closed form, a double quadrature, and a Monte Carlo count of points that lie in both solids.

Answer

(a) The volume is

\[ V = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{a\cos\theta} \int_{-\sqrt{a^{2}-r^{2}}}^{\sqrt{a^{2}-r^{2}}} r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{2a^{3}(3\pi - 4)}{9} = \frac{2}{3}a^{3}\left(\pi - \frac{4}{3}\right) \approx 1.2055\,a^{3}. \]

(b) The solid is drawn by plotting the sphere and the cylinder on the same axes. The sphere is \(x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2} = a^{2}\) and the cylinder is \((x - a/2)^{2} + y^{2} = (a/2)^{2}\). The part of the sphere inside the cylinder is the solid of part (a).

Notation

SymbolMeaning
cylindrical coordinatesThe triple \((r,\theta,z)\), where \(r\) is the distance from the \(z\)-axis, \(\theta\) is the angle from the positive \(x\)-axis, and \(z\) is the usual height. The volume element is \(dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\).
\(r = a\cos\theta\)A circle through the origin with centre \((a/2, 0)\) and radius \(a/2\). In Cartesian form it is \(x^{2} + y^{2} = ax\).
\(-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2\)The set of angles where \(\cos\theta \ge 0\). Outside this range the bound \(r \le a\cos\theta\) would give a negative radius, which is impossible.
\(\left|\sin\theta\right|\)The height factor at the wall of the cylinder. It is positive on the right half of the base and negative on the left half, and the slice volume uses its absolute value.
intersection curveThe set of points on both surfaces, \(z = \pm\sqrt{a(a - x)}\). It is also called a Viviani curve. The two branches are the upper and lower rims of the solid.
overlapThe solid is the intersection of the ball \(x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2} \le a^{2}\) and the solid cylinder \((x - a/2)^{2} + y^{2} \le (a/2)^{2}\).
