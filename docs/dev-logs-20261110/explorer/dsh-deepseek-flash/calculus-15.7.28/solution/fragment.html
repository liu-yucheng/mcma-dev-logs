Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 28

The Solid of an Iterated Integral: Bounding Surfaces and Volume

Problem

Sketch the solid whose volume is given by the iterated integral, name its bounding surfaces, and compute the volume.

\[ \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{2-y}\int_{0}^{4-y^{2}} dx\,dz\,dy. \]

The exercise shows only the integral, so the first task is to read the solid out of the three sets of limits.

Reading the solid

The differentials name the variables from the inside out. The innermost integral is over \(x\), the middle integral is over \(z\), and the outer integral is over \(y\). Attach each pair of limits to its variable:

\[ 0 \le x \le 4 - y^{2}, \qquad 0 \le z \le 2 - y, \qquad 0 \le y \le 2. \]

Each limit is a surface. The upper limit of \(x\) gives the parabolic cylinder \(x = 4 - y^{2}\). That equation does not contain \(z\), so the surface is a sheet of copies of the parabola \(x = 4 - y^{2}\), translated along the \(z\)-direction. The upper limit of \(z\) gives the tilted plane \(z = 2 - y\), which is also independent of \(x\). The three remaining limits give the coordinate planes \(x = 0\), \(z = 0\), and \(y = 0\).

The limits of \(z\) and \(y\) do not mention \(x\). They alone describe the shadow that the solid casts on the \(yz\)-plane:

\[ D = \{(y,z) : 0 \le y \le 2,\; 0 \le z \le 2 - y\}. \]

The set \(D\) is the triangle with vertices \((0,0)\), \((0,2)\), and \((2,0)\). The limit \(x = 4 - y^{2}\) depends on \(y\) only, so the \(x\)-fibre above a point of \(D\) has the same length on every horizontal cut at that height.

At \(y = 2\) both the \(x\)-limit and the \(z\)-limit are zero, so the solid collapses to the point \((0,2,0)\). At \(y = 0\) the cross-section is the full rectangle \(0 \le x \le 4\), \(0 \le z \le 2\), and the volume of that rectangle is \(8\). The volume of the solid is therefore smaller than \(8\), which the final value confirms.

Solution

Step 1 — Write the volume as a triple integral

The volume of a solid is the triple integral of the constant \(1\) over the solid. Insert the six limits:

\[ V = \iiint_{E} 1 \, dV = \int_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{2-y}\int_{0}^{4-y^{2}} dx\,dz\,dy. \]

Step 2 — Integrate with respect to \(x\)

The variables \(y\) and \(z\) are constant on the inner integral, so the inner integral is the length of the \(x\)-fibre:

\[ \int_{0}^{4-y^{2}} dx = \left[ x \right]_{0}^{4-y^{2}} = 4 - y^{2}. \]

Step 3 — Integrate with respect to \(z\)

The result depends on \(y\) only, so it leaves the middle integral as a constant factor:

\[ \int_{0}^{2-y} \left(4 - y^{2}\right) dz = \left(4 - y^{2}\right)\left[ z \right]_{0}^{2-y} = \left(2 - y\right)\left(4 - y^{2}\right). \]

Expand the product before the last integration:

\[ \left(2 - y\right)\left(4 - y^{2}\right) = 8 - 4y - 2y^{2} + y^{3}. \]

Step 4 — Integrate with respect to \(y\)

Integrate the cubic term by term:

\[ \int_{0}^{2} \left(8 - 4y - 2y^{2} + y^{3}\right) dy = \left[ 8y - 2y^{2} - \frac{2y^{3}}{3} + \frac{y^{4}}{4} \right]_{0}^{2}. \]

Evaluate at the upper limit:

\[ 16 - 8 - \frac{16}{3} + 4 = 12 - \frac{16}{3} = \frac{36 - 16}{3} = \frac{20}{3}. \]

The lower limit gives zero, so the volume is \(20/3 \approx 6.667\).

Step 5 — Confirm the region with a second order of integration

A different order tests the reading of the solid. Solve \(x = 4 - y^{2}\) for \(y\) to get \(y = \sqrt{4 - x}\), and keep \(z\) between \(0\) and \(2 - y\):

\[ V = \int_{0}^{4}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{4-x}}\int_{0}^{2-y} dz\,dy\,dx. \]

The inner integral gives \(2 - y\), and the middle integral gives

\[ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{4-x}} \left(2 - y\right) dy = 2\sqrt{4 - x} - \frac{4 - x}{2}. \]

Integrate both terms from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 4\):

\[ \int_{0}^{4} 2\sqrt{4 - x}\,dx = \frac{32}{3}, \qquad \int_{0}^{4} \frac{4 - x}{2}\,dx = 4, \qquad V = \frac{32}{3} - 4 = \frac{20}{3}. \]

Both orders give the same volume, so the sketch and the limits describe one solid.

Answer

The solid is the set of points that satisfy

\[ 0 \le x \le 4 - y^{2}, \qquad 0 \le z \le 2 - y, \qquad 0 \le y \le 2, \]

and its volume is

\[ V = \frac{20}{3} \approx 6.667. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(\iiint_{E} f \, dV\)The triple integral of the function \(f\) over the three-dimensional region \(E\); it is the limit of sums of \(f\) times small volumes.
\(E\)The solid region of integration, that is, the set of points that satisfy the three pairs of inequalities.
\(dV\)The volume element. In Cartesian coordinates \(dV = dx\,dz\,dy\), and the order of the three differentials fixes the order of the three integrations.
\(D\)The projection (shadow) of \(E\) on the \(yz\)-plane, here the triangle \(0 \le y \le 2\), \(0 \le z \le 2 - y\).
inner, middle, outerThe order of the three integrals. The inner integral runs over \(x\), the middle integral over \(z\), and the outer integral over \(y\).
\(x = 4 - y^{2}\)A parabolic cylinder. The curve \(x = 4 - y^{2}\) in the \(xy\)-plane is copied along the \(z\)-direction, so the surface is curved in \(y\) and straight in \(z\).
\(z = 2 - y\)A plane that contains the \(x\)-direction. It is the top face of the solid and it descends as \(y\) grows.
fibreThe segment of the solid above one point of \(D\). Its length is the value of the inner integral at that point.
\(\le\)Less than or equal to. The solid includes its boundary surfaces, which does not change the volume.

Verification

Three scripts support the page. All runs use the bundled Python interpreter.

  • part1_step1.py checks the limits at five heights and estimates the volume by Monte Carlo sampling of the box \( [0,4] \times [0,2] \times [0,2] \). It prints \(6.66653\) against \(20/3 = 6.66667\), a deviation of \(-0.04\) standard errors.
  • part1_step2.py repeats the three integrations with SymPy, prints the inner result \(4 - y^{2}\), the expanded middle result \(y^{3} - 2y^{2} - 4y + 8\), and the outer result \(20/3\). It also evaluates the order \(dz\,dy\,dx\) and obtains \(20/3\).
  • part1_step3.py evaluates both orders with 20-digit mpmath quadrature. Both values equal \(6.666666666666667\), and both differences from \(20/3\) print as \(0.0\).
