Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 46

Moment of Inertia of a Solid Cone about Its Axis

Problem

Assume that the solid has constant density \(k\). Find the moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis of the solid cone \(\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}} \le z \le h\).

Reading the solid

The lower surface is the cone \(z = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\), which opens upward from the origin. The upper surface is the horizontal plane \(z = h\). The two surfaces meet where \(h = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\), that is, on the circle of radius \(h\) in the plane \(z = h\). The solid is a cone of height \(h\) whose base radius also equals \(h\).

Cylindrical coordinates fit the rotational symmetry. Put \(x = r\cos\theta\) and \(y = r\sin\theta\). Then \(x^{2} + y^{2} = r^{2}\), so the cone surface is simply \(z = r\) and the solid is described by

\[ r \le z \le h, \qquad 0 \le r \le h, \qquad 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi. \]

The volume element is \(dV = r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta\). The factor \(r\) is the circumference of the shell at radius \(r\).

Every point of the solid at radius \(r\) has distance \(r\) from the \(z\)-axis, and the perpendicular distance enters the moment of inertia as its square. The moment density is therefore \(k r^{2}\):

\[ I_{z} = \iiint_{E} (x^{2} + y^{2}) \rho \, dV = k \iiint_{E} r^{2} \, dV. \]

Solution

Step 1 — Set up the iterated integral

Insert the bounds and the volume element:

\[ I_{z} = k \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{h} \int_{r}^{h} r^{2} \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta = k \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{h} \int_{r}^{h} r^{3} \, dz \, dr \, d\theta. \]

The two powers of \(r\) combine: one factor is the distance squared, and the other is the circumference factor of the shell.

Step 2 — Integrate with respect to \(z\)

The integrand does not depend on \(z\), so the inner integral gives the height of the shell:

\[ \int_{r}^{h} r^{3} \, dz = r^{3}(h - r). \]

Step 3 — Integrate with respect to \(r\)

Expand the product and integrate term by term:

\[ \int_{0}^{h} r^{3}(h - r) \, dr = \int_{0}^{h} \left( h r^{3} - r^{4} \right) dr = h \cdot \frac{h^{4}}{4} - \frac{h^{5}}{5} = h^{5}\left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{5} \right) = \frac{h^{5}}{20}. \]

Step 4 — Integrate with respect to \(\theta\)

The last integral runs over a full turn:

\[ I_{z} = k \int_{0}^{2\pi} \frac{h^{5}}{20} \, d\theta = k \cdot 2\pi \cdot \frac{h^{5}}{20} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10}. \]

Step 5 — Check the answer against the mass

The mass of the cone is one third of the mass of the enclosing cylinder:

\[ m = \iiint_{E} k \, dV = k \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{h} \int_{r}^{h} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta = 2\pi k \int_{0}^{h} r(h - r) \, dr = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3}. \]

Divide the moment by the mass. The density and the height cancel in a way that shows how the moment scales:

\[ \frac{I_{z}}{m} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}/10}{\pi k h^{3}/3} = \frac{3 h^{2}}{10}, \qquad I_{z} = \frac{3}{10} m h^{2}. \]

The factor \(3/10\) lies between the value \(1\) for a thin hoop of radius \(h\) and the value \(0\) for a point mass on the axis. That ordering is correct, because most of the mass of the cone sits close to the axis, where \(r\) is small.

Answer

\[ I_{z} = \frac{\pi k h^{5}}{10} = \frac{3}{10} m h^{2}, \qquad m = \frac{\pi k h^{3}}{3}. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(k\)The constant density of the solid, in mass per unit volume.
\(z = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}\)The cone surface: its height above the \(xy\)-plane equals the distance from the \(z\)-axis.
\(h\)The height of the cone. The base radius also equals \(h\), because the surface reaches \(z = h\) at \(r = h\).
\(r, \theta, z\)Cylindrical coordinates: \(r\) is the distance from the \(z\)-axis, \(\theta\) the angle in the \(xy\)-plane, \(z\) the height.
\(dV\)The volume element \(r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta\). The extra factor \(r\) appears because a shell of radius \(r\) has circumference \(2\pi r\).
\(I_{z}\)The moment of inertia about the \(z\)-axis, \(\iiint (x^{2} + y^{2}) \rho \, dV\). It measures resistance to rotation about that axis.
\(m\)The mass of the solid, \(\iiint \rho \, dV\).
shellThe thin cylindrical wall of radius \(r\), thickness \(dr\), and height \(h - r\) that the integral adds up.

Verification

Three scripts confirm the result.

  • part1_step1.py integrates the shell form \(2\pi k r^{3}(h - r)\) with mpmath at \(h = 1\), \(k = 1\) and obtains \(0.314159265358979\), which equals \(\pi/10\). It also samples four million points in the enclosing cylinder and reports \(0.314157\), a deviation of \(-0.01\) standard errors from \(\pi/10\).
  • part1_step2.py repeats the derivation in SymPy. It prints \(k r^{3}(h - r)\) after the \(z\)-integral, \(h^{5}k/20\) after the \(r\)-integral, and \(\pi h^{5} k/10\) after the \(\theta\)-integral. It also confirms \(m = \pi h^{3} k/3\) and \(I_{z}/m = 3h^{2}/10\).
  • part1_step3.py repeats the quadrature at \(h = 2\) and \(k = 3\). It obtains \(30.159289474462\) from both the quadrature and the closed form, and the same value from \(3mh^{2}/10\). The relative difference is below \(10^{-25}\).
