Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 29

Six Orders of Integration over an Elliptic Paraboloid Cap

Problem

Express the integral \(\iiint_{E} f(x,y,z)\,dV\) as an iterated integral in six different ways. The region \(E\) is the solid that is bounded by the two surfaces

\[ y = 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}, \qquad y = 0. \]

The integrand stays general, so the whole task is to get six sets of limits right.

Reading the solid

The surface \(y = 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}\) is an elliptic paraboloid. It opens toward the negative \(y\)-direction and its apex is the point \((0,4,0)\). The second surface is the plane \(y = 0\). The two surfaces meet where

\[ 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2} = 0, \qquad \text{that is} \qquad x^{2} + 4z^{2} = 4. \]

The rim is an ellipse with semi-axis \(2\) along \(x\) and semi-axis \(1\) along \(z\). Its area is \(2\pi \approx 6.283\). The solid \(E\) is the closed region above that ellipse and below the cap.

Cut \(E\) with the horizontal plane \(y = y_{0}\). A point of the cut satisfies \(x^{2} + 4z^{2} \le 4 - y_{0}\), so the cut is an ellipse with semi-axes \(\sqrt{4-y_{0}}\) and \(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y_{0}}\), and its area is \(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(4-y_{0}\right)\). At \(y = 0\) the cut is the base. At \(y = 4\) the cut shrinks to the apex \((0,4,0)\).

The volume adds the cut areas from \(y = 0\) to \(y = 4\):

\[ V = \int_{0}^{4} \frac{\pi}{2}\left(4-y\right) dy = \frac{\pi}{2}\left[4y - \frac{y^{2}}{2}\right]_{0}^{4} = \frac{\pi}{2}\cdot 8 = 4\pi \approx 12.566. \]

Two flat shadows carry the six orders. The projection of \(E\) on the \(xy\)-plane is the region under the parabola \(y = 4 - x^{2}\) between \(x = -2\) and \(x = 2\), shown in panel (a) below. The base is the ellipse \(x^{2} + 4z^{2} \le 4\) in the \(xz\)-plane, shown in panel (b). Each order reads one shadow, and the third variable is then a fibre.

Solution

Step 1 — Count the six orders

The inner integral may run over \(x\), over \(y\), or over \(z\). After that choice two variables remain, and the middle integral takes one of them. The count is \(3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6\). The six orders group by the variable that is integrated first:

  • first \(y\): \(dy\,dx\,dz\) and \(dy\,dz\,dx\);
  • first \(x\): \(dx\,dy\,dz\) and \(dx\,dz\,dy\);
  • first \(z\): \(dz\,dy\,dx\) and \(dz\,dx\,dy\).

Each order needs only the two shadows above. The base ellipse fixes the pair of variables that stay in the \(xz\)-plane. The cap fixes the pair that stays in a vertical plane.

Step 2 — Integrate \(y\) first

Order 1: \(dy\,dx\,dz\). Fix \(z\) first. The base ellipse gives \(x^{2} \le 4 - 4z^{2}\), that is \(|x| \le 2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\). Above each point of the base the fibre in \(y\) runs from the plane \(y = 0\) to the cap:

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}^{2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}-4z^{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dx\,dz. \]

Order 2: \(dy\,dz\,dx\). Fix \(x\) first. The same ellipse gives \(z^{2} \le \frac{1}{4}\left(4-x^{2}\right)\), that is \(|z| \le \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}\). The fibre in \(y\) is unchanged:

\[ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}-4z^{2}} f(x,y,z)\,dy\,dz\,dx. \]

Step 3 — Integrate \(x\) first

Order 3: \(dx\,dy\,dz\). Fix \(z\), then \(y\). The shadow on the \(yz\)-plane is bounded by the line \(y = 4 - 4z^{2}\), so \(0 \le y \le 4 - 4z^{2}\). For fixed \(y\) inside that range the \(x\)-fibre runs between the two halves of the ellipse:

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{4-4z^{2}}\int_{-\sqrt{4-4z^{2}-y}}^{\sqrt{4-4z^{2}-y}} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz. \]

Order 4: \(dx\,dz\,dy\). Fix \(y\) first. Every horizontal cut obeys \(0 \le y \le 4\). The cut shrinks as \(y\) grows: its \(z\)-half-width is \(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y}\), and its \(x\)-half-width is \(\sqrt{4-y-4z^{2}}\). Those two widths give the order \(dx\,dz\,dy\):

\[ \int_{0}^{4}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y}}\int_{-\sqrt{4-y-4z^{2}}}^{\sqrt{4-y-4z^{2}}} f(x,y,z)\,dx\,dz\,dy. \]

Step 4 — Integrate \(z\) first

Order 5: \(dz\,dy\,dx\). The \(x\)-range is the full width of the base, \(-2 \le x \le 2\). The projection on the \(xy\)-plane gives \(0 \le y \le 4 - x^{2}\). The \(z\)-fibre has half-length \(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}-y}\):

\[ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}-y}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}-y}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dy\,dx. \]

Order 6: \(dz\,dx\,dy\). Fix \(y\) first. The widest \(x\) occurs at \(z = 0\), so \(|x| \le \sqrt{4-y}\) and then \(|z| \le \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y-x^{2}}\):

\[ \int_{0}^{4}\int_{-\sqrt{4-y}}^{\sqrt{4-y}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y-x^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y-x^{2}}} f(x,y,z)\,dz\,dx\,dy. \]

Step 5 — Check the six orders with \(f = 1\)

Set \(f = 1\). Every order must return the volume \(4\pi \approx 12.566\). One chain from each group shows the algebra.

Order 1, with the inner integral equal to the length of the \(y\)-fibre:

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}^{2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\left(4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}\right) dx\,dz = \int_{-1}^{1}\frac{32}{3}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{3/2} dz = \frac{32}{3}\cdot\frac{3\pi}{8} = 4\pi. \]

Order 3, with the inner integral equal to the length of the \(x\)-fibre:

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{4-4z^{2}} 2\sqrt{4-4z^{2}-y}\,dy\,dz = \int_{-1}^{1}\frac{4}{3}\left(4-4z^{2}\right)^{3/2} dz = 4\pi. \]

Order 5, with the inner integral equal to the length of the \(z\)-fibre:

\[ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}} \sqrt{4-x^{2}-y}\,dy\,dx = \frac{2}{3}\int_{-2}^{2}\left(4-x^{2}\right)^{3/2} dx = \frac{2}{3}\cdot 6\pi = 4\pi. \]

The two standard integrals are \(\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{3/2}dz = \frac{3\pi}{8}\) and \(\int_{-2}^{2}\left(4-x^{2}\right)^{3/2}dx = 6\pi\). A script prints both. All six orders return \(4\pi\), so the six sets of limits describe one solid.

Step 6 — Check a second integrand

Use \(f = x^{2}z^{2}\). The value is no longer a volume, so the check is stronger: the six orders must agree with each other and with an independent computation. The script prints \(1.047197551196598\) for every order.

The independent computation scales the base to the unit disk. Put \(x = 2u\), \(z = v\), and \(y = w\). The volume element becomes \(2\,du\,dv\,dw\), the integrand becomes \(4u^{2}v^{2}\), and the solid becomes \(u^{2} + v^{2} \le 1\) with \(0 \le w \le 4\left(1 - u^{2} - v^{2}\right)\). The \(w\)-integral gives the height \(4\left(1 - r^{2}\right)\), so the whole integral is \(32\) times a radial factor \(R\) and an angular factor \(A\):

\[ \iiint_{E} x^{2}z^{2}\,dV = 32\,R\,A, \]
\[ R = \int_{0}^{1} r^{5}\left(1 - r^{2}\right)dr, \qquad A = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}\theta\,\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta. \]

The script prints \(R = 1/24\) and \(A = \pi/4\), so

\[ 32 \cdot \frac{1}{24} \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{3} \approx 1.047. \]

This value agrees with all six orders, and it comes from a different set of limits, so the six sets of limits are correct.

Answer

The solid is \(E = \{(x,y,z) : x^{2} + 4z^{2} \le 4,\; 0 \le y \le 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}\}\). The six iterated integrals are grouped by the variable that is integrated first.

First \(y\):

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}^{2\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}-4z^{2}} f\,dy\,dx\,dz, \]
\[ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}-4z^{2}} f\,dy\,dz\,dx. \]

First \(x\):

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{4-4z^{2}}\int_{-\sqrt{4-4z^{2}-y}}^{\sqrt{4-4z^{2}-y}} f\,dx\,dy\,dz, \]
\[ \int_{0}^{4}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y}}\int_{-\sqrt{4-y-4z^{2}}}^{\sqrt{4-y-4z^{2}}} f\,dx\,dz\,dy. \]

First \(z\):

\[ \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{0}^{4-x^{2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}-y}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}-y}} f\,dz\,dy\,dx, \]
\[ \int_{0}^{4}\int_{-\sqrt{4-y}}^{\sqrt{4-y}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y-x^{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y-x^{2}}} f\,dz\,dx\,dy. \]

With \(f = 1\) every order gives the volume

\[ V = 4\pi \approx 12.566. \]

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(\iiint_{E} f\,dV\)The triple integral of the function \(f\) over the solid \(E\). It is a limit of sums of \(f\) times small volumes.
\(f(x,y,z)\)The integrand. It stays general here, so it is written \(f\) in the six final displays.
\(dV\)The volume element. In Cartesian coordinates \(dV = dx\,dy\,dz\), and the order of the three differentials fixes the order of the three integrations.
\(y = 4 - x^{2} - 4z^{2}\)The elliptic paraboloid cap. It opens toward negative \(y\) and its apex is \((0,4,0)\).
\(y = 0\)The base plane. The solid sits above this plane.
\(x^{2} + 4z^{2} = 4\)The rim, an ellipse. It is the curve where the cap meets the base plane.
semi-axisOne half of an axis of an ellipse. The base ellipse has semi-axis \(2\) along \(x\) and semi-axis \(1\) along \(z\).
\(\sqrt{4-y}\)The semi-axis along \(x\) of the horizontal cut at height \(y\). The other semi-axis is \(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4-y}\).
fibreThe straight segment of \(E\) above one point of a flat shadow. Its length is the value of the innermost integral at that point.
\(u, v, w\)Scaled variables for the second integrand: \(u = x/2\), \(v = z\), and \(w = y\). They turn the base ellipse into the unit disk.
\(r, \theta\)Polar coordinates in the unit disk of the scaled variables: \(u = r\cos\theta\) and \(v = r\sin\theta\).
\(R, A\)The radial factor and the angular factor of the scaled integral. Their product times \(32\) gives the value of the integral.
inner, middle, outerThe three positions in an iterated integral. The inner integral is evaluated first, the outer integral last.
\(\le\)Less than or equal to. The solid includes its boundary, which does not change the value of the integral.

Verification

Three step scripts and two figure scripts support this page. All runs use the bundled Python interpreter.

  • part1_step1.py prints the semi-axes of the base, the area \(2\pi\), the cut areas at heights \(0,1,2,3,4\), and the slice sum \(12.5663706143591725\) against \(4\pi = 12.566370614359173\). It also prints the \(y\)-fibre heights and four membership tests.
  • part1_step2.py evaluates each of the six orders with SymPy, one integral at a time. It prints the middle results \(\frac{32}{3}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{3/2}\), \(\frac{4}{3}\left(4-4z^{2}\right)^{3/2}\), and \(\frac{2}{3}\left(4-x^{2}\right)^{3/2}\), then \(4\pi\) for all six orders with a difference of \(0\) each, the two standard integrals \(\frac{3\pi}{8}\) and \(6\pi\), and the scaled factors \(\frac{1}{24}\) and \(\frac{\pi}{4}\) for the second integrand.
  • part1_step3.py runs an 80-node Gauss-Legendre rule on all six orders, with nodes pushed toward the ends of each interval. For \(f = 1\) the largest difference from \(4\pi\) is \(1.03\times 10^{-13}\). For \(f = x^{2}z^{2}\) the largest difference from \(\pi/3\) is \(2.00\times 10^{-15}\).
  • part1_figure1.py and part1_figure2.py write both PNG files and both Base64 sidecar files at \(170\) dpi with serif fonts, and print each path and its length.
