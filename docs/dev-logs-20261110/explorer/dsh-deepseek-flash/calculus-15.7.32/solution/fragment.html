Stewart, Calculus: Early Transcendentals · Section 15.7 · Exercise 32

Six Iterated Integrals for a Tetrahedron

Problem

Express the integral

\[ \iiint_{E} f(x,y,z) \, dV \]

as an iterated integral in six different ways. The solid \(E\) is bounded by the planes \(x = 2\), \(y = 2\), \(z = 0\), and \(x + y - 2z = 2\).

Reading the solid

Each plane cuts a face of the solid. A corner is the common point of three of the planes:

\[ x = 2,\ z = 0,\ x + y - 2z = 2 \ \Longrightarrow\ y = 0, \]
\[ y = 2,\ z = 0,\ x + y - 2z = 2 \ \Longrightarrow\ x = 0, \]
\[ x = 2,\ y = 2,\ z = 0 \ \Longrightarrow\ z = 0, \]
\[ x = 2,\ y = 2,\ x + y - 2z = 2 \ \Longrightarrow\ z = 1. \]

The corners are \(A = (2,0,0)\), \(B = (0,2,0)\), \(C = (2,2,0)\), and \(D = (2,2,1)\). The solid is the tetrahedron \(ABCD\). Its faces are the triangle \(ABC\) in the plane \(z = 0\), the triangle \(ACD\) in the plane \(x = 2\), the triangle \(BCD\) in the plane \(y = 2\), and the triangle \(ABD\) in the plane \(x + y - 2z = 2\).

Every point of the base has \(z = 0\) and \(x + y \ge 2\), and the apex \(D\) gives \(x + y - 2z = 2\). Thus \(E\) lies in the half-space \(x + y - 2z \ge 2\), together with \(x \le 2\), \(y \le 2\), and \(z \ge 0\). Solve the first inequality for \(z\):

\[ z \le \frac{x + y - 2}{2}. \]

The upper bound is negative when \(x + y < 2\), so the base is the triangle \(T = \{(x,y) : 0 \le x \le 2,\ 2 - x \le y \le 2\}\). The base has area \(2\) and the apex \(D\) has height \(1\), so the volume is \(2/3\).

Solution

Step 1 — Put \(z\) first

Fix a point \((x,y)\) of the base \(T\). The vertical fibre above it runs from the base to the sloping face:

\[ 0 \le z \le \frac{x + y - 2}{2}. \]

Integrate \(z\) first, then \(y\), then \(x\). The base \(T\) gives \(0 \le x \le 2\) and \(2 - x \le y \le 2\):

\[ \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{2-x}^{2} \int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx. \]

Swap the two outer integrations. The same triangle \(T\) reads \(0 \le y \le 2\) and \(2 - y \le x \le 2\):

\[ \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{2-y}^{2} \int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dx \, dy. \]

Step 2 — Put \(y\) first

Project \(E\) onto the \(xz\)-plane. The face \(x = 2\) and the face \(z = 0\) are vertical planes, and the sloping plane gives the lower limit for \(y\):

\[ x + y - 2z \ge 2 \ \Longrightarrow\ y \ge 2 + 2z - x. \]

The plane \(y = 2\) gives the upper limit. The projection is the triangle \(0 \le x \le 2\), \(0 \le z \le x/2\), because the lower limit must not exceed \(2\):

\[ \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{x/2} \int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z) \, dy \, dz \, dx. \]

Swap the two outer integrations again. The triangle \(0 \le x \le 2\), \(0 \le z \le x/2\) also reads \(0 \le z \le 1\) and \(2z \le x \le 2\):

\[ \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{2z}^{2} \int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f(x,y,z) \, dy \, dx \, dz. \]

Step 3 — Put \(x\) first

The solid is symmetric in \(x\) and \(y\): the planes \(x = 2\) and \(y = 2\) play the same role, and the sloping plane is symmetric under the exchange of \(x\) and \(y\). Project \(E\) onto the \(yz\)-plane instead. The sloping plane gives the lower limit for \(x\):

\[ x \ge 2 + 2z - y, \qquad x \le 2, \]

and the projection is the triangle \(0 \le y \le 2\), \(0 \le z \le y/2\). Hence

\[ \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{y/2} \int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z) \, dx \, dz \, dy. \]

Swap the two outer integrations. The triangle \(0 \le y \le 2\), \(0 \le z \le y/2\) also reads \(0 \le z \le 1\) and \(2z \le y \le 2\):

\[ \iiint_{E} f \, dV = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{2z}^{2} \int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f(x,y,z) \, dx \, dy \, dz. \]

Step 4 — Check the six orders with \(f = 1\)

Set \(f = 1\). Each iterated integral then returns the volume of \(E\), which is \(2/3\). Substitute \(f = 1\) in the first order and integrate:

\[ \int_{0}^{2} \int_{2-x}^{2} \frac{x + y - 2}{2} \, dy \, dx = \int_{0}^{2} \left[ \frac{(x + y - 2)^{2}}{4} \right]_{2-x}^{2} dx = \int_{0}^{2} \frac{x^{2}}{4} \, dx = \frac{2}{3}. \]

The middle integral uses \(y = 2\), where the numerator is \(x\), and \(y = 2 - x\), where the numerator is \(0\). All six orders give \(2/3\) (see the verification section).

Answer

The six orders of integration are

\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{2} \int_{2-x}^{2} \int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} f \, dz \, dy \, dx &&\hspace{2.4em}\int_{0}^{2} \int_{2-y}^{2} \int_{0}^{(x+y-2)/2} f \, dz \, dx \, dy \\[2pt] &\int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{x/2} \int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f \, dy \, dz \, dx &&\hspace{2.4em}\int_{0}^{1} \int_{2z}^{2} \int_{2+2z-x}^{2} f \, dy \, dx \, dz \\[2pt] &\int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{y/2} \int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f \, dx \, dz \, dy &&\hspace{2.4em}\int_{0}^{1} \int_{2z}^{2} \int_{2+2z-y}^{2} f \, dx \, dy \, dz \end{aligned} \]

Every order equals the volume \(2/3\) when \(f = 1\).

Notation

SymbolMeaning
\(\iiint_{E} f \, dV\)The triple integral of \(f\) over the solid \(E\), the limit of sums of \(f\) times small volumes.
\(E\)The tetrahedron with vertices \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\).
\(dV\)The volume element, \(dV = dz \, dy \, dx\) in any order of the three differentials.
\(A, B, C, D\)The four vertices of \(E\): \((2,0,0)\), \((0,2,0)\), \((2,2,0)\), and \((2,2,1)\).
\(x + y - 2z = 2\)The sloping face \(ABD\). It meets the base along the line \(x + y = 2\).
\(T\)The base triangle \(ABC\) in the plane \(z = 0\), \(0 \le x \le 2\), \(2 - x \le y \le 2\).
\((x+y-2)/2\)The height of the sloping face above the point \((x,y)\) of \(T\).
\(2 + 2z - x\)The value of \(y\) on the sloping face at fixed \(x\) and \(z\), obtained from \(x + y - 2z = 2\).

Verification

Three independent computations support the six orders.

  • part1_step1.py builds the tetrahedron from the four planes. The scalar triple product of the edges at \(A\) is \(-4\), so the volume is \(2/3 = 0.666666666667\). The base has area \(2\) and the apex has height \(1\), which gives the same volume. A Monte Carlo estimate is \(0.66758 \pm 0.00075\), which is \(1.23\) standard errors above \(2/3\).
  • part1_step2.py evaluates all six orders with SymPy. With \(f = 1\) every order gives \(2/3\). With \(f = xyz\) every order gives \(19/45\), and the inner \(z\)-integral is \(xy(x + y - 2)^{2}/8\).
  • part1_step3.py evaluates all six orders with 60-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature. With \(f = 1\) every order gives \(0.666666666666669\), with a spread of \(8.882 \times 10^{-16}\). With \(f = xyz\) the six orders give values from \(0.422222222222220\) to \(0.422222222222228\), a spread of \(7.827 \times 10^{-15}\). The exact value is \(19/45 = 0.422222222222222\). An mpmath computation of one order gives \(0.6666666666666667\).
